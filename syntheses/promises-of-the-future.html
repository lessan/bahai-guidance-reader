Promises of the Future: What the Writings Foretell for the Cause, for Humanity and for Particular Places

Last updated 13 October 2026

What this page covers

The Writings contain many promises about what is still to come: for the Faith itself, for the human race, and for particular places such as Ṭihrán, Mount Carmel, ‘Akká and the House of Bahá’u’lláh in Baghdad. This page gathers them at survey depth. Three narrower articles go further into three of them: under-these-banners (the welcome at ‘Akká), land-of-ta (the Kitáb-i-Aqdas on Ṭihrán) and stages-to-the-most-great-peace (the Lesser Peace, the Most Great Peace and the stages between them).

Each promise below is given in three parts:

  1. The passage, verbatim, with its author and the work or letter in which it appears.
  2. What authoritative texts say has been fulfilled or begun: the statements of Shoghi Effendi and the Universal House of Justice (and, where He comments on His own or His Father’s words, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá). Nothing is listed as fulfilled on this page’s own authority.
  3. What the texts leave open: timing, means, and conditions.

Where this page adds an observation of its own, it is marked Observation (this page). Pilgrims’ notes and historical narrative are labelled when used.

All quotations are copied from the texts held in this knowledge base; the file for each is listed in the references at the end.

How the Writings themselves frame prophecy

Bahá’u’lláh, in the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, quotes His own verses on the Land of Ṭá as examples of things foretold, and then states a general principle:

“All that hath been sent down hath and will come to pass, word for word, upon earth. No possibility is left for anyone either to turn aside or protest.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Epistle to the Son of the Wolf

Shoghi Effendi, writing to the Bahá’ís of Iran, quotes a verse of Bahá’u’lláh to the same effect:

“Such God-given promises and sublime glad-tidings that the Pen of the Most High and the divinely aided and inspired fingers of the Centre of the Covenant have inscribed will, according to the following blessed verse, be unquestionably fulfilled. How enthralling is His Word: ‘Whatsoever hath streamed forth from the Pen of the Most High hath either already occurred or shall assuredly come to pass. Not a single letter thereof will remain unrealized, for verily the fair-minded shall behold it established upon the throne of fulfilment.’” — Shoghi Effendi, letter to the Bahá’ís of Iran, in Fire and Light (compiled by the Universal House of Justice, 1986)

Two limits apply alongside that certainty. First, the dates are not given. A letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi in 1946, quoted by the Universal House of Justice on 29 July 1974, says of the two peaces:

“All we know is that the Lesser and the Most Great Peace will come—their exact dates we do not know.” — Letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi, 1946, quoted in a letter of the Universal House of Justice, 29 July 1974 (Peace compilation)

Second, some promises carry an explicit condition, such as “if it be His Will” in the verse on the ruler of Ṭihrán, or “Should success crown your enterprise” in the Tablets of the Divine Plan. Those conditions are noted where they occur.

The Kitáb-i-Aqdas also gives an example of how the authoritative texts record a fulfilment. Its paragraph 90 reads “We hear the lamentations of Berlin, though she be today in conspicuous glory.” The note to that paragraph in the authorised English edition reports:

“In God Passes By Shoghi Effendi states that the ‘oppressively severe treaty’ that was imposed on Germany following its defeat in the first World War ‘provoked “the lamentations [of Berlin]” which, half a century before, had been ominously prophesied.’” — Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 121

That is the pattern followed below: the verse, then the authoritative statement that it has come to pass, or the absence of one.

1. “Under these banners”: the arrival at ‘Akká

The passage

In the Lawḥ-i-Sayyáḥ, revealed in Adrianople before His exile to ‘Akká, Bahá’u’lláh described His coming arrival there:

“Upon Our arrival, We were welcomed with banners of light, whereupon the Voice of the Spirit cried out saying: ‘Soon will all that dwell on earth be enlisted under these banners.’” — Bahá’u’lláh, Lawḥ-i-Sayyáḥ, quoted in God Passes By, ch. XI; also in Bahá’í Sacred Writings, 3.27.1

Shoghi Effendi gives the setting. Bahá’u’lláh “as attested by Nabíl in his narrative, had, as far back as the first years of His banishment to Adrianople, alluded to that same city in His Lawḥ-i-Sayyáḥ, designating it as the ‘Vale of Nabíl,’ the word Nabíl being equal in numerical value to that of ‘Akká.” (God Passes By, ch. XI). The arrival itself, in 1868, was the start of what Bahá’u’lláh named the “Most Great Prison”.

What authoritative texts say

The Universal House of Justice quotes the passage in its letter of 28 November 2023, reviewing the first century of the Formative Age. It places it in an account of how the Holy Land became the centre of the Faith: “The spiritual potency of that land was immeasurably enhanced by His presence and the interment of His sacred remains and, soon after, those of His Herald, Himself a Manifestation of God. It is now the point to which every Bahá’í heart is drawn, the focal centre of their devotions, the goal of every aspiring pilgrim.”

The same letter describes the present extent of the community:

“This community—the people of Bahá, ardent lovers of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá—now millions strong, has today spread to some one hundred thousand localities in 235 countries and territories. It has emerged from obscurity to occupy its place on the world stage.” — Universal House of Justice, 28 November 2023

Neither Shoghi Effendi nor the House of Justice, in the texts held here, says that the promise “all that dwell on earth” has been fulfilled. The 2023 letter reports growth; it does not equate it with the promise.

What is left open

“Soon” is not dated. A parallel passage from ‘Abdu’l-Bahá describes the same end state in terms of a banner: “in this Dispensation of the Lord of Hosts all nations and peoples will enter beneath His shadow” (Some Answered Questions, ch. 12, ¶8, quoted more fully in §6 below). The fuller study of the Tablet, its date and the arrival is at under-these-banners.

2. “Let nothing grieve thee, O Land of Ṭá”

The passage

Kitáb-i-Aqdas ¶91–93, addressed to Ṭihrán, the city of Bahá’u’lláh’s birth:

“Let nothing grieve thee, O Land of Ṭá, for God hath chosen thee to be the source of the joy of all mankind. He shall, if it be His Will, bless thy throne with one who will rule with justice, who will gather together the flock of God which the wolves have scattered. Such a ruler will, with joy and gladness, turn his face towards, and extend his favors unto, the people of Bahá. He indeed is accounted in the sight of God as a jewel among men. Upon him rest forever the glory of God and the glory of all that dwell in the kingdom of His revelation.

”Rejoice with great joy, for God hath made thee ‘the dayspring of His light,’ inasmuch as within thee was born the Manifestation of His Glory. Be thou glad for this name that hath been conferred upon thee—a name through which the daystar of grace hath shed its splendor, through which both earth and heaven have been illumined.

“Erelong will the state of affairs within thee be changed, and the reins of power fall into the hands of the people. Verily, thy Lord is the All-Knowing. His authority embraceth all things. Rest thou assured in the gracious favor of thy Lord. The eye of His loving-kindness shall everlastingly be directed towards thee. The day is approaching when thy agitation will have been transmuted into peace and quiet calm. Thus hath it been decreed in the wondrous Book.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Aqdas, ¶91–93 (also Gleanings, LVI)

The notes to the Aqdas explain the name and the birth:

“’Ṭá’ is the initial letter of Ṭihrán, the capital of Iran. Bahá’u’lláh has often chosen to represent certain place names by reference to their initial letter. According to the abjad system of reckoning, the numerical value of Ṭá is nine, which equals the numerical value of the name Bahá.” — Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 122

Note 123 adds that “within thee was born the Manifestation of His Glory” refers to “the birth of Bahá’u’lláh in Ṭihrán on 12 November 1817.”

The passage contains four distinct promises: that Ṭihrán is chosen to be “the source of the joy of all mankind”; that a just ruler, favourable to the Bahá’ís, may come to its throne (“if it be His Will”); that “the reins of power” will “fall into the hands of the people”; and that its “agitation” will be “transmuted into peace and quiet calm”.

Bahá’u’lláh’s own later comment

In the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, Bahá’u’lláh quotes ¶91 and then adds: “These verses were revealed previously. Now, however, the following verse hath been sent down: ‘O God, my God! Bahá beseecheth Thee and imploreth Thee, by the lights of Thy countenance and the billows of the ocean of Thy Revelation, and the effulgent splendors of the Sun of Thine utterance, to aid the Sháh to be fair and equitable. If it be Thy wish, bless Thou, through him, the throne of authority and sovereignty.’” He then quotes ¶92–93 in full. The promise of a just ruler is therefore paired, in this later work, with a prayer that the reigning Sháh might himself become one.

Shoghi Effendi

In The Promised Day Is Come (1941), Shoghi Effendi reads ¶91 as a prophecy of a just king devoted to the Faith, placing it beside two other prophecies of Bahá’u’lláh:

“In the Lawḥ-i-Ra’ís He actually and categorically prophesies the rise of such a king: ‘Erelong will God raise up from among the kings one who will aid His loved ones. He, verily, encompasseth all things. He will instill in the hearts the love of His loved ones. This, indeed, is irrevocably decreed by One Who is the Almighty, the Beneficent.’ … In the Kitáb-i-Aqdas He visualizes in these words the elevation to the throne of His native city, ‘the Mother of the World’ and ‘the Dayspring of Light,’ of a king who will be adorned with the twin ornaments of justice and of devotion to His Faith” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come

In a letter to the Bahá’ís of Iran (translated in Fire and Light; undated in that source), Shoghi Effendi quotes ¶91 and ¶93 and a Tablet of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, and then states what is to come:

“Moreover, the following significant and soul-stirring words have been set down by the pen of the Centre of the Covenant: ‘Erelong will it be witnessed that the government of the native land of the Blessed Perfection will become the most honoured government of this world, and Írán will become the most prosperous of all lands. This indeed is a token of God’s bountiful favour, and verily in this is there a lesson unto every beholder.’” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted by Shoghi Effendi, letter to the Bahá’ís of Iran, in Fire and Light

“However, the essential thing is patience, fortitude, courage, and audacity. The day is approaching when that country will have turned into a blissful paradise, when the wronged ones of Persia will have become the pride of the world and the leaders of mankind. And this is a promise that will not be belied.” — Shoghi Effendi, same letter

The same letter explains why suffering comes first. Earlier in it he writes that the trials in Iran were “a preliminary measure for the fulfilment of His gracious pledge”, provided “so that the glory of His Cause may presently be unveiled before the assemblage of man and, in the days to come, His Faith may be rendered victorious and become securely established in that blessed land.”

The Universal House of Justice

The House of Justice’s letters to the Bahá’ís of Iran (authorised translations from Persian) repeatedly name Iran’s future:

“But you who have surrendered your hearts to the Blessed Beauty have no doubt of the bright future of Iran.” — Universal House of Justice, 21 March 2010, to the believers in the Cradle of the Faith

“In one of His blessed Tablets, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá states, ‘The future of Iran is of the utmost greatness, majesty, and glory, for it is the native land of the Blessed Beauty. All the nations of the earth will turn to and regard Iran with high esteem. Know of a certainty that it will make such progress as to dazzle the eyes of the mighty and the learned throughout the world.’ And in another instance He writes, ’’This house of sorrows will become a rose-garden one day; grieve not!’ Exert the utmost effort and sincerity in service to Iran and its people, and sorrow no more.’” — Universal House of Justice, 20 March 2021, to the friends of God in the sacred land of Iran

On what has begun, the House of Justice wrote on 24 October 2018:

“Consider, however, that many of those souls in the early years of the Cause did not live to see with their outer eyes the effects and results of their own sacrifices, while today, you witness with your very eyes the collective transformation taking place in your country and the removal of historic misunderstandings and suspicion about the Cause of God among many of your compatriots.” — Universal House of Justice, 24 October 2018, to the Bahá’ís of Iran

And in its Riḍván 2026 message it called the Bahá’ís of Iran “a golden example of a community that has long sheltered the flame of hope”, and added: “To so many of their compatriots they have been, and continue to be, a beacon of hope, a fount of compassion and insight, and trusted companions on the path of service.”

What is left open

  • The ruler. The promise is conditional (“if it be His Will”). No authoritative text in this corpus identifies such a ruler as having appeared.
  • “The reins of power fall into the hands of the people.” No authoritative interpretation of ¶93 was found in this corpus: neither Shoghi Effendi nor the House of Justice, in the texts held here, links it to a particular event. Observation (this page): readers sometimes connect it with Iran’s constitutional and later political changes; that connection is not made in the authoritative texts consulted, and this page does not make it.
  • The “source of the joy of all mankind”, “peace and quiet calm”, “the most prosperous of all lands”. All are stated as future. The House of Justice in 2018 describes a “collective transformation” under way in attitudes towards the Faith; it does not describe the promises as fulfilled.

The full study is at land-of-ta. For the present situation of the community in Iran, see SituationOfBahaisInIran.

3. Other places in Iran: the Land of Khá and the Land of Káf and Rá

The Aqdas addresses two further regions of Iran.

Khurásán

“O Land of Khá! We hear from thee the voice of heroes, raised in glorification of thy Lord, the All-Possessing, the Most Exalted. Blessed the day on which the banners of the divine Names shall be upraised in the kingdom of creation in My Name, the All-Glorious. On that day the faithful shall rejoice in the victory of God, and the disbelievers shall lament.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Aqdas, ¶94

Note 124: “A reference to the Iranian province of Khurásán and neighboring areas, which include the city of ‘Ishqábád (Ashkhabad).”

Kirmán

“O Land of Káf and Rá! We, verily, behold thee in a state displeasing unto God, and see proceeding from thee that which is inscrutable to anyone save Him, the Omniscient, the All-Informed; and We perceive that which secretly and stealthily diffuseth from thee. With Us is the knowledge of all things, inscribed in a lucid Tablet. Sorrow not for that which hath befallen thee. Erelong will God raise up within thee men endued with mighty valor, who will magnify My Name with such constancy that neither will they be deterred by the evil suggestions of the divines, nor will they be kept back by the insinuations of the sowers of doubt. With their own eyes will they behold God, and with their own lives will they render Him victorious. These, truly, are of those who are steadfast.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Aqdas, ¶164

Note 176 identifies Káf and Rá as “the first two consonants of Kirmán, the name of a city and province of Iran.” Note 177 explains the rebuke: it refers to “the intrigues of a group of Azalís, followers of Mírzá Yaḥyá … associated with the city of Kirmán”, who “not only sought to undermine the Faith, but involved themselves in political intrigues which culminated in the assassination of Náṣiri’d-Dín Sháh.”

What is left open

The notes identify the places and, for Kirmán, the cause of the rebuke. They do not say that either promise (“the banners of the divine Names”, “men endued with mighty valor”) has been fulfilled. Observation (this page): note 124 names ‘Ishqábád, where, as Shoghi Effendi records in God Passes By, the believers built “the first Mashriqu’l-Adhkár of the Bahá’í world”; the note itself makes no connection between that building and ¶94, and neither do the other authoritative texts consulted here.

4. The Holy Land: Carmel, Haifa and ‘Akká

The passage

Shoghi Effendi records that Bahá’u’lláh revealed the Tablet of Carmel during a visit to the mountain, “when His tent was pitched in the vicinity of the Carmelite Monastery” (God Passes By, ch. XI). It ends with this promise:

“Call out to Zion, O Carmel, and announce the joyful tidings: He that was hidden from mortal eyes is come! His all-conquering sovereignty is manifest; His all-encompassing splendor is revealed. Beware lest thou hesitate or halt. Hasten forth and circumambulate the City of God that hath descended from heaven, the celestial Kaaba round which have circled in adoration the favored of God, the pure in heart, and the company of the most exalted angels. … Ere long will God sail His Ark upon thee, and will manifest the people of Bahá who have been mentioned in the Book of Names.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Tablet of Carmel, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh

Earlier in the same Tablet: “Rejoice, for God hath in this Day established upon thee His throne, hath made thee the dawning-place of His signs and the dayspring of the evidences of His Revelation.”

Shoghi Effendi

Shoghi Effendi tied the fulfilment of the last sentence to a specific future. In his letter of 21 December 1939, written after he had transferred the remains of the Purest Branch and of his mother, Navváb, to Mount Carmel near the resting place of the Greatest Holy Leaf, he wrote that this spot,

“under the wings of the Báb’s overshadowing Sepulcher, and in the vicinity of the future Mashriqu’l-Adhkár which will be reared on its flank, is destined to evolve into the focal center of those world-shaking, world-embracing, world-directing administrative institutions, ordained by Bahá’u’lláh and anticipated by ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, and which are to function in consonance with the principles that govern the twin institutions of the Guardianship and the Universal House of Justice. Then, and then only, will this momentous prophecy which illuminates the concluding passages of the Tablet of Carmel be fulfilled: ‘Erelong will God sail His Ark upon thee (Carmel), and will manifest the people of Bahá who have been mentioned in the Book of Names.’” — Shoghi Effendi, 21 December 1939, This Decisive Hour

In the same letter he described the Holy Land as “a land which, geographically, spiritually and administratively, constitutes the heart of the entire planet”. In God Passes By he wrote of the Shrine of the Báb that it would not, “as the years go by, and the institutions revolving around the World Administrative Center of the future Bahá’í Commonwealth are gradually established, cease to manifest the latent potentialities with which that same immutable purpose has endowed it. Resistlessly will this Divine institution flourish and expand, however fierce the animosity which its future enemies may evince, until the full measure of its splendor will have been disclosed before the eyes of all mankind.” (God Passes By, ch. XVIII).

The glossary of the published volume Messages from the Universal House of Justice 2001–2022 adds: “Shoghi Effendi explains that the Ark in this passage refers to the Bahá’í Administrative Center on Mount Carmel and that the dwellers of the Ark are the members of the Universal House of Justice.” The glossary is an editorial aid in that volume; the Shoghi Effendi text it summarises was not found in this corpus.

What the Universal House of Justice says has been done

On 5 June 1975, announcing the construction of its Seat, the House of Justice quoted this passage of the 1939 letter and recorded that the International Archives building, “completed in the summer of 1957 as one of the last major achievements of Shoghi Effendi’s Guardianship”, “set the style for the remaining structures which, as described by him, were to be raised in the course of time in the form of a far-flung arc on the slope of Mount Carmel.”

On 24 May 2001, at the completion of the projects on Mount Carmel, it wrote:

“The majestic buildings that now stand along the Arc traced for them by Shoghi Effendi on the slope of the Mountain of God, together with the magnificent flight of garden terraces that embrace the Shrine of the Báb, are an outward expression of the immense power animating the Cause we serve. They offer timeless witness to the fact that the followers of Bahá’u’lláh have successfully laid the foundations of a worldwide community transcending all differences that divide the human race, and have brought into existence the principal institutions of a unique and unassailable Administrative Order that shapes this community’s life. In the transformation that has taken place on Mount Carmel, the Bahá’í Cause emerges as a visible and compelling reality on the global stage, as the focal centre of forces that will, in God’s good time, bring about the reconstruction of society, and as a mystic source of spiritual renewal for all who turn to it.” — Universal House of Justice, 24 May 2001

The letter of 28 November 2023 summarises the century: “The world spiritual and administrative centre of the Faith has been established across the twin holy cities of ‘Akká and Haifa.” It adds that in 1987 the efforts begun by Shoghi Effendi “culminated in the formal recognition of the status of the Bahá’í World Centre as the spiritual and administrative centre of the worldwide Bahá’í community”, and that “in 2019 construction began in ‘Akká, near the Riḍván Garden, on a fitting Shrine to serve as the final resting place of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá.”

What is left open

  • The Mashriqu’l-Adhkár on Carmel. Shoghi Effendi speaks of “the future Mashriqu’l-Adhkár which will be reared on its flank” (1939). In The Advent of Divine Justice (1938) he lists among future tasks “the building of the third Mashriqu’l-Adhkár of the Bahá’í world in the outskirts of the city of Ṭihrán, to be followed by the rise of a similar House of Worship in the Holy Land itself.” Neither is described as built in the texts consulted.
  • The remaining building on the Arc. The glossary of the 2001–2022 volume states: “The International Bahá’í Library is yet to be completed.”
  • “Then, and then only.” Shoghi Effendi ties the full sense of “sail His Ark” to the full functioning of the world administrative institutions. The House of Justice’s 2001 letter describes the buildings as “an outward expression” and the Cause as the “focal centre of forces that will, in God’s good time, bring about the reconstruction of society”. It does not declare the prophecy fulfilled.

A pilgrim’s account, labelled as such

Esslemont’s Bahá’u’lláh and the New Era records a description of the future of Haifa and ‘Akká that Mírzá Aḥmad Sohrab “recorded in his diary” as spoken by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá on 14 February 1914: “In the future the distance between ‘Akká and Haifa will be built up, and the two cities will join and clasp hands, becoming the two terminal section of one mighty metropolis.” This is a diary record of spoken words, not a Tablet, and it carries the weight of a pilgrim’s note only. It is included because it is often quoted, not as authority.

For the Arc projects and the World Centre, see ArcProject and BahaiWorldCentre.

5. The House of Bahá’u’lláh in Baghdad: abased, then exalted

The passage

“This is not the first humiliation inflicted upon My House. In days gone by the hand of the oppressor hath heaped indignities upon it. Verily, it shall be so abased in the days to come as to cause tears to flow from every discerning eye. Thus have We unfolded to thee things hidden beyond the veil, inscrutable to all save God, the Almighty, the All-Praised. In the fullness of time, the Lord shall, by the power of truth, exalt it in the eyes of all men. He shall cause it to become the Standard of His Kingdom, the Shrine round which will circle the concourse of the faithful.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, LVIII

What authoritative texts say has been fulfilled

This is one of the few promises in this survey where the authoritative texts state plainly that the first half has come to pass.

Shoghi Effendi, describing the seizure of the House by Shí‘ah opponents during the reconstruction ordered by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, and the case that went as far as the League of Nations, wrote of the dispute: “Though as yet unresolved through a combination of causes, religious as well as political, it has already remarkably fulfilled Bahá’u’lláh’s own prediction, and will, in its own appointed time, as the means for its solution are providentially created, fulfill the high destiny ordained for it by Him in His Tablets.” (God Passes By, ch. XXIII)

On 17 July 2013 the Universal House of Justice reported that the House had been destroyed:

“Thus, it was with utter shock and desolating grief that the Bahá’ís in Baghdad discovered on 26 June that the ‘most holy habitation’ of Bahá’u’lláh had been razed almost to the ground to make way for the construction of a mosque.” — Universal House of Justice, 17 July 2013

The same letter states: “Events over the last one hundred and fifty years have borne out that to which Bahá’u’lláh had thus alluded.” It closes with the second half of the promise:

“Bahá’u’lláh foresaw that the Most Great House would be subjected to terrible indignities, but He also stated that, no matter what adversities might arise, the Cause was divinely protected. Let every believer take heart. In a moving apostrophe addressed to that House, the Ancient Beauty asserted: ‘God hath, in the world of creation, adorned thee with the jewel of His remembrance. Such an ornament no man can, at any time, profane.’ He gave a promise, too, that, notwithstanding all that would befall the Blessed House, the future glory of that sanctified place was assured: ‘In the fullness of time, the Lord shall, by the power of truth, exalt it in the eyes of all men. He shall cause it to become the Standard of His Kingdom, the Shrine round which will circle the concourse of the faithful.’” — Universal House of Justice, 17 July 2013

What is left open

“In the fullness of time.” Neither the means nor the time of the exaltation is given.

6. The Most Great Peace and the stages toward it

The passages

Bahá’u’lláh, in 1890, to the Cambridge orientalist Edward G. Browne, as Browne recorded the interview:

“these fruitless strifes, these ruinous wars shall pass away and the ’Most Great Peace’ shall come.” — Bahá’u’lláh, as recorded by E. G. Browne; quoted by Shoghi Effendi in God Passes By, ch. XI

These words come from Browne’s account of the interview, not from a Tablet. Shoghi Effendi quotes them in God Passes By and The Promised Day Is Come, and the Universal House of Justice closes The Promise of World Peace (October 1985) with them: “we cite the emphatic promise of Bahá’u’lláh: ‘These fruitless strifes, these ruinous wars shall pass away, and the ’Most Great Peace’ shall come.’”

To the kings, having been refused, Bahá’u’lláh offered the lesser stage:

“Now that ye have refused the Most Great Peace, hold ye fast unto this, the Lesser Peace, that haply ye may in some degree better your own condition and that of your dependents.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, CXIX

And on the change of order:

“Soon will the present-day order be rolled up, and a new one spread out in its stead.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, IV

“By My Self! The day is approaching when We will have rolled up the world and all that is therein, and spread out a new order in its stead.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, CXLIII

‘Abdu’l-Bahá: the seven candles of unity

In a Tablet to Jane Elizabeth Whyte, whom Century of Light identifies as the wife of a former Moderator of the Free Church of Scotland, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá listed seven unities as “candles”:

“Behold how its light is now dawning upon the world’s darkened horizon. The first candle is unity in the political realm, the early glimmerings of which can now be discerned. The second candle is unity of thought in world undertakings, the consummation of which will erelong be witnessed. The third candle is unity in freedom which will surely come to pass. The fourth candle is unity in religion which is the cornerstone of the foundation itself, and which, by the power of God, will be revealed in all its splendor. The fifth candle is the unity of nations—a unity which in this century will be securely established, causing all the peoples of the world to regard themselves as citizens of one common fatherland. The sixth candle is unity of races, making of all that dwell on earth peoples and kindreds of one race. The seventh candle is unity of language, i.e., the choice of a universal tongue in which all peoples will be instructed and converse. Each and every one of these will inevitably come to pass, inasmuch as the power of the Kingdom of God will aid and assist in their realization.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, no. 15

In Some Answered Questions He describes the end state:

“One of the great events which is to occur in the Day of the manifestation of that Incomparable Branch is the hoisting of the Standard of God among all nations. By this is meant that all nations and kindreds will be gathered together under the shadow of this Divine Banner, which is no other than the Lordly Branch itself, and will become a single nation. Religious and sectarian antagonism, the hostility of races and peoples, and differences among nations will be eliminated. All men will adhere to one religion, will have one common faith, will be blended into one race and become a single people. All will dwell in one common fatherland, which is the planet itself.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Some Answered Questions, ch. 12, ¶7

He adds in the next paragraph: “in this Dispensation of the Lord of Hosts all nations and peoples will enter beneath His shadow.” (¶8). In the same paragraph ‘Abdu’l-Bahá states that another part of this prophecy, the gathering of the scattered Jewish people in the Holy Land, “has begun to materialize”; this page does not examine that part.

Shoghi Effendi on the stages

In The Promised Day Is Come (1941), after quoting the Some Answered Questions passage, Shoghi Effendi wrote: “This is the stage which the world is now approaching, the stage of world unity, which, as ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá assures us, will, in this century, be securely established.”

In “The Unfoldment of World Civilization” (11 March 1936) he defines the Most Great Peace as “a peace that must inevitably follow as the practical consequence of the spiritualization of the world and the fusion of all its races, creeds, classes and nations”, and describes the world commonwealth:

“The unity of the human race, as envisaged by Bahá’u’lláh, implies the establishment of a world commonwealth in which all nations, races, creeds and classes are closely and permanently united, and in which the autonomy of its state members and the personal freedom and initiative of the individuals that compose them are definitely and completely safeguarded.” — Shoghi Effendi, “The Unfoldment of World Civilization”, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh

The same letter continues with a world legislature, executive and tribunal, a world language, the reconciliation of science and religion, and the end of “Destitution on the one hand, and gross accumulation of ownership on the other”. It ends:

“A world federal system, ruling the whole earth and exercising unchallengeable authority over its unimaginably vast resources, blending and embodying the ideals of both the East and the West, liberated from the curse of war and its miseries, and bent on the exploitation of all the available sources of energy on the surface of the planet, a system in which Force is made the servant of Justice, whose life is sustained by its universal recognition of one God and by its allegiance to one common Revelation—such is the goal towards which humanity, impelled by the unifying forces of life, is moving.” — Shoghi Effendi, “The Unfoldment of World Civilization”

In a letter of 5 June 1947 he traced a process running from the First World War through the League of Nations and the birth of the United Nations, which “must, however long and tortuous the way, lead, through a series of victories and reverses, to the political unification of the Eastern and Western Hemispheres, to the emergence of a world government and the establishment of the Lesser Peace, as foretold by Bahá’u’lláh and foreshadowed by the Prophet Isaiah. It must, in the end, culminate in the unfurling of the banner of the Most Great Peace, in the Golden Age of the Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh.” (Citadel of Faith)

What the Universal House of Justice says has been fulfilled or begun

On the twentieth-century promise of the unity of nations. The House of Justice addressed this directly in its letter of 29 July 1974:

“It is true that ‘Abdu’l-Bahá made statements linking the establishment of the unity of nations to the twentieth century. For example: ‘The fifth candle is the unity of nations—a unity which, in this century, will be securely established, causing all the peoples of the world to regard themselves as citizens of one common fatherland.’ And, in The ‘Promised Day Is Come’, following a similar statement quoted from ‘Some Answered Questions’, Shoghi Effendi makes this comment: ‘This is the stage which the world is now approaching, the stage of world unity, which, as ‘Abdu’l-Bahá assures us, will, in this century, be securely established.’” — Universal House of Justice, 29 July 1974 (Peace compilation)

The letter then quotes the 1946 statement that the exact dates of the two peaces are not known.

At the end of the century the question came back. A letter written on behalf of the House of Justice by its Department of the Secretariat on 19 April 2001, replying to a believer who thought the Writings placed the Lesser Peace before the end of 2000, separated the two promises:

“In reviewing this material it becomes apparent that there is nothing in the authoritative Bahá’í writings to indicate that the Lesser Peace would be established before the end of the twentieth century. However, there are clear statements affirming that the unity of nations would be, in the words of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, ‘securely established’ during the twentieth century.” — Letter written on behalf of the Universal House of Justice, 19 April 2001 (Messages from the Universal House of Justice 1986–2001)

It then stated that this promise has been fulfilled:

“The twentieth century has been distinguished by the emergence of the unity of nations, to which both Shoghi Effendi and the House of Justice have referred in the enclosed document. This movement, the evidence of which accumulates with each passing day, stands in sharp contrast to the nationalistic tenor of the nineteenth century, and is an evidence of the spirit of a new age moving in the hearts of humankind. Viewed from this perspective, there can be no doubt that the promise of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá has been fulfilled, and the unity of nations securely established in the century now concluded. The further expansion and strengthening of this consciousness of world solidarity in the years to come will have their effect in the political realm, and will influence the evolution towards world government.” — Letter written on behalf of the Universal House of Justice, 19 April 2001

The same letter defines the term: “The unity of nations can be taken as that unity which arises from a recognition among the peoples of the various nations, that they are members of one common human family”, whereas “the Lesser Peace will initially be a political unity arrived at by decision of the various governments of the world.” It adds: “There may well be setbacks, and conflicts may erupt periodically, as humanity proceeds towards the emergence and consolidation of the Lesser Peace, giving rise in due course to the establishment of the Most Great Peace.” The Research Department memorandum enclosed with the letter describes the unity of nations as “one stage—and indeed a highly significant step—in the lengthy process of the establishment of the Lesser Peace.”

Century of Light (2001), a review of the twentieth century which the House of Justice describes in its foreword as “prepared under our supervision” and commends to the friends, goes through the seven candles one by one at the century’s end. It says:

“While it will be decades—or perhaps a great deal longer—before the vision contained in this remarkable document is fully realized, the essential features of what it promised are now established facts throughout the world. In several of the great changes envisioned—unity of race and unity of religion—the intent of the Master’s words is clear and the processes involved are far advanced, however great may be the resistance in some quarters.” — Century of Light, ch. XI

On “unity in the political realm” it says that “Shoghi Effendi has explained that the reference is to unity which sovereign states achieve among themselves, a developing process the present stage of which is the establishment of the United Nations.” On “unity of nations” it says that the Master’s promise “looked forward to today’s widespread acceptance among the peoples of the world of the fact that, however great the differences among them may be, they are the inhabitants of a single global homeland.” Century of Light is a document the House of Justice supervised and commended, not one of its own letters; its reading agrees with the 19 April 2001 letter above.

On the stages. A letter written on behalf of the House of Justice on 31 January 1985 sets the sequence:

“We know, however, that peace will come in stages. First, there will come the Lesser Peace, when the unity of nations will be achieved, then gradually the Most Great Peace—the spiritual as well as social and political unity of mankind, when the Bahá’í World Commonwealth, operating in strict accordance with the laws and ordinances of the Most Holy Book of the Bahá’í Revelation, will have been established through the efforts of the Bahá’ís.” — Letter written on behalf of the Universal House of Justice, 31 January 1985 (Peace compilation)

The same letter says the Lesser Peace “will initially be a political unity arrived at by decision of the governments of various nations; it will not be established by direct action of the Bahá’í community.”

On the present. The letter of 28 November 2023 describes “the movement of the world towards Bahá’u’lláh’s vision” as advancing “in countless halting steps, in occasional dramatic leaps, and with intermittent stretches where progress stalls or is even reversed”, and states:

“None can anticipate precisely what course the forces of disintegration are destined to take, what violent convulsions will yet assail humanity in this travailing age, or what obstacles and opportunities may arise, until the process reaches its culmination in the appearance of that Great Peace that will signalize the arrival of the stage when, recognizing the unity and wholeness of humankind, the nations will ‘put away the weapons of war, and turn to the instruments of universal reconstruction’. One thing, however, is certain: The process of integration will also accelerate, knitting together ever more closely the efforts of those who are learning to translate Bahá’u’lláh’s teachings into reality with those in the wider society who seek justice and peace.” — Universal House of Justice, 28 November 2023

What is left open

The dates of both peaces (“their exact dates we do not know”); the course of the “forces of disintegration”; and, for the Lesser Peace, the point at which the Faith will have “a direct influence on it”, which the 31 January 1985 letter places “later on, in God’s good time”. The full treatment of the stages is at stages-to-the-most-great-peace; the concept page is MostGreatAndLesserPeace.

7. The future of the Cause itself: from obscurity to a world commonwealth

The passages

Shoghi Effendi set out the stages through which the Faith itself must pass. The fullest list is in The Advent of Divine Justice (25 December 1938), among “opportunities of the distant future”:

“the multitudinous issues that must be faced, the obstacles that must be overcome, and the responsibilities that must be assumed, to enable a sore-tried Faith to pass through the successive stages of unmitigated obscurity, of active repression, and of complete emancipation, leading in turn to its being acknowledged as an independent Faith, enjoying the status of full equality with its sister religions, to be followed by its establishment and recognition as a State religion, which in turn must give way to its assumption of the rights and prerogatives associated with the Bahá’í state, functioning in the plenitude of its powers, a stage which must ultimately culminate in the emergence of the worldwide Bahá’í Commonwealth, animated wholly by the spirit, and operating solely in direct conformity with the laws and principles of Bahá’u’lláh.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Advent of Divine Justice

In God Passes By (1944) he gives the same sequence in shorter form, describing the Faith’s evolution “through the successive stages of repression, of emancipation, of recognition as an independent Revelation, and as a state religion” which “must lead to the establishment of the Bahá’í state and culminate in the emergence of the Bahá’í World Commonwealth” (ch. XXIV). In The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh he writes that Spiritual Assemblies will in time become Houses of Justice, adding “those powers, duties, and prerogatives necessitated by the recognition of the Faith of Bahá’u’lláh, not merely as one of the recognized religious systems of the world, but as the State Religion of an independent and Sovereign Power.”

In his letter of 5 June 1947 he lists what the Formative Age will see, and what the Golden Age will see:

“During this Formative Age of the Faith, and in the course of present and succeeding epochs, the last and crowning stage in the erection of the framework of the Administrative Order of the Faith of Bahá’u’lláh—the election of the Universal House of Justice—will have been completed, the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, the Mother-Book of His Revelation, will have been codified and its laws promulgated, the Lesser Peace will have been established, the unity of mankind will have been achieved and its maturity attained, the Plan conceived by ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá will have been executed, the emancipation of the Faith from the fetters of religious orthodoxy will have been effected, and its independent religious status will have been universally recognized, whilst in the course of the Golden Age, destined to consummate the Dispensation itself, the banner of the Most Great Peace, promised by its Author, will have been unfurled, the World Bahá’í Commonwealth will have emerged in the plenitude of its power and splendor, and the birth and efflorescence of a world civilization, the child of that Peace, will have conferred its inestimable blessings upon all mankind.” — Shoghi Effendi, 5 June 1947, Citadel of Faith

What authoritative texts say has been fulfilled or begun

Recognition as an independent religion by an Islamic court. Shoghi Effendi records that on 10 May 1925 an Islamic appellate court in Egypt, ruling against three Bahá’ís, declared: “The Bahá’í Faith is a new religion, entirely independent, with beliefs, principles and laws of its own”. He calls this “the positive, the startling and indeed the historic assertion” and says it opened “a new chapter in the evolution of the Faith itself” (God Passes By, ch. XXIV).

Emergence from obscurity. The Universal House of Justice has stated that this stage has been passed. In its Riḍván 1984 message it said of the persecuted Bahá’ís of Iran: “Their heroic and exemplary steadfastness has been the mainspring in bringing the Cause out of obscurity”. On 17 December 1985, when the United Nations General Assembly adopted a resolution referring to the Bahá’ís of Iran, it cabled: “FOR FIRST TIME NAME PRECIOUS FAITH BAHÁ’U’LLÁH MENTIONED HIGHEST MOST WIDELY REPRESENTATIVE INTERNATIONAL FORUM YET ESTABLISHED” (the message is headed “Affirmation of Emergence from Obscurity”). Its Riḍván 1986 message states that the steadfastness of the Persian friends brought the Cause to the agenda of the General Assembly, “accomplishing its emergence from the obscurity which characterized and sheltered the first period of its life.” In 2023 it wrote that the community “has emerged from obscurity to occupy its place on the world stage.”

Items on the 1947 list. Two items have since been carried out, as recorded in the texts:

  • The election of the Universal House of Justice took place in 1963, the beginning of its published messages (Messages from the Universal House of Justice 1963–1986).
  • On codification, the introduction to the English Kitáb-i-Aqdas states that the Synopsis and Codification that Shoghi Effendi began was “continued on the basis of his work, and the resulting volume was released in 1973.”

Recognition at the World Centre. The 28 November 2023 letter records the 1987 “formal recognition of the status of the Bahá’í World Centre” by the civil authorities in Israel (see §4).

The Plan of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. On 26 March 2016 the House of Justice wrote that the Divine Plan “continues at the present time” and spoke of “future stages, one after another, reaching as far as the fringes of the Golden Age.” It is described as in progress, not executed.

What is left open

Emancipation from “the fetters of religious orthodoxy” in the countries Shoghi Effendi named (in 1938, “Persia, ‘Iráq, and Egypt”), universal recognition of independent status, recognition as a state religion, a Bahá’í state, and the World Bahá’í Commonwealth are all described as future in the texts consulted. The House of Justice’s 2023 letter expects the community to grow “through crisis and victory, with many an unexpected turn, through countless stages of the Formative and Golden Ages to the end of the Dispensation.”

8. America, Canada and the nations

The passages

‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s Tablets of the Divine Plan (1916–1917), addressed to the Bahá’ís of the United States and Canada, contain promises about the continent and its believers:

“Likewise, the continent of America is, in the eyes of the one true God, the land wherein the splendors of His light shall be revealed, where the mysteries of His Faith shall be unveiled, where the righteous will abide and the free assemble.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet 9 (Northeastern States)

“Exert yourselves; your mission is unspeakably glorious. Should success crown your enterprise, America will assuredly evolve into a center from which waves of spiritual power will emanate, and the throne of the Kingdom of God will, in the plenitude of its majesty and glory, be firmly established.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet 11 (Central States)

“The full measure of your success is as yet unrevealed, its significance still unapprehended. Erelong ye will, with your own eyes, witness how brilliantly every one of you, even as a shining star, will radiate in the firmament of your country the light of divine Guidance, and will bestow upon its people the glory of an everlasting life.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet 7 (United States and Canada)

“The moment this divine Message is carried forward by the American believers from the shores of America and is propagated through the continents of Europe, of Asia, of Africa and of Australasia, and as far as the islands of the Pacific, this community will find itself securely established upon the throne of an everlasting dominion. Then will all the peoples of the world witness that this community is spiritually illumined and divinely guided. Then will the whole earth resound with the praises of its majesty and greatness.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet 7

And of Canada:

“Again I repeat that the future of Canada, whether from a material or a spiritual standpoint, is very great. Day by day civilization and freedom shall increase. The clouds of the Kingdom will water the seeds of guidance which have been sown there.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet 13 (Canada and Greenland)

“The Destiny of America”

Shoghi Effendi closes The Advent of Divine Justice (25 December 1938) with a section titled “The Destiny of America”. He quotes words of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá from His American travels: “May this American Democracy be the first nation to establish the foundation of international agreement. May it be the first nation to proclaim the unity of mankind. May it be the first to unfurl the Standard of the Most Great Peace.” He then distinguishes two processes: the work of the Bahá’í community, and the course of the nation, which “whether through the agency of its government or otherwise, is gravitating, under the influence of forces that it can neither comprehend nor control, towards such associations and policies, wherein, as indicated by ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, her true destiny must lie.” He foresees a dark period first:

“Then, and only then, will the American nation, molded and purified in the crucible of a common war, inured to its rigors, and disciplined by its lessons, be in a position to raise its voice in the councils of the nations, itself lay the cornerstone of a universal and enduring peace, proclaim the solidarity, the unity, and maturity of mankind, and assist in the establishment of the promised reign of righteousness on earth.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Advent of Divine Justice

He also says of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s promises to the American believers that they are “so vibrant with promises that not even the triumphant consummation of the Seven Year Plan can fulfill”.

What the Universal House of Justice says

On 26 March 2016, at the centenary of the first of the Tablets, the House of Justice described them as “one of the mighty Charters of His Father’s Faith” and reviewed the century of response: “a grassroots stirring, an organic flowering, a resistless movement that has grown imperceptibly at times and at others in great surges to eventually embrace the entire world”. It also said: “You have written the story of the unfoldment of the Divine Plan on the scroll of its first century. Before you, beloved friends, lies stretched out the blank scroll of the future”.

On 22 July 2020, writing to the Bahá’ís of the United States, it said that “A moment of historic portent has arrived for your nation”, one that “holds significance not only for the destiny of America anticipated in the Sacred Writings, but also for the mission entrusted to your community by the hand of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá”. In 2023 it recalled that Shoghi Effendi in The Advent of Divine Justice “promised, however, that the time would come when they would be called upon to engage their fellow citizens in a process of working for the healing and betterment of their nation. That time has now come. And it has come not only for the Bahá’ís of America, but for the Bahá’ís of the world”.

Every nation

The same 2023 letter widens the frame beyond any one country: “Every people and every nation has a part to play in the next stage in the fundamental reconstruction of human society. All have unique insights and experiences to offer for the building of a unified world.”

What is left open

Shoghi Effendi says plainly that he cannot trace the course: “To trace the exact course which, in these troubled times and pregnant years, this nation will follow would be impossible.” The promise to America in Tablet 11 is conditional (“Should success crown your enterprise”). The House of Justice in 2020 refers to “the destiny of America anticipated in the Sacred Writings” as still ahead.

9. The Golden Age and a world civilization

The passages

Shoghi Effendi places the furthest promises in the Golden Age, the last of the three ages of the Dispensation. In “The Unfoldment of World Civilization” (1936) he writes:

“The emergence of a world community, the consciousness of world citizenship, the founding of a world civilization and culture—all of which must synchronize with the initial stages in the unfoldment of the Golden Age of the Bahá’í Era—should, by their very nature, be regarded, as far as this planetary life is concerned, as the furthermost limits in the organization of human society, though man, as an individual, will, nay must indeed as a result of such a consummation, continue indefinitely to progress and develop.” — Shoghi Effendi, “The Unfoldment of World Civilization”, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh

In the same letter he calls his own generation “the ‘generation of the half-light,’ living at a time which may be designated as the period of the incubation of the World Commonwealth envisaged by Bahá’u’lláh”, and warns: “We may well believe, we who are called upon to experience the operation of the dark forces destined to unloose a flood of agonizing afflictions, that the darkest hour that must precede the dawn of the Golden Age of our Faith has not yet struck.”

In 1947 he described the Divine Plan as “hastening thereby the advent of that Golden Age which must witness the proclamation of the Most Great Peace and the unfoldment of that world civilization which is the offspring and primary purpose of that Peace.” (Citadel of Faith). He also limited how far it is useful to look ahead: “Not ours, however, to unriddle the workings of a distant future, or to dwell upon the promised glories of a God-impelled and unimaginably potent Revelation. Ours, rather, the task to cast our eyes upon, and bend our energies to meet, the challenging requirements of the present hour.”

What the Universal House of Justice says

The House of Justice treats the Golden Age as ahead. In 2016 it prayed “to ensure still greater victories in meeting the challenges of future stages, one after another, reaching as far as the fringes of the Golden Age”, and quoted Shoghi Effendi’s words that the present tasks “may hasten ‘the advent of that Golden Age which must witness the proclamation of the Most Great Peace and the unfoldment of that world civilization which is the offspring and primary purpose of that Peace.’” In 2023 it described the Faith’s path as running “through countless stages of the Formative and Golden Ages to the end of the Dispensation.”

In its Riḍván 2026 message it names the source of the community’s hope: “When the world’s horizons darken, hope becomes a scarce and precious resource—but one with which the community of the Greatest Name has been richly blessed because of its conviction about the future of humanity and because of what it has learned from its own experience.”

What is left open

When the Golden Age begins is not stated. Shoghi Effendi says the “darkest hour” precedes it; the House of Justice describes the present as the Formative Age.

10. What the texts leave open, taken together

Across the promises surveyed, the authoritative texts hold a consistent pattern.

  1. The outcome is certain; the dates are not given. “All that hath been sent down hath and will come to pass” (Bahá’u’lláh); “their exact dates we do not know” (1946 letter on Shoghi Effendi’s behalf).
  2. Suffering often comes before fulfilment. Shoghi Effendi says this of Iran (trials as “a preliminary measure for the fulfilment of His gracious pledge”), of America (“The immediate future must … be dark and oppressive for that nation”) and of the world (“the darkest hour that must precede the dawn of the Golden Age”). The House of Justice in 2023 says no one “can anticipate precisely what course the forces of disintegration are destined to take”.
  3. Some promises are conditional. “If it be His Will” (the ruler of Ṭihrán); “Should success crown your enterprise” (America). The Tablets of the Divine Plan tie the American promises to the believers’ own action.
  4. Fulfilment is declared rarely and specifically. In the texts consulted, the authoritative statements of fulfilment are specific: the abasement of the House in Baghdad (Shoghi Effendi; the House of Justice, 2013); the unity of nations “securely established” in the twentieth century, as distinct from the Lesser Peace (letter on behalf of the House of Justice, 19 April 2001); the emergence from obscurity (House of Justice, 1984–2023); the recognition of the Faith as an independent religion by the Egyptian court (Shoghi Effendi); the Berlin prophecy (Shoghi Effendi, recorded in the Aqdas notes); the completed Arc buildings and terraces, described as “an outward expression” (House of Justice, 2001). The large promises (the Most Great Peace, Ṭihrán as “the source of the joy of all mankind”, “all that dwell on earth … under these banners”, the World Commonwealth) are described as future.
  5. The means include human effort. The 31 January 1985 letter says the Bahá’í World Commonwealth “will have been established through the efforts of the Bahá’ís”; the 1947 letter says the Divine Plan hastens the Golden Age; the 2016 letter says present labours “may hasten” it. Shoghi Effendi’s own counsel was to give attention to “the challenging requirements of the present hour”.

What could not be verified

  • Original Tablets not located in this corpus. The Lawḥ-i-Sayyáḥ is available here only in the English sentence quoted by Shoghi Effendi and in Bahá’í Sacred Writings; the Persian or Arabic original was not checked. The Tablet of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá quoted by Shoghi Effendi in Fire and Light (“Erelong will it be witnessed that the government of the native land…”) and the two Tablets quoted by the House of Justice on 20 March 2021 (“The future of Iran is of the utmost greatness…”, “This house of sorrows will become a rose-garden one day…”) are not identified by name or date in those sources.
  • The Shoghi Effendi letter in Fire and Light is not dated in the source text held here; it is identified only as a letter to the Bahá’ís of Iran in the section “From the Letters of Shoghi Effendi”.
  • Shoghi Effendi’s explanation of “the Ark” is reported in the editorial glossary of Messages from the Universal House of Justice 2001–2022; his own words on it were not found in this corpus.
  • The 19 April 2001 letter and its enclosed Research Department memorandum are quoted from Messages from the Universal House of Justice 1986–2001 (message 356, “The Lesser Peace”, signed by the Department of the Secretariat). The text of that volume in this knowledge base is a conversion that lower-cased most capital letters (“bahá’í”, “shoghi effendi”, “lesser peace”). The words quoted here match it letter for letter, but capitalisation of names, sentence openings and “Lesser Peace”/“Most Great Peace” was restored by this page and should be checked against the printed volume.
  • Kitáb-i-Aqdas ¶93 (“the reins of power fall into the hands of the people”) has no authoritative interpretation in this corpus.
  • Present-day status of the remaining projects (the Mashriqu’l-Adhkár on Mount Carmel, the Mashriqu’l-Adhkár near Ṭihrán, the International Bahá’í Library) is taken from the latest texts in the corpus; no web research was done for this page, and these facts may have changed.
  • The Browne interview is a visitor’s record of Bahá’u’lláh’s spoken words. Shoghi Effendi and the House of Justice both quote it as Bahá’u’lláh’s promise; the page follows them but notes its form.
