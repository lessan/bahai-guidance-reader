An Atom as the Sun

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

Several passages say that deeds weigh more in this Day than they would in another, and they give the reason as the Day itself. An atom is as the sun and a drop as the ocean because of when the atom and the drop are offered. The same Day that magnifies a small act also asks for an act worthy of it: Bahá’u’lláh calls the deed “matchless.” This page sets out both statements and how the texts hold them together. It is one facet of The greatness of this Day.

The existing article Promises of Assistance has a section, “The small made great”, on the gnat and the eagle and the ant and the elephant. Those passages say what confirmation does for a weak soul. This page does not repeat them. It asks why the Day itself changes what an act is worth.

The Day as the measure

A door open wider than heaven and earth

In The Advent of Divine Justice Shoghi Effendi quotes a series of passages from Bahá’u’lláh under the theme of the greatness of the Day of God. The Tablets they come from are not identified in that book. The first of the passages that bear on small deeds reads:

“This Day a door is open wider than both heaven and earth. The eye of the mercy of Him Who is the Desire of the worlds is turned towards all men. An act, however infinitesimal, is, when viewed in the mirror of the knowledge of God, mightier than a mountain. Every drop proffered in His path is as the sea in that mirror. For this is the Day which the one true God, glorified be He, hath announced in all His Books, unto His Prophets and His Messengers.” (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

The measure in this passage is “the mirror of the knowledge of God.” The act is not larger in itself; it is larger as God sees it. The passage ends with a reason (“For this is the Day…”) and the reason is that the Day was announced in all the earlier Scriptures. The size of the act follows from the standing of the Day.

An atom, a drop, a breath

The next passage in the same series states the equivalence directly:

“These days are God’s days, a moment of which ages and centuries can never rival. An atom, in these days, is as the sun, a drop as the ocean. One single breath exhaled in the love of God and for His service is written down by the Pen of Glory as a princely deed. Were the virtues of this Day to be recounted, all would be thunderstruck, except those whom thy Lord hath exempted.” (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

Two conditions are named for the breath: it is “in the love of God” and “for His service.” The text does not say that any breath counts. It also says the recording is done: the Pen of Glory writes the breath down. Shoghi Effendi places both passages in the same collection of extracts, and elsewhere in that collection is a shorter statement of the same comparison:

“A fleeting moment, in this Day, excelleth centuries of a bygone age.” (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

This sentence sets time against time. The atom and the drop compare sizes; this one compares a moment with centuries. The page quotes only the first sentence of the extract, which continues after an omission in Shoghi Effendi’s text.

One righteous work and the acts of centuries

In the Kitáb-i-Íqán Bahá’u’lláh is explaining the “Day of Resurrection,” and has just argued that no day is greater than this Day. He writes:

“One righteous work performed in this Day, equaleth all the virtuous acts which for myriads of centuries men have practiced”

The sentence in the Íqán continues with a correction: “nay, We ask forgiveness of God for such a comparison! For verily the reward which such a deed deserveth is immensely beyond and above the estimate of men.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 153) The comparison with centuries of virtue is offered and then withdrawn as too small. Here the Day is the stated ground: the argument just before it is that nothing greater than this Day can be conceived.

A jewel that cannot be lost

“If one speck of a jewel be lost and buried beneath a mountain of stones, and lie hidden beyond the seven seas, the Hand of Omnipotence would assuredly reveal it in this Day, pure and cleansed from dross.” (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 111; source Tablet not identified)

This passage is about what is hidden or lost, not about deeds as such. It adds that in this Day a small thing buried out of sight is not forgotten. Read next to the passages above, it says that the one who offers a small thing is not left unrecorded.

The deed that the Day asks for

Matchless

“SAY: O men! This is a matchless Day. Matchless must, likewise, be the tongue that celebrateth the praise of the Desire of all nations, and matchless the deed that aspireth to be acceptable in His sight. The whole human race hath longed for this Day, that perchance it may fulfill that which well beseemeth its station, and is worthy of its destiny.” (Gleanings XVI.1)

Here the argument runs the other way. Because the Day has no equal, the tongue and the deed offered in it “must” also have none. The word is a requirement, and the deed is one that “aspireth to be acceptable.” The sentence about the human race gives the standard: the Day is the occasion on which humanity can do what “well beseemeth its station.”

The two sets of passages do not contradict each other. The first says that the Day raises the worth of an atom, a drop, a breath. The second says that the Day calls for an offering to match it. The texts give no figure for either and do not set one against the other.

Goodly deeds as the hosts of God

“In this Most Great Revelation goodly deeds and a praiseworthy character are regarded as the hosts of God, likewise is His blessed and holy Word. These hosts are the lodestone of the hearts of men and the effective means for unlocking doors. Of all the weapons in the world this is the keenest.” (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 17.91)

This passage says what deeds do in this Revelation. They are called “hosts of God,” next to His Word, and are said to draw hearts and to unlock doors. The setting is the Tablet’s instruction to “center your attention unceasingly upon that which will cause the Word of God to be exalted.” In the Tablet, a deed is an instrument of the Cause, which is why the Day’s greatness applies to it.

Detachment and the Name of the Lord

“SHOULD any man, in this Day, arise and, with absolute detachment from all that is in the heavens and all that is on the earth, set his affections on Him Who is the Dayspring of God’s holy Revelation, he will, verily, be empowered to subdue all created things, through the potency of one of the Names of the Lord, his God, the All-Knowing, the All-Wise. Know thou of a certainty that the Daystar of Truth hath, in this Day, shed upon the world a radiance, the like of which bygone ages have never witnessed.” (Gleanings CXLIX)

The capacity here is given to one person (“any man”) on condition of “absolute detachment” and love for the Manifestation, and it comes “through the potency of one of the Names of the Lord.” The empowerment is the Lord’s. The last sentence again gives the Day’s radiance as the reason.

A related statement is in Gleanings CIX: “The heights which, through the most gracious favor of God, mortal man can attain, in this Day, are as yet unrevealed to his sight.” (Gleanings CIX.1) It says the limit of what a person can reach in this Day is not yet known, and that what can be reached comes “through the most gracious favor of God.”

Anticipations before the Báb, and ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s restatement

The Báb, through ‘Abdu’l-Bahá

Shoghi Effendi quotes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá quoting the Báb:

“Should a tiny ant desire, in this day, to be possessed of such power as to be able to unravel the abstrusest and most bewildering passages of the Qur’án, its wish will no doubt be fulfilled, inasmuch as the mystery of eternal might vibrates within the innermost being of all created things.” (The Báb, as quoted by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá in a Tablet, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 69; the Báb’s original text not identified)

The Báb’s statement uses “in this day” for the same reason as Bahá’u’lláh’s. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s comment, in the same paragraph, is that if so helpless a creature can be given so subtle a capacity, “how much more efficacious must be the power released through the liberal effusions of the grace of Bahá’u’lláh.” The ant belongs to the pair of images already treated in Promises of Assistance; it is cited here for its words “in this day” and for the Báb as its speaker.

Siyyid Káẓim, quoted by Shoghi Effendi

In God Passes By Shoghi Effendi lists what the herald of the Bábí Dispensation and his successor foretold. Of Siyyid Káẓim-i-Rashtí, the disciple and successor of Shaykh Aḥmad-i-Aḥsá’í, he records the words said to his disciples near the end of his life:

“To have attained unto that Day of Days is to have attained unto the crowning glory of past generations, and one goodly deed performed in that age is equal to the pious worship of countless centuries.” (Siyyid Káẓim-i-Rashtí, quoted in God Passes By, GPB VI.20)

Siyyid Káẓim was a leader of the Shaykhí school before the Báb. He is not a Bahá’í author, and his words are not Bahá’í scripture. Shoghi Effendi cites them as a foretelling. What the passage shows is that the idea of one deed outweighing centuries was said of the coming age before it came; the authority for the idea in this page rests on Bahá’u’lláh’s own words above, in the Íqán and in the Tablets quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá: the drop and the mote

“May your hearts ever receive inspiration from the denizens of the Concourse on high. May the drop become as the great sea; may the mote dazzle as the shining sun.” (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Additional Tablets, Extracts and Talks, ATET 70.2)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá turns Bahá’u’lláh’s comparison into a prayer for his hearers. The prayer is made as a wish (“May the drop become…”), and it is joined to inspiration from the Concourse on high.

Two talks on sowing

In two talks recorded in notes, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá speaks of the present as a time of sowing. In a talk in The Promulgation of Universal Peace he says: “These are the days of seed sowing. These are the days of tree planting.” (Promulgation, PUP 3.6) In a talk in Paris Talks he says: “Today the seed is sown, the grain falls upon the earth, but behold the day will come when it shall rise a glorious tree and the branches thereof shall be laden with fruit.” (Paris Talks 21.6) These are notes of spoken words, not texts he wrote. They restate, in the image of a seed, that the small act belongs to this period and that its fruit comes later. They add nothing the written texts above do not also say, so this page does not rely on them for any point.

How this facet connects

  • The Creative Word: the speck of dust that becomes a sun and the dewdrop that becomes an ocean appear in the same Tablet extract as the jewel passage, as things Bahá’u’lláh says is in His power. That facet gives the source of the small thing’s capacity, which this page takes as given.
  • Envied by the Concourse on High: ‘Abdu’l-Bahá links the prayer for the drop and the mote to inspiration from the Concourse on high. That facet shows who watches the deed.
  • Grace Poured Out on All: the Day’s favor is on all men, and the open door in the first passage is for “all men.” That facet covers the gift; this one covers what is returned.
  • The Day Can Be Missed: the Íqán passage on one righteous work comes in an argument against those who deprive themselves of the Day’s grace. That facet covers the loss; this one the gain.
  • Promises of Assistance: explains confirmation of the weak and the conditions attached. This page gives the other ground, the Day.
  • “Dearly Beloved Friends”: The Advent of Divine Justice Compilation, Traced: traces the compilation in which Shoghi Effendi gathered the extracts quoted from ADJ 110–111 here.

What the sources do not settle

  • The Tablets from which the ADJ 110–111 extracts come are not identified in this corpus, so the exact wording around them, and their addressees, are not known here.
  • The Báb’s words quoted by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá are known only through that chain; the Báb’s own text is not identified.
  • The texts compare worth (atom and sun, drop and ocean, one work and centuries) but give no rule for measuring a particular act. They name conditions (”in the love of God and for His service,“ detachment) without a list of which acts qualify as ”matchless.“
  • The Íqán passage compares one work to centuries and then withdraws the comparison as too small; the sources do not say whether a number is meant either way.
  • The two talks on seed sowing are recorded notes; the original wording of the talks is not in this corpus.

For songwriting

Singable phrases

  1. ”An atom, in these days, is as the sun, a drop as the ocean.“ (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in ADJ 110)
  2. ”One single breath exhaled in the love of God“ (ADJ 110)
  3. ”written down by the Pen of Glory as a princely deed“ (ADJ 110)
  4. ”This Day a door is open wider than both heaven and earth.“ (ADJ 110)
  5. ”Every drop proffered in His path is as the sea“ (ADJ 110)
  6. ”A fleeting moment, in this Day, excelleth centuries“ (ADJ 110)
  7. ”This is a matchless Day.“ (Gleanings XVI.1)
  8. ”matchless the deed that aspireth to be acceptable in His sight“ (Gleanings XVI.1)
  9. ”One righteous work performed in this Day“ (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 153)
  10. ”May the drop become as the great sea“ (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, ATET 70.2)
  11. ”may the mote dazzle as the shining sun“ (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, ATET 70.2)
  12. ”the Hand of Omnipotence would assuredly reveal it in this Day“ (ADJ 111)
  13. ”goodly deeds and a praiseworthy character are regarded as the hosts of God“ (TB 17.91)

Concrete images

  • An atom beside the sun; a drop beside the ocean; an act beside a mountain, seen in a mirror.
  • A single breath written down by the Pen of Glory.
  • A door open wider than heaven and earth.
  • A speck of a jewel buried under stones beyond the seven seas, brought up clean.
  • A drop and a mote, wished into a sea and a sun.
  • A seed in the ground (from the two talks, if used with care).

A possible emotional arc

  1. Smallness admitted: the singer is one atom, one drop (ADJ 110, ”An atom, in these days“).
  2. The door: this Day is open wider than heaven and earth, for all men (ADJ 110, ”a door is open“).
  3. The weight: one breath, one moment, one deed is counted by God (ADJ 110; Íqán KI 153).
  4. The ask: a deed worthy of the Day, ”matchless“ (Gleanings XVI.1).
  5. The wish: May the drop become the sea (ATET 70.2).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not say any act, whatever the motive, equals centuries. The passages name ”the love of God and… His service“ and detachment as the setting.
  • Do not give a figure or a date. ”Myriads of centuries“ is a comparison the Íqán itself withdraws as too small.
  • Do not attribute the atom and drop passages to a named Tablet; the source Tablet is not identified. Say ”Bahá’u’lláh“ only, or ”Bahá’u’lláh, as quoted by Shoghi Effendi.“
  • Do not attribute Siyyid Káẓim’s words to Bahá’u’lláh or to Shoghi Effendi. He is not a Bahá’í author.
  • Do not present the seed-sowing talks as written Tablets of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá.
  • Do not say the person’s own power makes the deed great; the texts credit the Day, the favor of God and the ”potency of one of the Names of the Lord.“
  • Do not make ”matchless“ a boast. In the text it is a requirement placed on the tongue and the deed, not a description of the singer.
