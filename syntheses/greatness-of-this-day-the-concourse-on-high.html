Envied by the Concourse on High

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

The Writings describe the inhabitants of the unseen world looking at this Day. The Concourse on high, the dwellers of Paradise and the holy ones of past ages are said to cry out over the earth, to direct their gaze at the believers, to long to return, and to envy. Bahá’u’lláh says the Day has no peer even among the denizens of the Concourse on high. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá says the holy ones of earlier ages asked for one moment of it and did not receive it. From these statements the texts draw two consequences for those who are alive now: thankfulness for a bounty they did not earn, and a responsibility to use it.

This page sets out those texts. It is one facet of The greatness of this Day. The separate promise that the Concourse on high will assist those who arise is treated in Promises of Assistance and is not repeated here.

The unseen world speaks about this Day

The earth, the realm of glory and the cups of Paradise

Two passages in the Gleanings give speech to the unseen world. In the first, Bahá’u’lláh writes:

“This is the Day whereon the unseen world crieth out: “Great is thy blessedness, O earth, for thou hast been made the footstool of thy God, and been chosen as the seat of His mighty throne.” The realm of glory exclaimeth: “Would that my life could be sacrificed for thee, for He Who is the Beloved of the All-Merciful hath established His sovereignty upon thee, through the power of His Name that hath been promised unto all things, whether of the past or of the future.”” (Gleanings XIV.6)

The congratulation is addressed to the earth, and the reason given is that God’s sovereignty is established on it. The realm of glory wishes it could give its life for the earth. The wish runs from the higher world toward the lower one, the reverse of the usual direction of sacrifice.

Further on in the same chapter of the Gleanings:

“This is the Day whereon He Who is the Revealer of the names of God hath stepped out of the Tabernacle of glory, and proclaimed unto all who are in the heavens and all who are on the earth: “Put away the cups of Paradise and all the life-giving waters they contain, for lo, the people of Bahá have entered the blissful abode of the Divine Presence, and quaffed the wine of reunion, from the chalice of the beauty of their Lord, the All-Possessing, the Most High.”” (Gleanings XIV.13)

Here the dwellers of the heavens are told that what Paradise offers has been exceeded by what the people of Bahá have received. The text does not say Paradise is lessened. It says the people of Bahá are at the Presence itself, and that the cups of Paradise are no longer needed by comparison.

In Gems of Divine Mysteries Bahá’u’lláh makes the same comparison as a statement:

“Know then that the paradise that appeareth in the day of God surpasseth every other paradise and excelleth the realities of Heaven.” (Gems of Divine Mysteries, GDM 58)

The Day that has no peer

In Days of Remembrance Bahá’u’lláh puts the Concourse on high among those who have not attained what is now given:

“Say: This is the Day for which the Pen of the Most High hath ordained no peer, and whose like the denizens of the Concourse on high and the realities of the Prophets and Messengers of God have never attained. Blessed, then, be He Who hath made manifest this hallowed and sacred, this mighty and wondrous Day!” (Days of Remembrance, DOR 10.7)

This is the strongest statement on the page. The comparison is not with other people but with the Concourse on high and with the Prophets themselves. The sentence says that none of them has attained a Day like this one. The word “peer” in the first clause is about the Day: God has ordained none equal to it.

In another Tablet in the same book, one that begins “O concourse of ardent lovers!”, Bahá’u’lláh tells of a night on which the Spirit sang to the Concourse:

”This is the night wherein the Spirit intoned such a melody as to throw into commotion the inner realities of all men, proclaiming: “Rejoice, O Concourse on high, within your retreats of Paradise!”” (Days of Remembrance, DOR 44.2)

The passage does not say which night it is. It places the Concourse in “retreats of Paradise” and calls them to rejoice there. That is a different picture from the passages below, in which they long to come back to the world. The two are set side by side in the final section.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá: the gaze, the envy and the longing to return

‘Abdu’l-Bahá turns the picture toward the believers. He describes what the Company on high does when it looks at those who hold to the Covenant:

”This day, from the realms of the All-Glorious, from the Kingdom of Holiness where hosannas of glorification and praise rise up, the Company on high direct their gaze upon you.“ (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, SWAB 182.1)

This is the opening of a Tablet to those who are holding fast to the Covenant and Testament. In the Tablet, the Company on high cry “Glad tidings!” when their gaze falls on such gatherings. The passage places the observers in the Kingdom of Holiness and the observed on earth.

In another Tablet, to steadfast friends, he writes of grace falling on the earth, and of what it does to the world’s standing:

”O ye steadfast loved ones! The grace of God is beating down upon mankind, even as the rains in spring, and the rays of the manifest Light have made this earth to be the envy of heaven. But alas, the blind are deprived of this bounty, the heedless are closed off from it, the withered despair of it, the faded are dying away—so that even as flooding waters, this endless stream of grace passeth back into its primal source in a hidden sea. Only a few receive this grace and take their share of it. Wherefore, let us put our hopes in whatever the strong arm of the Beloved can bring about.“ (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, SWAB 221.2)

The envy of heaven and the loss of the grace are in the same paragraph. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá states that the earth is envied because of the light, and also that most people receive little of what is poured out. The privilege is therefore not that everyone alive has taken the bounty. It is that the bounty is present, and that a few take their share. He answers the loss with hope placed in what the Beloved can bring about, not with despair.

The holy ones of past ages

Shoghi Effendi, in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, gathers testimony from ‘Abdu’l-Bahá on the greatness of this Day. He gives the first passage below as from “one of His earliest Tablets”; for the other two he names no Tablet. Three of the passages bear on this facet.

The first is about thankfulness:

”How thankful must we be for having been made in this Day the recipients of so overwhelming a favor! Would that we had ten thousand lives that we might lay them down in thanksgiving for so rare a privilege, so high an attainment, so priceless a bounty!“ (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted in Shoghi Effendi, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.31; Tablet not identified)

The second says who looked for this Day and did not see it:

”The holy ones of past ages and centuries have, each and all, yearned with tearful eyes to live, though for one moment, in the Day of God. Their longings unsatisfied, they repaired to the Great Beyond. How great, therefore, is the bounty of the Abhá Beauty Who, notwithstanding our utter unworthiness, hath through His grace and mercy breathed into us in this divinely-illumined century the spirit of life, hath gathered us beneath the standard of the Beloved of the world, and chosen to confer upon us a bounty for which the mighty ones of bygone ages had craved in vain.“ (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted in Shoghi Effendi, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.31; Tablet not identified)

The third turns from the past to the present, from those who died without seeing the Day to those who now wish to return:

”The souls of the well-favored among the concourse on high … the sacred dwellers of the most exalted Paradise, are in this day filled with burning desire to return unto this world, that they may render such service as lieth in their power to the threshold of the Abhá Beauty.“ (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted in Shoghi Effendi, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.31; Tablet not identified)

Together these three passages state what envy consists of. The holy ones wanted to live in this Day and could not. The souls in Paradise want to return and serve. What the believers have is the opportunity to serve in the world while the Day lasts. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá adds that the cause is not their worth: he says “notwithstanding our utter unworthiness”, and places the choice with the Abhá Beauty. The envy of the Concourse is therefore not a verdict on the believers’ merit. It is a measure of what has been given them.

The Concourse on high in other roles

Two passages show the Concourse in roles other than envy. Both are useful to a reader, because they limit what can be said.

The Concourse that consoles

Shoghi Effendi, in God Passes By, quotes Bahá’u’lláh’s account, in a Tablet, of a dream He had in Baghdád during His last years there, in which the Prophets and Messengers gathered around Him and wept. The Concourse on high then spoke:

”Thereupon the Concourse on high addressed Me saying: ‘…Erelong shalt Thou behold with Thine own eyes what no Prophet hath beheld.… Be patient, be patient.‘“ (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in Shoghi Effendi, God Passes By, GPB VIII.40; Tablet not identified)

Here the Concourse speaks to Bahá’u’lláh, not about the believers. It tells Him that He will see what no Prophet has seen. The statement belongs with the passages above because it also says that this Day contains something the earlier Messengers did not behold. In the longer account, the Prophets weep for Bahá’u’lláh, and the Concourse on high addresses Him until dawn.

The Concourse that comes to the one who speaks

Bahá’u’lláh promises that those who speak His Name are visited:

”Whoso openeth his lips in this Day and maketh mention of the name of his Lord, the hosts of Divine inspiration shall descend upon him from the heaven of My name, the All-Knowing, the All-Wise. On him shall also descend the Concourse on high, each bearing aloft a chalice of pure light.“ (Gleanings CXXIX.3)

This is the “descend with chalices of light” image of this facet. It belongs equally to the assistance facet, and Promises of Assistance treats it under “The hosts of the Concourse on high”. It is cited here for one reason: it shows the Concourse moving toward a person who simply speaks the Name. The passage sets no condition beyond that.

A further passage in the Gleanings, addressed to Salmán, speaks of a different honor, to be received in the world to come:

”By the righteousness of Him Who, in this Day, crieth within the inmost heart of all created things: “God, there is none other God besides Me!” If any man were to arise to defend, in his writings, the Cause of God against its assailants, such a man, however inconsiderable his share, shall be so honored in the world to come that the Concourse on high would envy his glory. No pen can depict the loftiness of his station, neither can any tongue describe its splendor.” (Gleanings CLIV.1)

Here the envy is attached to a particular act, defending the Cause in writing, and to the world to come. It is the same word as in the passages above, but the grounds differ. In the ‘Abdu’l-Bahá passages the Concourse envies those alive in this Day. In this one the Concourse would envy the honor of a defender. “Dearly Beloved Friends” traces how Shoghi Effendi used this passage in The Advent of Divine Justice.

The Universal House of Justice: the privilege and the responsibility

The Universal House of Justice draws the two consequences that the facet scope names.

On 24 May 2001, the House of Justice addressed the believers gathered for the events marking the completion of the projects on Mount Carmel. It recalled words of Bahá’u’lláh revealed on that mountain and then wrote:

”Such a privilege carries with it an equally great responsibility, the responsibility to do our part, whatever the sacriﬁce, whatever the difﬁculty, to see that the poignant desire expressed by Bahá’u’lláh on that historic occasion is fulﬁlled: “Oh, how I long to announce unto every spot on the surface of the earth, and to carry to each one of its cities, the glad-tidings of this Revelation—a Revelation to which the heart of Sinai hath been attracted, and in whose name the Burning Bush is calling: ‘Unto God, the Lord of Lords, belong the kingdoms of earth and heaven.’”” (Universal House of Justice, 24 May 2001)

The privilege named there is that of having witnessed, “with our own eyes”, the beginning of the fulfilment of Bahá’u’lláh’s words. The responsibility it names is to carry out His wish that the glad-tidings reach every place. The message does not mention the Concourse on high. It is included here because it states, for the believers, what the Writings above imply: that to be among those the holy ones envied is to be given something to do.

Twenty years later the House of Justice wrote of the kind of life that answers it:

”How immensely blessed are those souls who, alive to the greatness of this Day and the signiﬁcance of their actions, strive for the emergence of a society shaped by the divine teachings.“ (Universal House of Justice, 30 December 2021)

The blessing is placed on souls who are alive to the greatness of the Day and to the significance of their actions, and who strive for a society shaped by the teachings. The sentence has two conditions: awareness of the Day, and effort. The same message describes that society as far distant, so the blessing does not wait on its completion.

How this facet connects

  • The Day the Prophets Longed For covers the longing of the Prophets and the peoples of earlier dispensations. This page takes up the same longing as it appears among the holy ones and the Concourse after death, and the part of it that ‘Abdu’l-Bahá turns into thankfulness.
  • An Atom as the Sun shows what the believer is made into in this Day. It supplies the reason the Concourse has something to envy: the station given to an ordinary soul.
  • All Things Rejoice gathers the passages in which creation, land and sea, and the unseen world rejoice. The cries of the unseen world in Gleanings XIV.6 and XIV.13 belong to both pages. Here they are read as the heavens speaking about the earth.
  • The King of Days sets out why the Day outranks other days. The statement that its like has not been attained by the Concourse on high is one of the claims that page rests on.
  • Promises of Assistance treats the Concourse as helper. This page treats it as observer and as envier. “Dearly Beloved Friends” shows how Shoghi Effendi arranged the Writings on the station of the believers in The Advent of Divine Justice, including the passage on the defender of the Cause.

What the sources do not settle

  • Three of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s passages (WOB 6.31) are quoted by Shoghi Effendi from Tablets that are not identified in the text. Shoghi Effendi calls the first one of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s earliest Tablets; the other two are given without a Tablet. The selection and order are Shoghi Effendi’s. One passage carries an ellipsis in the source.
  • The texts use “Concourse on high”, “Company on high” and “concourse on high” (in lower case, in the quotation from ‘Abdu’l-Bahá). Whether these render one Arabic or Persian term is not shown in this corpus, and the page does not treat them as separate groups.
  • DOR 44.2 places the Concourse “within your retreats of Paradise” and tells it to rejoice. WOB 6.31 says the dwellers of Paradise long to come back to the world. The passages do not say whether these are the same souls at different times, or two aspects of one condition. The texts do not reconcile them.
  • DOR 44.2 does not name the night. The corpus text of the Tablet shown here does not either.
  • The envy in Gleanings CLIV.1 is toward the glory of a defender of the Cause in the world to come, while the envy in SWAB 221.2 is of the earth in the present. They are not the same claim, and the texts do not say how they relate.
  • The Concourse’s words to Bahá’u’lláh (GPB VIII.40) come from a Tablet that Shoghi Effendi does not name. The passage does not say what “what no Prophet hath beheld” refers to.
  • The House of Justice messages do not discuss the Concourse on high. Their link to this facet is the privilege and responsibility of being alive in the Day, not the doctrine of the unseen world.

Concrete images

  • The earth as the footstool of God and the seat of His throne (Gleanings XIV.6).
  • Cups and life-giving waters of Paradise put away; the wine of reunion from the chalice of the Beloved (Gleanings XIV.13).
  • Each member of the Concourse carrying a chalice of pure light down to one who speaks the Name (Gleanings CXXIX.3).
  • A melody sung in the night that stirs the inner realities of all people (DOR 44.2).
  • The Company on high looking down from the Kingdom of Holiness (SWAB 182.1).
  • Spring rain falling on the earth, and flood water running back into a hidden sea (SWAB 221.2).
  • Holy ones with tearful eyes who died without seeing the Day (WOB 6.31).
  • Souls in Paradise who want to return and serve at a threshold (WOB 6.31).
  • The Burning Bush and Sinai attracted to the Revelation (Universal House of Justice, 24 May 2001).

A possible emotional arc

  1. The unseen world announces: the earth is congratulated and the cups of Paradise are put aside (Gleanings XIV.6, XIV.13).
  2. The holy ones of past ages wait with tearful eyes and do not live to see the Day (WOB 6.31, second passage).
  3. The souls in Paradise ask to return and serve, and the Company on high look down at those who hold to the Covenant (WOB 6.31, third passage; SWAB 182.1).
  4. Thankfulness: ten thousand lives would not be enough to repay the bounty, and it was not earned (WOB 6.31, first passage).
  5. Response: to speak the Name and be visited by the Concourse, and to strive for a society shaped by the teachings (Gleanings CXXIX.3; Universal House of Justice, 30 December 2021).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not say the Concourse on high envies each believer personally, or that the believers are better than the holy ones. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá says the bounty is given “notwithstanding our utter unworthiness”.
  • Do not say the earlier Prophets are lesser. The passages say they did not attain the Day, and that the Day has no peer. They do not rank the Messengers.
  • Do not attribute WOB 6.31 to Shoghi Effendi. He quotes it; the words are ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s. Name the Tablets as “not identified”.
  • Do not attribute the House of Justice’s two sentences to Bahá’u’lláh or ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. The embedded sentence “Oh, how I long to announce…” is Bahá’u’lláh’s; the sentence before it is the House of Justice’s.
  • Do not turn SWAB 221.2 into a statement that everyone receives the grace. The passage says only a few do.
  • Do not use the envy of the Concourse for the defender of the Cause in the same line as the envy of the earth; they are separate claims.
  • Do not give a date or a place to the night of DOR 44.2, and do not say who the “Spirit” is. The passage does not.
  • Do not invent words for the Concourse beyond the quoted speeches (GPB VIII.40; SWAB 182.1).
