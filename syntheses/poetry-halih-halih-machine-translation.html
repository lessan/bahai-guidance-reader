About the text

  • Source file: [source] (retrieved 2026-10-17 from `https://oceanoflights.org/bahaullah-st-008-fa/`).
  • Published edition cited in header: Ganj-i-Sháyigán (گنج شايگان), pp. 33–35, under the title Lawḥ-i-Halih Halih yá Bishárat (لوح هله هله يا بشارت), attributed to Bahá’u’lláh (حضرت بهاءالله).
  • Form and internal evidence: 24 Persian verses followed by 1 closing prose paragraph (placed immediately after the verses in the source block). Each of the 24 verses consists of three short, internally rhyming rhythmic clauses (except verses 1 and 2, which have two clauses) followed by the verb `آمد` (ámad, ”hath come“ — omitted only in verse 2) and the unchanging refrain `هله هله هله یا بشارت` (halih halih halih yá bishárat, ”Hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!“). Within the poem itself, the verses celebrate the advent of the Maid of Immortality (Ḥúr-i-baqá / Ḥúr-i-iláhí) from the Exalted Paradise (firdaws-i-‘alá) and the manifestation of the Divine Beloved under titles including the Davidic melody from the Divine Lote-Tree (verse 8), the protection of Bahá from the Dayspring of Há’ (verse 9), the Ḥijází Falcon with an ‘Iráqí melody from the forearm of the King (verse 17), the All-Forgiving Countenance from the Court of the Most Near (verse 18), the luminous Dove from spiritual Midian (verse 20), the sheer Beauty and Essence of the Majesty of Truth with the Most Great Sign (verse 22), the Countenance of the Desired One with the Most Great Mercy (verse 23), and the Exalted Lord (verse 24). The closing prose paragraph states that this wondrous remembrance (dhikr-i-badí‘) hath come from the immortal rose-garden (gulshan-i-báqí) so that the lovers of the Beauty of the Beloved may occupy themselves therewith in sweet melodies and not forget the holy, divine homeland (vaṭan-i-quds-i-iláhí).
  • Historical context (exact date, place of revelation, recipient): Not established in the source file’s header.

Literal translation

1.

حور بقا از فردوس علا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

The Maid of Immortality from the Exalted Paradise hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

2.

با چنگ و نوا هم با کاسه حمرا هله هله هله یا بشارت

With harp and melody, and with the crimson cup — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

3.

با غمزه جانی با مزه فانی با رقص و نوا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

With a soul-ravishing glance, with a self-annihilating taste, with dance and melody hath She come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

4.

با گیسوی مشکین با لعل نمکین از نزد خدا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

With musk-scented tresses, with winsome ruby [lips], from the presence of God hath She come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

5.

دو طیف ز ابرویش صد تیر ز مژگانش بهر دل ما آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

Two apparitions from Her eyebrows, a hundred arrows from Her eyelashes, for the sake of our heart have come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

6.

جانها برهش دلها ببرش جمله فنا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

Souls in Her path, hearts at Her side, have all come to annihilation — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

7.

با کفه بیضأ با گیسوی سودا چون اژدر موسی آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

With a White Palm, with a raven-black tress like the serpent of Moses hath She come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

8.

این نغمه داودی از سدره لاهوتی باروح مسیحا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This melody of David from the Divine Lote-Tree with the Spirit of the Messiah hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

9.

با جذب وفا با صون بهأ از مشرق ها آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

With the attraction of fidelity, with the safeguarding of Bahá, from the Dayspring of Há’ hath [He] come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

10.

با نور هدائی از صبح لقا با طور سنا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

With the light of guidance from the Morn of Reunion, with the Sinai of splendour hath [He] come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

11.

این نغمه جان در نزد جانان از بلبل لا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This melody of the soul, in the presence of the Beloved, from the Nightingale of “Lá” hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

12.

با مژده وصلی این حور الهی از شاخه طوبی آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

With a glad-tidings of reunion, this Divine Maiden from the branch of the Ṭúbá-tree hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

13.

این عاشق فانی این طیر ترابی در ره معشوق فدا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This evanescent lover, this bird of dust, hath come as a sacrifice in the path of the Beloved — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

14.

بر گردن عاشق سیف جفا از عرش وفا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

Upon the neck of the lover, the sword of tribulation from the Throne of fidelity hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

15.

این نامه قدسی با هد هد تازی از شهر سبا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This holy epistle with the Arabian hoopoe from the city of Sheba hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

16.

این وجهه باقی ازامر الهی باید بیضأ آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This Eternal Countenance, by the command of God, with the White Hand hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

17.

این باز حجازی با لحن عراقی ازساعد شاه آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This Ḥijází Falcon, with an ‘Iráqí melody, from the forearm of the King hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

18.

این طلعت غفاری با جذبه بهاجی از ساحت ادنا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This All-Forgiving Countenance, with joyous attraction, from the Court of the Most Near hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

19.

این بلبل مغنی از گلبن قدسی با کف وصل آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This singing Nightingale, from the holy rose-bush, with the hand of union hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

20.

این ورقه نورأ از مدین روحا با نور و ضیأ آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This luminous Dove, from the spiritual Midian, with light and radiance hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

21.

این شاهد یزدان این مست می جانان با جام تولا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

This Witness of God, this one intoxicated with the wine of the Beloved, with the cup of loving devotion hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

22.

آن صرف جمال حق آن جوهر اجلال حق باآیت کبری آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

That sheer Beauty of Truth, that Essence of the Majesty of Truth, with the Most Great Sign hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

23.

آن طلعت مقصود آن وجهه معبود با رحمت عظمی آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

That Countenance of the Desired One, that Visage of the Adored One, with the Most Great Mercy hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

24.

جانها به وصالش دلها به نثارش کان رّب علا آمد هله هله هله یا بشارت

Souls unto reunion with Him, hearts as an offering unto Him, for that Exalted Lord hath come — hail, hail, hail, O glad-tidings!

Closing prose paragraph.

ابن ذکر بدیع از گلشن باقی آمد تا عاشقان جمال جانان آتش حب از دل و جان در کمال اطمینان به بدایع لحنهای خوش به آن مشغول شوند که شاید از جذبه آن عاکفان کعبه عرفان بشور آیند و وطن قدس الهی را فراموش نفرمایند.

This [lit. ”Son of“, misprint for ín, ”This“] wondrous remembrance hath come from the everlasting rose-garden, so that the lovers of the Beauty of the Beloved, [kindling] the fire of love from heart and soul, in the utmost assurance, with wondrous, sweet melodies may occupy themselves therewith, that haply through the ecstasy thereof the dwellers at the Ka‘bih of divine knowledge may be stirred into rapture and may not forget the holy, divine homeland.

Closing prose paragraph.

This wondrous remembrance hath come forth from the everlasting rose-garden, that the lovers of the Beauty of the Beloved, kindling the fire of love within heart and soul, may in the utmost assurance occupy themselves therewith in wondrous and melodious tones, so that haply, through the ecstasy it inspireth, they that dwell at the Ka‘bih of divine knowledge may be stirred into rapture and never forget their holy, their divine homeland.

Uncertain readings

VerseWord or phraseProblemAlternatives considered
3با مزه فانی (bá maziy-i-fání)Paired in internal rhyme with `با غمزه جانی` (bá ghamziy-i-jání, ”with a soul-ravishing glance“): `مزه` (mazih) literally means ”taste, savour, relish, or appetizer served with wine“, and `فانی` (fání) means ”evanescent / annihilating“. It can mean a draught/taste that induces faná (self-annihilation), or `مزه` could be a dialectical/graphical variant of a parallel noun.(1) ”with a self-annihilating taste / all-effacing draught“ (chosen); (2) ”with fleeting/mortal relish“.
5دو طیف ز ابرویش (du ṭayf zi abrúyash)`طیف` (ṭayf) means ”phantom, apparition, vision, or rainbow arc“. However, in classical Persian imagery, the Beloved’s two arched eyebrows are standardly compared to two curved swords (du sayf, `دو سیف`) paired with the hundred arrows (`صد تیر`) of the eyelashes. `طیف` (ṭayf) may therefore be either a deliberate visionary image (”two visions/arcs from Her eyebrows“) or a phonetic/orthographic substitution for `سیف` (sayf, ”sword“).(1) Follow the printed text `طیف`: ”Two apparitions / visions from Her eyebrows“ (chosen); (2) Read as phonetic variant of `سیف` (sayf): ”Two swords from Her eyebrows“.
9با صون بهأ از مشرق ها (bá ṣawn-i-Bahá az mashriq-i-Há)Two points: (a) `صون` (ṣawn) means ”safeguarding, protection, preservation“, though in unpointed or lightly pointed manuscripts `صون` closely resembles `ضوء` (ḍaw’, ”light, radiance“) or `لون` (lawn, ”hue“); (b) `از مشرق ها` is written with `ها` detached and rhymes with `وفا` (vafá) and `بهأ` (Bahá), indicating the Arabic letter Há’ (`ه`, symbol of the Divine Essence / Hú), rather than the Persian plural suffix -há (”from the daysprings“).(1) ”with the safeguarding of Bahá, from the Dayspring of Há’“ (chosen); (2) if `ضوء`: ”with the radiance of Bahá“; if plural -há: ”from the daysprings“.
11بلبل لا (bulbul-i-Lá)`لا` (Lá, ”No / Not“) alludes either to the negation Lá iláha (”No God is there [save God]“) that sweeps away all created things, or by abbreviation to Lá-makán (”the Placeless“), rhyming with Jánán / baqá.(1) Literal: ”Nightingale of ‘Lá’“ / Readable: ”Nightingale of the Placeless“ (chosen); (2) ”Nightingale of negation [of all save God]“.
16باید بیضأ (báyad bayḍá → bá yad-i-bayḍá)Orthographic run-on in the source file: `باید` is printed as one word without a space (just as `باروح` in verse 8 and `باآیت` in verse 22 omit the space after `با`). Reading it as the verb báyad (”must“) breaks both the syntax and the three-part rhythmic parallelism (`این وجهه باقی / از امر الهی / با ید بیضأ آمد`); it is unmistakably `با یدِ بیضأ` (bá yad-i-bayḍá, ”with the White Hand“ of Moses, echoing `با کفه بیضأ` in verse 7).(1) Read as `با یدِ بیضأ`: ”with the White Hand“ (chosen); (2) taking `باید` as a verb (”must“) is grammatically untenable here.
18با جذبه بهاجی از ساحت ادنا (bá jadhbiy-i-bahhájí az sáḥat-i-adná)`بهاجی` (bahhájí or bahájí) derives from Arabic bahaj / bahháj (”joyous, gladdening, resplendent“), while also echoing Bahá; `ساحت ادنا` (sáḥat-i-adná) alludes to Qur’án 53:9 (qába qawsayni aw adná, ”two bows’ length off, or nearer still“, i.e. the Court of the Most Near), though al-adná in other contexts can mean ”the nearer/nether world“. Since the preposition is `از` (”from“), ”from the Court of the Most Near“ fits the descent of the Manifestation from the station of aw adná.(1) ”with joyous/rapturous attraction, from the Court of the Most Near“ (chosen); (2) ”with Bahá-like attraction, from the innermost court“.
19با کف وصل (bá kaff-i-vaṣl)`کف وصل` (kaff-i-vaṣl, ”palm/hand of union“) lacks the -á termination found in the third clause of almost every other verse (unless pronounced vaṣlá with poetic alif-i-ishbá‘); furthermore, in connection with a singing Nightingale (`بلبل مغنی`), `کف` (kaff, palm/hand or clapping) could also be a graphical variant of `دف` (daff, tambourine/frame-drum).(1) Follow the printed text `با کف وصل`: ”with the hand of union“ / ”bearing the gift of union in His hand“ (chosen); (2) if `دف وصل`: ”with the tambourine of union“.
20این ورقه نورأ از مدین روحا (ín varaqiy-i-nawrá az Madyan-i-rúḥá)`ورقه` (varaqih / varqá’) can mean ”Dove“ (varqá’, the sacred dove) or ”Leaf“ (varaqih); `نورأ` (nawrá’) is the Arabic feminine adjective ”luminous, radiant, blossoming“; `روحا` (rúḥá) is rúḥ (”spirit“) with a poetic -á ending for the rhyme with nawrá’ and ḍiyá’.(1) ”This luminous Dove, from the spiritual Midian“ (chosen); (2) ”This luminous Leaf, from the Midian of the spirit“.
24کان رّب علا آمد (k-án Rabb-i-‘Alá ámad)`کان` can be read as the conjunction + demonstrative `که آن` (k-án, ”for that [Exalted Lord hath come]“) or as the noun `کانِ` (kán-i-, ”the Mine of [the Exalted Lord hath come]“). Note also the misplaced tashdíd on `رّب` in the source file for `ربّ` (Rabb).(1) Read `کان` as `که آن` (k-án): ”for that Exalted Lord hath come“ (chosen); (2) Read as kán: ”the Mine of the Exalted Lord hath come“.
Proseابن ذکر بدیع ... آتش حب از دل و جان (Ibn-i-dhikr-i-badí‘ ... átash-i-ḥubb az dil u ján)Two textual problems in the closing prose paragraph: (a) The first word is printed `ابن` (ibn, ”son of“), which is an unmistakable typographical/OCR error (one dot under bá’ instead of two dots under yá’) for `این` (ín, ”This [wondrous remembrance]“), matching the anaphoric `این ... آمد` across the poem; (b) `آتش حب از دل و جان` (”the fire of love from heart and soul“) stands without an explicit governing verb or preposition before `در کمال اطمینان ... به آن مشغول شوند`, suggesting either an elliptical absolute phrase (”[with] the fire of love in heart and soul“) or the omission of a participle such as bar-furúkhtih (”having kindled“).(1) Emend `ابن` to `این` (”This wondrous remembrance“) while noting literal ”Son of“ in brackets, and supply ”[kindling] the fire of love from heart and soul“ (chosen); (2) Read `به آتش حب` (”with the fire of love“).
