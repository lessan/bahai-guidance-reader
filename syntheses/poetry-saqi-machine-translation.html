About the text

  • Source file: [source] (retrieved 2026-10-17 from `https://oceanoflights.org/bahaullah-st-098-fa/`).
  • Published edition cited in header: Má’idiy-i-Ásmání (مائده آسمانى), vol. 4, pp. 209–211, under the title Sáqí az ghayb-i-baqá (ساقی از غیب بقا), attributed to Bahá’u’lláh (حضرت بهاءالله).
  • Form and internal evidence: A Persian ghazal of 15 couplets (bayts), printed as 30 single-hemistich (miṣrá‘) lines in the source file, monorhymed in -ár (عذار، کردگار، بیار، خمار، گذار، نگار، کنار، بیار، میار، افتخار، بیقرار، یار، یار، برآر، اقتدار، زار) in the meter ramal-i-muthamman-i-maḥdhúf (fá‘ilátun fá‘ilátun fá‘ilátun fá‘ilun), with a metrical shift in the final couplet. Within the poem itself, verse 8 names `”Bahá“` (`گر وصل ” بها “ داری طلب`, ”if thou seekest union with ‘Bahá’“) and the closing verse (verse 15) uses the appellation `”Darvísh“` (`درویش`, ”Dervish“). Couplet 4 (lines 7–8) is quoted by Bahá’u’lláh Himself in The Seven Valleys ([source], lines 160–161, and note 27 at line 659: ”From a poem of Bahá’u’lláh“).
  • Historical context (date, place, recipient): Not established in the source file’s header.

Literal translation

The 30 single-hemistich lines of the source file form 15 rhymed couplets (bayts). Both the couplet number (`1`–`15`) and the hemistich/source-line number (`a`/`b`, lines `1`–`30`) are given below.

1a (line 1).

ساقی از غیب بقا برقع بر افکن از عذار

O Cup-Bearer from the unseen realm of immortality, cast aside the veil from [Thy] countenance,

1b (line 2).

تا بنوشم خمر باقی از جمال کردگار

so that I may drink the eternal wine from the Beauty of the Creator.

2a (line 3).

آنچه در خمخانه داری نشکند صفرای عشق

That which thou hast in the wine-cellar doth not quench the burning fever of love;

2b (line 4).

زان شراب معنوی ساقی همی بحری بیار

of that wine of the spirit, O Cup-Bearer, bring forth an ocean!

3a (line 5).

تا که این مستور شیدائی در آید در خروش

So that this veiled one of love-madness may burst into clamour;

3b (line 6).

تا که این مخمور ربانی بر آید زین خمار

so that this divinely wine-sick one may emerge from this wine-languor.

4a (line 7).

نار عشقی بر فروز و جمله هستی‌ها بسوز

Kindle a fire of love and burn away all existences;

4b (line 8).

پس قدم بردار و اندر کوی عشاقان گذار

then lift thy foot and set it within the quarter of the lovers.

5a (line 9).

تا نگردی فانی از وصف وجود ای مرد راه

Until thou becomest annihilated from the attribute of existence, O man of the path,

5b (line 10).

کی چشی خمر بقا از لعل نوشین نگار

when shalt thou taste the wine of immortality from the sweet ruby [lips] of the Beloved?

6a (line 11).

پای نه بر فرق ملک آنگه در آ در ظلّ فقر

Set thy foot upon the crown of earthly dominion, then enter beneath the shadow of poverty,

6b (line 12).

تا ببینی ملک باقی را کنون از هر کنار

so that thou mayest behold the everlasting Kingdom even now from every side.

7a (line 13).

گر خیال جان همی هستت بدل اینجا میا

If the thought of life still abideth in thy heart, come not hither;

7b (line 14).

گر نثار جان و دل داری بیا و هم بیار

if thou hast the offering of soul and heart, come, and bring [them] too!

8a (line 15).

رسم ره اینست گر وصل ” بها “ داری طلب

The custom of the path is this, if thou seekest union with ”Bahá“;

8b (line 16).

گر نباشی مرد این ره دور شو زحمت میار

if thou art not a man of this path, depart [and] bring not trouble.

9a (line 17).

گر همی خواهی که گردی واقف از اسرار عشق

If thou desirest to become aware of the mysteries of love,

9b (line 18).

چشم عبرت برگشا بربند راه افتخار

open the eye of discernment [and] bar the way of vainglory,

10a (line 19).

تا ببینی طور موسی طائف اینجا آمده

so that thou mayest behold the Sinai of Moses come hither circumambulating;

10b (line 20).

تا ببینی روح عیسی را ز عشقش بیقرار

so that thou mayest behold the Spirit of Jesus restless from love of Him;

11a (line 21).

تا بیابی دفتر توحید از زلفین دوست

so that thou mayest find the scroll of Divine Unity from the two tresses of the Friend;

11b (line 22).

تا بخوانی مصحف تجرید از خدین یار

so that thou mayest read the scripture of detachment from the two cheeks of the Beloved.

12a (line 23).

هین بکش خمر فرح از چشمه حیوان عشق

Lo, quaff the wine of joy from the fountain of the living waters of love,

12b (line 24).

تا به فیروزی سر اندازی همی در پای یار

so that in triumph thou mayest cast thy head at the feet of the Beloved.

13a (line 25).

مردگانند در این انجمن اندر ره دوست

They are dead ones in this assembly, in the path of the Friend;

13b (line 26).

ای مسیحای زمان هان نفسی گرم برآر

O Messiah of the Age, lo, breathe forth a warm breath,

14a (line 27).

تا که برپرند اطیار وجود از سجن تن

so that the birds of existence may fly up from the prison of the body

14b (line 28).

تا فضای لا مکان در ظلّ صاحب اقتدار

unto the expanse of the Placeless, beneath the shadow of the Possessor of Might.

15a (line 29).

درویش جهان سوخت از این شعلهٴ جانسوز الهی

O Dervish, the world hath burned from this soul-consuming divine flame;

15b (line 30).

وقت آنست که کنی زنده از این نغمه زار

the time hath come that Thou quicken [it] to life through this plaintive melody.

Readable translation

Note: Couplet 4 (lines 7–8) is rendered below using the authorised translation in The Seven Valleys ([source], lines 160–161).

1 (lines 1–2).

O Cup-Bearer of the unseen realm of immortality, fling aside the veil from Thy countenance,

That I may quaff the eternal wine from the Beauty of the Creator!

2 (lines 3–4).

What thou keepest in the wine-cellar cannot quench the burning fever of love;

Of that wine of the spirit, O Cup-Bearer, bring forth an ocean!

3 (lines 5–6).

That this veiled one, frenzied with love, may burst forth into clamour;

That this divinely languishing one may be delivered from his wine-thirst.

4 (lines 7–8).

Kindle the fire of love and burn away all things;

Then set thy foot into the land of the lovers.

5 (lines 9–10).

Until thou art effaced from every attribute of existence, O wayfarer,

How shalt thou taste the wine of immortality from the sweet ruby lips of the Beloved?

6 (lines 11–12).

Set thy foot upon the crown of earthly dominion, and enter beneath the shadow of poverty,

That thou mayest behold the everlasting Kingdom even now on every side.

7 (lines 13–14).

If any thought of life still linger in thy heart, come not hither;

If thou holdest soul and heart ready for sacrifice, come, and bring them too!

8 (lines 15–16).

Such is the law of the path, if thou seekest union with Bahá;

If thou art not a man for this path, begone, and trouble us not.

9 (lines 17–18).

Wouldst thou become acquainted with the mysteries of love,

Open the eye of discernment and bar the way of vainglory,

10 (lines 19–20).

That thou mayest behold the Sinai of Moses come hither to circle in adoration;

That thou mayest behold the Spirit of Jesus restless with love for Him;

11 (lines 21–22).

That thou mayest discover the scroll of Divine Unity in the two tresses of the Friend;

That thou mayest read the volume of detachment upon the two cheeks of the Beloved.

12 (lines 23–24).

Haste, quaff the wine of joy from the fountain of the living waters of love,

That in triumph thou mayest fling thy head at the feet of the Beloved!

13 (lines 25–26).

Dead are they in this assembly along the path of the Friend;

O Messiah of the Age, breathe forth a warm and quickening breath,

14 (lines 27–28).

That the birds of being may soar up from the prison of the body

Unto the expanse of the Placeless, beneath the shadow of the Lord of Might!

15 (lines 29–30).

O Dervish, the world hath been set ablaze by this soul-consuming divine flame;

The time is come for Thee to quicken the sorrowing to life through this melody!

Uncertain readings

VerseWord or phraseProblemAlternatives considered
1a (line 1)ساقی از غیب بقا (sáqí az ghayb-i-baqá)Ambiguous prepositional attachment: `از غیب بقا` (”from the unseen realm of immortality“) can modify `ساقی` (”O Cup-Bearer from the unseen realm of immortality“) or modify the imperative `بر افکن` (”cast aside the veil from out the unseen realm of immortality“).(1) ”O Cup-Bearer from the unseen realm of immortality, cast aside the veil...“ (chosen); (2) ”O Cup-Bearer, from the unseen realm of immortality cast aside the veil...“
2a (line 3)نشکند صفرای عشق (nashkanad ṣafráy-i-‘ishq)Classical humoral idiom: ṣafrá is literally ”yellow bile / choler“, which in Greco-Islamic medicine causes raging thirst and feverish heat; ṣafrá shikastan means to break or allay bilious heat/thirst.(1) ”doth not quench the burning fever of love“ (chosen); (2) ”doth not break the bilious heat of love“; (3) ”doth not allay the raging thirst of love“.
3a (line 5)مستور شیدائی (mastúr-i-shaydá’í)`مستور` (mastúr) literally means ”veiled, concealed, cloistered, or chaste one“, contrasting with bursting into open clamour (`در آید در خروش`); however, mastúr can also be read as ”one veiled in love-madness“, or could reflect an internal rhyme with `مخمور` (makhmúr, wine-sick) in 3b.(1) ”this veiled one of love-madness“ (chosen); (2) ”this hidden ecstatic“; (3) possible graphical variant of `مسرور` (masrúr, joyous) or `مأسور` (ma’súr, captive), though mastúr makes strong antithetical sense with khurúsh.
11a–11b (lines 21–22)زلفین دوست / خدین یار (zulfayn-i-dúst / khaddayn-i-yár)Mystical paradox and Arabic dual forms: in Persian Sufi poetics, the dark tresses (zulf) normally symbolize multiplicity (kathrat) that veils unity, whereas the luminous cheek (khadd) symbolizes unity (vaḥdat). Here, even the two tresses (zulfayn) reveal the scroll of Divine Unity (tawḥíd), and the two cheeks (khaddayn) form the two facing pages of the scripture (muṣḥaf) of detachment (tajríd).Rendered literally as ”two tresses“ and ”two cheeks“ to preserve the dual imagery of a two-page open codex (daftar / muṣḥaf).
13a (line 25)مردگانند در این انجمن اندر ره دوست (murdigán-and dar ín anjuman andar rah-i-dúst)Ambiguous syntax and slight metrical irregularity (dar ín anjuman requires reading dar in anjuman with a short i): `اندر ره دوست` (”in the path of the Friend“) can describe lovers who have died/lain down their lives in the path of the Friend and await the Messiah’s breath to soar from the body (14a), or describe the company as spiritually lifeless with respect to the path of the Friend.(1) ”They are dead ones in this assembly, in the path of the Friend“ (chosen, leaving both nuances open); (2) ”Slain in the path of the Friend are those in this assembly“; (3) ”Dead are those in this assembly toward the path of the Friend“.
15a (line 29)درویش جهان سوخت از این شعلهٴ جانسوز الهی (Darvísh jahán súkht az ín shu‘liy-i-jánsúz-i-iláhí)Both metrical and syntactic ambiguity in the maḳṭa‘ (closing signature couplet): `الهی` (iláhí) breaks the -ár monorhyme of the first hemistich (though non-rhyming first hemistichs are normal outside the maṭla‘, this hemistich is also hypermetrical relative to the preceding ramal lines). Furthermore, `درویش` (Darvísh) may be a vocative/pen-name (”O Dervish, the world hath burned...“) or stand in iḍáfih with `جهان` (darvísh-i-jahán, ”The dervish of the world hath burned...“).(1) Vocative/signature takhalluṣ: ”O Dervish, the world hath burned from this soul-consuming divine flame“ (chosen); (2) Iḍáfih construction: ”The dervish of the world hath burned from this soul-consuming divine flame“.
15b (line 30)وقت آنست که کنی زنده از این نغمه زار (vaqt-i-án-ast kih kuní zindih az ín naghmih zár)Three possible readings of `از این نغمه زار` and the implied object of `کنی زنده` (”Thou make alive“): (a) `نغمهٔ زار` (naghmiy-i-zár, ”by this plaintive/mournful melody“, with the object ”the world“ supplied from 15a); (b) `از این نغمه، زار` (zár as the direct object of kuní zindih: ”that Thou quicken the weeping/lowly one by this melody“); (c) `نغمه‌زار` (naghmih-zár as a compound noun with -zár, ”from this bower/garden of melody“).(1) Literal: ”quicken [it] to life through this plaintive melody“ / Readable: ”quicken the sorrowing to life through this melody“ (chosen); (2) ”quicken [it] to life from this bower of melody“.
