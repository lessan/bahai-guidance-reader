All Things Rejoice

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

The Writings say that the coming of this Day is met with joy, and they say who is joyful: land and sea, the faculties of hearing and sight, the realities of all created things, and the Realm above. They also tell people to join in: to sing, to dress well, to give thanks, and to celebrate the holy days. The Universal House of Justice applies the same joy to one act, telling another soul that the Day of God has dawned. This page sets out those texts. It is one page of a series; see The greatness of this Day.

The passages

Land and sea, and everything that can be seen

Bahá’u’lláh writes:

“Verily this is the Day in which both land and sea rejoice at this announcement, the Day for which have been laid up those things which God, through a bounty beyond the ken of mortal mind or heart, hath destined for revelation.”

(Gleanings XI.4)

Gleanings XI is addressed to Mount Carmel. Its opening lines tell Carmel to “call out to Zion” and announce that He who was hidden from mortal eyes has come. The passage names two parts of the physical world, land and sea, as rejoicing at what is announced. It gives the reason in the same sentence: the Day holds things that God has reserved for revelation and that no mind or heart could have reached by itself.

A second passage extends the witness from land and sea to everything visible. Bahá’u’lláh writes, in a Tablet addressed to Mihrábán:

“In this glorious Day, everything that can be seen is a witness and calleth all men unto the one true God. Say! This is the Day in which the sun of spiritual discernment is shining forth in the heaven of true understanding. Blessed is he that hath perceived and recognized it. Whatsoever was foretold in former times hath now been fulfilled.”

(Additional Tablets and Extracts 71.2)

Here things do more than rejoice. They call people to God. The last sentence ties the call to fulfilment: what was foretold has now happened.

Bahá’u’lláh also describes how created things took in the advent itself. The passage comes from a Tablet for the festival of Riḍván in Days of Remembrance:

“At the advent of this Manifestation the realities of all created things were filled with joy, and all seized the cup of ecstasy with the hands of longing and rapture and drank thereof the choicest wine for love of this Beauty—a Beauty that hath appeared through the power of truth, arrayed with the ornament of God, the Sovereign, the Just, the All-Wise. Blessed, then, be He Who, through this Revelation, hath attracted the hearts of the well-favoured of the Lord!”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance 10.6, Riḍván)

The joy has a cause that the text names: love for the Beauty that has appeared. The “well-favoured of the Lord” are blessed in the last line, which separates them from the rest of “all created things” described earlier in the passage. Everything is joyful; those whose hearts He has drawn are called blessed.

Shoghi Effendi quotes a further passage in The Advent of Divine Justice from a Tablet he does not name:

“The world of being shineth, in this Day, with the resplendency of this Divine Revelation. All created things extol its saving grace, and sing its praises. The universe is wrapt in an ecstasy of joy and gladness. The Scriptures of past Dispensations celebrate the great Jubilee that must needs greet this most great Day of God. Well is it with him that hath lived to see this Day, and hath recognized its station.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

This passage states the scope most widely: the whole universe. It also connects to the Scriptures of the past, which anticipated this Day as a Jubilee. The final sentence moves from the universe to the individual who has lived to see it.

“This is my Day”: the faculties speak

Two passages in Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh give the human faculties a voice. They come from the section “Excerpts from Other Tablets”, and the file does not name the Tablets they were taken from. In the first, Bahá’u’lláh writes:

“In this Day the faculty of hearing exclaimeth, “This is my Day, wherein I hearken unto the wondrous Voice coming from the precincts of the Prison of my Lord, the Perspicuous, the Hearing.” And the faculty of sight calleth aloud, “Verily this is my Day, for I behold the Dayspring of glory shining resplendent at the bidding of Him Who is the Ordainer, the All-Powerful.””

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.35)

In the second, He writes:

“In this Day the inner ear exclaimeth and saith: Indeed well is it with me, today is my day, inasmuch as the Voice of God is calling aloud. And the essence of vision crieth out: Blessed am I, this is my day, for the Ancient Beauty is shining resplendent from the most exalted Horizon.”

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.97)

Each faculty says that this Day is its own, and each names what it receives: the ear hears the Voice, the eye sees the Dayspring. The first passage places the Voice in “the precincts of the Prison”, which locates the Day in Bahá’u’lláh’s imprisonment and not in a distant ideal. The second speaks of the “inner ear” and the “essence of vision”, so the faculties meant may be the spiritual ones, though the text does not say so in as many words. A reader should keep to what is written: the faculties, whatever their kind, are described as glad to be alive in this Day.

A festival in the Realm above, and a call to hasten

In the Tablet known as Words of Paradise, Bahá’u’lláh writes:

“In this Day a great festival is taking place in the Realm above; for whatsoever was promised in the sacred Scriptures hath been fulfilled. This is the Day of great rejoicing. It behooveth everyone to hasten towards the court of His nearness with exceeding joy, gladness, exultation and delight and to deliver himself from the fire of remoteness.”

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 6.56, Words of Paradise)

The cause of the festival is the same as in the passage from Additional Tablets: the promises of the Scriptures are fulfilled. The text then moves from description to instruction. Because the Realm above is celebrating, everyone is told to hurry toward God with joy. Remoteness from God is called a fire, so joyful approach is also relief from it.

In the Tablet of Glad-Tidings, Bahá’u’lláh adds that thanks for this Day cannot be paid in full:

“We yield praise unto God—hallowed and glorified be He—for whatsoever He hath graciously revealed in this blessed, this glorious and incomparable Day. Indeed if everyone on earth were endowed with a myriad tongues and were to continually praise God and magnify His Name to the end that knoweth no end, their thanksgiving would not prove adequate for even one of the gracious favors We have mentioned in this Tablet. Unto this beareth witness every man of wisdom and discernment, of understanding and knowledge.”

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 3.29, Glad-Tidings)

Praise is not presented as an optional addition to joy. The passage says that no amount of it would match even one of the favours named in that Tablet.

The holy days: sing, dress, give thanks

The Days of Remembrance collection gathers Bahá’u’lláh’s Tablets for the holy days, and three of its passages carry the instruction to celebrate in specific terms.

The first is from the Súriy-i-Qalam (Súrih of the Pen), in the Riḍván section:

“O inhabitants of the realms of the seen and the unseen! Sing, O sing the most joyous melodies on this Festival of God which hath appeared with the power of truth and to which the former and latter generations had never attained, could ye but know it. This is the Day whereon the Pen of God hath absolved all who are in the heavens and on earth. Thus hath His eternal command shone forth from the dayspring of His Pen, that ye may rejoice in your souls and be of those whose hearts are gladdened.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance 23.13, Riḍván)

The address is to everything in the seen and unseen worlds, and the command is to sing. The text gives two reasons: the Festival is one that earlier generations never reached, and on it God’s Pen has “absolved all who are in the heavens and on earth”. The stated purpose of the command is that people “rejoice in your souls”, so the singing is meant to be a real expression and not a formality.

The second is from a Tablet for the Declaration of the Báb:

“O peoples of the earth! Make mention of God on this Day whereon the Spirit hath spoken and the realities of those who were created by the Word of God, the Mighty, the Exalted, have ascended unto Him. It behoveth everyone on this day to rejoice with exceeding gladness, to clothe himself in his finest attire, to celebrate the praise of his Lord, and to give thanks unto Him for this most great favour. Blessed are they that have apprehended the purpose of God, and woe betide the heedless.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance 28.6, Declaration of the Báb)

This is the plainest instruction on how to keep a holy day: rejoice, wear one’s finest clothes, praise the Lord, give thanks. The passage ends with a warning that applies to those who do not take the day to heart. Note that it is addressed to “the peoples of the earth” and not only to believers.

The third is from the Tablet for the Birth of the Báb, which Bahá’u’lláh addresses to the night of the birth:

“Blessed art thou, O night! For through thee was born the Day of God, a Day which We have ordained to be the lamp of salvation unto the denizens of the cities of names, the chalice of victory unto the champions of the arenas of eternity, and the dawning-place of joy and exultation unto all creation.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance 40.2, Birth of the Báb)

Here joy is called something the Day itself provides: it is the “dawning-place” of joy for “all creation”. The same passage that blesses the night calls the Day of God “the lamp of salvation” and “the chalice of victory”. The joy therefore belongs to what the Day brings and is not only a human reaction to it.

Bahá’u’lláh also tells the people of Bahá to rejoice in remembering a particular day:

“Rejoice with exceeding gladness, O people of Bahá, as ye call to remembrance the Day of supreme felicity, the Day whereon the Tongue of the Ancient of Days hath spoken, as He departed from His House, proceeding to the Spot from which He shed upon the whole of creation the splendors of His name, the All-Merciful. God is Our witness. Were We to reveal the hidden secrets of that Day, all they that dwell on earth and in the heavens would swoon away and die, except such as will be preserved by God, the Almighty, the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.”

(Gleanings XIV.19)

The passage speaks of a day on which He “departed from His House” for “the Spot” from which His name shone on creation. The file does not name the Tablet or the day. Two things are in the passage: a command to rejoice, and a statement that what happened is greater than can be told without destroying those who hear it.

In a talk of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá

In a talk recorded in notes by Frank E. Osborne, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá remarks to him that he is always smiling. Mr. Osborne answers that faces should reflect happiness in His presence. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá replies:

“This is the day of Bahá’u’lláh, the age of the Blessed Perfection, the cycle of the Greatest Name. If you do not smile now, for what time will you await and what greater happiness could you expect? This is the springtime of manifestation. The vernal shower has descended from the cloud of divine mercy; the life-giving breeze of the Holy Spirit is wafting the perfume of blossoms. From field and meadow rises a fragrant breath of thanksgiving like pure incense ascending to the throne of God. The world has become a new world; souls are quickened, spirits renewed, refreshed.”

(‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in a talk recorded in The Promulgation of Universal Peace, PUP 75.3; notes, not a text written by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá)

This is a recorded talk and the page does not rely on it for any point the written Tablets do not make. They do make this one: the Day is a time for joy, and the fields join in with thanksgiving. The talk gives the same teaching as a reason for ordinary cheerfulness.

The House of Justice

The Universal House of Justice quotes the first passage on this page when describing what it saw in Haifa. In its message of 24 May 2001, written after a week of events that included the opening of the Terraces on Mount Carmel, it wrote:

“We who have been privileged to gather here this week have witnessed, with our own eyes, the dawning fulﬁllment of the words revealed by the Lord of Hosts on this mountain over a century ago, words which cause the very atoms of the earth to vibrate: “Verily this is the Day in which both land and sea rejoice at this announcement, the Day for which have been laid up those things which God, through a bounty beyond the ken of mortal mind or heart, hath destined for revelation.””

(Universal House of Justice, 24 May 2001)

The House says the words are being fulfilled in what it has seen, and that they were revealed “on this mountain”, Carmel. It treats the rejoicing of land and sea as something happening, and not only as a text from the past.

In its Riḍván 2017 message, written for the two hundredth anniversary of Bahá’u’lláh’s birth, the House of Justice turns the joy into a task for every believer. It wrote:

“We entreat the one Beloved that the whole of this bicentennial year may be ﬁlled with this joy that is purest and sweetest: telling another soul of the dawning of the Day of God.”

(Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 2017)

Of all the joys that the Writings describe, the House names this one as the purest and sweetest, and asks that the year be filled with it. The act is small and available to anyone: telling one other person.

How this facet connects

  • Envied by the Concourse on High covers the heavenly side of the festival. The passage here (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 6.56) says the Realm above holds a great festival; that page sets out what the Writings say of the beings there and why they long for this Day.
  • Grace Poured Out on All gives the gifts that are the cause of the joy on this page. The reasons given here (absolution in Days of Remembrance 23.13, bounties beyond the mind and heart in Gleanings XI.4) are developed there.
  • The Day the Prophets Longed For covers the fulfilment of the promises. Several passages here give fulfilment as the reason for rejoicing; that page shows what was promised and by whom.
  • The Divine Springtime takes up the images of season and renewal that ‘Abdu’l-Bahá uses in the talk quoted here. It gives the written texts for them.
  • The Fast covers the month of fasting that ends with Naw-Rúz as a feast. The holy days that these Tablets call for celebration, and the instruction to rejoice, fit alongside the discipline described there; the Fast page gives the law.
  • “Dearly Beloved Friends”: The Advent of Divine Justice Compilation, Traced traces the compilation in which Shoghi Effendi placed the passage quoted at ADJ 110, and what is known of its Tablet.

What the sources do not settle

  • The Tablet behind ADJ 110 is not identified in the sources here, so the passage cannot be placed in a particular occasion or addressee.
  • The two passages on the faculties (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.35 and 17.97) are in a section of excerpts. The file does not name their Tablets, so the occasion and the recipient are not known.
  • The passages on the holy days are said in Days of Remembrance to belong to named occasions (Riḍván, the Declaration of the Báb, the Birth of the Báb). Whether the instruction to wear one’s finest attire is a law for all believers or an exhortation specific to the day in question is not stated in the passage itself; this page treats it as what the passage says and nothing more.
  • Gleanings XIV.19 tells the people of Bahá to remember ”the Day of supreme felicity“. The passage does not name the day, and the file does not name the Tablet.
  • ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s talk is recorded in notes by a listener. The wording is the note-taker’s record.

A possible emotional arc for one song

  1. Announcement: land and sea rejoice at what has been announced (Gleanings XI.4).
  2. Each sense answers: the ear hears the Voice, the eye sees the Dayspring, and each says ”this is my Day“ (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.35, 17.97).
  3. The festival widens: the Realm above, the whole universe (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 6.56; ADJ 110).
  4. The invitation: sing, dress, give thanks (Days of Remembrance 23.13, 28.6).
  5. The one act: telling another soul (Riḍván 2017).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not put the faculties’ words in a general believer’s mouth as a quotation. ”This is my Day“ is spoken by the faculty of hearing and the faculty of sight in Bahá’u’lláh’s text, and by the ear and eye in the second passage.
  • Do not attribute the Osborne talk (PUP 75.3) to ‘Abdu’l-Bahá as a written Tablet. It is a talk recorded in notes.
  • Do not name the Tablet behind ADJ 110, 17.35 or 17.97; the sources do not identify them.
  • Do not say that Bahá’u’lláh prescribes a specific dress, or a particular song, for the holy days. The text says ”finest attire“ and ”joyous melodies“ and no more.
  • Do not turn ”rejoice“ into a promise that the world is already at peace. The texts say the Day has come and the promises are fulfilled in its advent; they do not say that every effect has appeared.
  • Do not put the Riḍván 2017 wish into Bahá’u’lláh’s mouth. It is the House of Justice speaking.
  • Do not claim that the ”hidden secrets of that Day“ are known. Bahá’u’lláh says they are not told (Gleanings XIV.19).
