Grace Poured Out on All

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

Bahá’u’lláh, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá and Shoghi Effendi describe this Day as one in which God’s favour is given out to everyone and everything, not held back for a few. The texts name the gifts (mercy, bounty, forgiveness, healing, wealth for the poor), say that each person receives according to what he can hold, and say that the gifts reach beyond those who hear the Call, to the parents of believers and to righteous souls who died before it. This page sets out those statements. It is one page in the series The greatness of this Day.

The passages

Favour poured on men and infused into all things

Bahá’u’lláh begins a Tablet in the Gleanings with the Day itself and what has been poured out in it:

“THIS is the Day in which God’s most excellent favors have been poured out upon men, the Day in which His most mighty grace hath been infused into all created things.”

(Gleanings IV.1)

Two verbs carry the statement. Favours are “poured out upon men”, which is addressed to people. Grace is “infused into all created things”, which goes further than people. The next section of the Gleanings repeats the opening and widens the scope to “the whole of mankind”:

“THIS is the Day whereon the Ocean of God’s mercy hath been manifested unto men, the Day in which the Daystar of His loving-kindness hath shed its radiance upon them, the Day in which the clouds of His bountiful favor have overshadowed the whole of mankind.”

(Gleanings V.1)

In Gleanings CXLII Bahá’u’lláh speaks to ‘Alí, and the passage says the same of all creation:

“This is the Mercy that hath encompassed the entire creation, the Day whereon the grace of God hath permeated and pervaded all things.”

(Gleanings CXLII.1)

The Kitáb-i-Íqán says it with an effect on the writer:

“In this day the breeze of God is wafted, and His Spirit hath pervaded all things. Such is the outpouring of His grace that the pen is stilled and the tongue is speechless.”

(The Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 197)

Bahá’u’lláh does not say that the grace is a reward for those who have earned it. The texts above describe something that has already happened to “men”, to “the whole of mankind”, to “all things”. The question they leave open is what a person does with it, which the next group takes up.

Mercy, bounty and forgiveness named

In a Tablet in Days of Remembrance Bahá’u’lláh gives the Day its occasion and its gifts:

“This is the Day whereon the Sun of Beauty dawned above the horizon of the countenance of God, the Most Exalted, the Most Great, and the clouds of bounty rained down, and the trees of Paradise yielded such fruits as God hath apportioned to them that turn unto Him with radiant hearts in this Dispensation. Blessed, then, be God, Who hath ordained this surpassing grace!”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance 10.9)

Here the bounty falls like rain, and the fruit is apportioned “to them that turn unto Him”. The rain is general; the fruit is received by those who turn. The passage holds both without comment.

A prayer in the same book lists who has been helped:

“On this Day the poor have been enriched through the wonders of Thine incomparable wealth, the abased have been exalted through the manifold revelations of Thy majesty and glory, the sinful have partaken of the wine of Thy forgiveness, the sick have quaffed from the waters of Thy gracious healing, the disconsolate have taken refuge beneath the shade of the tree of Thy hope and bestowal, and the destitute have attained the shores of the sea of Thy grace and favour.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance 11.8)

The words are addressed to God, in a prayer that begins by declaring this Day “unequalled amongst all the days of Thy world”. Six kinds of need are named: poverty, abasement, sin, sickness, sorrow and destitution. For each, the prayer says that the need has been met “on this Day”. The verbs are in the past tense. Sin is placed in the same list as sickness and poverty, and forgiveness is given as one of the gifts of the Day and not as something the sinful must first deserve.

Shoghi Effendi connects this to the Declaration in the Riḍván garden. In God Passes By he quotes the titles Bahá’u’lláh gave that festival, and then writes:

“He has, in His Kitáb-i-Aqdas, characterized it as the Day whereon “all created things were immersed in the sea of purification,””

(God Passes By IX.7)

The sentence is Shoghi Effendi’s, and the phrase in inner quotation marks is Bahá’u’lláh’s, from the Kitáb-i-Aqdas. In the same paragraph Shoghi Effendi quotes from another Tablet that Bahá’u’lláh calls the occasion a Day on which “the breezes of forgiveness were wafted over the entire creation”. That second quotation is not on this page’s list of checked passages, so it is only pointed to here.

The door and the receptacle

Bahá’u’lláh adds a condition to the outpouring, and it is about the receiver:

“The whole duty of man in this Day is to attain that share of the flood of grace which God poureth forth for him.”

(Gleanings V.4)

The paragraph continues in the Gleanings: “Let none, therefore, consider the largeness or smallness of the receptacle. The portion of some might lie in the palm of a man’s hand, the portion of others might fill a cup, and of others even a gallon-measure.” The grace is not rationed by God according to merit. It differs in how much each person is able to take, and the duty is to take one’s own share, not a larger person’s share. The text also says not to count the size of one’s own vessel.

Bahá’u’lláh speaks of the entrance to all this in a prayer:

“Thou art He, O my God, Who hath unlocked the gate of heaven with the key of Thy Name, the Ever-Blessed, the All-Powerful, the All-Glorious, the Most Great, and hast summoned all mankind to the ocean of Thy presence.”

(Prayers and Meditations CLXXVI.1)

The gate is opened by God, and the summons goes to “all mankind”. A separate passage, quoted by Shoghi Effendi in The Advent of Divine Justice, says that in this Day “a door is open wider than both heaven and earth”; it is set out on the page An Atom as the Sun, where it belongs to the argument about small deeds.

Bahá’u’lláh also states what a person receives who drinks:

“He that partaketh of the waters of My Revelation will taste all the incorruptible delights ordained by God from the beginning that hath no beginning to the end that hath no end.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.24; source Tablet not identified)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá on the age of bounty

‘Abdu’l-Bahá describes the Day as an event in the world, which anyone can observe:

“Praise be to Him Who hath fashioned this new era, this age of majesty, even as an unfolding pageant where the realities of all things can be exposed to view. Now are clouds of bounty raining down and the gifts of the loving Lord are clearly manifest; for both the seen and the unseen worlds have been illumined, and the Promised One hath come to earth and the beauty of the Adored One hath shone forth.”

(‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá 19.2)

He gives the reason for the bounty: “the Promised One hath come to earth”. In an opening to the friends He writes in the same voice:

“The light of guidance is resplendent. The morning-star of the Concourse on High ascendeth. The cloud of mercy raineth down. The sun of bestowal blazeth and dazzleth.”

(‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Additional Tablets, Extracts and Talks, ATET 204.1)

Both passages use the present tense: the clouds “are raining down”, the cloud of mercy “raineth down”. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá is describing what is happening as he writes, not what will happen.

Grace that reaches beyond the hearers

Two passages say that the grace of this Day extends to people who did not themselves recognise the Cause. The first is about parents. Bahá’u’lláh writes to a correspondent who had mentioned the station of parents in the next world:

“One of the special bounties of this Dispensation is that the sun of divine favour will shine upon the parents of any soul who hath recognized the Dayspring of the Cause of God, even though they themselves may not have attained to His recognition.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Additional Tablets and Extracts from Tablets Revealed by Bahá’u’lláh, ATE 24.1)

The passage says “the parents of any soul who hath recognized” Him: the condition is placed on the child, and the favour reaches the parents. It calls this “one of the special bounties of this Dispensation”, so the text does not present it as a general rule of earlier dispensations. The Tablet ends by telling the questioner to “render thanks”.

The second passage is from ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in answer to a question about souls who died before they heard the Call:

“As for thy question concerning those righteous souls who passed away ere they heard the Call of this Revelation, know thou that those who ascended unto God ere they heard this Call, but who followed the precepts of Christ and walked in the Straight Path—these verily attained, after ascending to the Divine Kingdom, unto the Refulgent Light.”

(‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Extract from a Tablet, Additional Tablets, Extracts and Talks, ATET 215.1)

The answer speaks of one group: those who “followed the precepts of Christ and walked in the Straight Path”. It does not state a rule about every soul who lived before the Revelation. What it does say is that those souls were not shut out for having died too early: they “attained” the “Refulgent Light” in the next world.

The Guardian on the effusion of grace

Shoghi Effendi, in the part of The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh where he has just quoted Bahá’u’lláh on the outpouring of divine grace in this Dispensation, concludes:

“Such, dearly-beloved friends, is the effusion of celestial grace vouchsafed by the Almighty to this age, this most illumined century!”

(The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 4.34)

He goes on in the same paragraph to say that the friends stand too close to the Revelation to judge its size, and that they should avoid any thought, word or deed that might dim its brilliance. For Shoghi Effendi the grace is a reason for care in conduct, as well as for gratitude.

How this facet connects

  • All Things Rejoice: this page is about what is given to all created things; that page is about how they respond. “Infused into all created things” and “pervaded all things” are the same reach, seen from the giver’s side here and the receivers’ side there.
  • An Atom as the Sun: the door “open wider than both heaven and earth” and the weight of small deeds are set out there. This page adds the receiving side, in which a person’s share is a palm, a cup or a gallon-measure, and no share is counted as small.
  • The Divine Springtime: rain, clouds and fruit recur in the passages on this page (“clouds of bounty”, “the trees of Paradise”). That page follows the same images as a description of the season of the world.
  • The Day Can Be Missed: the grace is poured out on all, and the duty is “to attain that share”. That page deals with the texts that say a person can fail to take it.
  • Promises of Assistance: that article collects the promises of confirmation to those who arise to serve. This page is about the earlier fact the promises rest on, that grace has already been given to everyone.

What the sources do not settle

  • The Tablet containing Additional Tablets and Extracts 24.1 (the parents’ bounty) is not named in the file. The source gives the question (about the station of parents in the next world) but not the questioner or the date.
  • The passage quoted in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh 6.24 (“He that partaketh of the waters of My Revelation”) is given without a Tablet name, and the original wording is not in this corpus.
  • The answer on righteous souls before the Call (ATET 215.1) names one group, followers of Christ. The passage does not say whether followers of other earlier Messengers are included, and this page does not say so either.
  • The passage on parents does not say what “the sun of divine favour will shine upon” them means in the next world, or whether it is limited to certain parents.
  • Whether “infused into all created things” (Gleanings IV.1) refers to the same thing as grace “poured out upon men” is not stated. The passages use different verbs and different scopes without comment.

For songwriting

Singable phrases (exact, with source):

  • “God’s most excellent favors have been poured out upon men” (Gleanings IV.1)
  • “the Ocean of God’s mercy hath been manifested unto men” (Gleanings V.1)
  • “the clouds of His bountiful favor have overshadowed the whole of mankind” (Gleanings V.1)
  • “the flood of grace which God poureth forth for him” (Gleanings V.4)
  • “the grace of God hath permeated and pervaded all things” (Gleanings CXLII.1)
  • “the clouds of bounty rained down” (Days of Remembrance 10.9)
  • “the poor have been enriched” (Days of Remembrance 11.8)
  • “the sinful have partaken of the wine of Thy forgiveness” (Days of Remembrance 11.8)
  • “the sick have quaffed from the waters of Thy gracious healing” (Days of Remembrance 11.8)
  • “the breeze of God is wafted” (The Kitáb-i-Íqán 197)
  • “the pen is stilled and the tongue is speechless” (The Kitáb-i-Íqán 197)
  • “The cloud of mercy raineth down” (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, ATET 204.1)
  • “The sun of bestowal blazeth and dazzleth” (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, ATET 204.1)

Concrete images:

  • Rain from clouds, over all of mankind (Gleanings V.1; Days of Remembrance 10.9; ATET 204.1).
  • An ocean, and a flood, with a person standing at its edge (Gleanings V.1, V.4; Days of Remembrance 11.8, “the shores of the sea”).
  • A palm of a hand, a cup, a gallon-measure: three sizes of vessel held under the same flood (Gleanings V.4).
  • Wine of forgiveness, waters of healing, a tree giving shade (Days of Remembrance 11.8).
  • Trees of Paradise with fruit apportioned to each (Days of Remembrance 10.9).
  • A gate unlocked with a key (Prayers and Meditations CLXXVI.1).
  • A pen stilled and a tongue speechless (The Kitáb-i-Íqán 197).
  • Sun shining on a parent (ATE 24.1).

A possible emotional arc for one song:

  1. Arrival: the Day is announced as one where favours have already been poured out (Gleanings IV.1, V.1).
  2. The list: the poor, the abased, the sinful, the sick, the sorrowing and the destitute, each named and each helped (Days of Remembrance 11.8).
  3. The measure: the flood is large, and each takes a palm, a cup or a gallon; no one’s share is small (Gleanings V.4).
  4. The reach: grace extends to parents and to souls who died before the Call (ATE 24.1; ATET 215.1, taken with its limit).
  5. Silence: the writer cannot say more (The Kitáb-i-Íqán 197), and the song ends on thanks (Days of Remembrance 10.9, “Blessed, then, be God”).

What a lyric must not claim:

  • Do not say that every soul is saved or forgiven regardless of response. The texts say grace is poured out and that a person’s duty is “to attain that share”; Days of Remembrance 10.9 says the fruit is apportioned to “them that turn unto Him”.
  • Do not extend ATET 215.1 beyond its stated group. It speaks of righteous souls who followed Christ. Do not write that all who died before the Revelation attained the Refulgent Light.
  • Do not extend ATE 24.1 to all parents. The condition is that the child has recognised “the Dayspring of the Cause of God”.
  • Do not turn “the poor have been enriched” into a promise of material wealth for any person. The passage is in a prayer, and “incomparable wealth” is God’s.
  • Do not attribute the Days of Remembrance prayer words to a speaker other than Bahá’u’lláh, nor put “all created things were immersed in the sea of purification” in Shoghi Effendi’s mouth: it is Bahá’u’lláh’s phrase from the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, quoted by the Guardian.
  • Do not name a Tablet for the WOB 6.24 passage (“the waters of My Revelation”); it is not identified.
  • Do not invent a quotation for the “door wider than heaven and earth” other than the text given in The Advent of Divine Justice.
