The Coming of Age of Humanity: the Day of maturity and unity

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

The Writings present this Day as the Day in which the human race reaches maturity. The texts speak of a human race with stages of life like those of a person: infancy, childhood, adolescence, and a coming of age that is its unification in one world order and one civilization. This page collects what Bahá’u’lláh, ʻAbdu’l-Bahá, Shoghi Effendi and the Universal House of Justice say on that point. It is one facet of the series The greatness of this Day.

One distinction runs through the page and the texts keep it exact. The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh has already released the powers that bring humanity to maturity, and those powers are at work now. The coming of age itself, in Shoghi Effendi’s words, “is yet to come”. The Universal House of Justice describes the present as the end of humanity’s adolescence, with the coming of age ahead.

The passages

The Day for the unity of mankind

Bahá’u’lláh states what this Day asks of people:

It beseemeth all men, in this Day, to take firm hold on the Most Great Name, and to establish the unity of all mankind. (Gleanings C.7)

He also states what the Day is for, from the side of human nature:

The potentialities inherent in the station of man, the full measure of his destiny on earth, the innate excellence of his reality, must all be manifested in this promised Day of God. (Gleanings CLXII.1)

The first passage names a task, the unity of all mankind, and the means, the Most Great Name. The second says that the Day is the time in which human capacity is realised in full. Together they give the two sides of the facet: maturity is the full use of what humanity is made for, and unity is how it shows.

ʻAbdu’l-Bahá states the same in one sentence:

This day is the day of union, the day of the ingathering of all mankind. (Selections from the Writings of ʻAbdu’l-Bahá 207.2)

In another passage He adds that this is possible now and was not possible before:

Hence the unity of all mankind can in this day be achieved. Verily this is none other but one of the wonders of this wondrous age, this glorious century. Of this past ages have been deprived, for this century—the century of light—hath been endowed with unique and unprecedented glory, power and illumination. (Selections from the Writings of ʻAbdu’l-Bahá 15.6)

The claim is about the age, not about the people in it: earlier ages were deprived of this possibility because they lacked the power and illumination that this century has received.

The new World Order

In the Kitáb-i-Aqdas Bahá’u’lláh speaks of an order that has already acted on the world:

The world’s equilibrium hath been upset through the vibrating influence of this most great, this new World Order. Mankind’s ordered life hath been revolutionized through the agency of this unique, this wondrous System—the like of which mortal eyes have never witnessed. (The Kitáb-i-Aqdas 181)

The verbs are in the past tense: the equilibrium “hath been upset”. Shoghi Effendi reads the disturbance of the world as a result of the Revelation and as a stage on the way to the coming of age. He writes in Citadel of Faith:

The creative energies released at the hour of the birth of His Revelation, endowing mankind with the potentialities of the attainment of maturity are deranging, during the present transitional age, the equilibrium of the entire planet as the inevitable prelude to the consummation in world unity of the coming of age of the human race. (Citadel of Faith 49.3)

Here the order of events is stated plainly. The energies were released at the birth of the Revelation. They are deranging the planet’s equilibrium in the present transitional age. The consummation, which is the coming of age in world unity, comes after. The upheaval is described as a prelude to maturity, not as its opposite.

The stages of humanity, and the station of this Day

ʻAbdu’l-Bahá, in Some Answered Questions, gives the stages as a statement about the Manifestations’ stations. In chapter 30 He interprets the account of Adam and the tree of life, and says of the tree of life that it is “the station of the Word of God and His universal Manifestation”. He continues:

For the station of Adam, with regard to the appearance and manifestation of the divine perfections, was that of the embryo; the station of Christ was that of coming of age and maturation; and the dawning of the Most Great Luminary was the station of the perfection of the essence and the attributes. (Some Answered Questions 30.7)

The three stages (embryo, coming of age, perfection) describe the appearance of divine perfections in the Manifestations of each period. The passage does not say that the human race came of age at the time of Christ. It sets Bahá’u’lláh’s Revelation at the stage of the perfection of the essence and the attributes, beyond the stage of maturation. The passage should not be read as a timetable for the human race; for that, see the statements of Shoghi Effendi and the House of Justice below.

In a talk recorded in The Promulgation of Universal Peace, ʻAbdu’l-Bahá says what follows for the needs of the present:

That which was applicable to human needs during the early history of the race could neither meet nor satisfy the demands of this day and period of newness and consummation. Humanity has emerged from its former degrees of limitation and preliminary training. (The Promulgation of Universal Peace 130.4; a talk recorded in notes)

In the same talk, after describing the condition of the world, He says that the bounties of this century of maturity are infinite:

While this is true and apparent, it is, likewise, evident that the Lord of mankind has bestowed infinite bounties upon the world in this century of maturity and consummation. The ocean of divine mercy is surging, the vernal showers are descending, the Sun of Reality is shining gloriously. Heavenly teachings applicable to the advancement in human conditions have been revealed in this merciful age. (The Promulgation of Universal Peace 130.7; a talk recorded in notes)

Because these two passages are from a recorded talk, this page uses them only for points that the written texts above also make: that the Day is a new stage, and that the teachings suited to it have been revealed.

A sign of maturity named by Bahá’u’lláh

The notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, prepared at the Bahá’í World Centre, quote a statement of Bahá’u’lláh on what maturity looks like. The notes do not name the Tablet. The Promised Day Is Come quotes the same passage and names it as Bahá’u’lláh’s Tablet addressed to Shaykh Salmán:

One of the signs of the maturity of the world is that no one will accept to bear the weight of kingship. Kingship will remain with none willing to bear alone its weight. That day will be the day whereon wisdom will be manifested among mankind. (Bahá’u’lláh, Tablet to Shaykh Salmán, quoted in The Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 194)

This is one concrete sign, given by Bahá’u’lláh, of a mature world. It concerns how authority is carried, and it is put in the future (“that day will be the day”).

The Guardian: the Great Age is still to come

Shoghi Effendi is exact about time. In The Promised Day Is Come he writes:

The ages of its infancy and childhood are past, never again to return, while the Great Age, the consummation of all ages, which must signalize the coming of age of the entire human race, is yet to come. The convulsions of this transitional and most turbulent period in the annals of humanity are the essential prerequisites, and herald the inevitable approach, of that Age of Ages, “the time of the end,” in which the folly and tumult of strife that has, since the dawn of history, blackened the annals of mankind, will have been finally transmuted into the wisdom and the tranquility of an undisturbed, a universal, and lasting peace, in which the discord and separation of the children of men will have given way to the worldwide reconciliation, and the complete unification of the divers elements that constitute human society. (The Promised Day Is Come 288)

Two things are stated. Infancy and childhood are over for good. The coming of age belongs to the Great Age, which has not arrived. He then describes the moment of its recognition:

Then will the coming of age of the entire human race be proclaimed and celebrated by all the peoples and nations of the earth. Then will the banner of the Most Great Peace be hoisted. (The Promised Day Is Come 301)

In God Passes By he places the coming of age among the events of the Golden Age of the Faith, and says that this Age is not yet here:

The Golden Age of the Faith itself that must witness the unification of all the peoples and nations of the world, the establishment of the Most Great Peace, the inauguration of the Kingdom of the Father upon earth, the coming of age of the entire human race and the birth of a world civilization, inspired and directed by the creative energies released by Bahá’u’lláh’s World Order, shining in its meridian splendor, is still unborn and its glories unsuspected. (God Passes By, Retrospect and Prospect 13)

The sentence lists what the Golden Age must witness, and the coming of age is one item in that list. It also says that the civilization is ”inspired and directed by the creative energies released by Bahá’u’lláh’s World Order“: the energies are already released, and the civilization is not yet formed. The notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas summarise how Shoghi Effendi uses the term:

The coming of age of the human race has been associated by Shoghi Effendi with the unification of the whole of mankind, the establishment of a world commonwealth, and an unprecedented stimulus to “the intellectual, the moral, and spiritual life of the entire human race.” (The Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 194.5)

The Universal House of Justice: adolescence ending, maturity ahead

The House of Justice describes the present stage with the same language of life stages. In a message of October 1985 it wrote:

The human race, as a distinct, organic unit, has passed through evolutionary stages analogous to the stages of infancy and childhood in the lives of its individual members, and is now in the culminating period of its turbulent adolescence approaching its long-awaited coming of age. (Universal House of Justice, October 1985)

In its message of 1 November 2022 it wrote:

Humanity is now in the concluding period of its turbulent adolescence and is going through a period of transition. Standing at the threshold of a long-awaited coming of age, its needs are no longer served by the ideas and behaviours of prior stages. Bahá’u’lláh has appeared to humanity in this period of its adolescence, when it is in need of maturation. As you are well aware, He has provided the means for establishing the unity of humankind, the hallmark of a mature world. (Universal House of Justice, 1 November 2022)

Two points are added to the Guardian’s statements. The present is named: it is the concluding period of adolescence, and humanity stands at the threshold, not past it. And Bahá’u’lláh’s part is named: He appeared in this period and ”has provided the means for establishing the unity of humankind“. The message of 28 November 2023 states His purpose in the same terms:

Bahá’u’lláh’s purpose is to usher in a new stage in human development—the organic and spiritual unity of the peoples and nations of the world—signalizing thereby the coming of age of the human race and characterized, in the fullness of time, by the emergence of a world civilization and culture. (Universal House of Justice, 28 November 2023)

The phrase ”in the fullness of time“ again puts the civilization ahead.

The Introduction to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, written by the House of Justice, applies this to Bahá’u’lláh and to His Book:

This year, the 149th of the Bahá’í era, marks the Centenary of the Ascension of Bahá’u’lláh, Bearer of the universal Revelation of God destined to lead humanity to its collective coming of age. (The Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Introduction 1)

To build anew the whole world” is the claim and challenge of His Message, and the Kitáb-i-Aqdas is the Charter of the future world civilization that Bahá’u’lláh has come to raise up. (The Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Introduction 2)

The Revelation is described as ”destined to lead“ humanity to the coming of age, and the Kitáb-i-Aqdas as the charter of a civilization that is ”future“. In One Common Faith the House of Justice says why the Revelation can do this:

Through His revelation, the principles required for the collective coming of age of the human race have been invested with the one power capable of penetrating to the roots of human motivation and of altering behaviour. (One Common Faith 52)

Principles of unity were known before. What this Day adds is a power that changes motive and behaviour. In Century of Light the House of Justice states what ʻAbdu’l-Bahá announced and what He did not:

At the heart of the Master’s message was the announcement that the long-promised Day for the unification of humanity and the establishment on earth of the Kingdom of God had come. (Century of Light III.17)

Rather, the Master proclaimed the coming of age of humankind and the emergence of a global civilization in which the development of the whole range of human potentialities will be the fruit of the interaction between universal spiritual values, on the one hand, and, on the other, material advances that were even then still undreamed of. (Century of Light III.17)

The first passage says the Day ”had come“. The second uses the verb ”proclaimed“ for the coming of age and ”will be“ for the civilization. The proclamation is made; the fruit is in the future.

How this facet connects

  • The Creative Word treats the power that the Revelation carries. This page relies on it where the texts speak of ”creative energies released“ and of a power that alters behaviour; that page says what the power is and how it works.
  • The King of Days places this Day above all other days, and gives the ranking that the statement about the ”Most Great Luminary“ in Some Answered Questions 30 fits into. This page adds the human side: what the Day brings to the human race.
  • The Resurrection treats the Day as the Resurrection. This page treats the same Day from the side of the human race as a whole; the two pages are read together.
  • The Day Can Be Missed is the caution that goes with this page: a Day in which unity is possible is still one that people can fail to recognise.
  • The Stages to the Most Great Peace sets out the sequence (the Lesser Peace, the Most Great Peace) that leads toward the coming of age; this page does not repeat that sequence.
  • Promises of the Future collects what the Writings say is yet to come, which is the part of this facet that the Guardian places in the Great Age.

What the sources do not settle

  • The texts do not give a date for the coming of age or for the Great Age or the Golden Age. They say these are ”yet to come“ and ”still unborn“.
  • Shoghi Effendi uses ”coming of age“ for the unification of the race and its consequences. The House of Justice says humanity is at the threshold. The texts here do not say how a moment of ”arrival“ would be recognised beyond the statement of The Promised Day Is Come 301 that it will be proclaimed by all peoples and nations.
  • Some Answered Questions 30.7 assigns ”coming of age and maturation“ to the station of Christ, as a station of the Manifestation. The same words, applied by Shoghi Effendi and the House of Justice to the human race, refer to a later event. The texts treat these as different uses and do not reconcile them in a single sentence.
  • The Tablet to Shaykh Salmán is named in The Promised Day Is Come, not in the notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas; the passage on kingship is quoted in both. The wording is from the English translation of that Tablet as quoted; the original wording is not in this corpus.
  • The two talks from The Promulgation of Universal Peace are recorded notes of what ʻAbdu’l-Bahá said, not texts He wrote.
  • The quotation from the Introduction to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, 2, begins with a closing quotation mark in the source file, because the sentence quotes an earlier phrase; it is copied as it stands.

For songwriting

Singable phrases

  • ”take firm hold on the Most Great Name“ (Gleanings C.7)
  • ”establish the unity of all mankind“ (Gleanings C.7)
  • ”the full measure of his destiny on earth“ (Gleanings CLXII.1)
  • ”This day is the day of union“ (Selections 207.2)
  • ”the day of the ingathering of all mankind“ (Selections 207.2)
  • ”the century of light“ (Selections 15.6)
  • ”The world’s equilibrium hath been upset“ (Kitáb-i-Aqdas 181)
  • ”that of the embryo“ (Some Answered Questions 30.7)
  • ”The ages of its infancy and childhood are past“ (The Promised Day Is Come 288)
  • ”never again to return“ (The Promised Day Is Come 288)
  • ”Then will the banner of the Most Great Peace be hoisted“ (The Promised Day Is Come 301)
  • ”is still unborn and its glories unsuspected“ (God Passes By, Retrospect and Prospect 13)
  • ”One of the signs of the maturity of the world“ (Bahá’u’lláh, Tablet to Shaykh Salmán, Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 194)
  • ”Standing at the threshold of a long-awaited coming of age“ (Universal House of Justice, 1 November 2022)

Concrete images

  • Stages of a life: embryo, infancy, childhood, adolescence, coming of age (Some Answered Questions 30.7; The Promised Day Is Come 288; Universal House of Justice, October 1985).
  • A banner hoisted, and peoples and nations celebrating together (The Promised Day Is Come 301).
  • A threshold (Universal House of Justice, 1 November 2022).
  • A king’s weight that no one will bear alone (Bahá’u’lláh, Tablet to Shaykh Salmán).
  • The planet’s equilibrium upset (Kitáb-i-Aqdas 181; Citadel of Faith 49.3).
  • A century of light (Selections 15.6).
  • An ingathering (Selections 207.2).

A possible emotional arc for one song

  1. Past stages gone: ”The ages of its infancy and childhood are past“ (The Promised Day Is Come 288).
  2. The present shaking: ”The world’s equilibrium hath been upset“ (Kitáb-i-Aqdas 181), understood as a prelude (Citadel of Faith 49.3).
  3. The call: ”establish the unity of all mankind“ (Gleanings C.7); ”the day of the ingathering of all mankind“ (Selections 207.2).
  4. At the threshold: ”Standing at the threshold of a long-awaited coming of age“ (Universal House of Justice, 1 November 2022).
  5. The promise, held in the future tense: ”Then will the banner of the Most Great Peace be hoisted“ (The Promised Day Is Come 301).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not say the human race has already come of age. Shoghi Effendi says the coming of age is ”yet to come“; the House of Justice says humanity stands at its threshold.
  • Do not say the Golden Age or the Great Age has begun. God Passes By says it is ”still unborn“.
  • Do not turn ”coming of age“ into a date or a year, and do not say it will happen by a given event or generation.
  • Do not use ”coming of age“ for the human race in the sense of Some Answered Questions 30.7, where it describes the station of Christ.
  • Do not attribute the words of the House of Justice about adolescence to Bahá’u’lláh or to Shoghi Effendi.
  • Do not attribute the Salmán passage to a different Tablet; Shoghi Effendi names it as the Tablet to Shaykh Salmán in The Promised Day Is Come.
  • Do not present the upheaval as the aim; the texts call it a prelude and a prerequisite.
  • Do not quote the talks in The Promulgation of Universal Peace as ʻAbdu’l-Bahá’s written words.
