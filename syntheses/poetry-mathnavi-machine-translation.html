  • What the poem itself states: The poem opens by invoking the Divine Beloved as the ”Life of the Throne“ (Ḥayátu’l-‘Arsh), the ”Sun of loving-kindness“ (Khurshíd-i-Vidád), ”Bahá“ (Couplets 10, 21, 30, 115, 161), and ”Bahá’u’lláh“ (Couplet 179), calling upon Him to rend the veils, raise the dead through the trumpet-blast of the ”Seraphiel of Glory“ (Saráfíl-i-Bahá, Couplet 30), and plant the Divine Lote-Tree (Sidrih) in the soil of the heart (Couplets 31–35). Any further historical circumstances of composition (such as exact date or city of revelation) are not stated in the source header or text and are therefore marked not established here.

Literal translation

1.

اى حيات العرش خورشيد وداد

O Life of the Throne (Ḥayátu’l-‘Arsh), Sun of loving-kindness (Vidád),

که جهان‌و‌امکان‌چه تونورى نزاد

for the world and contingency (imkán) bore no light like Thee!

2.

گر نبودى خلق محجوب از لقا

Were creation not veiled from reunion (liqá),

يک دو حرفى گفتم‌ از سرّ بقا

I would have spoken a word or two of the mystery of immortality (baqá),

3.

تا که جانها جمله مرهونت شوند

so that souls might all become pledged to Thee,

تا که دلها جمله ‌مجنونت شوند

so that hearts might all become enraptured for Thee;

4.

تا ببينى عالمى مجنون و مست

so that Thou mightest behold a world enraptured and intoxicated,

روحها بهر نثار اندر‌ دو ‌دست

with spirits in both hands for offering (nithár);

5.

تا رسد امر تو اى فخر زمان

so that when Thy command (amr) arriveth, O Pride of the age,

بر فشانند بر قدومت رايگان

they may scatter [them] freely at Thy coming.

6.

سر‌برآر ازکوه جان‌خورشيد وار

Raise Thy head from the mountain of the soul like the sun,

تا ببينندت عيان از ‌هر‌کنار

that they may behold Thee manifestly from every side.

7.

جلوه ده آنروى همچون ماه را

Display in splendour that face like unto the moon;

سبز‌و‌‌خرّم کن ز لطف ‌اين‌گاه را

make verdant and joyous through grace this place.

8.

قطره مى‌جويد ز بحرت کوثرى

The drop seeketh from Thy sea a Kawthar;

کوثرى کن ز‌انکه شاه مهترى

make [it] a Kawthar, for Thou art the supreme King.

9.

ذرّه گشته ملتمس نور تو را

The mote hath become a suppliant for Thy light;

وا ‌دهش‌ از لطف بيچون ‌و چرا

bestow it upon it out of unconditional grace.

10.

دانه بگشاده دهان سوى سما

The seed hath opened its mouth toward heaven,

تا بيايد بر‌ وى‌‌ از فضلت بها

that there may come upon it from Thy bounty splendour (bahá).

11.

قطره هاى رحمتت بر وى ببار

Rain down the drops of Thy mercy upon it,

اى مليک‌ عرش و اى مير ديار

O Sovereign of the Throne and O Prince of the realm!

12.

خرق‌کن‌اين پردهء صد‌‌توى را

Rend asunder this hundred-fold veil;

خوش تماشا ده کنون‌ آنروى را

graciously grant the vision now of that Face.

13.

زانکه در فضلت نباشد شبهه‌اى

For in Thy bounty there is no doubt;

بهر ما بر بند ز لطفت توشه‌اى

bind up for us out of Thy grace a provision.

14.

مشرق ‌کل‌ کن‌ کنون‌اين‌ غرب را

Make this West now the Dayspring (mashriq) of all;

بهجت مل ده کنون اين شرب را

give the joy of wine (mul) now to this draught.

15.

نور دل را نور ده ز انوار نور

Give light to the light of the heart from the lights of Light,

تا ببينند ‌‌از ‌رخت‌ انوار ‌طور

that they may behold from Thy cheek the lights of Sinai (Ṭúr).

16.

هان بکش آن تيغ اللّهيت را

Lo, draw forth (bikash) that divine sword of Thine;

هين بکش اين‌ دشمنان ‌دينت را

behold, slay (bikush) these enemies of Thy religion!

17.

بر فروزان نار‌ ربّانيت را

Kindle Thy Lordly fire;

خوش بسوزان ملحد‌ حربيت را

burn up well Thy warring heretic.

18.

جمله خفّاشند اى خورشيد روز

All are bats, O Sun of day;

سر بر آر‌ و جمله ظلمانى بسوز

raise Thy head and burn up all the darkened ones.

19.

صاف‌کن اين درد غم‌آلوده را

Clarify these sorrow-tainted dregs;

نور ده ‌‌اين شمع شب افسرده را

give light to this night-quenched candle.

20.

عالمى قائم بتو چون تو‌‌بجان

A world subsisteth through Thee, as Thou art in the soul,

تا شود پيدا ز امرت ‌کن ‌فکان

that ”Be, and it was“ (Kun fa-kán) may become manifest through Thy command.

21.

اى بهاى جان بياد روى تو

O Glory of the soul (Baháy-i-Ján), in remembrance of Thy face

نکته هاگويم همى از خوى تو

I continually utter subtleties concerning Thy nature;

22.

تا بر آرم جانها را از خرد

that I may lift souls beyond reason (khirad),

تا ببينم درّ عشقت که خرد

that I may see who will purchase (kharad) the pearl of Thy love.

23.

بر فروزم آتشى اندر ‌جهان

I will kindle a fire within the world,

تا بسوزم پرده هاى قدسيان

that I may burn away the veils of the inmates of holiness (qudsíyán).

24.

حور معنى را بر‌‌آرم از حجاب

I will bring forth the Maid of the spirit (Ḥúr-i-ma‘ní) from behind the veil;

نور غيبى را کنم کشف نقاب

I will unveil the unseen Light.

25.

رمزى از اسرار عشق سرمدى

A mystery from the mysteries of everlasting love

باز گويم چون بجان باز آمدى

will I recount, since Thou hast returned to the soul.

26.

خوش‌بيا‌اى‌طير‌‌نارى در بيان

Come forth gladly, O Bird of Fire, into utterance (bayán),

تا نماند وصف هستى در ميان

so that no attribute of self-existence may remain between.

27.

پاک‌کن اين قلبهاى پر حسد

Purify these hearts full of envy;

نقد کن اين قلب‌هاى بى رصد

turn into pure coin these counterfeit, unwatched hearts (qalbhá),

28.

تا که بيهوشان عهدت اى‌کريم

so that the senseless ones of Thy Covenant, O Generous One,

هم بهوش آيند از جام قديم

may likewise come to their senses through the Ancient Cup.

29.

بلکه از‌الحان قدس اى يار ما

Nay rather, through the melodies of holiness, O our Beloved (Yár),

دورکن هم هوش و بى هوشى زما

banish both sense and senselessness from us.

30.

اى سرافيل بها اى‌شاه جان

O Seraphiel of Glory (Saráfíl-i-Bahá), O King of the soul,

يک حياتى عرضه کن بر ‌مردگان

offer a [new] life unto the dead!

31.

سدره اوّل بود ‌ز ‌اغصان دل

The primal Lote-Tree (Sidrih) is of the branches (aghṣán) of the heart;

وا رهانش از هوا و آب و گل

deliver it from desire and water and clay,

32.

تا‌ز‌جوهر و ز‌عرض فارغ شود

that it may be freed from substance and from accident,

تا ز شمعش شمسها بازغ شود

that from its candle suns may dawn forth.

33.

اين‌نهالت‌غرس‌کن‌در‌ارض‌دل

Plant this sapling of Thine in the soil of the heart;

پس مقدّس دارش از‌‌اشراق و ظل

then keep it sanctified above sunrise and shadow.

34.

هم‌تو‌حفظ از‌مختلف‌بادش نما

Do Thou both guard it from contrary winds,

هم ز وهم مشرک آزادش نما

and set it free from the idle fancy of the polytheist.

35.

اصل او ثابت نما در‌ارض‌جان

Make its root firm in the earth of the soul;

فرع او را بگذران‌ از آسمان

cause its branch to pass beyond heaven.

36.

نو بهارى تو ز نو آور عيان

Bring forth manifestly anew a new springtime,

تا زحشرت بر جهند اين مردگان

that through Thy resurrection (ḥashr) these dead may leap forth.

37.

جوش‌درياهاى‌عشق‌از‌جوش‌تو

The surging of the seas of love is from Thy surging;

هوش اطيار بقا ‌از هوش تو

the consciousness of the birds of immortality is from Thy consciousness.

38.

بوى پيراهن بوز از مصر جان

Waft the fragrance of the Robe [of Joseph] from the Egypt of the soul;

سدره موسى نما اينجا عيان

make manifest here the Lote-Tree of Moses.

39.

اى نگار از روى توآمد بهار

O Beauteous One (Nigár), from Thy face the springtime hath come;

زين بهار ‌آمد حقايق بيشمار

from this springtime have come realities beyond number.

40.

هرگل‌از‌وى‌دفترى‌ازحسن‌دوست

Every rose thereof is a scroll of the beauty of the Friend (Dúst);

هر دل از وى کوثرى از فضل هوست

every heart thereby is a Kawthar of the bounty of Him (Hú).

41.

اين‌بهاران را‌خزان نايد ز پى

No autumn followeth after this springtime;

جمله گلها طائف اندر ‌حول وى

all roses circumambulate around it.

42.

اين‌بهارى‌نه‌که‌جان‌‌‌درکش‌کند

This is not a springtime that the soul can comprehend;

اين بهارى که روانها را کند

this is a springtime that uprooteth [or: fashioneth] spirits.

43.

آن بهاران شوق خوبان آورد

That [earthly] springtime bringeth yearning for fair ones,

و اين بهاران‌ عشق يزدان آورد

whilst this springtime bringeth the love of God (Yazdán).

44.

آن بهاران را فنا باشد عقب

That springtime hath evanescence (faná) in its wake,

و اين بهاران را بقا باشد لقب

whilst this springtime hath immortality (baqá) as its title.

45.

آن‌بهار‌از‌فصل خيزد در‌جهان

That springtime ariseth from the season in the world,

و اين بهار از نور روى دلستان

whilst this springtime [ariseth] from the light of the face of the Ravisher of hearts (Dilistán).

46.

آن بهاران‌‌ لاله ها آرد برون

That springtime bringeth forth tulips,

و اين بهاران ناله ها دارد کنون

whilst this springtime holdeth lamentations now.

47.

اين بهار سرمدى از نور شاه

This everlasting springtime, through the light of the King,

بر زده خرگاه تا عرش اله

hath pitched its royal pavilion unto the Throne of God.

48.

جمله‌در‌خرگاه‌او داخل‌شدند

All have entered into His pavilion;

گر تو چشمت هست بنگر هوشمند

if thou hast eyes, behold, O discerning one!

49.

شاه ما چون پرده از رخ بفکند

When our King casteth the veil from His cheek,

اين بهاران خيمه بر گردون زند

this springtime pitcheth its tent upon the firmament.

50.

يار ما چون بفکند از رخ نقاب

When our Beloved (Yár) casteth the veil from His cheek,

اين بهاران بر فروزد بى ‌حجاب

this springtime shineth forth unveiled.

51.

ما برويش در بهاران اندريم

Through His face we are in springtime;

ما ز‌‌رويش در گلستان ننگريم

because of His face we look not upon the rose-garden.

52.

ما بذکرش فارغيم از ذکر کان

Through His remembrance we are independent of the mention of the mine [of contingent being];

ما ز شمسش بازغيم اندر جهان

through His sun we are dawning within the world.

53.

گر‌نسيمى‌بر‌وزد زين‌خوش بهار

Should a breeze blow from this goodly springtime,

يوسفان بينى که آيند در نظار

thou wilt see Josephs coming forth into view.

54.

گر نسيمى بر وزد زين بوستان

Should a breeze blow from this orchard,

يوسفان روح بينى در جهان

thou wilt see Josephs of the spirit in the world.

55.

جسمها‌بينى‌که‌گردد‌همچو روح

Thou wilt see bodies becoming like unto spirit,

روح را هر دم رسد صد گون‌‌فتوح

whilst to the spirit there arriveth every moment a hundred kinds of victories.

56.

اين ربيع قدس جانان هر‌دمى

This holy spring of the Soul of souls (Jánán) at every moment

صد بيان دارد ولی کو محرمى

hath a hundred utterances, yet where is a confidant?

57.

اين بيان باشد ‌مقدّس از لسان

This utterance is sanctified above tongue;

کى بمعنيش رسند اين ناکسان

how can these base ones attain unto its meaning?

58.

اين ‌بيان ‌از‌گفت ‌و‌لفظ‌و‌صوت ‌نيست

This utterance is not of speech and word and sound;

اين ‌بيان‌جانست و‌اورا موت نيست

this utterance is spirit, and for it there is no death.

59.

عاشقان بينى تو اندر اين بهار

Thou seest lovers in this springtime

جان نثار آورده هر دم صد هزار

bringing their souls as an offering every moment by the hundred thousand.

60.

اين بهار عزّ ‌روحانى ‌بود

This is the springtime of spiritual glory;

اين ربيع قدس ربّانى بود

this is the spring of Lordly holiness.

61.

گر وزد بر تو نسيمى زين سبا

Should a breeze waft upon thee from this Sheba (Sabá),

جان فانيّت کشد جام بقا

thy mortal soul will quaff the cup of immortality.

62.

گرنسيمى‌آيدت از‌کوى دوست

Should a breeze come to thee from the quarter of the Friend (Dúst),

جان‌فدايش‌کن‌که‌اينجان‌هم‌از اوست

sacrifice thy soul for it, for this soul too is from Him.

63.

لالهء توحيد بين‌ در اين بهار

Behold the tulip of divine unity (tawḥíd) in this springtime;

سنبل تجريد بين از زلف يار

behold the hyacinth of detachment (tajríd) from the tress of the Beloved (Yár).

64.

غنچه‌هاى‌معرفت‌زين‌طرف‌‌جو

The buds of divine knowledge (ma‘rifat) along this stream-bank

جملگى از شوق او در جستجو

are all in search out of yearning for Him.

65.

سروهايش حاکى از قدّ نگار

Its cypresses tell of the stature of the Beauteous One (Nigár);

سبزه هايش دفترى از خدّ يار

its greenswards are a scroll of the cheek of the Beloved (Yár).

66.

بلبلانش مست از جام الست

Its nightingales (bulbulán) are intoxicated with the cup of Alast;

قمريانش از جمال دوست مست

its ringdoves (qumríyán) are intoxicated with the beauty of the Friend (Dúst).

67.

عندليبان در هواى وصل او

The warblers (‘andalíbán), in longing for reunion with Him,

جمله مستند از نسيم فضل هو

are all intoxicated by the breeze of the bounty of Him (Hú).

68.

نغمه اين بلبل ار ظاهر شود

Should the melody of this Nightingale become manifest,

جان خلقان از حسد طاهر شود

the souls of creatures would become purified from envy.

69.

بحر‌معنى زين‌بيان موّاج شد

The ocean of inner meaning became billowing through this utterance;

فلک هستى زين کرم لجلاج شد

the ark of existence became tossed amidst the deep through this generosity.

70.

هر‌شقائق که بر‌آيد ‌زين بهار

Every anemone that springeth forth from this springtime

صد حقايق بر دمد از سرّ يار

breatheth forth a hundred realities from the mystery of the Beloved.

71.

بوى‌مشک‌آيد‌همى از‌جعد‌يار

The fragrance of musk cometh continually from the curl of the Beloved;

دست فضلش ميکند بر تو نثار

the hand of His bounty scattereth [it] upon thee.

72.

زلف‌او‌همچون سمندر‌بين بنار

Behold His tress like a salamander in the fire,

کو همى گردد بنار ‌روى يار

as it circleth in the fire of the face of the Beloved.

73.

عندليب قدسى از هجران دوست

The holy Warbler (‘andalíb-i-qudsí), from separation from the Friend,

ناله ها دارد‌که سوزد مغز و پوست

uttereth lamentations that burn marrow and skin.

74.

گر زدرد هجر خود آهى کشد

Should He draw a sigh from the pain of His separation,

شعله اندر جان خاصان افکند

He would cast a flame into the souls of the chosen ones.

75.

غير خاصانرا نباشد زين نصيب

Save for the chosen ones, none hath a portion of this;

وا‌مگير از لطف اين‌فضل اى‌حبيب

withhold not out of grace this bounty, O Best-Beloved (Ḥabíb)!

76.

بر وزان مشک الهى را زجان

Cause the divine musk to waft from the soul,

تا ز عطرت بو برند اين‌ناکسان

that these base ones may catch a scent from Thy perfume.

77.

اين بهار روح باشد جاودان

This springtime of the spirit is everlasting,

نى بهارى کز پيش آيد خزان

not a springtime after which autumn cometh.

78.

زين بهار‌قدس روح آيد برون

From this springtime of holiness the Spirit cometh forth,

و ز هوايش نور نوح آيد برون

and from its air the light of Noah cometh forth.

79.

بر نشاند اهل کشتى را‌بفلک

He seateth the people of the Ark in the Ship [or: raiseth them to the firmament],

پس ببخشد هرکه را صد‌گونه ملک

then bestoweth upon every one a hundred kinds of dominion.

80.

اى جمال اللّه برون‌آ‌از نقاب

O Beauty of God (Jamálu’lláh), come forth from behind the veil,

تا برون آيد ز مغرب آفتاب

that the sun may rise forth from the West!

81.

نافهء علم لدنّى بر گشا

Unbind the musk-sac of innate knowledge (‘ilm-i-ladunní);

مخزن‌ اسرار غيبى بر گشا

unlock the treasury of unseen mysteries,

82.

تا‌ز‌مشکت بو‌برند‌اين مردگان

that these dead ones may catch a fragrance from Thy musk,

تا ز‌خمرت‌خوش شوند‌‌اين بيهشان

that these senseless ones may be gladdened by Thy wine.

83.

اين‌ذلی‌لارض وحدت را ز‌جود

Clothe this lowly one (dhalíl) of the earth of unity, out of bounty,

خلعت عزّت ‌بپوشان اى ودود

in the robe of honour, O All-Loving One (Vadúd)!

84.

فانيى را پوش از ثوب بقا

Clothe an evanescent one in the garment of immortality;

فقر بحتى ‌را چشان شهد غنا

cause sheer poverty to taste the honey of wealth,

85.

تا برون آيد بکلّى از حجاب

that he may come forth wholly from behind the veil,

بر ‌درد امکان و هستى را نقاب

and rend the veil of contingency and self-existence;

86.

بى‌خود‌و سرمست آيد او برون

selfless and intoxicated may he come forth,

شمع سان اندر زجاج راجعون

like a candle within the glass of ”unto Him do we return“ (ráji‘ún).

87.

چونکه‌اين‌خار‌از‌گلستانت‌دميد

Since this thorn hath sprouted from Thy rose-garden,

صد گلستان ‌آر از وى تو پديد

bring Thou forth a hundred rose-gardens from it.

88.

هر گلستان را باسمى زن رقم

Inscribe every rose-garden with a Name;

پس بهر برگى نما سر‌ّ قدم

then upon every petal manifest the mystery of pre-existence (qidam),

89.

تا که انوار رخت آيد عيان

so that the lights of Thy cheek may become manifest,

پر کند نورت زمين و آسمان

and Thy light may fill earth and heaven.

90.

بر‌وزان‌بادى‌ز‌رحمت ‌اى کريم

Cause a wind of mercy to blow, O Generous One;

بر دران احجاب‌ غفلت زين سقيم

rend asunder the veils of heedlessness from this sick one.

91.

در پناه سدرهء خود جاى ده

Grant an abode in the shelter of Thine own Lote-Tree

روحهاى پاک اى سلطان مه

unto pure spirits, O great Sultan!

92.

بابى از رضوان معنى بر گشا

Open a gate of the Riḍván of inner meaning;

سدّ مکن اين باب از بهر خدا

bar not this gate, for the sake of God,

93.

تا‌درآيم بى حجاب اندر‌جهان

that I may enter unveiled into the world,

تا کنم‌ رمزى ز احسانت بيان

that I may expound a mystery of Thy beneficence.

94.

گفت اللّه اللّه اى مرد نکو

He said: ”God, God [beware], O goodly man!

رمز حق در نزد نادانان مگو

Speak not the mystery of Truth (Ḥaqq) before the ignorant!

95.

اللّه اللّه ‌اى لسان اللّه راز

“God, God, O Tongue of God (Lisánu’lláh)! The secret

نرم‌نرمک‌گوى ‌و با مردم بساز

speak very gently, and bear with the people;

96.

هم‌مگر‌لطف توگيرد دستشان

”perchance Thy grace too may take their hand,

پس کند فارغ ز بيم اين و آن

and thus make them free from the fear of this one and that.“

97.

پر معنى بر گشا طيّار شو

Spread the wing of inner meaning, become soaring;

در هواى قرب او سيار شو

become a traveller in the air of nearness unto Him.

98.

قرب او با‌جان‌نه در‌طى قدم

Nearness to Him is through the soul, not in traversing with the foot (qadam);

چون بجان پوئى در آئى در قدم

when thou journeyest with the soul, thou enterest into pre-existence (qidam).

99.

پس به‌آنى ‌طى افلاک وجود

Then in a single instant the traversing of the spheres of existence

نيست‌مشکل‌چون‌شوى زاهل سجود

is not difficult, when thou becomest of the people of prostration.

100.

در بيان اين بگويم نکته‌اى

In explanation of this I will speak a subtle point,

تا برى از آب حيوان حصّه‌اى

that thou mayest carry away a portion of the Water of Life;

101.

تا شوى واقف ز رضوان بقاء

that thou mayest become aware of the Riḍván of immortality,

تا برى راهى باقليم لقا

that thou mayest find a way to the clime of reunion;

102.

تا بطىّ الارض معنى پى برى

that thou mayest discover the spiritual folding up of the earth (ṭayyu’l-arḍ-i-ma‘ní),

تا چه روح اندر هوايش بر پرى

that like the spirit thou mayest fly up into His air.

103.

چون‌تو‌هستى‌اين‌زمان‌در‌دام‌گل

Since thou art at this time in the snare of clay,

کى برى بوئى تو از رضوان دل

how shalt thou catch a fragrance from the Riḍván of the heart?

104.

پس برهنه شو تو از ثوب قيود

Strip thyself naked, then, of the garment of fetters;

پس مقدّس کن تو جانرا از حدود

then sanctify thy soul from limitations.

105.

ظلمت دل را ز نورش‌کن‌منير

Illumine the darkness of the heart with His light,

تا شوى در ملک جانها تو امير

that thou mayest become a prince in the kingdom of souls.

106.

چونکه‌ظلمت‌رفت‌نورش‌مشرق‌است

When darkness hath departed, His light is shining;

بر دلت انوار طورش بارق است

upon thy heart the lights of His Sinai are flashing.

107.

چونکه‌ليلت‌رفت‌صبح آمد‌‌پديد

When thy night hath departed, the morn hath appeared,

هم نسيم عزّ روحانى وزيد

and the breeze of spiritual glory hath blown.

108.

پس‌تو‌اين‌ظلمات‌و‌اين‌نفس‌تباه

Then thou [art] this darkness and this ruined self;

آب حيوانش تجلّى اله

its Water of Life is the effulgence of God (tajallíy-i-Iláh).

109.

گر‌تو‌زين‌ظلمات نفست‌بگذرى

If thou pass beyond this darkness of thy self,

بى تعب از خمر حيوان بر خورى

without toil thou shalt partake of the Wine of Life.

110.

پس تو اندر‌ظل‌خضر‌جان‌در آ

Enter thou, then, beneath the shadow of the Khiḍr of the soul,

تا شوى فارغ از اين ظلمت سرا

that thou mayest be freed from this abode of darkness.

111.

آن‌خضر‌نوشيد و‌برهيد از‌ممات

That Khiḍr drank and was delivered from death,

وين خضر‌بخشد دو صد‌عين حيات

whilst this Khiḍr bestoweth two hundred Fountains of Life (‘ayn-i-ḥayát).

112.

آب حيوان بر همه انفاق‌ کرد

He hath expended the Water of Life upon all,

خود نموده جان نثار شاه فرد

whilst Himself offering His soul unto the Peerless King.

113.

آن‌خضر‌جهدى نمود آنگه رسيد

That Khiḍr strove a striving and then attained;

زين خضر‌صد چشمه‌آنى شد پديد

from this Khiḍr a hundred fountains appeared in an instant.

114.

آن‌خضر‌‌شد از پى چشمه دوان

That Khiḍr went running after the fountain,

وين‌خضر را چشمه ها از پى روان

whilst after this Khiḍr the fountains flow behind.

115.

اى‌بهاى‌جان تو‌باز‌آ زين شکار

O Glory of the soul (Baháy-i-Ján), return Thou from this hunt,

تاکنى صيد معانى صد هزار

that Thou mayest hunt a hundred thousand spiritual meanings.

116.

صيد گورانرا بهل از بهر ‌گور

Leave the hunting of wild asses (gúrán) for the grave (gúr);

صيد معنى آر از صحراى طور

bring the quarry of inner meaning from the plain of Sinai.

117.

صيد‌کردى جان‌عشاقان‌بدشت

Thou hast hunted the souls of lovers in the plain,

تا‌که جانها‌جمله از هستى گذشت

until all souls passed beyond self-existence.

118.

نيست فرصت تا‌تو از‌اسرار گل

There is no opportunity for Thee to speak of the mysteries of the Rose

پيش بلبل گوئى اى سلطان گل

before the nightingale, O Sultan of the Rose!

119.

بر‌پران بازى ز ساعد اى نگار

Let fly a falcon (bází) from Thy forearm, O Beauteous One (Nigár),

تا که باز آرد معانى زان ديار

that it may bring back (báz árad) spiritual meanings from yonder realm.

120.

اين‌زمان‌سيمرغ‌معنى‌صيد‌‌کن

At this time hunt the Símurgh of inner meaning;

بر گشا گنجى تو از مفتاح کن

unlock a treasure with the key of ”Be!“ (Kun).

121.

آنچه‌کردى‌وعده‌اکنون‌کن‌وفا

Fulfil now that which Thou didst promise,

اى ز نورت روشن اين‌‌ارض و سما

O Thou through Whose light this earth and heaven are radiant!

122.

از بهار خود بکن خرّم جهان

Make the world joyous through Thine own springtime,

تا که رضوانت شود رشک جنان

so that Thy Riḍván may become the envy of the gardens of Paradise.

123.

از حقائق بس شقائق بر‌دمان

Cause many anemones of realities to bloom forth

در فضاى اين بهارستان جان

in the expanse of this spring-garden of the soul.

124.

پس ز‌هر‌گل رمز‌بلبل‌کن‌عيان

Then from every rose make manifest the mystery of the nightingale;

شرح مل در دل بگو با خسروان

speak the commentary of wine within the heart unto the monarchs.

125.

زانکه‌اينجا‌اين‌زمان‌نامحرم‌است

For here at this time there is the uninitiated stranger (ná-maḥram),

محرم‌و‌نامحرم‌‌‌اينجا‌چون‌هم است

and confidant and stranger are here together.

126.

اى صباى صبح ‌از زلفين يار

O morning zephyr (ṣabáy-i-ṣubḥ), from the two tresses of the Beloved

نافه هاى مشک روحانى بيار

bring sacs of spiritual musk.

127.

اى سحاب فضل روحانى ببار

O cloud of spiritual bounty, rain down,

تا صدف لوءلوء همى آرد ببار

that the mother-of-pearl may bring forth pearls into fruition.

128.

ذکر طىّ الارض معنى باز ماند

The mention of the spiritual folding up of the earth remained behind [unfinished]. [Partner hemistich omitted in source; see Uncertain readings.]

پس تو اى مخمور از‌جام غرور

Then, O thou who art drunken with the cup of pride,

129.

نار نفست را بدل ميکن بنور

change the fire of thy self into light,

تا‌کنى طى جهان در‌يک نفس

that thou mayest traverse the world in a single breath,

130.

تا رها گردى ز حبس اين قفس

that thou mayest be delivered from the prison of this cage.

پيش از آن‌که اندرائى‌ظل‌دوست

Before thou enterest beneath the shadow of the Friend,

131.

نى خبر از مغز دارى نى ز پوست

thou hast neither knowledge of the kernel nor of the husk;

پاى معنيّت بگل باشد فرو

the foot of thy spirit is sunk in clay,

132.

بى خبر ‌ز انوار آن روى نکو

unaware of the lights of that goodly face.

چون‌بظلّ‌شاه‌جان مسکن‌کنى

When thou makest thine abode beneath the shadow of the King of the soul,

133.

آن زمان دل از جهانى بر کنى

then shalt thou tear thy heart away from a world.

اول ساعت بدى اندر تراب

At the first of the hour thou wast in the dust;

134.

آخر ساعت گذشتى ز آفتاب

at the end of the hour thou hast passed beyond the sun.

پس بآنى طى عالم‌هاى جان

Thus in a single instant the traversing of the worlds of the soul

135.

بى قدم کردى تو‌اى سالک بدان

hast thou accomplished without foot, O wayfarer, know this!

اين‌زمان‌بوئى ز‌عطرستان‌جان

At this time a fragrance from the perfume-garden of the soul

136.

بر وزيد و شد معطر اين جهان

blew forth, and this world became perfumed.

باز‌مشک‌جان از‌آن‌رضوان‌جود

Again the musk of the soul from that Riḍván of bounty

137.

بر وزيد و برد جمله آنچه بود

blew forth and carried away all that was.

هوش‌و‌بى‌هوشى‌زدست‌اينجا‌برفت

Sense and senselessness here slipped from hand;

138.

مست و هشيارى همه يکجا برفت

drunkenness and sobriety all departed together.

صحو‌شد‌هم‌محو‌و‌محوى‌هم‌نماند

Sobriety (ṣaḥv) too was effaced (maḥv), and even effacement remained not;

139.

مست شد‌هشيار و‌صحوى هم‌نماند

the drunken became sober, and even sobriety remained not.

آنچه‌بود از‌اسم و‌رسم اين‌جهان

Whatsoever existed of name and custom of this world

140.

فانى آمد چونکه شد شاهم عيان

became evanescent when my King became manifest.

زانکه اسما‌گر‌دو‌صد‌قرن او پرد

For should [the realm of] names fly for two hundred ages,

141.

مى نيارد که ز قدرش بو برد

it could never catch a scent of His station.

آنچه‌چشمت ديد و‌هم‌گوشت شنيد

Whatsoever thine eye hath seen and thine ear hath heard,

142.

او ز جمله پاک آمد اى رشيد

He is sanctified above it all, O rightly guided one.

پس‌تو‌با‌اين‌گوش‌وچشم‌اى‌بى‌بصر

How then, with this ear and eye, O sightless one,

143.

کى شوى از ‌سرّ جانان با خبر

shalt thou become aware of the mystery of the Soul of souls (Jánán)?

چشم ديگر بر گشا از يار نو

Open another eye through the new Beloved,

144.

گوش ديگر باز کن آنگه شنو

open another ear, and then hear!

همچنين در‌کلّ اعضا‌اين‌بدان

Know this likewise in respect of all the limbs,

246.

تا رهى از قيد ‌اين ظلماتيان

that thou mayest be delivered from the bondage of these dwellers in darkness.

گوش‌تو‌چون‌نغمهء رازش‌شنيد

When thine ear heard the melody of His secret,

247.

رازهاى جانى از سازش شنيد

it heard spiritual secrets from His instrument.

چونکه صنع ايزدى گشته عيان

Since the divine handiwork hath become manifest,

248.

چشم بر او کن از اين‌خلق جهان

fix thine eye upon Him, turning away from this creation of the world.

گر تو‌با‌چشمش‌جهانرا بنگرى

If thou lookest upon the world with His eye,

249.

بر هزاران ملک معنى پى برى

thou wilt discover thousands of realms of inner meaning.

مى نبيند چشم او جز روى او

His eye seeth naught save His face;

250.

مى نپرد مرغ او جز‌کوى او

His bird flieth not save unto His quarter.

از‌وصالش‌جان‌عشاقان بسوخت

Through reunion with Him the souls of lovers were consumed,

251.

و ز‌ فراقش نار دلها بر فروخت

and through separation from Him the fire of hearts blazed up.

پس بسوزد عاشق بيجان و سر

Thus the lover burneth, soulless and headless,

252.

هم ز هجر و هم ز وصلش اى پسر

both from separation from Him and from reunion with Him, O son!

پس‌تو‌عشق‌حق‌رفيق‌خود بدان

Know thou, then, the love of Truth as thy companion,

253.

تا شوى پرّان ز قيد اين جهان

that thou mayest fly forth from the bondage of this world.

عشق‌آن‌باشدکه‌جان‌فانى‌کنى

Love is this: that thou make thy soul evanescent,

254.

جان و دل در ملک باقى افکنى

and cast soul and heart into the everlasting Kingdom.

سرّ اين‌معنى شنو‌گر پى برى

Hear the secret of this meaning, if thou canst fathom it,

255.

تا به معراج الهى بر پرى

that thou mayest soar unto the divine ascension;

تا‌که نخلت بار روحانى دهد

that thy palm-tree may yield spiritual fruit,

256.

ميوه هاى قدس نورانى دهد

and yield luminous fruits of holiness.

اى نسيم از‌زلف او عطرى بيار

O breeze, bring a perfume from His tress;

257.

اى غمام از فضل هو رشحى ببار

O clouds, rain down a sprinkling from the bounty of Him (Hú),

تا رياض جان عشاقان او

that the gardens of the souls of His lovers

258.

لاله هاى عشق آرد بس نکو

may bring forth tulips of love most fair.

اين دل عاشق بود عرش ‌اله

This heart of the lover is the Throne of God,

259.

چونکه پاک آمد ‌ز قيد ما سواه

when it hath become purified from the bondage of aught save Him (má siváh).

چون ز‌حبّش‌بيت او‌‌معمور شد

When through His love His House became flourishing,

260.

او به بيت و بيت او مستور شد

He was concealed within the House, and His House was concealed [in Him].

بيت‌ او جز ‌دل نباشد اى جوان

His House is naught save the heart, O youth! [Partner hemistich omitted in source; see Uncertain readings.]

261.

چونکه قلبت پاک‌شد از‌نور او

When thy heart hath become purified through His light,

شد مقامش چونکه آمد طور او

it hath become His station, since it hath become His Sinai (Ṭúr).

262.

چونکه‌بيت اللّه‌عاشق‌شد‌‌‌تمام

When the House of God (Baytu’lláh) of the lover became complete,

جلوه معشوق آمد‌ بر دوام

the effulgence of the Beloved came perpetually.

263.

باز‌عشق‌آمد‌حجاب‌عقل‌سوخت

Again love came and burned the veil of intellect;

خرمن‌عرفان و علم و‌فضل سوخت

it burned the harvest of gnosis and knowledge and learning.

264.

چونکه‌غيرش‌نيست‌دربيت‌اى‌پسر

Since there is none save Him in the House, O son,

جمله حکم او بدان تو سر بسر

know thou all to be His decree from end to end.

265.

پس‌تو‌‌چشم‌و‌گوش‌و‌دست‌از‌‌‌او‌بدان

Know thou, then, eye and ear and hand to be from Him;

او ببيند ‌او بگيرد آن زمان

it is He Who seeth, He Who graspeth at that time.

266.

جان‌عارف‌مسجد‌اقصاى اوست

The soul of the knower (‘árif) is His Farthest Mosque (Masjid-i-Aqṣá);

مخزن اسرار او ادناى اوست

it is the treasury of the mysteries of His “or nearer” (Aw Adná).

267.

چاره‌ئى اکنون ز‌نو بايد نمود

A remedy must now be devised anew;

اين نصيحت را بجان بايد شنود

this counsel must be heard with the soul:

268.

هم ز‌هجر‌و‌وصل هر‌دو در‌گذر

Pass beyond both separation and reunion,

تا رسى در رفرف اصل اى پسر

that thou mayest attain unto the celestial pavilion (rafraf) of the Source, O son!

269.

تا‌تو در‌هجرى يقين در آتشى

So long as thou art in separation, assuredly thou art in fire;

هم ز وصلش در تب و هم ناخوشى

and from reunion with Him thou art in fever and disquiet.

270.

پاى نه بر عرصه پاک بقا

Set thy foot upon the pure expanse of immortality,

که بود غيرش در آن ‌ميدان فنا

for in that arena aught save Him is evanescence.

271.

گر حديث کان لللّه خوانده‌اى

If thou hast read the tradition Kána’lláh (“God was [and naught else beside Him]”),

ور تو رمز ليس ‌غيره ديده‌اى

and if thou hast beheld the mystery of Laysa ghayruh (“There is none other than He”),

272.

پاى همت اندرين ره تو گذار

set the foot of high resolve upon this path,

تا شوى فارغ ز وصل و هجر يار

that thou mayest become independent of reunion with and separation from the Beloved.

273.

چونکه دانستى يقين ز اسرار

Since thou hast known with certainty from the mysteries [of]

جان که‌نباشد غير‌يزدان در ميان

the soul that there is none save God (Yazdán) between,

274.

پس ز‌آب‌جان‌بران‌خاشاک را

drive away, then, the drift-straw from the water of the soul,

تا ببينى جلوه‌ آن پاک را

that thou mayest behold the effulgence of that Pure One;

275.

تا ببينى تو وصال اندر وصال

that thou mayest behold reunion within reunion,

تا ببينى در دلت نور جمال

that thou mayest behold within thy heart the light of Beauty.

276.

اين بود وصلی‌که‌ضد‌‌نبود ورا

This is a reunion that hath no opposite unto it (va-rá),

بلکه هجرش مى نباشد از ورا

nay, no separation followeth behind it (az vará).

277.

وصل‌و‌هجر‌تو‌بود‌شرک‌اى پسر

Thy reunion and thy separation are polytheism (shirk), O son,

گر‌تو ‌دارى گوش بر‌پند پدر

if thou lendest an ear unto the counsel of the Father.

278.

زين دو عقبه چون‌هما‌برپر‌برو

Fly up and pass beyond these two steep passes like the Humá,

تا هواى ‌وحدت سلطان هو

unto the heaven of the unity of the Sultan of “He” (Hú).

279.

ليک ترسم ‌که بلغزد پاى تو

Yet I fear lest thy foot slip,

وهم بد پيدا شود ‌در راى تو

and an evil fancy appear in thy thought.

280.

واجب آمد شرح اين‌معنى‌کنم

It hath become incumbent that I expound this meaning,

بيخ وسواس دل از بُن بر کنم

and pluck out the root of whispering doubt of the heart from its foundation,

281.

تا نيفتى زين بيان اندر غرور

lest through this utterance thou fall into delusion,

وارهى از‌کبر و ناز و شرّ و شور

and that thou mayest be delivered from arrogance and self-conceit and evil and tumult.

282.

وصل او را تو تجلّيش بدان

Know thou reunion with Him to be His effulgence (tajallí),

که شده بيچند و‌چون در تو عيان

which hath become manifest within thee beyond quantity and quality.

283.

نور او در تو وديعه او بود

His light within thee is His trust;

جهد آن کن تا‌که او ظاهر شود

strive therefore that it may become manifest.

284.

پس‌تو‌وصل‌‌او ز‌خود‌‌جو‌‌اى‌‌نگار

Seek thou, then, reunion with Him within thyself, O fair one,

تا نه بينى بعد از اين هجران يار

that henceforth thou mayest never behold separation from the Beloved.

285.

مخزن کنز الهى هم توئى

Thou thyself art the treasury of the divine Treasure,

ليک از غفلت پى اينان دوى

yet out of heedlessness thou runnest after these [earthly] ones.

286.

تا نگردد در ‌تو اوصافش عيان

So long as His attributes become not manifest within thee,

خويش را در هجر و گمراهى بدان

know thyself to be in separation and error.

287.

او ز‌جود‌ خود‌ نکردت‌ بى‌‌نصيب

Out of His own bounty He hath not left thee portionless

از صفات و اسم و رسمش اى لبيب

of His attributes and name and mark, O man of understanding!

288.

او زلطفش بابها بر تو گشود

Out of His grace He hath opened gates unto thee;

تو مبند آن باب ها همچون ‌يهود

close thou not those gates like the Jews.

289.

چون ‌شنيدى‌‌‌ ناله نى را ‌‌ز‌عشق

Since thou hast heard the lament of the reed-flute (nay) out of love,

اين زمان بشناس او را هم ز عشق

recognize Him now likewise through love.

290.

چون‌ شنيدى‌صوت‌نى‌نائى نگر

When thou hast heard the voice of the reed, behold the Flute-player (Ná’í),

تا نباشى بى‌خبر از شه مگر

lest haply thou remain unaware of the King.

291.

چونکه ‌نائى‌در‌ جهان‌ ‌اغيار ديد

Because the Flute-player beheld strangers in the world,

زان سبب نى را حجاب خود گزيد

therefore He chose the reed as His veil.

292.

پس‌تو ‌بر ‌در ‌‌‌اين‌حجابت ‌يکزمان

Rend thou, then, this veil of thine for a moment,

تا که جز نائى نه بينى در جهان

that thou mayest behold naught in the world save the Flute-player.

293.

همچو صفدر بردران احجاب را

Like the valiant warrior (Ṣafdar), rend the veils asunder,

تا ببينى جلوه وهّاب را

that thou mayest behold the effulgence of the All-Bountiful (Vahháb).

294.

همچو نى بخروش تو اندر‌ فراق

Like the reed, cry out in separation,

تا که آيد نائيت اندر وثاق

that thy Flute-player may enter into thy chamber.

295.

چون‌در‌آيد‌ نائى ‌دل در‌‌ خروش

When the Flute-player of the heart breaketh into cry,

سينه هاى عاشقان آيد به جوش

the breasts of lovers come into fervour.

296.

آتشى بفروز زين ‌نى تو همى

Kindle thou continually a fire from this reed,

تا بسوزى در جهان وصف منى

that thou mayest burn away in the world the attribute of I-ness (maní).

297.

از منى‌چون ‌ميم‌سوزد در ‌جهان

When from maní (`منی`, “I-ness”) the letter mím (`م`) is burned away in the world,

غير نى باقى نماند در ميان

naught save nay (`نی`, “the reed” / “nothing”) remaineth between!

298.

چونکه ‌گردد ‌چشمت از‌ نورش‌ بصير

When thine eye becometh seeing through His light,

غير نائى خود نبينى اى خبير

thou wilt see none at all save the Flute-player, O informed one.

299.

پس ز‌ نائى بشنو اين اسرارها

Hear, then, these mysteries from the Flute-player,

تا برى بوئى ‌از اين گلزارها

that thou mayest catch a fragrance from these rose-gardens.

300.

يک شرر از نار ‌عشقش‌‌ بر‌فروخت

A single spark of the fire of His love blazed forth;

خرمن هستى سلطانى بسوخت

it burned the harvest of existence of a sultan.

301.

چون‌ جمالش پرده از‌ رخ برکشيد

When His beauty drew back the veil from His cheek,

پرده اجلال سلطانان دريد

He rent the veil of the majesty of sultans.

302.

خورد ‌چون ‌تيرى ز‌مژگان نگار

When he was struck by an arrow from the eyelashes of the Beauteous One,

بر دريد او صدر جان شهريار

it pierced the breast of the soul of the monarch.

303.

تاج شاهى را ز‌سر‌ آندم فکند

That very moment he cast the royal crown from his head;

بنده گشت و آنگه ‌افتاد او به بند

he became a servant and then fell into bonds.

304.

همچو‌ ‌صيدى دست ‌‌صيادى‌ ‌فتاد

Like a quarry he fell into the hand of a hunter,

يا چه کاهى‌ در دم بادى فتاد

or like a straw he fell before the blast of a wind.

305.

گر بود پيکى رود سوى عراق

Were there a courier to go toward ‘Iráq,

شرح گويد درد هجران و فراق

he would recount the tale of the pain of separation and parting:

306.

کز‌ فراقت‌جان‌ مشتاقان ‌بسوخت

“Through separation from Thee, the souls of the yearning ones have been consumed;

تير هجرت سينهء شاهان بدوخت

the arrow of Thy separation hath pierced the breasts of kings!”

307.

در ميان ما و تو اى شهر ‌جان

Between us and thee, O City of the soul,

صد هزاران قاف باشد در ميان

a hundred thousand Mount Qáfs lie between.

308.

نيست پيکى جز‌که آه پر شرر

There is no courier save a spark-filled sigh,

يا رود باد صبا گويد خبر

or should the morning zephyr go and tell the tidings.

309.

دست از‌ نخلش‌ بسى‌کوتاه‌ ماند

Our hand hath remained far short of its palm-tree;

جان ز هجرش بحرها ‌از چشم راند

from separation from it, the soul hath caused oceans to flow from the eye.

310.

اى صبا‌ از‌ پيش جانان يکزمان

O morning zephyr (ṣabá), from the presence of the Soul of souls for a moment

خوش بران تا کوى آن زورائيان

speed gladly unto the quarter of those people of Zawrá’ [Baghdad],

311.

پس بگويش‌کى‌مدينه کردگار

then say unto it: “O City of the Creator,

چون‌بماندى‌چونکه‌رفت از‌برت‌يار

how hast thou remained since the Beloved departed from thy bosom?

312.

يار تو در حبس و زندان مبتلا

”Thy Beloved is afflicted in captivity and prison,

چون حسين اندر زمين کربلا

like unto Ḥusayn in the land of Karbilá:

313.

يک حسين‌ و ‌صد ‌هزارانش يزيد

“a single Ḥusayn, and a hundred thousand Yazíds against Him;

يک حبيب و اين همه ديو عنيد

a single Best-Beloved (Ḥabíb), and all these stubborn demons;

314.

چون کليم اندر ميان قبطيان

”like Kalím [Moses] amidst the Copts,

يا چه روح اللّه ميان سبطيان

or like the Spirit of God [Jesus] amidst the tribes [of Israel];

315.

همچو يوسف اندر افتاده بچاه

“like Joseph fallen into the well,

آن چهى که نبودش پايان و راه

that well which hath neither bottom nor way out;

316.

بلبلت شد مبتلی اندر قفس

”thy Nightingale hath become afflicted within a cage,

بسته شد هم زين قفس راه نفس

and through this cage the very passage of breath hath been barred.“

Readable translation

1. O Life of the Throne, O Sun of loving-kindness, the world of being hath never borne a light like unto Thee!

2. Were mankind not veiled from Thy presence, I would have uttered a word or two of the mystery of immortality,

3. That every soul might become pledged unto Thee, and every heart enraptured by Thy love;

4. That Thou mightest behold a world enraptured and intoxicated, bearing its spirits in both hands to lay them down as an offering;

5. That when Thy summons arriveth, O Pride of the age, they might freely scatter their lives before Thy feet.

6. Rise Thou, sun-like, above the mountain of the soul, that all may behold Thee manifestly from every side.

7. Reveal in splendour that moon-like Countenance, and through Thy grace make this realm verdant and joyous.

8. The drop seeketh from Thine ocean a fount of Kawthar; vouchsafe a Kawthar unto it, for Thou art the supreme King.

9. The mote imploreth Thy light; bestow it upon it out of Thine unconditional grace.

10. The seed hath opened its mouth toward heaven, that from Thy bounty glory (bahá) may descend upon it.

11. Rain down upon it the drops of Thy mercy, O Sovereign of the Throne and Prince of the realm!

12. Rend asunder this hundred-fold veil, and graciously grant us now the vision of that Face.

13. For in Thy bounty there can be no doubt; bind Thou up for us, out of Thy grace, a provision for the way.

14. Make this West now the Dayspring of all; impart unto this draught the gladness of mystic wine.

15. Illumine the light of the heart with the splendours of Thy Light, that men may behold from Thy cheek the radiance of Sinai.

16. Lo, draw forth Thy divine sword; behold, slay these enemies of Thy Faith!

17. Kindle Thy Lordly fire, and consume utterly the warring infidel.

18. All are bats, O Sun of day; lift up Thy head and consume all the dwellers in darkness.

19. Clarify these sorrow-tainted dregs, and shed light upon this night-quenched candle.

20. A whole world subsisteth through Thee, even as Thou abidest in the soul, that through Thy command ”Be, and it is!“ may become manifest.

21. O Glory of the soul (Baháy-i-Ján), in remembrance of Thy Countenance I continually utter mysteries concerning Thy nature,

22. That I may bear souls away beyond the bounds of reason, and see who will purchase the pearl of Thy love.

23. I will kindle a fire within the world, that I may burn away the veils of the inmates of holiness.

24. I will bring forth the Maid of the spirit from behind the veil, and lift the veil from the unseen Light.

25. A mystery from the mysteries of everlasting love will I unfold, since Thou hast returned unto the soul.

26. Come forth gladly into utterance, O Bird of Fire, that no trace of self-existence may remain between!

27. Purify these envy-laden hearts, and transmute into pure gold these counterfeit, unguarded hearts,

28. That they who lie senseless in the Day of Thy Covenant, O Generous One, may come to themselves through the Ancient Cup.

29. Nay rather, through the melodies of holiness, O our Beloved, banish from us both sense and senselessness alike!

30. O Seraphiel of Glory (Saráfíl-i-Bahá), O King of the soul, breathe forth new life upon the dead!

31. The Divine Lote-Tree is the primal branch of the heart; deliver it from the elements of air and water and clay,

32. That it may be freed from substance and accident, and that from its taper suns may dawn forth.

33. Plant Thou this sapling of Thine in the soil of the heart, and keep it sanctified above sunrise and shadow.

34. Guard it Thyself from contrary winds, and set it free from the idle fancies of them that join partners with God.

35. Make firm its root in the earth of the soul, and cause its branch to soar beyond the heavens.

36. Bring forth manifestly anew a fresh springtime, that through Thy resurrection these dead may spring to life.

37. The surging of the oceans of love is from Thy surging; the consciousness of the birds of immortality is from Thy consciousness.

38. Waft the fragrance of Joseph’s Robe from the Egypt of the soul, and make manifest here the Burning Bush of Moses!

39. O Beauteous One, from Thy Face the springtime hath come, and from this springtime realities beyond number have appeared.

40. Every rose thereof is a scroll of the beauty of the Friend; every heart thereby is a Kawthar of the bounty of Him Who is the All-Sufficing.

41. No autumn followeth after this springtime, and all the roses circle in adoration around it.

42. This is not a springtime which the soul can compass; this is a springtime that ravisheth the spirits away.

43. That earthly springtime awakeneth yearning for fair ones, whilst this springtime bringeth the love of God.

44. That springtime hath evanescence following in its wake, whilst this springtime hath immortality for its name.

45. That springtime ariseth from the turning of the season in the world, whilst this springtime dawneth from the light of the Face of the Ravisher of hearts.

46. That springtime bringeth forth tulips from the earth, whilst this springtime raiseth up the lamentations of lovers now.

47. Through the light of the King, this everlasting springtime hath pitched its royal pavilion even unto the Throne of God.

48. All have entered beneath His pavilion; if thou hast eyes to see, behold, O discerning one!

49. When our King casteth the veil from His cheek, this springtime pitcheth its tent upon the firmament.

50. When our Beloved flingeth the veil from His Face, this springtime shineth forth unveiled.

51. Through His Face we abide amidst springtime; because of His Face we turn not our gaze toward the rose-garden.

52. Through His remembrance we have no need to mention the mines of earth; through His sun we dawn forth across the world.

53. Should a breeze waft from this goodly springtime, thou wilt behold Josephs coming forth into view.

54. Should a breeze blow from this orchard, thou wilt behold Josephs of the spirit throughout the world.

55. Thou wilt see bodies becoming even as spirit, whilst unto the spirit there cometh every moment a hundredfold victory.

56. This holy springtime of the Soul of souls hath at every moment a hundred utterances — yet where is a confidant to hear?

57. This utterance is sanctified above the tongue; how can the base-born attain unto its meaning?

58. This utterance is not of speech, or word, or sound; this utterance is spirit itself, and knoweth no death.

59. In this springtime thou beholdest lovers offering up their souls every moment by the hundred thousand.

60. This is the springtime of spiritual glory; this is the vernal season of Lordly holiness.

61. Should a breeze waft upon thee from this Sheba, thy mortal soul will quaff the cup of immortality.

62. Should a breeze come unto thee from the quarter of the Friend, lay down thy soul for its sake, for this soul too is from Him.

63. Behold the tulip of divine unity in this springtime; behold the hyacinth of detachment waving from the tress of the Beloved.

64. Along the margin of this stream, the buds of divine knowledge are all in quest out of yearning for Him.

65. Its cypresses tell of the stature of the Beauteous One; its greenswards are a scroll of the cheek of the Beloved.

66. Its nightingales are intoxicated with the cup of Alast; its ringdoves are drunken with the beauty of the Friend.

67. The warblers, in their longing for reunion with Him, are all intoxicated by the breeze of His bounty.

68. Should the melody of this Nightingale be revealed, the souls of all creatures would be cleansed of envy.

69. Through this utterance the ocean of inner meaning hath billowed high; through this generosity the ark of existence hath been tossed upon the deep.

70. Every anemone that springeth forth from this springtime breatheth a hundred realities from the mystery of the Beloved.

71. The fragrance of musk wafteth continually from the curl of the Beloved, and the hand of His bounty scattereth it upon thee.

72. Behold His tress like a salamander in the flame, circling ever within the fire of the Face of the Beloved.

42b`اين بهارى که روانها را کند` (ín bahárí kih ravánhá rá kanad / kunad)Unvowelled `کند` can be read kanad (from kandan, ”uproots/tears away/ravishes [spirits from self]“) or kunad (from kardan, ”makes/fashions [spirits]“). Since 42a ends in `درکش کند` (dark-ash kunad), reading kanad in 42b avoids an identical radíf-less rhyme repetition and fits the contrast (”not a spring that the soul grasps, but a spring that tears away/ravishes spirits“).(1) kanad (”uprooteth/ravisheth spirits“); (2) kunad (”fashioneth/maketh spirits“). Noted both in literal translation.
52a`ما بذکرش فارغيم از ذکر کان` (má bi-dhikrash fárighím az dhikr-i-kán)`کان` (kán) literally means ”mine/quarry“ (source of gems/gold), or can be a poetic contraction/variant for `اکوان` / `کون` (contingent existence).(1) ”mine [of contingent being / earthly treasure]“; (2) colloquial/poetic spelling for kawn (”existence“).
64a`غنچه‌هاى معرفت زين طرف جو` (ghunchih-háy-i-ma‘rifat zín ṭarf-i-jú / ṭaraf jú)`زين طرف جو` can be read as an iḍáfih phrase z-ín ṭarf-i-jú (”along this bank of the stream“, matching the garden imagery of tulip, hyacinth, cypress, greensward in 63–65) or as imperative z-ín ṭaraf jú (”seek from this side“).(1) z-ín ṭarf-i-jú (”along this stream-bank“); (2) z-ín ṭaraf jú (”seek on this side“). Because 64b (`جملگى از شوق او در جستجو`, ”all are in search out of yearning for Him“) has ”buds“ as the subject, reading 64a as a modifier of ”buds“ (ṭarf-i-jú, ”by the margin of this stream“) makes the syntax of the couplet much smoother.
69b (and 79a, 163b, 189b)`فلک` (fulk vs. falak)Unvowelled `فلک` can be read fulk (”ark/ship“) or falak (”celestial sphere/firmament“). In 69b (`لجلاج شد`, tossed in the deep sea), 163b (`فلک لفظى بشکنى` paired with `موجى از درياى ژرف`), and 189b (`اى نوح فلک تن شکن`), marine imagery strongly favours fulk (”ark/ship“). In 79a (`بر نشاند اهل کشتى را بفلک`), either ”embarketh the people of the Ark upon the celestial Ship (fulk)“ or ”raiseth the people of the Ark unto the firmament (falak)“ is possible.Both readings noted in 79a; fulk (”ark/ship“) adopted in 69b, 163b, 189b, and falak (”firmament“) in 186b (`از فلک بگذر هم از معراج جسم`).
77b`نى بهارى کز پيش آيد خزان` (nay bahárí k-az pay-ash áyad khazán)`پيش` here must be read pay-ash (”after it / in its wake“, echoing 41a `اين بهاران را خزان نايد ز پى`), not písh (”before/in front“).Read as k-az pay-ash (”after which autumn cometh“).
83a`اين ذلی‌لارض وحدت را ز جود`Typographical/OCR segmentation error in the OceanOfLights source text: `ذلی‌لارض` is a mis-segmented printing of `ذليل ارض` (dhalíl-i-arḍ-i-vaḥdat, ”this lowly one of the earth of unity“).Read as `ذليل ارض وحدت` (dhalíl-i-arḍ-i-vaḥdat).

148b`کو بدى بود و نباشد اين شگفت` (k-ú bi-dí búd va nabáshad ín shigift)`بدى` without vowel marks could look like badí (”evil“) or budí (”were“), but in the classical Sufi anecdote of the Day of Alast (compare ‘Aṭṭár and Rúmí) the first gnostic answers that he remembers the call of Alast ”as though it were yesterday“ (`به دى`, bi-dí).Read `بدى` as `به دى` (bi-dí, ”yesterday“).
171b`اى تو ماه امر و شاه انّما` (ay tú Máh-i-Amr u Sháh-i-Innamá)Allusion in `شاه انّما` (Sháh-i-Innamá, ”King of Innamá“).Refers to Qur’ánic verses beginning with Innamá revealed in praise of ‘Alí and the Family of the Prophet — specifically Qur’án 76:9 (Innamá nuṭ‘imukum li-wajhi’lláh, ”We feed you only for the sake of God“) and Qur’án 5:55 (Innamá walíyyukumu’lláh, ”Your Walí is only God and His Messenger and those who believe...“).

196a`هم تبارش گردن دوران خمد` (ham tabár-ash gardan-i-dawrán khamad)`هم تبارش` (”also its lineage/family“ or a scribal/typographical variant for `هم ز بارش`, ham zi bár-ash, ”and under its weight/burden“).(1) Read `ز بارش` / `تبارش` as ”before its might / under its weight the neck of the age boweth down“; (2) read `تبارش` literally as ”its kindred/lineage“.
