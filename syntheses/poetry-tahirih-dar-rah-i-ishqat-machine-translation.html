The model was asked to translate from the original only, without consulting other English translations; to check its rendering of key terms against Shoghi Effendi’s translations first (the table of terms); to give a literal version aligned with the original verse by verse; then a readable version; and to list the readings it is unsure of. About the text

  • Source title: در ره عشقت ای صنم (Dar rah-i-‘ishqat ay ṣanam) (Ṭáhirih, attributed) — original Persian
  • Author field in source header: Ṭáhirih (Qurrat al-‘Ayn), attributed
  • Edition in source header: Persian Wikipedia article ‘اشعار طاهره قرةالعین’ (oldid 43809681), Persian text as shown (retrieved 2026-10-17)
  • Source notes (copied unchanged from header): Persian Wikipedia, ”اشعار طاهره قرةالعین”, quoted there by Shahab Firdausi, who ”confirms” that many poems are attributed to her and gives these as ones that are surely hers. The article does not corroborate that; other poems listed on the same page are disputed.
  • What the poem itself says: A Persian ghazal-i-musammaṭ (internally rhymed ghazal) of 8 couplets (16 hemistichs/lines in the source file), with the refrain (radíf) `منم` (manam, ”am I“) at the end of every couplet (and both hemistichs of the opening couplet). Each couplet is divided into four rhythmic quarters (mufta‘ilun mufá‘ilun), the first three rhyming internally and the fourth ending in `-á manam`. The poem contrasts the exalted station and attributes of the Beloved (`تویی`, tú’í, ”art Thou“—milk and sugar, branch and fruit, sun and moon, date-palm and fresh date, Ka‘bih, idol, monastery, and sanctuary) with the utter lowliness and nothingness of the lover (`منم`, manam, ”am I“—enamoured of tribulation, atom and floating dust-mote, destitute lover, less than a drop from the Beloved’s sea), culminating in union with the Beloved (`بی حد انتها منم`, ”boundless and infinite am I“), the Beloved’s call (`به نزد من بیا`, ”Come nigh unto Me“), and the closing takhalluṣ `طاهره` (Ṭáhirih) as the dust beneath the Beloved’s feet, intoxicated with the wine of presence (liqá), awaiting bounty (‘aṭá) and confessing transgression (khaṭá). Any further historical details of composition are not established.

Literal translation

(8 couplets / 16 hemistichs, numbered line by line to match the 16 poem lines of the source file.)

1 (Couplet 1a).

در ره عشقت ای صنم، شیفته بلا منم

In the path of Thy love, O Idol, the one enamoured of tribulation am I;

2 (Couplet 1b).

چند مغایرت کنی با غمت آشنا منم

how long wilt Thou show estrangement? The one intimate with Thy sorrow am I.

3 (Couplet 2a).

پرده به روی بسته‌ای، زلف به هم شکسته‌ای

Thou hast bound a veil upon Thy countenance; Thou hast curled Thy locks together;

4 (Couplet 2b).

از همه خلق رسته‌ای، از همگان جدامنم

Thou art freed from all creation; severed from all others am I.

5 (Couplet 3a).

شیر تویی، شکر تویی، شاخه تویی، ثمر تویی

The milk art Thou, the sugar art Thou; the branch art Thou, the fruit art Thou;

6 (Couplet 3b).

شمس تویی، قمر تویی، ذره منم، هبا منم

the sun art Thou, the moon art Thou; the atom am I, the floating dust-mote am I.

7 (Couplet 4a).

نخل تویی، رطب تویی، لعبت نوش لب تویی

The date-palm art Thou, the fresh date art Thou; the sweet-lipped beauty art Thou;

8 (Couplet 4b).

خواجهٔ با ادب تویی، عاشق بی نوا منم

the gracious Master art Thou; the destitute lover am I.

9 (Couplet 5a).

کعبه تویی، صنم تویی، دیر تویی، حرم تویی

The Ka‘bih art Thou, the Idol art Thou; the monastery art Thou, the sanctuary art Thou;

10 (Couplet 5b).

دلبر محترم تویی، عاشق بی نوا منم

the revered Ravisher of hearts art Thou; the destitute lover am I.

11 (Couplet 6a).

من ز یم تو نیم نم، نی ز کم و ز بیش هم

From Thy sea I am but half a dewdrop [or: I am not even a dewdrop], nor of the little or the much either;

12 (Couplet 6b).

چون به تو متصل شدم، بی حد انتها منم

yet when I became united with Thee, boundless and infinite am I.

13 (Couplet 7a).

شاهد شوخ دلربا، گفت به نزد من بیا

The winsome, heart-ravishing Beloved said, “Come nigh unto Me—

14 (Couplet 7b).

رسته ز کبر از ریا، مظهر کبریا منم

freed from pride, from hypocrisy, the Manifestation of Grandeur am I.”

15 (Couplet 8a).

طاهره، خاک پای تو، مست می لقای تو

Ṭáhirih, the dust beneath Thy feet, intoxicated with the wine of Thy presence—

16 (Couplet 8b).

منتظر عطای تو، معترف خطا منم

awaiting Thy bounty, confessing my transgression am I.

Readable translation

1 (Couplet 1a). In the pathway of Thy love, O Beloved, enamoured of tribulation am I;

2 (Couplet 1b). How long wilt Thou treat me as a stranger, when the intimate of Thy sorrow am I?

3 (Couplet 2a). Thou hast drawn a veil across Thy face and tossed Thy curling locks together;

4 (Couplet 2b). Thou art detached from all creation, and severed from all else am I.

5 (Couplet 3a). Thou art the milk, Thou art the sugar; Thou art the branch, Thou art the fruit;

6 (Couplet 3b). Thou art the sun, Thou art the moon; the atom am I, the floating mote of dust am I.

7 (Couplet 4a). Thou art the palm, Thou art the ripened date; Thou art the sweet-lipped Beauty;

8 (Couplet 4b). Thou art the gracious Lord and Master; the destitute and helpless lover am I.

9 (Couplet 5a). Thou art the Ka‘bih, Thou art the Idol; Thou art the cloister, Thou art the sanctuary;

10 (Couplet 5b). Thou art the revered Ravisher of hearts; the destitute and helpless lover am I.

11 (Couplet 6a). Out of Thine ocean I am scarce half a dewdrop—nay, counted neither among the little nor the much;

12 (Couplet 6b). Yet since I have attained union with Thee, boundless and infinite am I!

13 (Couplet 7a). That winsome, heart-entrancing Beloved cried out: “Come nigh unto Me!

14 (Couplet 7b). Sanctified above pride and hypocrisy, the Manifestation of Grandeur am I!”

15 (Couplet 8a). Ṭáhirih is the dust beneath Thy feet, inebriated with the wine of reunion with Thee;

16 (Couplet 8b). Expectant of Thy bounty and confessing my transgression am I.

Uncertain readings

VerseWord or phraseProblemAlternatives considered
2 (1b)چند مغایرت کنی (chand mugháyarat kuní)Mugháyarat literally means ”otherness / contrariety / mutual difference“; with kardan and contrasted with `آشنا` (áshná, ”familiar / intimate“), it means ”to act like a stranger / to show estrangement or aloofness“.(1) ”How long wilt Thou show estrangement / treat me as a stranger?“ (chosen); (2) ”How long wilt Thou contend / oppose?“.
5 (3a)شیر تویی، شکر تویی (shír tú’í, shikar tú’í)`شیر` (shír) is homographic in Persian between ”milk“ and ”lion“. Paired with `شکر` (shikar, ”sugar“), shír u shikar (”milk and sugar“) is a standard classical Persian idiom for sweetness and union.(1) ”The milk art Thou, the sugar art Thou“ (chosen); (2) ”The lion art Thou“ (discordant with shikar).
7 (4a)نخل تویی، رطب تویی (nakhl tú’í, ruṭab tú’í)Allusion to Qur’án 19:25 (wa-huzzí ilayki bi-jidh‘i’n-nakhlati tusáqiṭ ‘alayki ruṭaban janiyyan, ”And shake towards thyself the trunk of the palm-tree: it will drop upon thee fresh-ripened dates“), a frequent image in Bábí and Persian mystical poetry for divine grace and fruitful revelation.Rendered literally as ”date-palm / palm“ and ”fresh date / ripened date“ (chosen).
8 & 10 (4b & 5b)خواجهٔ با ادب تویی، عاشق بی نوا منمIn the source text, the fourth quarter-line `عاشق بی نوا منم` (‘áshiq-i-bí-navá manam, ”the destitute lover am I“) is repeated identically at the end of both couplet 4 (line 8) and couplet 5 (line 10). Also `با ادب` (bá-adab) applied to `خواجه` (khájih, Master/Lord) means ”gracious / courteous / noble in bearing“.Preserved the exact repetition in both literal and readable translations, rendering khájiy-i-bá-adab as ”the gracious Master“ (chosen).
11 (6a)من ز یم تو نیم نم (man zi yamm-i-tú ním nam)Crucial unvocalized homograph: `نیم` can be read either as (1) ním (”half“: ním(-i) nam, ”half a drop of moisture / half a dewdrop“ from Thy sea), or (2) níyam / nayam (1st person singular negative copula, ”I am not“: ”From Thy sea I am not [even] a dewdrop, nor of the little or the much either“). Both ním-i-nam and níyam nam scan identically (`- u -`) in the meter (mufta‘ilun mufá‘ilun, `- u u -u - u -`).(1) Give both readings in the literal translation and combine their force in the readable version (”scarce half a dewdrop—nay, counted neither among the little nor the much“, chosen); (2) Render solely as ”half a drop“ or solely as ”not a drop“.
12 (6b)بی حد انتها منم (bí-ḥadd-i-intihá manam)Printed without the conjunction `و` (u, ”and“) between `حد` (ḥadd, limit) and `انتها` (intihá, end). In meter (`- u u -u - u -`), it scans either with an iḍáfih (*bí-ḥad-d-i-in-ti-há ma-nam, ”without limit of end“) or with an unwritten/elided u (bí-ḥad-d u in-ti-há ma-nam*, ”without limit or end“).Rendered as ”boundless and infinite“ (chosen).
13–14 (7a–7b)شاهد شوخ دلربا، گفت به نزد من بیا / رسته ز کبر از ریا، مظهر کبریا منمAmbiguity of quotation boundary between couplet 7a and 7b: in 7a, the winsome Beloved (sháhid-i-shúkh-i-dil-rubá) says, ”Come nigh unto Me!“ In 7b (`رسته ز کبر از ریا، مظهر کبریا منم`), the speaker of `منم` (”am I“) could be: (a) the Beloved continuing to speak (”Come nigh unto Me: sanctified above pride and hypocrisy, the Manifestation of Grandeur am I!“), or (b) the lover/poet speaking in the state of union described in line 12 (”Having escaped from pride and hypocrisy, the manifestation of grandeur am I“), or (c) `رسته ز کبر از ریا` modifying the lover who comes nigh (”Come nigh unto Me, freed from pride and hypocrisy, [for] the Manifestation of Grandeur am I“). Note also the asyndeton `ز کبر از ریا` (without `و`), scanning *ras-tih zi kib-r(i) az ri-yá*.(1) Construe line 14 as the continuation of the Beloved’s speech introduced by `گفت` in line 13 (chosen, as Maẓhar-i-Kibriyá ”Manifestation of Grandeur“ properly designates the Divine Beloved, while in the very next line Ṭáhirih calls herself ”the dust beneath Thy feet ... confessing my transgression“); (2) Construe line 14 as spoken by the poet in ecstatic union (shaṭḥ).
