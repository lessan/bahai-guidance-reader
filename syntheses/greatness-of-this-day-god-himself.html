The Day of God Himself

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

Bahá’u’lláh calls this Day “the Day of God Himself.” The texts below give three reasons. The One promised in every earlier Revelation has appeared. The Beloved now calls His lovers, where before they searched for Him. The veils that hid the divine mysteries have been removed. The texts also fix a limit: the One who appears is the Manifestation of God, and the Writings deny that God’s Essence descends into a human form. This page sets out both the claim and the limit. It is one facet of The greatness of this Day.

The passages

The Promised One is seen and heard

Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Gleanings:

VERILY I say, this is the Day in which mankind can behold the Face, and hear the Voice, of the Promised One.

(Gleanings VII.1)

The sentence names two senses, sight and hearing, and says that both are now possible for mankind. A second passage in the Gleanings uses the same two senses and names the earlier occasion of the Voice:

Every discerning eye can, in this Day, perceive the dawning light of God’s Revelation, and every attentive ear can recognize the Voice that was heard from the Burning Bush.

(Gleanings CXXVI.3)

The Voice heard by Moses and the Voice heard now are identified. The condition is that the eye be discerning and the ear attentive. The Revelation is present to everyone; perceiving it is a matter of the one who looks and listens.

The Beloved calls His lovers

Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Gleanings:

For whereas in days past every lover besought and searched after his Beloved, it is the Beloved Himself Who now is calling His lovers and is inviting them to attain His presence.

(Gleanings CLI.2)

This passage describes a reversal. In earlier days the seeker went in search of God. In this Day God calls, and the invitation is to His presence. In the Tablet of Carmel, Bahá’u’lláh, addressing Mount Carmel, bids it make the same announcement:

“Call out to Zion, O Carmel, and announce the joyful tidings: He that was hidden from mortal eyes is come! His all-conquering sovereignty is manifest; His all-encompassing splendor is revealed.

(Bahá’u’lláh, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 1.4)

The words are spoken to Carmel, not by it: Carmel is told to call out to Zion. The announcement is that the One hidden is now come, and that His sovereignty and splendor are manifest. The Writings elsewhere say what “hidden” does and does not mean, and the section on the limit below takes that up.

“The Day of God Himself”

Two passages use the phrase itself. In the first, Bahá’u’lláh gives a command to the hearer:

Say: This is the Day of God Himself; fear ye God and be not of them that have disbelieved in Him.

(Bahá’u’lláh, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 17.42)

In the second, He addresses the high priests:

Say, O high priests! Shake off your slumber, rouse yourselves from unconsciousness, incline your inner ears to the melody of the All-Sufficing, and conduct yourselves in a manner that beseemeth the Day of God Himself.

(Bahá’u’lláh, Additional Tablets and Extracts, ATE 71.5)

Both passages draw a conduct from the title. The first ties it to fear of God and to belief. The second asks for waking, rousing and listening, which are the acts the sleepers have not done. The title is given as a reason for how to behave, and not only as praise.

A third passage declares the Day complete:

The Day of God hath been fulfilled, for lo, the Lord hath been unveiled;

(Bahá’u’lláh, The Call of the Divine Beloved, CDB 1.10)

The phrase “the Lord hath been unveiled” gives the reason for the fulfilment. The Day of God is fulfilled because He is unveiled.

Sovereignty and descent

Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Gleanings:

THE Pen of Revelation exclaimeth: “On this Day the Kingdom is God’s!” The Tongue of Power is calling: “On this Day all sovereignty is, in very deed, with God!”

(Gleanings XV.1)

This is the Day whereon the All-Merciful hath come down in the clouds of knowledge, clothed with manifest sovereignty.

(Gleanings XVIII.6)

Bahá’u’lláh writes in a Tablet of the volume revealed after the Kitáb-i-Aqdas:

This is the Day that hath been illumined by the effulgent light of the Countenance of God—the Day when the Tongue of Grandeur is calling aloud: The Kingdom is God’s, the Lord of the Day of Resurrection.

(Bahá’u’lláh, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 17.78)

Three things are said here. Sovereignty belongs to God. The All-Merciful ”hath come down,“ and the manner of the descent is ”in the clouds of knowledge,“ so it is described as a descent in knowledge and not as a descent in flesh. The Day is lit by ”the Countenance of God.“ The phrase ”hath come down“ has to be read together with the passages on the limit, below, which state that God’s Essence does not descend.

The Resurrection named in TB 17.78 has its own page in this series: The Resurrection.

The veils removed

In the Days of Remembrance, Bahá’u’lláh writes:

This is the Day whereon fire and water were joined together as one, and the veils were removed from the face of all mysteries, inasmuch as the Beauty of the Unconstrained came forth arrayed in the raiment of His own Self, the Help in Peril, the Almighty, the Incomparable. All glory be to this Day whose advent hath cheered the eyes of the well-favoured of God!

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance, DOR 10.18)

The cause of the removal is given in the clause that follows ”inasmuch as“: the Beauty of the Unconstrained came forth ”arrayed in the raiment of His own Self.“ Another passage in the same volume is a prayer, and it says what the Day has done in the world:

Magnified, then, be this Day whereon Thou hast revealed unto all things the effulgent lights of Thy glorious unity and cast upon all creation the radiance of Thy sovereign and transcendent oneness, the Day whereon Thou hast lifted the veil of concealment from the countenance of Thy beauty; burned away, through Thy gracious favour, the shrouds of idle fancy blinding the eyes of the people; and summoned all to partake of Thy nearness and reunion. Immensely glorified be this Day whereon the oceans of splendour and grace have surged and the rivers of bounty and justice have flowed, a Day whereon Thy bounty hath attained such a degree that every stammering tongue hath celebrated Thy praise, every blind eye hath beheld the lights of Thy beauty, and every deaf ear hath hearkened unto the glorious strains of the Dove of Thy oneness.

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance, DOR 11.7)

The prayer is addressed to God, and what it thanks Him for is that He has ”lifted the veil of concealment“ and ”summoned all to partake of Thy nearness and reunion.“ The summons is to nearness, which agrees with the passage from Gleanings CLI.2 above. In a third passage Bahá’u’lláh again speaks in His own voice:

Say: This is a Day whereon God hath made His own Self known and revealed it unto all who are in the heavens and on earth, a day whereon He hath established His sovereign ascendancy over the kingdoms of revelation and creation. How exalted, then, is this holy, this most blessed and best-beloved grace! This is a Day, moreover, whereon the Ancient Beauty hath appeared with such an adorning as to cause the veils to be rent asunder, and the mysteries to be revealed, and the fruits to spring forth, and all things to utter the praise of their Lord, the Unconstrained—a Day whereon the earth and all that it holdeth, and the heavens and all that they contain, and the mountains and all that they conceal, and the oceans and all that they treasure in their depths, have laid bare their secrets, though the people remain veiled therefrom. This is a day whereon the idols of misbelief and worldly desire have been shattered and the Ancient Beauty hath ascended His mighty throne.

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance, DOR 23.11)

Here the secrets of creation are said to be laid bare, and the people still ”remain veiled therefrom.“ The veils are removed on God’s side; whether a person sees remains a separate matter. That distinction is the subject of The Day Can Be Missed.

The end of the Prophetic Cycle

Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Gleanings:

This Day, however, is unique, and is to be distinguished from those that have preceded it. The designation “Seal of the Prophets” fully revealeth its high station. The Prophetic Cycle hath, verily, ended. The Eternal Truth is now come.

(Gleanings XXV)

This passage states the claim of a different kind of Day, and it states it in four short sentences. The earlier Days belonged to the Prophetic Cycle, and that cycle has ended. What has come is called ”The Eternal Truth.“

In a written passage, ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá says the same in terms of the human race:

IN cycles gone by, each one of the Manifestations of God hath had His own rank in the world of existence, and each hath represented a stage in the development of humanity. But the Manifestation of the Most Great Name—may my life be a sacrifice for His loved ones—was an expression of the coming of age, the maturing of man’s inmost reality in this world of being. For the sun is the source and wellspring of light and heat, the focal point of splendors, and it compriseth all the perfections that are made manifest by the other stars which have dawned upon the world. Make thou an effort that thou mayest take thy place under the sun and receive an abundant share of its dazzling light. In truth do I tell thee, once thou hast attained this station, thou shalt behold the saints bowing down their heads in all humility before Him. Haste thou to life before death cometh; haste thou to the spring season before autumn draweth in; and before illness striketh, haste thou to healing—that thou mayest become a physician of the spirit who, with the breaths of the Holy Spirit, healeth all manner of sickness in this famed and glorious age.

(Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, SWAB 27)

‘Abdu’l‑Bahá compares the earlier Manifestations, each of a rank and a stage, to the other stars, and the Manifestation of the Most Great Name to the sun that comprises their perfections. The same page of this series that treats the longing of earlier Prophets is The Day the Prophets Longed For.

Shoghi Effendi comments in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh:

The Faith of Bahá’u’lláh should indeed be regarded, if we wish to be faithful to the tremendous implications of its message, as the culmination of a cycle, the final stage in a series of successive, of preliminary and progressive revelations. These, beginning with Adam and ending with the Báb, have paved the way and anticipated with an ever-increasing emphasis the advent of that Day of Days in which He Who is the Promise of All Ages should be made manifest.

(Shoghi Effendi, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.16)

Shoghi Effendi places the earlier Revelations, from Adam to the Báb, as preliminary and progressive. They anticipated the advent of ”He Who is the Promise of All Ages.“

Who this One is: the station and its measure

Bahá’u’lláh’s own words about His station are strong. In a passage quoted by Shoghi Effendi in God Passes By, the Tablet is not identified:

But for Him no Divine Messenger would have been invested with the Robe of Prophethood, nor would any of the sacred Scriptures have been revealed.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in God Passes By, GPB VI.22; source Tablet not identified)

The sentence is quoted here as supplied; who ”Him“ names is determined by its context in God Passes By (VI.22), which this page does not reproduce.

Bahá’u’lláh also says that the Revelation was not fully apprehended by the earlier Manifestations:

Verily I say! No one hath apprehended the root of this Cause. It is incumbent upon everyone, in this day, to perceive with the eye of God, and to hearken with His ear. Whoso beholdeth Me with an eye besides Mine own will never be able to know Me. None among the Manifestations of old, except to a prescribed degree, hath ever completely apprehended the nature of this Revelation.

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

This passage says that to know the Revelation one must use ”the eye of God“ and not another eye. It does not say how this relates to the question of the Essence; its subject is apprehension of the Revelation, and the passage says the earlier Manifestations apprehended it ”to a prescribed degree.“ The article “Dearly Beloved Friends”: The Advent of Divine Justice Compilation, Traced traces the compilation in which this passage stands.

‘Abdu’l‑Bahá writes of the same coming, and of the people who slept through it:

O YE who are the chosen ones of the Abhá Kingdom! Praise ye the Lord of Hosts for He, riding upon the clouds, hath come down to this world out of the heaven of the invisible realm, so that East and West were lit by the glory of the Sun of Truth, and the call of the Kingdom was raised, and the heralds of the realm above, with melodies of the Concourse on high, sang out the glad tidings of the Coming. Then the whole world of being did quiver for joy, and still the people, even as the Messiah saith, slept on: for the day of the Manifestation, when the Lord of Hosts descended, found them wrapped in the slumber of unknowing. As He saith in the Gospel, My coming is even as when the thief is in the house, and the goodman of the house watcheth not.

(Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, SWAB 17.1)

The limit: the Manifestation is not the Essence of God

The passages above use the language of God’s coming: ”the All-Merciful hath come down,“ ”the Lord hath been unveiled,“ ”He that was hidden … is come.“ Shoghi Effendi states in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh how these are to be read. He writes:

That Bahá’u’lláh should, notwithstanding the overwhelming intensity of His Revelation, be regarded as essentially one of these Manifestations of God, never to be identified with that invisible Reality, the Essence of Divinity itself, is one of the major beliefs of our Faith—a belief which should never be obscured and the integrity of which no one of its followers should allow to be compromised.

(Shoghi Effendi, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, from the passage on the Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh; source file `world-order-bahaullah.md`, line 634)

Earlier in the same passage Shoghi Effendi states the reason, in terms that bear on the word ”incarnate“:

The human temple that has been made the vehicle of so overpowering a Revelation must, if we be faithful to the tenets of our Faith, ever remain entirely distinguished from that “innermost Spirit of Spirits” and “eternal Essence of Essences”—that invisible yet rational God Who, however much we extol the divinity of His Manifestations on earth, can in no wise incarnate His infinite, His unknowable, His incorruptible and all-embracing Reality in the concrete and limited frame of a mortal being.

(Shoghi Effendi, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, source file `world-order-bahaullah.md`, line 626)

So ”the Day of God Himself“ means that God is present in this Day through His Manifestation, whose utterance is, in Bahá’u’lláh’s words quoted by Shoghi Effendi in the same passage, the ”Voice of Divinity, the Call of God Himself.“ It does not mean that the Essence of God has taken a human form. Shoghi Effendi also quotes the Kitáb-i-Íqán on the same point, that God ”is, and hath ever been, veiled in the ancient eternity of His Essence.“

The unveiling in the earlier passages is therefore the unveiling of what the Manifestation reveals of God. It is not a sight of the Essence. The Day is God’s in this sense: He has sent His Manifestation, and has made His own Self known through Him, as DOR 23.11 puts it in the sentence ”God hath made His own Self known.“

One passage addressed to the Pen

One more passage should be read with care, because it is short and absolute:

Canst thou discover any one but Me, O Pen, in this Day?

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance, DOR 6.4)

Here the speaker is the One who is revealing. The passage is addressed to the Pen, and it asks whether the Pen can find anyone but the Speaker. It is consistent with the limit stated above only when the Speaker is understood as the Manifestation, who speaks with the Voice of God.

What the sources do not settle

  • The Tablet of ADJ 110 and the Tablet of the sentence in GPB VI.22 are not identified in the files used. The sentence of GPB VI.22 is quoted without its antecedent for ”Him.“
  • The Tablets from which some passages come (for example the context of TB 1.4 and of CDB 1.10) are given here by reference only. This page does not name them.
  • The original wording of phrases such as ”the Day of God Himself“ is not in this corpus, so this page does not compare the Arabic and Persian.
  • DOR 6.4 is a single sentence. Its speaker is the One revealing, and the page reads it with the limit above; the sources used do not themselves add an explanation of that sentence.
  • ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s SWAB 17.1 and SWAB 27 are written texts (selections from Tablets). This page does not lean on recorded talks.

Concrete images

  • A lover who used to search, and now hears the Beloved calling.
  • Carmel called to announce the news to Zion.
  • Veils removed from the face of mysteries; veils rent asunder.
  • Fire and water joined as one.
  • The All-Merciful coming down in clouds of knowledge.
  • The Voice from the Burning Bush, heard again.
  • The blind eye that sees and the stammering tongue that praises (DOR 11.7).
  • The idols of misbelief and worldly desire shattered (DOR 23.11).
  • A house whose owner sleeps while the visitor arrives (SWAB 17.1).

A possible emotional arc

  1. The old search: every lover besought and searched after his Beloved (Gleanings CLI.2).
  2. The reversal: the Beloved Himself calls (Gleanings CLI.2).
  3. The news carried: ”He that was hidden … is come!“ (TB 1.4).
  4. The unveiling and the Voice: veils removed, the Voice of the Burning Bush heard (DOR 10.18; Gleanings CXXVI.3).
  5. The answer: waking to ”the Day of God Himself“ (TB 17.42; ATE 71.5).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not say that God, or the Essence of God, became a man or lives in Bahá’u’lláh. Shoghi Effendi calls the idea of divine incarnation ”crude and fantastic“ and incompatible with the Faith (World Order, `world-order-bahaullah.md` line 626).
  • Do not present Bahá’u’lláh as identical to ”the Essence of Divinity.“ He is the Manifestation of God (`world-order-bahaullah.md` line 634).
  • ”He that was hidden … is come“ and ”the Lord hath been unveiled“ are Bahá’u’lláh’s words about the Manifestation’s advent. Do not put them in the mouth of a character as if a visible God were speaking in person.
  • Do not attribute ”Canst thou discover any one but Me, O Pen“ or ”the Day of God Himself“ to someone other than Bahá’u’lláh.
  • Do not attribute the sun passage (SWAB 27) or the ”Lord of Hosts“ passage (SWAB 17.1) to Bahá’u’lláh; they are ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s.
  • Do not name the Tablet of ADJ 110 or GPB VI.22.
  • Do not say that the earlier Prophets were false or lesser in kind. The texts say the Cycle has ended and that the earlier ones apprehended this Revelation ”to a prescribed degree“; they do not say the earlier Revelations are void.
