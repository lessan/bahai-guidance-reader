The King of Days: the Day with no equal

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

Bahá’u’lláh calls this Day the King of Days and says it has no equal among the days that came before it or will come after it. The texts make three claims. The Day is the point at which earlier ages and Dispensations reach their end. Its station is above that of any Revelation, past or future. And it is “the Day which shall not be followed by night.” Shoghi Effendi and the Universal House of Justice read the last phrase as a statement about the continuity of divine guidance through the Covenant.

This page is one facet of the series The greatness of this Day. It collects the passages that rank this Day against all others. Other pages treat what the Day is in itself and what it brings.

The passages

The King of Days

Bahá’u’lláh uses the title in prayer. In Prayers and Meditations He says:

MAGNIFIED be Thy name, O my God, for that Thou hast manifested the Day which is the King of Days, the Day which Thou didst announce unto Thy chosen Ones and Thy Prophets in Thy most excellent Tablets, the Day whereon Thou didst shed the splendor of the glory of all Thy names upon all created things. (Prayers and Meditations LXXI.1)

The title is given with a reason: the Day was announced beforehand to the Prophets, and on it the glory of all God’s names reaches every created thing. A prayer in Days of Remembrance states the ranking more fully:

I bear witness, O my God, that Thou hast ordained this Day to stand unequalled amongst all the days of Thy world and unrivalled amidst all that Thou hast fashioned through Thy creative power. This is that primal Day which Thou hast singled out from among all other days, and exalted over all other times, and appointed as the King of Days unto all peoples, inasmuch as Thou didst manifest on this Day the tokens of Thy transcendent power and the evidences of Thy holy unity. Thou hast caused its radiance to surpass the splendour of the sun, the moon, and the stars, and to transcend the brightness of every lofty and glorious, every shining and brilliant light. Nay, Thou hast illumined this Day, O my Best-Beloved, with the very lights of Thine own inaccessible Being and with the full glory of Thine own exalted Essence. (Days of Remembrance 11.6)

The passage gives two grounds for the title: the Day shows God’s power and His unity, and its light exceeds every created light. The last sentence speaks of the Day, not of Bahá’u’lláh Himself; see “What the sources do not settle” below.

A Tablet quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice (the Tablet itself is not identified) gives the title together with a name for the One who came:

This is the King of Days, the Day that hath seen the coming of the Best Beloved, He Who through all eternity hath been acclaimed the Desire of the World. (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

Shoghi Effendi gathers several of these titles in God Passes By. He introduces them as Bahá’u’lláh’s own declaration, and the phrases in quotation marks are Bahá’u’lláh’s:

“This,” He furthermore declares, “is the king of days,” the “Day of God Himself,” the “Day which shall never be followed by night,” the “Springtime which autumn will never overtake,” “the eye to past ages and centuries,” (God Passes By VI.22)

Shoghi Effendi places these titles in a passage about the greatness of Bahá’u’lláh’s Revelation. The list shows that ”king of days“ and ”not followed by night“ belong together as names of one Day.

Past ages cannot rival it

Several passages state the comparison with earlier time directly. In Gleanings Bahá’u’lláh writes of a Day ”so blest that past ages and centuries can never hope to rival it“:

ALL glory be to this Day, the Day in which the fragrances of mercy have been wafted over all created things, a Day so blest that past ages and centuries can never hope to rival it, a Day in which the countenance of the Ancient of Days hath turned towards His holy seat. Thereupon the voices of all created things, and beyond them those of the Concourse on high, were heard calling aloud: “Haste thee, O Carmel, for lo, the light of the countenance of God, the Ruler of the Kingdom of Names and Fashioner of the heavens, hath been lifted upon thee.” (Gleanings XI.1)

The Tablet quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice (not identified) puts the comparison in terms of time:

A fleeting moment, in this Day, excelleth centuries of a bygone age.… Neither sun nor moon hath witnessed a day such as this Day. (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

The same quotation in that work continues with a statement that reaches forward as well as back:

That which hath been made manifest in this preeminent, this most exalted Revelation, stands unparalleled in the annals of the past, nor will future ages witness its like. (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

And in a Tablet in Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh He puts it as a statement about the station of the Revelation:

Glorified be God! One wondereth by what proof or reason the disbelievers among the people of the Bayán have turned away from the Lord of being. In truth the station of this Revelation transcendeth the station of whatever hath been manifested in the past or will be made manifest in the future. (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 6.46)

Together these give the claim two directions. Nothing before this Day equals it, and nothing after it will. In 6.46 the claim is applied to the people of the Bayán who turned away: Bahá’u’lláh wonders by what proof they did so.

Another quotation in the same work adds an image for the Day’s place in history:

Peerless is this Day, for it is as the eye to past ages and centuries, and as a light unto the darkness of the times. (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

The consummation of ages and Dispensations

Bahá’u’lláh describes this Day as the end point of earlier ages. In Gleanings LXXVI He connects it to the Báb:

Ages rolled away, until they attained their consummation in this, the Lord of days, the Day whereon the Daystar of the Bayán manifested itself above the horizon of mercy, the Day in which the Beauty of the All-Glorious shone forth in the exalted person of ‘Alí-Muḥammad, the Báb. (Gleanings LXXVI.3)

Here the Day begins with the Báb. This agrees with the 1 June 2018 message of the Universal House of Justice, which is quoted below. In Gleanings CXV and in Days of Remembrance Bahá’u’lláh says what is consummated:

In this most mighty Revelation all the Dispensations of the past have attained their highest and final consummation. (Gleanings CXV.10)

All days have attained their consummation in this most august of days, and all hours in this noblest of hours, and the Unseen hath willed to confer every grace upon the inmates of earth and heaven, to establish, before all who are in the kingdoms of revelation and creation, the Manifestation of God and His loftiness, and the sovereignty of God and His grandeur, that His favour might be made complete unto His servants and His bounty fulfilled unto His creation. And yet, when once He appeared, the eyes of all who had awaited Him were dazzled, save for those whom His might had protected and from whose sight He had lifted all worldly veils. Blessed, then, be He Who hath been made manifest through the power of truth in this wondrous, this shining vesture! (Days of Remembrance 10.4)

The second passage joins the consummation to a warning. The Day was awaited, and when it came many who had awaited it were dazzled and did not see it. Its greatness did not make it easy to recognize.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá writes in the same sense about the century in which the Revelation was given. Addressing the handmaids of the Lord, He writes:

In the ages to come, though the Cause of God may rise and grow a hundredfold and the shade of the Sadratu’l-Muntahá shelter all mankind, yet this present century shall stand unrivalled, for it hath witnessed the breaking of that Morn and the rising of that Sun. This century is, verily, the source of His Light and the dayspring of His Revelation. Future ages and generations shall behold the diffusion of its radiance and the manifestations of its signs. (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá 32.2)

This passage separates two things. The Cause may grow far beyond its present size, and still the century of the Revelation’s dawn has a distinction that growth does not remove. The later ages show the effects of what began in this one.

In a talk recorded in The Promulgation of Universal Peace (notes of a spoken address, not a text written by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá), He says, ”This is the consummate day of all the Prophets!“ (Promulgation 3.5). The written texts above make the same point.

Beyond the comprehension of men

Bahá’u’lláh also says the Day exceeds what people can measure.

The excellence of this Day is immensely exalted above the comprehension of men, however extensive their knowledge, however profound their understanding. (Gleanings LIII.2)

Great, immeasurably great is this Cause! Mighty, inconceivably mighty is this Day! (Gleanings CXV.12)

I testify before God to the greatness, the inconceivable greatness of this Revelation. Again and again have We, in most of Our Tablets, borne witness to this truth, that mankind may be roused from its heedlessness. (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

The last passage states the purpose of the repeated testimony: that mankind may be roused from heedlessness. Another quotation in the same work says what the Day would draw from people if its greatness were fully known:

Should the greatness of this Day be revealed in its fullness, every man would forsake a myriad lives in his longing to partake, though it be for one moment, of its great glory—how much more this world and its corruptible treasures! (Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

Because the Day cannot be fully described, Bahá’u’lláh tells His followers to keep mentioning it:

Make thou mention of this Day and magnify that which hath appeared therein. It will in truth suffice all mankind. (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 9.15)

Not followed by night

Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Summons of the Lord of Hosts:

For the Day of God is none other but His own Self, Who hath appeared with the power of truth. This is the Day that shall not be followed by night, nor shall it be bounded by any praise, would that ye might understand! (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts 1.63)

The passage says two things about the Day: it has no night after it, and no praise of it can set a limit to it. In Epistle to the Son of the Wolf He uses the sun as the image:

The Sun of Divine Utterance can never set, neither can its radiance be extinguished. (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf 44)

A passage in Days of Remembrance says where the light of this Day comes from:

Then were their voices lifted up in praise and exultation on this glorious Day, a Day whose radiance deriveth not from the sun and its rays but from the effulgent light of the Countenance of God, the King, the Exalted, the All-Bountiful. Blessed, then, be He Who hath caused it to appear through the power of truth and Who hath resurrected therein the souls of all humankind! (Days of Remembrance 10.16)

If the light is not the sun’s, it does not set when the sun sets.

Shoghi Effendi reads the phrase ”not followed by night“ in the context of Bahá’u’lláh’s passing in 1892. He describes the Bahá’í community at that time and what it found in ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. He comments:

The significance of the solemn affirmation that this is “the Day which shall not be followed by night” was now clearly apprehended. An orphan community had recognized in ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, in its hour of desperate need, its Solace, its Guide, its Mainstay and Champion. (God Passes By XV.6)

In the surrounding paragraph he states that ”the continuity of that unerring guidance vouchsafed to it since its birth was now assured.“ The phrase did not mean that no hard time would come; the community had just lost its Founder. It meant that guidance would not be withdrawn and the community’s recognition of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá showed this.

The Universal House of Justice makes the same reading for the present age. Its message of 14 March 1996 says:

The Bahá’í Dispensation is described in the words of its Founder as “a day that shall not be followed by night.” (Universal House of Justice, 14 March 1996)

The next sentence of that message reads: ”Through His Covenant, Bahá’u’lláh has provided an unfailing source of divine guidance that will endure throughout the Dispensation.“ The message goes on to say that authority to administer the affairs of the community is conferred on the Administrative Order to which the Covenant has given birth. The ”day without night“ is therefore tied to institutions that continue: the Covenant, and the Administrative Order it produced.

The scale of time

Shoghi Effendi states the duration of this Revelation in God Passes By, in a sentence that describes the Revelation born in the Síyáh-Chál of Ṭihrán:

A Revelation, hailed as the promise and crowning glory of past ages and centuries, as the consummation of all the Dispensations within the Adamic Cycle, inaugurating an era of at least a thousand years’ duration, and a cycle destined to last no less than five thousand centuries, signalizing the end of the Prophetic Era and the beginning of the Era of Fulfillment, unsurpassed alike in the duration of its Author’s ministry and the fecundity and splendor of His mission (God Passes By VI.26)

The figures are his: an era of at least a thousand years, and a cycle of no less than five thousand centuries. He also names the end of the Prophetic Era and the beginning of the Era of Fulfillment, and calls the Revelation the consummation of the Dispensations ”within the Adamic Cycle.“ The sentence is a long description and continues past the words quoted. In God Passes By he introduces it as the greatness of the Revelation, so it states a minimum for the Day, not an upper limit on what the Day contains.

He also quotes one of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s early Tablets on how rarely such a Day comes:

“Centuries, nay ages, must pass away, ere the Day-Star of Truth shineth again in its mid-summer splendor, or appeareth once more in the radiance of its vernal glory.” (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted in God Passes By VI.23; source Tablet not identified)

And in the first chapter he says what the Declaration of the Báb began:

With this historic Declaration the dawn of an Age that signalizes the consummation of all ages had broken. (God Passes By I.7)

Two Manifestations announced it

The House of Justice grounds the Day’s greatness in the fact that two Manifestations announced it. Its message of 1 June 2018, written for the bicentenary of the Birth of Bahá’u’lláh, says:

For the greatness of this Day, this “matchless Day” extolled by Bahá’u’lláh, is evident from the fact that its advent was announced by two divine Manifestations, Whose Birthdays are “accounted as one in the sight of God”. (Universal House of Justice, 1 June 2018)

This ties the earlier passages to a historical fact that Bahá’ís can name. The Báb and Bahá’u’lláh both appeared in this Day, and the Day is spoken of as one Day. This matches the opening of Gleanings LXXVI.3 above, where the Báb’s Revelation is the start of the Day Bahá’u’lláh calls the Lord of days.

How this facet connects

  • The Day the Prophets Longed For. This page rates the Day against all others; that page treats the Prophets’ expectation of it. The announcement named in Prayers and Meditations LXXI.1 (”which Thou didst announce unto Thy chosen Ones“) belongs to both.
  • The Day of God Himself. The title ”Day of God Himself“ appears beside ”king of days“ in God Passes By VI.22. That page takes up what it means that the Day is the Day of God; this page only records the titles side by side.
  • Envied by the Concourse on High. In Gleanings XI.1 the voices of the Concourse on high call Carmel to haste. That page gives the heavenly response to the Day; this page gives the ranking that explains why there is one.
  • The Coming of Age of Humanity. The time-scale in God Passes By VI.26 (the end of the Prophetic Era and the beginning of the Era of Fulfillment) is the frame for that page’s account of humanity’s maturity.
  • “Dearly Beloved Friends”: The Advent of Divine Justice Compilation, Traced. Most of the quotations here marked ADJ 110 come from one paragraph of The Advent of Divine Justice. That article traces how the compilation is built and where its parts come from.
  • The Stages to the Most Great Peace. The ”Day not followed by night“ with its Covenant and Administrative Order is the setting in which those stages unfold over the Dispensation.

What the sources do not settle

  • Six passages in this page come from Tablets quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice (ADJ 110) whose source Tablets the file does not identify. No Tablet name is given here. The quotations are Bahá’u’lláh’s words as Shoghi Effendi presents them.
  • The original-language wording of ”the Day which shall not be followed by night“ is not in this corpus. The page relies on the English of God Passes By, the Summons, and the House of Justice message.
  • Shoghi Effendi’s reading in God Passes By XV.6 ties the phrase to the community’s recognition of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. The House of Justice in 1996 ties it to the Covenant and the Administrative Order across the Dispensation. These are consistent. Neither passage gives a date or a length for the Day beyond what God Passes By VI.26 states.
  • Days of Remembrance 11.6 speaks of this Day as illumined with ”the full glory of Thine own exalted Essence.“ The sources on this page do not say how that sentence relates to the teaching that the Essence of God is unknowable and that the Manifestation is the means by which God is known. A reader should not draw a conclusion about Bahá’u’lláh’s own station from the sentence alone.
  • The talk recorded at Promulgation 3.5 is one line from notes of a spoken address and is not used here for any point the written texts do not also make.

Concrete images

  • The sun, moon and stars, with a Day whose light is brighter than all of them (Days of Remembrance 11.6).
  • A fleeting moment weighed against centuries (ADJ 110).
  • The Day-Star at midsummer, or in the first radiance of spring (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in God Passes By VI.23).
  • The eye to past ages and a light to the darkness of the times (ADJ 110; God Passes By VI.22).
  • Mount Carmel, with the voices of all created things calling it to haste (Gleanings XI.1).
  • The Sun that never sets (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf 44).
  • An orphan community finding its Guide (God Passes By XV.6).
  • The shade of the Sadratu’l-Muntahá sheltering all mankind (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Selections 32.2).

A possible emotional arc for one song

  1. Wonder at a Day beyond measure: ”Great, immeasurably great is this Cause“ (Gleanings CXV.12).
  2. Comparison with all earlier time: ”A fleeting moment, in this Day, excelleth centuries of a bygone age“ (ADJ 110).
  3. The warning that it was not easily seen: eyes ”dazzled“ even among those who had awaited it (Days of Remembrance 10.4).
  4. The Day that continues: no night after it, and a community that finds its Guide (Summons 1.63; God Passes By XV.6).
  5. A closing command to speak of it: ”Make thou mention of this Day“ (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 9.15).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not turn ”unequalled“ into a claim that earlier Revelations were of no worth. The texts say earlier Dispensations reach their consummation in this one.
  • Do not give a date or an end to the Day. Shoghi Effendi’s figures are ”at least a thousand years“ and ”no less than five thousand centuries,“ stated for the era and the cycle, and they are minimums.
  • Do not read ”not followed by night“ as a promise that there will be no difficulty. Shoghi Effendi and the House of Justice read it as the continuity of guidance through the Covenant.
  • Do not present Bahá’u’lláh as the Essence of God. Do not use the ”Essence“ sentence in Days of Remembrance 11.6 as a statement of His station.
  • Do not attribute the ADJ 110 passages to a named Tablet. Do not attribute the line ”This is the consummate day of all the Prophets!“ to ‘Abdu’l-Bahá as a written text; it is from a recorded talk.
  • Do not invent a quotation or merge two. ”King of Days“ and ”Day of God Himself“ are separate titles that Shoghi Effendi places side by side.
