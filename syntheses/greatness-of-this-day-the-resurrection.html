The Resurrection

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

The Scriptures of earlier religions promise a Day of Resurrection: the dead are raised and every soul is judged. The Báb and Bahá’u’lláh say that this Day is the appearance of a Manifestation of God. Those who are spiritually dead are raised by recognising Him, and a new creation begins. This page sets out what the texts say about that Day, who rises, and what rising asks of a believer. It is one facet of the series The greatness of this Day. The prophecies of each tradition are treated elsewhere in the series and are left out here.

The passages

What the Day of Resurrection means

The Báb defines the term in the Persian Bayán, in a chapter whose subject is the Day of Resurrection. He writes:

“In the estimation of God and according to the usage of such as are initiated into divine mysteries, what is meant by the Day of Resurrection is this, that from the time of the appearance of Him Who is the Tree of divine Reality, at whatever period and under whatever name, until the moment of His disappearance, is the Day of Resurrection.”

(Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.74)

The Day is not a single moment at the end of time. It is the whole period in which a Manifestation is present, “at whatever period and under whatever name.” The Báb adds that the Day brings things to completion:

“The stage of perfection of everything is reached when its resurrection occurreth. The perfection of the religion of Islám was consummated at the beginning of this Revelation; and from the rise of this Revelation until its setting, the fruits of the Tree of Islám, whatever they are, will become apparent.”

(Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.75)

Resurrection here means the stage at which a thing shows what it is. The earlier religion is brought to its perfection when the new Revelation appears, and its fruits show during that Revelation.

Bahá’u’lláh gives the same meaning in the Kitáb-i-Íqán, where He cites the tradition that when the Qá’im rises, that day is the Day of Resurrection. He then writes:

“This is the meaning of the “Day of Resurrection,” spoken of in all the scriptures, and announced unto all people. Reflect, can a more precious, a mightier, and more glorious day than this be conceived, so that man should willingly forego its grace, and deprive himself of its bounties, which like unto vernal showers are raining from the heaven of mercy upon all mankind?”

(The Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 152)

In the next paragraph of the Íqán, He ties the understanding of this term to detachment:

“Shouldst thou step into the realm of complete detachment, thou wilt readily testify that no day is mightier than this Day, and that no resurrection more awful than this Resurrection can ever be conceived.”

(The Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 153)

The Íqán claims that this Resurrection is the greatest that can be conceived, and it also says what prevents a person from seeing it. The obstacle is attachment to what the person already holds, not lack of evidence. In Days of Remembrance Bahá’u’lláh states the fulfilment plainly:

“The proof hath been fulfilled and the testimony established, inasmuch as the Resurrection hath come to pass through the appearance of God in the Manifestation of His own Self, the Ever-Abiding. And this, verily, is from the grace of God, the All-Glorious, the Most Bountiful.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance, DOR 44.16)

The paragraphs before this one in the same Tablet address the people of the Bayán. The Resurrection is said to have “come to pass,” in the past tense, and the cause given is the appearance of God in the Manifestation.

The trumpet and the hour

The traditions of Islám describe Isráfíl sounding a trumpet at the Resurrection. Bahá’u’lláh uses that figure for the Day of His own arising. In a passage in Days of Remembrance He writes:

“When matters came to such a pass, the Ancient Beauty bestirred Himself, and all things were inwardly and outwardly set in motion. Then He arose, and through His arising the Most Great Resurrection was ushered in betwixt earth and heaven. Whereupon the Spirit called out once again, before His presence: “O Isráfíl! By the righteousness of God, thou wert created for this day. Wherefore, sound thy trumpet to proclaim the advent of this Manifestation, that every mouldering bone may be quickened thereby!””

(Bahá’u’lláh, Days of Remembrance, DOR 10.21)

Here the Resurrection begins with the Manifestation’s own act of arising. The trumpet announces it and does not cause it.

In the Summons of the Lord of Hosts the same event is told as an hour that has struck:

“Say: Still your pens, O concourse of divines, for lo, the shrill voice of the Pen of Glory hath been lifted up between earth and heaven. Cast away all that ye possess and take fast hold of that which We have revealed unto you with power and authority. The Hour that was concealed within the knowledge of God hath struck, whereupon all the atoms of the earth have proclaimed: “The Ancient of Days is come in His great glory! Hasten unto Him, O peoples of the earth, with humble and contrite hearts.””

(Bahá’u’lláh, The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, SLH 1.107)

The divines are told to stop and to hold to what has been revealed. Bahá’u’lláh calls on the same group in a Tablet printed in the collection of Tablets revealed after the Kitáb-i-Aqdas:

“The sun is calling out: O concourse of the divines! The heaven of religions is split and the moon cleft asunder and the peoples of the earth are brought together in a new resurrection.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh Revealed after the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, TB 17.59)

The signs the divines expected at the Last Day (a split heaven, a cleft moon) are named, and they are said to have occurred in religion itself. The result is that the peoples are “brought together in a new resurrection.”

Every soul expires and a new creation is called into being

Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Gleanings:

“This is the Day whereon naught can be seen except the splendors of the Light that shineth from the face of Thy Lord, the Gracious, the Most Bountiful. Verily, We have caused every soul to expire by virtue of Our irresistible and all-subduing sovereignty. We have, then, called into being a new creation, as a token of Our grace unto men. I am, verily, the All-Bountiful, the Ancient of Days.”

(Gleanings XIV.5)

The passage has two acts in sequence. Every soul is made to expire, and then a new creation is called into being. The first is done by God’s sovereignty and the second is “a token of Our grace.” The speaker names Himself as the Ancient of Days. In another Tablet from that collection, Bahá’u’lláh says what a believer must hold about this new creation:

“Whoso firmly believeth today in the rebirth of man and is fully conscious that God, the Most Exalted, wieldeth supreme ascendancy and absolute authority over this new creation, verily such a man is reckoned with them that are endued with insight in this most great Revelation. Unto this beareth witness every discerning believer.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh Revealed after the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, TB 9.16)

Belief in rebirth is joined to belief that God holds authority over the new creation. The one who is reborn does not own the new life.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá uses the same terms:

“He hath laid down the foundations of the lofty Citadel, He hath inaugurated the Cycle of Glory, He hath brought forth a new creation on this day that is clearly Judgement Day—and still do the heedless stay fast in their drunken sleep.”

(‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, SWAB 4.2)

He names the day “Judgement Day” and says that the heedless sleep through it. The Day does not force its recognition on anyone.

The dead in their graves

The texts speak of a condition of death that is in people now, and of a rising out of it.

“Darkness hath been chased away by the dawning light of the mercy of thy Lord, the Source of all light. The breeze of the All-Merciful hath wafted, and the souls have been quickened in the tombs of their bodies.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh Revealed after the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, TB 8.34)

The tomb is the person’s own body, and the souls in it are the ones quickened. Bahá’u’lláh also describes the moment of rising in a Gleanings passage, with three outcomes:

“Say: The shout hath been raised, and the people have come forth from their graves, and arising, are gazing around them. Some have made haste to attain the court of the God of Mercy, others have fallen down on their faces in the fire of Hell, while still others are lost in bewilderment.”

(Gleanings XVII.4)

When the people rise, they do not all go the same way. Some hasten to God, some fall, and some do not know what to do. Another passage in the Gleanings says that the Day brings fear to the unprepared:

“This is the Day on which all eyes shall stare up with terror, the Day in which the hearts of them that dwell on earth shall tremble, save them whom thy Lord, the All-Knowing, the All-Wise, pleaseth to deliver.”

(Gleanings XVIII.3)

The terror is stated as a fact of the Day, together with an exception: those whom God delivers. A third passage, from Section CVI, is an invitation to the dead:

“Say: O ye who are as dead! The Hand of Divine bounty proffereth unto you the Water of Life. Hasten and drink your fill. Whoso hath been reborn in this Day, shall never die; whoso remaineth dead, shall never live.”

(Gleanings CVI.3)

In the Gleanings the call comes directly after Bahá’u’lláh asks His hearers to lift their voices “that haply they that are fast asleep may be awakened.” The two final clauses state what is at stake: the reborn do not die, and those who stay dead do not live. The condition is not described as punishment. It is the result of not taking what is offered.

Resurrection for every created thing

In a Tablet that warns against behaving like the people of the Bayán, Bahá’u’lláh adds a clarification about what resurrection includes:

“Know thou moreover that the former Manifestation affirmed that the return and rising of the spirits would occur on the Day of Resurrection, while in truth there is a return and resurrection for every created thing.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh Revealed after the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, TB 12.18)

The earlier teaching limited the rising to spirits. Bahá’u’lláh says that resurrection applies to every created thing. The Báb also links resurrection with perfection in every thing (SWB 11.75). Bahá’u’lláh’s statement widens the subject from souls to the whole of creation.

The believers become Isráfíl

The trumpet of Isráfíl is no longer one angel’s task. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá gives it to the believers. In the Tablet to the Bahá’ís of the United States and Canada, which calls them to travel and teach, He writes:

“I swear by Him, besides Whom there is none other God, that each one of you will become an Isráfíl of Life, and will blow the Breath of Life into the souls of others.”

(‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, Tablets of the Divine Plan, TDP 6.12)

Shoghi Effendi quotes this sentence in Citadel of Faith. A believer who has risen is therefore sent to raise others. Bahá’u’lláh’s words, quoted by Shoghi Effendi in The Advent of Divine Justice under the heading “The Paramount Importance of Teaching,” state the need and the means:

”Every body calleth aloud for a soul. Heavenly souls must needs quicken, with the breath of the Word of God, the dead bodies with a fresh spirit. Within every word a new spirit is hidden.“

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 114; source Tablet not identified)

The bodies are dead and need a soul. The means is the Word of God, spoken by believers. The text says a new spirit is hidden within every word, so the one who speaks it has something to give that does not come from the speaker.

How this facet connects

  • The Divine Springtime describes the same event as the earth coming back to life in spring. This page keeps to the figure of death and rising; that page gives the figure of the season, in which the quickening is a renewal of the whole earth.
  • The Creative Word treats the Word as the agent of creation. Here the Word is what raises the dead (ADJ 114 says a spirit is hidden within every word), and that page shows why a word can do it.
  • The Day Can Be Missed takes up those who sleep through the Day, whom ‘Abdu’l-Bahá calls “the heedless.” This page says that some rise, some fall and some are bewildered; that page asks why a person misses it.
  • The Day of God Himself gives the reason the Resurrection is so great: DOR 44.16 locates it in “the appearance of God in the Manifestation of His own Self.” That page sets out what that appearance is and how it is to be understood.
  • “Dearly Beloved Friends” traces the compilation in The Advent of Divine Justice from which ADJ 114 is taken, including the source of the passages Shoghi Effendi quotes.

What the sources do not settle

  • The Tablet from which ADJ 114 is taken is not identified in the corpus, so the occasion and the person addressed are unknown.
  • The Isráfíl quotation (DOR 10.21) is part of a narrative in which the Spirit speaks to Isráfíl. The text does not say whether the narrative is to be read as a vision, a symbol or an account of events; this page does not choose.
  • The Báb’s definition (SWB 11.74) limits the Day to the time of the Manifestation’s presence. Bahá’u’lláh’s passages speak of the Resurrection as having come, and of an arising at a moment. This page does not state how the two relate in time, and the sources quoted here do not state it either.
  • TB 12.18 says that every created thing has a resurrection but does not describe it; the passage goes on to say that He does not wish to mention anything not set forth in the Book. What this means for the things other than souls is not stated.
  • The words “shall never die” (Gleanings CVI.3) are not defined in the passage. The text does not say here whether it refers to the soul’s continued existence, which the Writings teach for all souls elsewhere, or to a condition of spiritual life.

For songwriting

  • ”Hasten and drink your fill“ (Gleanings CVI.3)
  • ”Whoso hath been reborn in this Day, shall never die“ (Gleanings CVI.3)
  • ”O ye who are as dead!“ (Gleanings CVI.3)
  • ”We have, then, called into being a new creation“ (Gleanings XIV.5)
  • ”the souls have been quickened in the tombs of their bodies“ (TB 8.34)
  • ”Darkness hath been chased away by the dawning light of the mercy of thy Lord“ (TB 8.34)
  • ”The shout hath been raised, and the people have come forth from their graves“ (Gleanings XVII.4)
  • ”The Ancient of Days is come in His great glory!“ (SLH 1.107)
  • ”The Hour that was concealed within the knowledge of God hath struck“ (SLH 1.107)
  • ”Every body calleth aloud for a soul“ (ADJ 114, Bahá’u’lláh)
  • ”Within every word a new spirit is hidden“ (ADJ 114, Bahá’u’lláh)
  • ”each one of you will become an Isráfíl of Life“ (TDP 6.12, ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá)
  • ”every mouldering bone may be quickened thereby!“ (DOR 10.21)

Concrete images

  • Graves, tombs and bones: people coming out of graves (Gleanings XVII.4), souls quickened in the tombs of their bodies (TB 8.34), a bone that is quickened (DOR 10.21).
  • Water of Life offered by a hand, and a person told to drink their fill (Gleanings CVI.3).
  • A trumpet sounded by Isráfíl (DOR 10.21), and a shrill voice lifted up between earth and heaven (SLH 1.107).
  • Sleep and drunkenness of the heedless (SWAB 4.2).
  • Darkness chased away, and a breeze that wafts (TB 8.34).
  • Vernal showers raining from the heaven of mercy (KI 152).
  • A body that calls aloud for a soul (ADJ 114).
  • People rising and gazing around them (Gleanings XVII.4).

A possible emotional arc

  1. Stillness and death: ”O ye who are as dead!“ The song begins with the condition of sleep and death (Gleanings CVI.3; SWAB 4.2).
  2. The shout and the hour: the hour has struck, and the Ancient of Days is come (SLH 1.107; DOR 10.21).
  3. Rising: people come out of their graves and look around (Gleanings XVII.4), the breeze wafts and the souls are quickened (TB 8.34).
  4. The offer: the Water of Life, hasten and drink (Gleanings CVI.3).
  5. Sending: those who have risen breathe into others, each an Isráfíl of Life (TDP 6.12; ADJ 114).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not present the Resurrection as the end of the world or a date on the calendar. The texts define it as the time of a Manifestation’s appearance (SWB 11.74; KI 152).
  • Do not present this Day as bodies leaving physical graves. The passages speak of souls quickened “in the tombs of their bodies” (TB 8.34) and of people “as dead” who are offered the Water of Life (Gleanings CVI.3).
  • Do not present Bahá’u’lláh as the Essence of God. The texts say “the appearance of God in the Manifestation of His own Self” (DOR 44.16); the facet on the Day of God Himself gives the limits.
  • Do not say that everyone rises to the same thing. The texts describe three outcomes (Gleanings XVII.4) and a terror from which only some are delivered (Gleanings XVIII.3).
  • Do not attribute the Isráfíl-of-Life promise (TDP 6.12) to Bahá’u’lláh or Shoghi Effendi. It is ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s, quoted by Shoghi Effendi. Do not attribute ADJ 114 to Shoghi Effendi; it is Bahá’u’lláh’s, in a Tablet not identified.
