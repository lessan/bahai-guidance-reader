The Stages to the Most Great Peace: What the Writings and Shoghi Effendi Foretell

Last updated 13 October 2026

What this page covers

Bahá’u’lláh named two peaces. Shoghi Effendi, in letters written between 1931 and 1954, arranged them with the rest of the future into an order of stages. This page follows that order and quotes the passages that state it:

  1. The present upheaval, which Shoghi Effendi describes as two processes at once: a breaking down of the old order and a building up of the new.
  2. The unity of nations, a growing recognition among peoples that they belong to one human family.
  3. The Lesser Peace, a political unification that the governments of the world arrive at themselves.
  4. The spiritualisation of that unity, which Shoghi Effendi says will follow when the world recognises the Faith of Bahá’u’lláh.
  5. The Most Great Peace, and with it the Bahá’í World Commonwealth.
  6. The Golden Age and the world civilisation that Shoghi Effendi calls the child of that Peace.

Between these sit the descriptions that give the stages their content: Shoghi Effendi’s account of a world legislature, executive, tribunal, language, script, currency and “world metropolis”, and his statements about how the world arrives there, which include catastrophe.

The page covers four things in turn. First, the root passages in the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. Second, Shoghi Effendi’s statement of the sequence. Third, his description of the future world. Fourth, his account of how it comes, and what the Universal House of Justice has said since about the stages and their timing. A last section gathers the material in a form a songwriter can use.

Two limits apply throughout. The texts fix the order of the stages and do not date them. And where this page describes what has been fulfilled, it reports only what an authoritative text says; its own observations are marked as such.

The page does not repeat what is already in the knowledge base. The concept page MostGreatAndLesserPeace gives the doctrine in outline. The Promise of World Peace is treated at length elsewhere, and auxiliary-language-and-peace treats the seventh issue in that statement. This page is about the sequence and the picture of the finished world.

All quotations are copied from the texts held in the knowledge base (`sources/reference_library/`). Where a passage is quoted second-hand, the page says where.

The root passages

Bahá’u’lláh: two peaces, offered to the rulers

The sentence that fixes the vocabulary is in a passage Shoghi Effendi attributes to Bahá’u’lláh’s Tablet to Queen Victoria and quotes in God Passes By and The Promised Day Is Come. The same words appear in Gleanings from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh, section CXIX, where no addressee is named. Bahá’u’lláh is addressing the kings of the earth:

“Now that ye have refused the Most Great Peace, hold ye fast unto this, the Lesser Peace, that haply ye may in some degree better your own condition and that of your dependents.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, CXIX

The next two paragraphs say what the Lesser Peace consists of:

“O rulers of the earth! Be reconciled among yourselves, that ye may need no more armaments save in a measure to safeguard your territories and dominions. Beware lest ye disregard the counsel of the All-Knowing, the Faithful.”

“Be united, O kings of the earth, for thereby will the tempest of discord be stilled amongst you, and your peoples find rest, if ye be of them that comprehend. Should anyone among you take up arms against another, rise ye all against him, for this is naught but manifest justice.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, CXIX

The Lesser Peace is therefore a defined arrangement: reconciliation among the powers, armaments reduced to what defence requires, and a collective response to any state that attacks another. In The Promised Day Is Come Shoghi Effendi introduces the passage by saying Bahá’u’lláh was “summoning them to cleave to the Lesser Peace, as distinct from that Most Great Peace which those who are fully conscious of the power of His Revelation and avowedly profess the tenets of His Faith can alone proclaim and must eventually establish.”

The Lesser Peace appears again in other Tablets. In the Bishárát (Glad-Tidings), it is the sixth glad-tiding:

“The sixth Glad-Tidings is the establishment of the Lesser Peace, details of which have formerly been revealed from Our Most Exalted Pen. Great is the blessedness of him who upholdeth it and observeth whatsoever hath been ordained by God, the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Bishárát, in Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh

In the Lawḥ-i-Dunyá (Tablet of the World) he summarises the principles revealed in the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, which he calls the Crimson Book, and places the Lesser Peace first:

“First: It is incumbent upon the ministers of the House of Justice to promote the Lesser Peace so that the people of the earth may be relieved from the burden of exorbitant expenditures. This matter is imperative and absolutely essential, inasmuch as hostilities and conflict lie at the root of affliction and calamity.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Lawḥ-i-Dunyá, in Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh

And in the ninth leaf of the Words of Paradise:

“The purging of such deeply-rooted and overwhelming corruptions cannot be effected unless the peoples of the world unite in pursuit of one common aim and embrace one universal faith. Incline your ears unto the Call of this Wronged One and adhere firmly to the Lesser Peace.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Words of Paradise (Kalimát-i-Firdawsíyyih), ninth leaf, in Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh

The second of these calls the Lesser Peace something the ministers of the House of Justice are to promote. The third connects it to a larger goal: the purging of corruption requires the peoples of the world to unite and to “embrace one universal faith.” The Lesser Peace is a step on the way to that.

The Most Great Peace has its own passages. In the second Ishráq of the Ishráqát (Splendors), Bahá’u’lláh says:

“We have enjoined upon all mankind to establish the Most Great Peace—the surest of all means for the protection of humanity. The sovereigns of the world should, with one accord, hold fast thereunto, for this is the supreme instrument that can ensure the security and welfare of all peoples and nations.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Ishráqát, second Ishráq, in Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh

The sovereigns are addressed here too, and the Most Great Peace is enjoined on “all mankind.” Shoghi Effendi’s distinction, quoted below, concerns who can bring the Most Great Peace about and on what basis. It does not limit who is called to it.

The assembly of the world’s rulers

Bahá’u’lláh describes the means by which the peace is to be built:

“The time must come when the imperative necessity for the holding of a vast, an all-embracing assemblage of men will be universally realized. The rulers and kings of the earth must needs attend it, and, participating in its deliberations, must consider such ways and means as will lay the foundations of the world’s Great Peace amongst men. Such a peace demandeth that the Great Powers should resolve, for the sake of the tranquillity of the peoples of the earth, to be fully reconciled among themselves. Should any king take up arms against another, all should unitedly arise and prevent him. If this be done, the nations of the world will no longer require any armaments, except for the purpose of preserving the security of their realms and of maintaining internal order within their territories.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, CXVII

The same section continues with a promise about language and a statement of the oneness of the earth:

“The day is approaching when all the peoples of the world will have adopted one universal language and one common script. When this is achieved, to whatsoever city a man may journey, it shall be as if he were entering his own home.”

“The earth is but one country, and mankind its citizens.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, CXVII

What Bahá’u’lláh told Edward Granville Browne

In April 1890 the Cambridge orientalist Edward Granville Browne spent five days at Bahjí and met Bahá’u’lláh four times. Browne wrote down what he heard. This is a visitor’s record, not a Tablet. It is quoted by Shoghi Effendi in God Passes By and The Promised Day Is Come, and by the Universal House of Justice in the closing line of The Promise of World Peace. The wording below is Browne’s, from his A Traveller’s Narrative, as reproduced in the 1992 introduction to Bahá’u’lláh issued by the Bahá’í International Community:

“That all nations should become one in faith and all men as brothers; that the bonds of affection and unity between the sons of men should be strengthened; that diversity of religion should cease, and differences of race be annulled—what harm is there in this?…Yet so it shall be; these fruitless strifes, these ruinous wars shall pass away, and the ‘Most great Peace’ shall come…” — Bahá’u’lláh to E. G. Browne, 1890, as recorded in Browne, A Traveller’s Narrative (New York: Bahá’í Publishing Committee, 1930), pp. xxxix–xl

Shoghi Effendi gives the sentence in a slightly different form: “these fruitless strifes, these ruinous wars shall pass away and the ‘Most Great Peace’ shall come” (God Passes By). In The Promised Day Is Come he adds a further sentence from the same interview: “These strifes and this bloodshed and discord must cease, and all men be as one kindred and one family.”

Bahá’u’lláh on the order that will replace the present one

Bahá’u’lláh says twice that the present order will be replaced:

“Soon will the present-day order be rolled up, and a new one spread out in its stead. Verily, thy Lord speaketh the truth, and is the Knower of things unseen.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, IV

“He Who is your Lord, the All-Merciful, cherisheth in His heart the desire of beholding the entire human race as one soul and one body. Haste ye to win your share of God’s good grace and mercy in this Day that eclipseth all other created Days.” — Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, CVII

And in section CXII he states the unity he has raised: “The tabernacle of unity hath been raised; regard ye not one another as strangers. Ye are the fruits of one tree, and the leaves of one branch.”

‘Abdu’l-Bahá: the treaty, and the seven candles

‘Abdu’l-Bahá describes in The Secret of Divine Civilization how the rulers would proceed. Shoghi Effendi quotes the passage in The Goal of a New World Order, and the Universal House of Justice quotes it in The Promise of World Peace:

“True civilization will unfurl its banner in the midmost heart of the world whenever a certain number of its distinguished and high-minded sovereigns—the shining exemplars of devotion and determination—shall, for the good and happiness of all mankind, arise, with firm resolve and clear vision, to establish the Cause of Universal Peace. They must make the Cause of Peace the object of general consultation, and seek by every means in their power to establish a Union of the nations of the world. They must conclude a binding treaty and establish a covenant, the provisions of which shall be sound, inviolable and definite. They must proclaim it to all the world and obtain for it the sanction of all the human race.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, The Secret of Divine Civilization

He then answers those who think this impossible:

“Endeavor, ceaseless endeavor, is required. Nothing short of an indomitable determination can possibly achieve it. Many a cause which past ages have regarded as purely visionary, yet in this day has become most easy and practicable. Why should this most great and lofty Cause—the daystar of the firmament of true civilization and the cause of the glory, the advancement, the well-being and the success of all humanity—be regarded as impossible of achievement? Surely the day will come when its beauteous light shall shed illumination upon the assemblage of man.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, The Secret of Divine Civilization

In a Tablet printed in Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá (no. 15), and identified in Century of Light as addressed to Jane Elizabeth Whyte, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá lists seven forms of unity as seven candles, and gives the first five an order:

“Behold how its light is now dawning upon the world’s darkened horizon. The first candle is unity in the political realm, the early glimmerings of which can now be discerned. The second candle is unity of thought in world undertakings, the consummation of which will erelong be witnessed. The third candle is unity in freedom which will surely come to pass. The fourth candle is unity in religion which is the cornerstone of the foundation itself, and which, by the power of God, will be revealed in all its splendour. The fifth candle is the unity of nations—a unity which in this century will be securely established, causing all the peoples of the world to regard themselves as citizens of one common fatherland. The sixth candle is unity of races, making of all that dwell on earth peoples and kindreds of one race. The seventh candle is unity of language, i.e., the choice of a universal tongue in which all peoples will be instructed and converse. Each and every one of these will inevitably come to pass, inasmuch as the power of the Kingdom of God will aid and assist in their realization.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, no. 15

Two of these candles matter for the stages: the first, “unity in the political realm,” and the fifth, “the unity of nations.” They are different, and a letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi explained the difference in 1936:

“This unity should be clearly distinguished from the ‘unity of nations’. The first is a unity which politically independent and sovereign states achieve among themselves; while the second is one which is brought about between nations, the difference between a state and a nation being that the former, as you know, is a political entity without necessarily being homogeneous in race, whereas the second implies national as well as political homogeneity.” — Letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi, 26 July 1936, in the compilation Peace (Research Department, 1985)

The “unity of nations” in this sense is a unity of peoples. The first candle is a unity of states, which is the kind of unity the Lesser Peace is described as (see the letters of the Universal House of Justice below). The Universal House of Justice draws on this distinction in 2001.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá also gave a large title to events of his own lifetime. Shoghi Effendi writes that the League of Nations and the work of President Woodrow Wilson were acclaimed by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s pen as “signalizing the dawn of the Most Great Peace” (Citadel of Faith, 5 June 1947; also The Advent of Divine Justice, 25 December 1938). The 2001 letter from the Universal House of Justice cites the phrase as an example of the orientation to process found throughout the writings on peace: the dawn is not the day.

Shoghi Effendi’s sequence

The statement in The Promised Day Is Come

The clearest single statement of the order is in the section “World Commonwealth” of The Promised Day Is Come, written on 28 March 1941, during the Second World War:

“To the general character, the implications and features of this world commonwealth, destined to emerge, sooner or later, out of the carnage, agony, and havoc of this great world convulsion, I have already referred in my previous communications. Suffice it to say that this consummation will, by its very nature, be a gradual process, and must, as Bahá’u’lláh has Himself anticipated, lead at first to the establishment of that Lesser Peace which the nations of the earth, as yet unconscious of His Revelation and yet unwittingly enforcing the general principles which He has enunciated, will themselves establish. This momentous and historic step, involving the reconstruction of mankind, as the result of the universal recognition of its oneness and wholeness, will bring in its wake the spiritualization of the masses, consequent to the recognition of the character, and the acknowledgment of the claims, of the Faith of Bahá’u’lláh—the essential condition to that ultimate fusion of all races, creeds, classes, and nations which must signalize the emergence of His New World Order.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come (28 March 1941), “World Commonwealth”

This one paragraph names four of the stages. The commonwealth is a gradual process. It leads at first to the Lesser Peace, which the nations establish for themselves while “as yet unconscious of His Revelation.” That step brings in its wake the spiritualisation of the masses, and the spiritualisation depends on recognising the Faith. The recognition is “the essential condition” of the fusion of races, creeds, classes and nations.

The next paragraph in the same section describes what follows:

“Then will the coming of age of the entire human race be proclaimed and celebrated by all the peoples and nations of the earth. Then will the banner of the Most Great Peace be hoisted. Then will the worldwide sovereignty of Bahá’u’lláh—the Establisher of the Kingdom of the Father foretold by the Son, and anticipated by the Prophets of God before Him and after Him—be recognized, acclaimed, and firmly established. Then will a world civilization be born, flourish, and perpetuate itself, a civilization with a fullness of life such as the world has never seen nor can as yet conceive. Then will the Everlasting Covenant be fulfilled in its completeness. Then will the promise enshrined in all the Books of God be redeemed, and all the prophecies uttered by the Prophets of old come to pass, and the vision of seers and poets be realized. Then will the planet, galvanized through the universal belief of its dwellers in one God, and their allegiance to one common Revelation, mirror, within the limitations imposed upon it, the effulgent glories of the sovereignty of Bahá’u’lláh, shining in the plenitude of its splendor in the Abhá Paradise, and be made the footstool of His Throne on high, and acclaimed as the earthly heaven, capable of fulfilling that ineffable destiny fixed for it, from time immemorial, by the love and wisdom of its Creator.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come (28 March 1941), “World Commonwealth”

The word “Then” opens each sentence, so the paragraph reads as a sequence of events that follow the spiritualisation of the masses: the coming of age proclaimed, the banner hoisted, the sovereignty of Bahá’u’lláh recognised, the civilisation born, the Covenant fulfilled, the planet transformed.

Shoghi Effendi follows it with a statement of what he does not claim to know:

“Not ours, puny mortals that we are, to attempt, at so critical a stage in the long and checkered history of mankind, to arrive at a precise and satisfactory understanding of the steps which must successively lead a bleeding humanity, wretchedly oblivious of its God, and careless of Bahá’u’lláh, from its calvary to its ultimate resurrection.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come, “World Commonwealth”

The ceiling of politics and the basis of the Most Great Peace

In The Unfoldment of World Civilization (11 March 1936) Shoghi Effendi says what political effort can and cannot reach:

“No machinery falling short of the standard inculcated by the Bahá’í Revelation, and at variance with the sublime pattern ordained in His teachings, which the collective efforts of mankind may yet devise can ever hope to achieve anything above or beyond that ‘Lesser Peace’ to which the Author of our Faith has Himself alluded in His writings.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization (11 March 1936)

And of the Most Great Peace:

“The Most Great Peace, on the other hand, as conceived by Bahá’u’lláh—a peace that must inevitably follow as the practical consequence of the spiritualization of the world and the fusion of all its races, creeds, classes and nations—can rest on no other basis, and can be preserved through no other agency, except the divinely appointed ordinances that are implicit in the World Order that stands associated with His Holy Name.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization

The two statements together mark the difference between the stages. The Lesser Peace is what human effort can reach by itself. The Most Great Peace follows from the spiritualisation of the world and rests on the ordinances of Bahá’u’lláh’s World Order.

Where Justice and Civilisation come in

In The Promised Day Is Come, Shoghi Effendi adds a chain of three terms. He first quotes Bahá’u’lláh: “These great oppressions,” He, moreover, foreshadowing humanity’s golden age, has written, “are preparing it for the advent of the Most Great Justice.” Then:

“This Most Great Justice is indeed the Justice upon which the structure of the Most Great Peace can alone, and must eventually, rest, while the Most Great Peace will, in turn, usher in that Most Great, that World Civilization which shall remain forever associated with Him Who beareth the Most Great Name.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come

The order is: oppressions, then the Most Great Justice, then the Most Great Peace, then the World Civilisation.

The whole arc in one sentence

On 5 June 1947, in a message to the Bahá’ís of the West, Shoghi Effendi set out what the Formative Age and the period after it would see. The sentence is long, and its clauses are in order:

“During this Formative Age of the Faith, and in the course of present and succeeding epochs, the last and crowning stage in the erection of the framework of the Administrative Order of the Faith of Bahá’u’lláh—the election of the Universal House of Justice—will have been completed, the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, the Mother-Book of His Revelation, will have been codified and its laws promulgated, the Lesser Peace will have been established, the unity of mankind will have been achieved and its maturity attained, the Plan conceived by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá will have been executed, the emancipation of the Faith from the fetters of religious orthodoxy will have been effected, and its independent religious status will have been universally recognized, whilst in the course of the Golden Age, destined to consummate the Dispensation itself, the banner of the Most Great Peace, promised by its Author, will have been unfurled, the World Bahá’í Commonwealth will have emerged in the plenitude of its power and splendor, and the birth and efflorescence of a world civilization, the child of that Peace, will have conferred its inestimable blessings upon all mankind.” — Shoghi Effendi, message of 5 June 1947, in Citadel of Faith

The Lesser Peace belongs to the Formative Age. The banner of the Most Great Peace, the World Bahá’í Commonwealth and the world civilisation belong to the Golden Age. The sentence puts the Lesser Peace, the unity of mankind and the attainment of its “maturity” in the earlier period.

In the same message he describes the processes outside the Bahá’í community that lead to the Lesser Peace. He starts from the First World War, passes through the League of Nations, the Atlantic Charter and the founding of the United Nations, and states where they lead:

“It must, however long and tortuous the way, lead, through a series of victories and reverses, to the political unification of the Eastern and Western Hemispheres, to the emergence of a world government and the establishment of the Lesser Peace, as foretold by Bahá’u’lláh and foreshadowed by the Prophet Isaiah. It must, in the end, culminate in the unfurling of the banner of the Most Great Peace, in the Golden Age of the Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh.” — Shoghi Effendi, message of 5 June 1947, “The Workings of Two Simultaneous Processes,” in Citadel of Faith

Two phrases here give the stages a texture: “however long and tortuous the way” and “a series of victories and reverses.” The 2001 letter of the Universal House of Justice, quoted below, picks up the same theme.

Three developments that synchronise

On 27 November 1954 Shoghi Effendi wrote to the National Spiritual Assembly of the United States, in a message about the Bahá’í buildings on Mount Carmel. He described the whole process as one that would:

“synchronize with two no less significant developments—the establishment of the Lesser Peace and the evolution of Bahá’í national and local institutions—the one outside and the other within the Bahá’í world—will attain its final consummation, in the Golden Age of the Faith, through the raising of the standard of the Most Great Peace, and the emergence, in the plenitude of its power and glory, of the focal Centre of the agencies constituting the World Order of Bahá’u’lláh.” — Shoghi Effendi, 27 November 1954, in Messages to the Bahá’í World, 1950–1957, pp. 74–75; quoted in the compilation Peace (Research Department, 1985)

Two things are said to run together, one outside the Bahá’í world and one inside it, and they culminate together in the Golden Age. The Universal House of Justice has pointed out that Shoghi Effendi is describing “not events, but processes or developments” and does not say that one depends on the other (letter of 14 December 1987, quoted in the 2001 memorandum below).

The three ages

God Passes By gives the largest framework. It divides the Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh into three ages:

“The Formative Period, the Iron Age, of that Dispensation was now beginning, the Age in which the institutions, local, national and international, of the Faith of Bahá’u’lláh were to take shape, develop and become fully consolidated, in anticipation of the third, the last, the Golden Age destined to witness the emergence of a world-embracing Order enshrining the ultimate fruit of God’s latest Revelation to mankind, a fruit whose maturity must signalize the establishment of a world civilization and the formal inauguration of the Kingdom of the Father upon earth as promised by Jesus Christ Himself.” — Shoghi Effendi, God Passes By

The Heroic or Apostolic Age came first; the Formative Age, the present one, is the second; the Golden Age is the third.

Letters on his behalf

Letters written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi by his secretaries answered questions about the stages. Two are relevant here. On 14 March 1939, in reply to a question about how the two peaces would be established after the “coming World War,” the secretary wrote:

“Your view that the Lesser Peace will come about through the political efforts of the states and nations of the world, and independently of any direct Bahá’í plan or effort, and the Most Great Peace be established through the instrumentality of the believers, and by the direct operation of the laws and principles revealed by Bahá’u’lláh and the functioning of the Universal House of Justice as the supreme organ of the Bahá’í superstate—your view on this subject is quite correct and in full accord with the pronouncements of the Guardian as embodied in ‘The Unfoldment of World Civilization’.” — Letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi, 14 March 1939, in the compilation Peace

And in 1946, in reply to a question about timing:

“All we know is that the Lesser and the Most Great Peace will come—their exact dates we do not know. The same is true as regards the possibility of a future war; we cannot state dogmatically it will or will not take place—all we know is that mankind must suffer and be punished sufficiently to make it turn to God.” — Letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 1946, quoted by the Universal House of Justice, 29 July 1974

The future world, as Shoghi Effendi describes it

The commonwealth in its parts

The fullest description is in The Unfoldment of World Civilization, in the section “World Unity the Goal.” It begins:

“The unity of the human race, as envisaged by Bahá’u’lláh, implies the establishment of a world commonwealth in which all nations, races, creeds and classes are closely and permanently united, and in which the autonomy of its state members and the personal freedom and initiative of the individuals that compose them are definitely and completely safeguarded.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization

It continues by listing the institutions of that commonwealth one by one:

“This commonwealth must, as far as we can visualize it, consist of a world legislature, whose members will, as the trustees of the whole of mankind, ultimately control the entire resources of all the component nations, and will enact such laws as shall be required to regulate the life, satisfy the needs and adjust the relationships of all races and peoples. A world executive, backed by an international Force, will carry out the decisions arrived at, and apply the laws enacted by, this world legislature, and will safeguard the organic unity of the whole commonwealth. A world tribunal will adjudicate and deliver its compulsory and final verdict in all and any disputes that may arise between the various elements constituting this universal system. A mechanism of world inter-communication will be devised, embracing the whole planet, freed from national hindrances and restrictions, and functioning with marvellous swiftness and perfect regularity. A world metropolis will act as the nerve center of a world civilization, the focus towards which the unifying forces of life will converge and from which its energizing influences will radiate. A world language will either be invented or chosen from among the existing languages and will be taught in the schools of all the federated nations as an auxiliary to their mother tongue. A world script, a world literature, a uniform and universal system of currency, of weights and measures, will simplify and facilitate intercourse and understanding among the nations and races of mankind.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization

The paragraph goes on to describe science and religion, and the press and the economy:

“In such a world society, science and religion, the two most potent forces in human life, will be reconciled, will coöperate, and will harmoniously develop. The press will, under such a system, while giving full scope to the expression of the diversified views and convictions of mankind, cease to be mischievously manipulated by vested interests, whether private or public, and will be liberated from the influence of contending governments and peoples. The economic resources of the world will be organized, its sources of raw materials will be tapped and fully utilized, its markets will be coördinated and developed, and the distribution of its products will be equitably regulated.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization

The next paragraph states what ends and what the freed energy goes to:

“National rivalries, hatreds, and intrigues will cease, and racial animosity and prejudice will be replaced by racial amity, understanding and coöperation. The causes of religious strife will be permanently removed, economic barriers and restrictions will be completely abolished, and the inordinate distinction between classes will be obliterated. Destitution on the one hand, and gross accumulation of ownership on the other, will disappear. The enormous energy dissipated and wasted on war, whether economic or political, will be consecrated to such ends as will extend the range of human inventions and technical development, to the increase of the productivity of mankind, to the extermination of disease, to the extension of scientific research, to the raising of the standard of physical health, to the sharpening and refinement of the human brain, to the exploitation of the unused and unsuspected resources of the planet, to the prolongation of human life, and to the furtherance of any other agency that can stimulate the intellectual, the moral, and spiritual life of the entire human race.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization

And he sums it up:

“A world federal system, ruling the whole earth and exercising unchallengeable authority over its unimaginably vast resources, blending and embodying the ideals of both the East and the West, liberated from the curse of war and its miseries, and bent on the exploitation of all the available sources of energy on the surface of the planet, a system in which Force is made the servant of Justice, whose life is sustained by its universal recognition of one God and by its allegiance to one common Revelation—such is the goal towards which humanity, impelled by the unifying forces of life, is moving.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization

Three points about this description. First, it keeps the member states: their “autonomy” and the “personal freedom and initiative” of individuals are “definitely and completely safeguarded.” Second, it is presented with a limit on what the author can see: “as far as we can visualize it.” Third, the last sentence names a religious basis (“universal recognition of one God,” “allegiance to one common Revelation”), which separates this description from a purely political federation.

The world super-state

Five years earlier, in The Goal of a New World Order (28 November 1931), Shoghi Effendi gave an account of the same institutions in connection with the passage from Bahá’u’lláh about the rulers’ collective security. He begins with a question:

“What else could these weighty words signify if they did not point to the inevitable curtailment of unfettered national sovereignty as an indispensable preliminary to the formation of the future Commonwealth of all the nations of the world? Some form of a world super-state must needs be evolved, in whose favor all the nations of the world will have willingly ceded every claim to make war, certain rights to impose taxation and all rights to maintain armaments, except for purposes of maintaining internal order within their respective dominions. Such a state will have to include within its orbit an international executive adequate to enforce supreme and unchallengeable authority on every recalcitrant member of the commonwealth; a world parliament whose members shall be elected by the people in their respective countries and whose election shall be confirmed by their respective governments; and a supreme tribunal whose judgment will have a binding effect even in such cases where the parties concerned did not voluntarily agree to submit their case to its consideration.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Goal of a New World Order (28 November 1931)

The passage continues with the world community this super-state serves:

“A world community in which all economic barriers will have been permanently demolished and the interdependence of Capital and Labor definitely recognized; in which the clamor of religious fanaticism and strife will have been forever stilled; in which the flame of racial animosity will have been finally extinguished; in which a single code of international law—the product of the considered judgment of the world’s federated representatives—shall have as its sanction the instant and coercive intervention of the combined forces of the federated units; and finally a world community in which the fury of a capricious and militant nationalism will have been transmuted into an abiding consciousness of world citizenship—such indeed, appears, in its broadest outline, the Order anticipated by Bahá’u’lláh, an Order that shall come to be regarded as the fairest fruit of a slowly maturing age.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Goal of a New World Order

The two descriptions differ in wording. The 1931 letter says “world parliament,” “international executive” and “supreme tribunal.” The 1936 letter says “world legislature,” “world executive” and “world tribunal,” and adds the communications system, the metropolis, the language, script, literature and currency. Both describe a government with legislative, executive and judicial parts.

Why the unity is called inevitable

In the same 1931 letter, Shoghi Effendi places this federation at the end of a sequence of ever-larger units:

“It represents the consummation of human evolution—an evolution that has had its earliest beginnings in the birth of family life, its subsequent development in the achievement of tribal solidarity, leading in turn to the constitution of the city-state, and expanding later into the institution of independent and sovereign nations.”

“The principle of the Oneness of Mankind, as proclaimed by Bahá’u’lláh, carries with it no more and no less than a solemn assertion that attainment to this final stage in this stupendous evolution is not only necessary but inevitable, that its realization is fast approaching, and that nothing short of a power that is born of God can succeed in establishing it.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Goal of a New World Order

In The Unfoldment of World Civilization he calls this the “coming of age of the entire human race” and the highest stage of organised life on the planet:

“The emergence of a world community, the consciousness of world citizenship, the founding of a world civilization and culture—all of which must synchronize with the initial stages in the unfoldment of the Golden Age of the Bahá’í Era—should, by their very nature, be regarded, as far as this planetary life is concerned, as the furthermost limits in the organization of human society, though man, as an individual, will, nay must indeed as a result of such a consummation, continue indefinitely to progress and develop.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization

The statement is bounded on both sides. The commonwealth is the furthest limit of how society can be organised on this planet. And individuals continue to progress without limit.

The Great Age to come

In The Promised Day Is Come he describes what that coming-of-age ends:

“The ages of its infancy and childhood are past, never again to return, while the Great Age, the consummation of all ages, which must signalize the coming of age of the entire human race, is yet to come. The convulsions of this transitional and most turbulent period in the annals of humanity are the essential prerequisites, and herald the inevitable approach, of that Age of Ages, ‘the time of the end,’ in which the folly and tumult of strife that has, since the dawn of history, blackened the annals of mankind, will have been finally transmuted into the wisdom and the tranquility of an undisturbed, a universal, and lasting peace, in which the discord and separation of the children of men will have given way to the worldwide reconciliation, and the complete unification of the divers elements that constitute human society.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come, “The Great Age to Come”

And in the same letter, on the extent of what lies ahead:

“God’s purpose is none other than to usher in, in ways He alone can bring about, and the full significance of which He alone can fathom, the Great, the Golden Age of a long-divided, a long-afflicted humanity. Its present state, indeed even its immediate future, is dark, distressingly dark. Its distant future, however, is radiant, gloriously radiant—so radiant that no eye can visualize it.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come, “God’s Purpose”

‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s picture, as Shoghi Effendi quotes it

In The Unfoldment of World Civilization Shoghi Effendi quotes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá on what “the hoisting of the Standard of God” would mean. He does not name the Tablet; the same words are quoted in The Promised Day Is Come. I could not trace the original Tablet in the texts held here, so the passage is cited through Shoghi Effendi:

“One of the great events, which is to occur in the Day of the manifestation of that incomparable Branch is the hoisting of the Standard of God among all nations. By this is meant that all nations and kindreds will be gathered together under the shadow of this Divine Banner, which is no other than the Lordly Branch itself, and will become a single nation. Religious and sectarian antagonism, the hostility of races and peoples, and differences among nations, will be eliminated. All men will adhere to one religion, will have one common faith, will be blended into one race and become a single people. All will dwell in one common fatherland, which is the planet itself.” — ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted by Shoghi Effendi in The Unfoldment of World Civilization

The closing words

The Unfoldment of World Civilization ends with a passage from Bahá’u’lláh, which Shoghi Effendi introduces as “written in anticipation of the golden age of His Faith—the age in which the face of the earth, from pole to pole, will mirror the ineffable splendors of the Abhá Paradise”:

“This is the Day whereon naught can be seen except the splendors of the Light that shineth from the face of thy Lord, the Gracious, the Most Bountiful. Verily, We have caused every soul to expire by virtue of Our irresistible and all-subduing sovereignty. We have then called into being a new creation, as a token of Our grace unto men. I am, verily, the All-Bountiful, the Ancient of Days. This is the Day whereon the unseen world crieth out: ‘Great is thy blessedness, O earth, for thou hast been made the foot-stool of thy God, and been chosen as the seat of His mighty throne!’” — Bahá’u’lláh, quoted by Shoghi Effendi at the end of The Unfoldment of World Civilization

How it comes: crisis, and two processes

Catastrophe before the settlement

Shoghi Effendi does not describe the arrival of the Lesser Peace as a smooth progression. He says repeatedly that suffering will come first, and that suffering is the means by which the world is joined together. In 1931:

“That the forces of a world catastrophe can alone precipitate such a new phase of human thought is, alas, becoming increasingly apparent. That nothing short of the fire of a severe ordeal, unparalleled in its intensity, can fuse and weld the discordant entities that constitute the elements of present-day civilization, into the integral components of the world commonwealth of the future, is a truth which future events will increasingly demonstrate.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Goal of a New World Order

In 1936, using the image of birth:

“We stand on the threshold of an age whose convulsions proclaim alike the death-pangs of the old order and the birth-pangs of the new. Through the generating influence of the Faith announced by Bahá’u’lláh this New World Order may be said to have been conceived. We can, at the present moment, experience its stirrings in the womb of a travailing age—an age waiting for the appointed hour at which it can cast its burden and yield its fairest fruit.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization, “Pangs of Death and Birth”

In 1941, with the Second World War under way, he opens The Promised Day Is Come with a description of the tempest in progress:

“A tempest, unprecedented in its violence, unpredictable in its course, catastrophic in its immediate effects, unimaginably glorious in its ultimate consequences, is at present sweeping the face of the earth.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come

And of the same event, as a judgment with two purposes:

“It is at once a visitation from God and a cleansing process for all mankind. Its fires punish the perversity of the human race, and weld its component parts into one organic, indivisible, world-embracing community.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Promised Day Is Come, “This Judgment of God”

He also asks the question directly, in the section “Divine Retribution” of The Unfoldment of World Civilization:

“Must humanity, tormented as she now is, be afflicted with still severer tribulations ere their purifying influence can prepare her to enter the heavenly Kingdom destined to be established upon earth?” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization, “Divine Retribution”

His answer is that the convulsions are the turbulence of adolescence:

“The long ages of infancy and childhood, through which the human race had to pass, have receded into the background. Humanity is now experiencing the commotions invariably associated with the most turbulent stage of its evolution, the stage of adolescence, when the impetuosity of youth and its vehemence reach their climax, and must gradually be superseded by the calmness, the wisdom, and the maturity that characterize the stage of manhood.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization, “Divine Retribution”

In the 1936 letter he also wrote that “the darkest hour that must precede the dawn of the Golden Age of our Faith has not yet struck.” The sentence is dated: he wrote it in 1936, before the Second World War. This page does not use it to say where the world stands now.

The twofold process

The central passage on how the old and the new run side by side is in the section “Universal Fermentation”:

“A twofold process, however, can be distinguished, each tending, in its own way and with an accelerated momentum, to bring to a climax the forces that are transforming the face of our planet. The first is essentially an integrating process, while the second is fundamentally disruptive. The former, as it steadily evolves, unfolds a System which may well serve as a pattern for that world polity towards which a strangely-disordered world is continually advancing; while the latter, as its disintegrating influence deepens, tends to tear down, with increasing violence, the antiquated barriers that seek to block humanity’s progress towards its destined goal. The constructive process stands associated with the nascent Faith of Bahá’u’lláh, and is the harbinger of the New World Order that Faith must erelong establish. The destructive forces that characterize the other should be identified with a civilization that has refused to answer to the expectation of a new age, and is consequently falling into chaos and decline.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization, “Universal Fermentation”

There are two ways of dividing the world’s events here, and the Universal House of Justice uses both. The first is Shoghi Effendi’s: integrating and disintegrating. The second divides actors: the Bahá’í community builds the integrating system, and the rest of the world, mostly unwittingly, tears down the barriers. The 1967 letter below carries the second further.

The Administrative Order as pattern

On the integrating side, the Administrative Order is the pattern for the commonwealth. In The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh (8 February 1934), he writes that it will “assert its claim and demonstrate its capacity to be regarded not only as the nucleus but the very pattern of the New World Order destined to embrace in the fullness of time the whole of mankind.”

In the 1936 letter he is explicit about what remains unfinished:

“Though the Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh has been delivered, the World Order which such a Revelation must needs beget is as yet unborn. Though the Heroic Age of His Faith is passed, the creative energies which that Age has released have not as yet crystallized into that world society which, in the fullness of time, is to mirror forth the brightness of His glory. Though the framework of His Administrative Order has been erected, and the Formative Period of the Bahá’í Era has begun, yet the promised Kingdom into which the seed of His institutions must ripen remains as yet uninaugurated. Though His Voice has been raised, and the ensigns of His Faith have been lifted up in no less than forty countries of both the East and the West, yet the wholeness of the human race is as yet unrecognized, its unity unproclaimed, and the standard of its Most Great Peace unhoisted.” — Shoghi Effendi, The Unfoldment of World Civilization, “Pangs of Death and Birth”

The Lesser Peace is a stage in a longer process

In Citadel of Faith (8 November 1948) Shoghi Effendi writes of “the wings of yet another conflict, destined to contribute a distinct, and perhaps a decisive, share to the birth of the new Order which must signalize the advent of the Lesser Peace.” The qualification “perhaps” is his own, and it shows how he speaks of the near future: a conflict contributes to the birth of the order that brings the Lesser Peace, and he does not say how large the contribution is.

The Universal House of Justice on the stages

The Promise of World Peace (October 1985)

The Universal House of Justice addressed The Promise of World Peace “To the Peoples of the World.” It repeats the arc in terms that suit a general audience:

“World peace is not only possible but inevitable. It is the next stage in the evolution of this planet—in the words of one great thinker, ‘the planetization of mankind.’” — Universal House of Justice, The Promise of World Peace (October 1985)

It states the choice between two ways of arriving:

“Whether peace is to be reached only after unimaginable horrors precipitated by humanity’s stubborn clinging to old patterns of behavior, or is to be embraced now by an act of consultative will, is the choice before all who inhabit the earth.” — Universal House of Justice, The Promise of World Peace

It quotes Shoghi Effendi’s account of the world super-state, quoted above, and Bahá’u’lláh’s call for the “vast, an all-embracing assemblage of men.” It says of the present time:

“The Bahá’í Faith regards the current world confusion and calamitous condition in human affairs as a natural phase in an organic process leading ultimately and irresistibly to the unification of the human race in a single social order whose boundaries are those of the planet. The human race, as a distinct, organic unit, has passed through evolutionary stages analogous to the stages of infancy and childhood in the lives of its individual members, and is now in the culminating period of its turbulent adolescence approaching its long-awaited coming of age.” — Universal House of Justice, The Promise of World Peace

And it ends with a sentence from Bahá’u’lláh: “In the earnestness of our desire to impart to you the fervor of our hope and the depth of our confidence, we cite the emphatic promise of Bahá’u’lláh: ‘These fruitless strifes, these ruinous wars shall pass away, and the ‘Most Great Peace’ shall come.’”

Peace will come in stages (31 January 1985)

A letter written on behalf of the Universal House of Justice on 31 January 1985 to an individual believer states the order briefly:

“We know, however, that peace will come in stages. First, there will come the Lesser Peace, when the unity of nations will be achieved, then gradually the Most Great Peace—the spiritual as well as social and political unity of mankind, when the Bahá’í World Commonwealth, operating in strict accordance with the laws and ordinances of the Most Holy Book of the Bahá’í Revelation, will have been established through the efforts of the Bahá’ís.” — Letter written on behalf of the Universal House of Justice, 31 January 1985, in Messages from the Universal House of Justice 1963–1986, no. 422

It then describes how the Lesser Peace itself develops:

“The Lesser Peace itself will pass through stages; at the initial stage the governments will act entirely on their own without the conscious involvement of the Faith; later on, in God’s good time, the Faith will have a direct influence on it in ways indicated by Shoghi Effendi in his ‘The Goal of a New World Order.’” — Letter written on behalf of the Universal House of Justice, 31 January 1985

The Lesser Peace therefore has an early phase in which the Faith plays no part and a later phase in which it does. The same letter says that the Bahá’ís are not waiting: “by promoting the principles of the Faith, which are indispensable to the maintenance of peace, and by fashioning the instruments of the Bahá’í Administrative Order, which we are told by the beloved Guardian is the pattern for future society, the Bahá’ís are constantly engaged in laying the groundwork for a permanent peace, the Most Great Peace being their ultimate goal.”

A unified body that still needs a soul (8 December 1967)

A letter of the Universal House of Justice of 8 December 1967, quoted in the compilation Peace, restates Shoghi Effendi’s two processes with an image:

“We are told by Shoghi Effendi that two great processes are at work in the world: the great Plan of God, tumultuous in its progress, working through mankind as a whole, tearing down barriers to world unity and forging humankind into a unified body in the fires of suffering and experience. This process will produce, in God’s due time, the Lesser Peace, the political unification of the world. Mankind at that time can be likened to a body that is unified but without life. The second process, the task of breathing life into this unified body—of creating true unity and spirituality culminating in the Most Great Peace—is that of the Bahá’ís, who are labouring consciously, with detailed instructions and continuing Divine guidance, to erect the fabric of the Kingdom of God on earth, into which they call their fellow men, thus conferring upon them eternal life.” — Universal House of Justice, 8 December 1967, in Wellspring of Guidance, pp. 131–134; quoted in the compilation Peace

This is the clearest description in the texts of the relation between the Lesser Peace and the spiritualisation that follows. The first is the unification of the body, the second is the giving of life to it.

Timing: the year 2000 was not a fixed date

Some Bahá’ís expected the Lesser Peace by the end of the twentieth century. The expectation rested on statements by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá that the unity of nations would be “securely established” in that century. The Universal House of Justice corrected it twice: first in 1974, then in a letter of 19 April 2001 that enclosed a memorandum from its Research Department.

In 1974 it wrote that ‘Abdu’l-Bahá had linked the unity of nations to the twentieth century and added the 1946 statement from Shoghi Effendi’s secretary quoted above: “their exact dates we do not know.”

In 2001 it answered a believer who had asked about “the timing for the occurrence of the lesser peace, in light of your impression that the Bahá’í writings anticipate its coming before the conclusion of the twentieth century; i.e., the end of December 2000.” The reply:

“In reviewing this material it becomes apparent that there is nothing in the authoritative Bahá’í writings to indicate that the lesser peace would be established before the end of the twentieth century. However, there are clear statements affirming that the unity of nations would be, in the words of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, ‘securely established’ during the twentieth century.” — Universal House of Justice, 19 April 2001, in Messages from the Universal House of Justice 1986–2001, no. 356

That is the House of Justice’s own wording. It does not say the Lesser Peace came in the twentieth century or that it had not; it says the writings do not set the twentieth century as its date, while the unity of nations is a different matter. On that, the letter continues:

“The twentieth century has been distinguished by the emergence of the unity of nations, to which both Shoghi Effendi and the House of Justice have referred in the enclosed document. This movement, the evidence of which accumulates with each passing day, stands in sharp contrast to the nationalistic tenor of the nineteenth century, and is an evidence of the spirit of a new age moving in the hearts of humankind. Viewed from this perspective, there can be no doubt that the promise of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá has been fulfilled, and the unity of nations securely established in the century now concluded.” — Universal House of Justice, 19 April 2001

So the House of Justice says the unity of nations, as a recognition among peoples, was securely established in the twentieth century, and the Lesser Peace is a separate matter. The letter then gives the expectation for the stage ahead:

“The further expansion and strengthening of this consciousness of world solidarity in the years to come will have their effect in the political realm, and will influence the evolution towards world government. It should not be imagined that the processes now moving in the world will be free from challenge or difficulty. There may well be setbacks, and conflicts may erupt periodically, as humanity proceeds towards the emergence and consolidation of the lesser peace, giving rise in due course to the establishment of the most great peace.” — Universal House of Justice, 19 April 2001

The enclosed memorandum quotes earlier statements that bear on the shape of the Lesser Peace. One, from a letter of 29 July 1996, describes the point at which it will have clearly arrived:

“Clearly, the emergence of the lesser peace will be a gradual process and its various stages will no doubt witness tests and setbacks, as well as great advances. It will certainly include, however, a development of historic importance: that point at which the majority of the world’s nation-states formally commit themselves to a global order comprising institutions and laws, and equipped with the means by which collective decisions can be enforced. While we cannot at present foresee the precise form that this development will take, much less the point at which it will occur, we recognize that it is a feature of the process of the lesser peace.” — Letter written on behalf of the Universal House of Justice, 29 July 1996, quoted in the memorandum of 19 April 2001

The memorandum also records that the House of Justice knows “of nothing in the Writings of the Faith to indicate that the establishment of the Lesser Peace depends on the completion of the Arc on Mount Carmel” (Secretariat, 14 December 1987). It quotes Shoghi Effendi’s description of how long the Formative Age will last, from his Riḍván 105 B.E. (1948) message:

“Its duration is unknown and lies concealed within the treasury of God’s knowledge. Its termination will coincide with the establishment of this most perfect, this most mighty order throughout the East and the West, the resplendent emergence of organic unity among the component parts of human society, and the consolidation of the foundations of the lesser peace among the governments and nations of the world.” — Shoghi Effendi, Riḍván 105 B.E., quoted in the memorandum of 19 April 2001

And it records the Universal House of Justice’s Riḍván 1996 message, which keeps the picture of the road to peace uncertain in length:

“However short the path to peace, it will be tortuous; however promising the anticipated event that will set its course, it must mature through a long period of evolution, with its attendant tests, setbacks and conflicts, towards the moment when it will have emerged, under the direct influences of God’s Faith, as the most great peace.” — Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 1996, quoted in the memorandum of 19 April 2001

More recent statements

The House of Justice has continued to describe present events as part of the Lesser Peace’s development. Two recent letters to individuals both state the same expectation.

In a letter of 24 June 2021 on the conflict in the Holy Land:

“Ultimately, political affairs must be reordered and the lesser peace established, for humanity is a single entity and the best interests of any part are inextricably bound up with the best interests of the whole.” — Universal House of Justice, 24 June 2021, in Messages from the Universal House of Justice 2001–2022, no. 370

And in a letter of 1 April 2022 on the war in Ukraine:

“The ultimate resolution is the embrace of the healing remedy brought by the Divine Physician, first in the form of the Lesser Peace, as you have observed, and then through attainment of the Most Great Peace. Prayer and acts of service may seem like a feeble and slow response when weapons of destruction are fired; they are, however, the means by which, ultimately, those weapons will be silenced forever.” — Universal House of Justice, 1 April 2022, in Messages from the Universal House of Justice 2001–2022, no. 391

A Riḍván 2003 message, written after the outbreak of war in the Middle East, uses the same phrase in passing, speaking of “this latest conflict in the unfoldment of the Lesser Peace.” It urges the Bahá’ís to study three of Shoghi Effendi’s letters in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh: “The Goal of a New World Order,” “America and the Most Great Peace,” and “The Unfoldment of World Civilization.”

What the texts do not say

  • No date for either peace. Shoghi Effendi’s secretary wrote in 1946 that “their exact dates we do not know,” and the Universal House of Justice has repeated it. The 2001 letter says that nothing in the authoritative writings fixes the Lesser Peace before the end of the twentieth century.
  • No single moment. Shoghi Effendi calls the commonwealth “a gradual process.” The House of Justice calls the Lesser Peace “a gradual process” with “stages” and “setbacks.” The 1996 letter names one development that it says will certainly be part of it: the majority of nation-states formally committing to a global order with institutions, laws and the means to enforce collective decisions. It adds that the form and the time cannot yet be foreseen.
  • Not a Bahá’í project at first. The Lesser Peace will “initially be a political unity arrived at by decision of the governments of various nations; it will not be established by direct action of the Bahá’í community.” The Bahá’ís build the pattern and promote the principles in the meantime.
  • Whether the League of Nations or the United Nations is the Lesser Peace. Shoghi Effendi treats each as a landmark in the process, and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá said the League was “incapable of establishing universal peace” (as quoted in the 2001 memorandum). Neither is called the Lesser Peace.
  • The form of the Supreme Tribunal and other institutions. Shoghi Effendi writes “as far as we can visualize it,” and the House of Justice says it cannot “foresee the precise form.”

A correction for the knowledge base

While preparing this page, I found three points in the concept page MostGreatAndLesserPeace that do not match the source files and should be corrected there:

  1. It cites the Lesser Peace quotation “Now that ye have refused the Most Great Peace…” as Gleanings CXX. In the text held here it is section CXIX. Section CXVII has the “vast, an all-embracing assemblage” passage.
  2. It attributes “First: It is incumbent upon the ministers of the House of Justice to promote the Lesser Peace…” to the Lawḥ-i-Maqṣúd. In Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh it is in the Lawḥ-i-Dunyá (Tablet of the World).
  3. It places “Incline your ears unto the Call of this Wronged One and adhere firmly to the Lesser Peace” in the “seventh leaf” of the Words of Paradise. In the text held here it is in the ninth leaf.

Material for a song

The notes for the song itself are kept separately. The stages give a natural structure of six movements, in the order Shoghi Effendi gives them: the upheaval, the unity of nations, the Lesser Peace, the spiritualisation of the world, the Most Great Peace, the Golden Age.

Phrases that are short enough to sing and are taken directly from the texts include:

  • “Now that ye have refused the Most Great Peace, hold ye fast unto this, the Lesser Peace” (Bahá’u’lláh)
  • “Be united, O kings of the earth, for thereby will the tempest of discord be stilled amongst you” (Bahá’u’lláh)
  • “these fruitless strifes, these ruinous wars shall pass away” (Bahá’u’lláh, to Browne)
  • “Soon will the present-day order be rolled up, and a new one spread out in its stead” (Bahá’u’lláh)
  • “The first candle is unity in the political realm” through “The seventh candle is unity of language” (‘Abdu’l-Bahá)
  • “All will dwell in one common fatherland, which is the planet itself” (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted by Shoghi Effendi)
  • “A mechanism of world inter-communication will be devised, embracing the whole planet” (Shoghi Effendi)
  • “A world metropolis will act as the nerve center of a world civilization” (Shoghi Effendi)
  • “Force is made the servant of Justice” (Shoghi Effendi)
  • “the banner of the Most Great Peace, promised by its Author, will have been unfurled” (Shoghi Effendi)
  • “a body that is unified but without life” (Universal House of Justice, 1967)
  • “Its present state, indeed even its immediate future, is dark, distressingly dark. Its distant future, however, is radiant, gloriously radiant” (Shoghi Effendi)
