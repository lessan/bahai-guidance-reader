The Divine Springtime: the coming of the Manifestation as spring

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

Bahá’u’lláh and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá describe the coming of a Manifestation of God as a season: the spring that follows winter in the natural world. The comparison is stated directly and repeatedly, and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá explains what it covers: warmth, rain, new growth, and the revival of people who had been spiritually dead. This page sets out those texts. It is one facet of the series The greatness of this Day.

The facet is specific. Other pages treat the Day as a resurrection, as the word that creates, or as the grace poured out on all. This page keeps to the image of the seasons and to what the Writings say that image establishes about this Day.

The passages

Bahá’u’lláh: the Springtime has come

Bahá’u’lláh announces the Springtime in the opening line of a passage that Gleanings numbers XIV. In the file, the same section goes on to speak of Bahá’u’lláh directing His steps to the Riḍván (Gleanings XIV, source file line 156). The file does not give the passage a Tablet name. Bahá’u’lláh addresses the Pen:

THE Divine Springtime is come, O Most Exalted Pen, for the Festival of the All-Merciful is fast approaching. Bestir thyself, and magnify, before the entire creation, the name of God, and celebrate His praise, in such wise that all created things may be regenerated and made new. (Gleanings XIV.1)

The Springtime is stated as present (“is come”), and its effect is stated as a task: the Pen is to speak so that “all created things may be regenerated and made new.” The renewal is what the Pen’s praise of God is for.

In another passage Bahá’u’lláh writes to His followers about what they are being offered:

God grant that, in these days of heavenly delight, ye may not deprive yourselves of the sweet savors of the All-Glorious God, and may partake, in this spiritual Springtime, of the outpourings of His grace. (Gleanings XXXV.5)

The sentence is a hope that the believers will take part. The Springtime is available; it can still be missed. A longer passage, addressed to “O my servants,” adds who is giving what:

It behooveth you to refresh and revive your souls through the gracious favors which, in this Divine, this soul-stirring Springtime, are being showered upon you. The Daystar of His great glory hath shed its radiance upon you, and the clouds of His limitless grace have overshadowed you. How high the reward of him that hath not deprived himself of so great a bounty, nor failed to recognize the beauty of his Best-Beloved in this, His new attire. (Gleanings LXXXV.1)

Two things happen together here: the sun shines and the clouds give shade and rain. The text places the responsibility on the hearer: to revive the soul, and to recognize the Best-Beloved. The Manifestation is recognizable but arrives in “new attire,” so recognition is the thing at stake.

Bahá’u’lláh in prayer: the river and the fruit

Two prayers of Bahá’u’lláh in Prayers and Meditations use the same image. In the first He gives thanks for the Day itself:

I render Thee thanks that Thou hast made known unto Thy servants this Day whereon the river that is life indeed hath flowed forth from the fingers of Thy bounty, and the springtime of Thy revelation and Thy presence hath appeared through Thy manifestation unto all who are in Thy heaven and all who are on Thy earth. (Prayers and Meditations CLXXVI.1)

The springtime “hath appeared … unto all who are in Thy heaven and all who are on Thy earth.” The scope is universal, and it comes “through Thy manifestation.” The Springtime is the revelation of God appearing through a Manifestation.

In the second prayer, the Springtime is what the hearts of God’s loved ones rejoice in, and its result is named:

The hearts of Thy dear ones, however, will rejoice only at the Divine Springtime of Thy tender mercies, whereby the hearts are quickened, and the souls are renewed, and the trees of human existence bear their fruits. (Prayers and Meditations CXVII.1)

The sequence is quickened hearts, renewed souls, and then fruit. The Springtime is for the sake of what human beings bear, not only for the feeling of joy.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá: the seasons explained

‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s fullest account is in Some Answered Questions, in the chapter on the spiritual and natural cycles. He first describes the natural year, and then states that the spiritual cycles of the Prophets follow the same pattern:

The spiritual cycles associated with the Prophets of God proceed in like manner. That is, the day of the advent of the Holy Manifestations is the spiritual springtime. It is divine splendour and heavenly grace; it is the wafting of the breeze of life and the dawning of the Sun of Truth. Spirits are revived, hearts are refreshed, souls are refined, all existence is stirred into motion, and human realities are rejoiced and grow in attainments and perfections. Universal progress is achieved, the souls are gathered up, and the dead are quickened to life—for it is the day of resurrection, the season of commotion and ferment, the hour of joy and gladness, and the time of rapture and abandon. (Some Answered Questions 14.7)

This passage defines the term. The “day of the advent” of a Manifestation is the spiritual springtime, and the effects follow in order: revival of spirits, movement in all existence, growth in “attainments and perfections,” and “universal progress.” The last sentence ties the season to the resurrection (see The Resurrection). It also calls the season one of “commotion and ferment,” which is not the same as calm.

In a letter, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá applies the image to the present age in plain terms of renewal:

Now the new age is here and creation is reborn. Humanity hath taken on new life. The autumn hath gone by, and the reviving spring is here. All things are now made new. Arts and industries have been reborn, there are new discoveries in science, and there are new inventions; even the details of human affairs, such as dress and personal effects—even weapons—all these have likewise been renewed. The laws and procedures of every government have been revised. Renewal is the order of the day. (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá 205.3)

Here the renewal is observable and specific: arts, industry, science, government. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá does not limit the spring to the inner life.

Another passage, from the same collection, speaks of the Sun of Truth at the spring equinox and looks ahead to the consequence:

O ye friends of God! Because, in this most momentous of ages, the Sun of Truth hath risen at the highest point of the spring equinox, and cast its rays on every clime, it shall kindle such tremulous excitement, it shall release such vibrations in the world of being, it shall stimulate such growth and development, it shall stream out with such a glory of light, and clouds of grace shall pour down such plentiful waters, and fields and plains shall teem with such a galaxy of sweet-smelling plants and blooms, that this lowly earth will become the Abhá Kingdom, and this nether world the world above. Then will this fleck of dust be as the vast circle of the skies, this human place the palace-court of God, this spot of clay the dayspring of the endless favors of the Lord of Lords. (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá 102.2)

The verbs are in the future (“shall kindle,” “shall stream out”). The sun has already risen; the full growth that results is still being described as to come. The two tenses together show the shape of the Day: it has begun, and its fruit is not yet complete. The passage names a destination, that the earth will become the Abhá Kingdom, but gives no date for it.

A third passage is a short description of the season itself:

In flower-spangled meadows hath the divine springtime pitched its tents, and the spiritual are inhaling sweet scents from the Sheba of the spirit, carried their way by the east wind. (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá 206.6)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá in talks: Bahá’u’lláh renewed the springtime

Three passages below come from talks of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá recorded in The Promulgation of Universal Peace. They are notes of what He said, not texts He wrote. They are used here for their explicit statement that the same springtime returns, a point the written texts above imply but do not put in this form.

In a talk, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá first names the springtime as the appearance of the Manifestations and gives the example of Christ:

The appearances of the Manifestations of God are the divine springtime. When Christ appeared in this world, it was like the vernal bounty; the outpouring descended; the effulgences of the Merciful encircled all things; the human world found new life. (The Promulgation of Universal Peace 4.3)

In the next paragraph of the same talk He says what Bahá’u’lláh did:

Bahá’u’lláh has come into this world. He has renewed that springtime. The same fragrances are wafting; the same heat of the Sun is giving life; the same cloud is pouring its rain, and with our own eyes we see that the world of existence is advancing and progressing. The human world has found new life. (The Promulgation of Universal Peace 4.4)

The word “renewed” matters. Spring is not a new kind of event: it is the same season returning. Christ’s coming was a springtime, and Bahá’u’lláh “has renewed that springtime.” This agrees with the written statement in Some Answered Questions that every Manifestation’s advent is a spiritual springtime.

In another talk, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá names the present century as the spring season and includes material progress in it:

The springtime of God is at hand. This century is, verily, the spring season. The world of mind and kingdom of soul have become fresh and verdant by its bestowals. It has resuscitated the whole realm of existence. On one hand, the lights of reality are shining; on the other, the clouds of divine mercy are pouring down the fullness of heavenly bounty. Wonderful material progress is evident, and great spiritual discoveries are being made. Truly, this can be called the miracle of centuries, for it is replete with manifestations of the miraculous. (The Promulgation of Universal Peace 56.4)

This talk pairs “material progress” with “spiritual discoveries.” The written letter (Selections 205.3) makes the same pairing, which is why the point does not rest on the talk alone.

How this facet connects

  • The Resurrection. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s account of the spiritual springtime ends by calling it “the day of resurrection.” The seasons image supplies the visible process (rain, growth, the dead quickened); the Resurrection page treats the same event under the name the Scriptures give it, the resurrection.
  • The Creative Word. In the Gleanings passage that opens this page, the Pen is to praise God “in such wise that all created things may be regenerated and made new.” The Creative Word page treats the Word as the agent of that making; this page treats the season in which it happens.
  • Grace Poured Out on All. The clouds, rain and “outpourings” in these texts are the same grace from another angle. That page deals with who receives it and on what terms; this one describes the weather in which it falls.
  • The King of Days. The Springtime is a recurring season, renewed with each Manifestation. The King of Days page deals with what is unequalled about this Day among all the days; the two pages together state both what this Day shares with earlier ones and what it does not.
  • The Hour of Dawn. Dawn and spring both mark the start of a light and a warmth. That article takes dawn as the hour set for prayer; the two images are related in the Writings (the “Sun of Truth,” the “Daystar”) but are different images.

What the sources do not settle

  • The file does not name the Tablet for Gleanings XIV. The series task describes several Gleanings spring passages as coming from the Lawḥ-i-Riḍván; the file shows only that XIV speaks of the Riḍván later in the same section. No other Gleanings passage on this page (XXXV, LXXXV) is identified with a Tablet in the file.
  • The phrase “Springtime which autumn will never overtake” is Bahá’u’lláh’s. Shoghi Effendi quotes it among Bahá’u’lláh’s titles for this Day in God Passes By VI.22, set out on The King of Days. The Tablet it comes from is not identified in this corpus, and the phrase is known here only as Shoghi Effendi quotes it.
  • The talks (Promulgation 4.3, 4.4, 56.4) are recorded notes. Their wording is a translator’s record of speech.
  • The texts say the Springtime has come and that the earth will bloom; they do not say how long the season lasts. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s account in Some Answered Questions describes a cycle in which spring is followed by other seasons. How that cycle applies to this Dispensation is not stated in the passages used here.

For songwriting

Singable phrases

  • ”Divine Springtime is come, O Most Exalted Pen“ (Gleanings XIV.1)
  • ”all created things may be regenerated and made new“ (Gleanings XIV.1)
  • ”this spiritual Springtime“ (Gleanings XXXV.5)
  • ”refresh and revive your souls“ (Gleanings LXXXV.1)
  • ”the clouds of His limitless grace“ (Gleanings LXXXV.1)
  • ”the river that is life indeed“ (Prayers and Meditations CLXXVI.1)
  • ”the trees of human existence bear their fruits“ (Prayers and Meditations CXVII.1)
  • ”The autumn hath gone by, and the reviving spring is here“ (Selections 205.3)
  • ”Renewal is the order of the day“ (Selections 205.3)
  • ”In flower-spangled meadows hath the divine springtime pitched its tents“ (Selections 206.6)
  • ”fields and plains shall teem with such a galaxy of sweet-smelling plants and blooms“ (Selections 102.2)
  • ”the wafting of the breeze of life“ (Some Answered Questions 14.7)
  • ”the dead are quickened to life“ (Some Answered Questions 14.7)
  • ”He has renewed that springtime“ (Promulgation 4.4, a talk)

Concrete images

  • Rain from clouds, sun at full height, a river flowing from fingers.
  • Meadows full of flowers, with tents pitched in them.
  • A breeze or east wind carrying scent from far away.
  • Trees bearing fruit.
  • A garment: the Beloved ”in this, His new attire.“
  • Autumn gone, and the old order of ”dress and personal effects“ and even weapons replaced.
  • A ”fleck of dust“ that becomes the ”vast circle of the skies.“

A possible emotional arc

  1. Announcement: the season has arrived (Gleanings XIV.1: ”The Divine Springtime is come“).
  2. Invitation: the warmth and rain are being given now, and the hearer is asked to take part (Gleanings XXXV.5, LXXXV.1).
  3. Wonder at the scene: meadows, scent, wind (Selections 206.6).
  4. Change in the world and in the heart: autumn gone, everything made new, the dead quickened (Selections 205.3; Some Answered Questions 14.7).
  5. Fruit and hope: hearts quickened, trees bearing fruit, the earth becoming the Abhá Kingdom as a promise not yet complete (Prayers and Meditations CXVII.1; Selections 102.2).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not say that spring comes only once. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá names Christ’s coming as a springtime and says Bahá’u’lláh ”has renewed that springtime“ (a talk); the season recurs with each Manifestation.
  • Do not say that the earth already is the Abhá Kingdom. Selections 102.2 says it ”will become“ so.
  • Do not attach a date or a length to the Springtime. No passage here does.
  • Do not attribute a Gleanings line to a named Tablet. The file names none for XIV, XXXV or LXXXV.
  • Do not credit ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s talk wording (”He has renewed that springtime“) as His written text; present it as His words in a talk.
  • Do not credit “Springtime which autumn will never overtake” to Shoghi Effendi. The words are Bahá’u’lláh’s, as Shoghi Effendi quotes them in God Passes By VI.22.
  • Do not describe the season as only comfort. The same texts call it ”commotion and ferment,“ and ask the hearer not to be deprived of it.
