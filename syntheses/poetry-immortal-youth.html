The Tablet of the Immortal Youth (Lawḥ-i-Ghulámu’l-Khuld)

Last updated 17 October 2026

The Tablet of the Immortal Youth is a Tablet of Bahá’u’lláh in which one refrain, “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth …”, closes almost every stanza. It is in Days of Remembrance, the volume of Bahá’u’lláh’s Writings for the nine Bahá’í Holy Days, in the section for the Declaration of the Báb. This page gives the whole authorised text, what the sources say about its date and occasion, how scholars read its images, and notes for anyone setting phrases of it to music. It is one page in the series Poetry in the Writings and by Bahá’í poets.

The poem

Place in the Writings. Days of Remembrance prints it as selection 27 (address `DOR 27.1` to `DOR 27.41`), under the heading “Declaration of the Báb”. The text opens with a headnote:

This is a remembrance of that which was revealed in the year sixty in the days of God, the Almighty, the Help in Peril, the All-Glorious, the All-Knowing.

(Days of Remembrance, selection 27, headnote)

“The year sixty”. The headnote does not give a calendar. The volume’s introduction says that the Badí‘ calendar was introduced by the Báb and confirmed by Bahá’u’lláh, “Who fixed its commencement at the year of the Báb’s declaration, 1844 (A.H. 1260)”. That sentence dates the Declaration of the Báb to A.H. 1260. It does not say that “the year sixty” of this Tablet is 1260. The link between the two comes from two other sources. John Walbridge, writing as a scholar, says the Tablet “begins with the announcement that it is ‘in commemoration of what hath been made manifest in the year sixty’” and adds “i.e. the Declaration of the Bab” (Sacred Acts, Sacred Space, Sacred Time, 1996). Ishráq Khávarí says that the Tablet “reverberates with the glorification of the dawning light of the Day of God which broke on the horizon of Shíráz through the appearance of the Báb” (The Writings of Bahá’u’lláh). Both are scholarship, not authoritative interpretation. The authorised volume places the Tablet under the Declaration of the Báb, which agrees with them.

Occasion and place of writing. Walbridge writes that the Tablet was composed “in honour of the holy day of the Declaration of the Bab”, that it “was certainly written in Baghdad”, and that it is “less clear” whether Bahá’u’lláh wrote it in the Garden of Riḍván, as “some sources claim”. He adds that it is not known whether it was written for a specific person. Khávarí says that it was revealed in Baghdad during the Riḍván festival. The two accounts differ on the Riḍván point (see “What is not settled”).

Language and form. Walbridge says the Tablet is written partly in Arabic and partly in Persian. The Arabic part is in rhymed prose, “a literary form that uses irregular rhyme and rhythm and occupies a place between poetry and prose”, in which short verses alternate with a refrain. It ends with a section in formal Persian prose. Khávarí agrees that the first part is in Arabic and the latter in Persian. Days of Remembrance does not describe the form, saying only in its introduction that its translations “endeavour to afford a glimpse of the poetic tone” of the Texts. In the authorised English, stanzas 1 to 35 carry the refrain and stanzas 36 to 41 do not. Stanza 35 ends “And this, truly, is a mighty cause!” The change from the refrain to direct address matches Walbridge’s description of the Persian conclusion.

Use. Walbridge writes that the Tablet “is chanted at commemorations of the Declaration of the Bab and other joyful occasions”, and that Munírih Khánum chanted it at her wedding to ‘Abdu’l-Bahá.

Other treatments. Adib Taherzadeh discusses the Tablet in volume 1 of The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, calling it “The Youth of Paradise”. Only the index of that book is in the corpus (`taherzadeh_revelation_indexes.md` line 185, pages 211 and 213–14), so this page does not report what Taherzadeh says. Check the book before citing him.

The text in English

Authorised translation

Authorised translation, Days of Remembrance, selection 27. Footnote markers are omitted here. Footnote 15 on stanza 1 reads “See Qur’án 7:107”, and footnote 16 on stanza 6 reads “Cf. Qur’án 12:31”. The headnote has no stanza number. Each numbered stanza is the paragraph `DOR 27.<number>`.

This is a remembrance of that which was revealed in the year sixty in the days of God, the Almighty, the Help in Peril, the All-Glorious, the All-Knowing.

1. Lo, the gates of Paradise were unlocked, and the hallowed Youth came forth bearing a serpent plain. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with crystal waters.

2. Upon His face was a veil woven by the fingers of might and power. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty name.

3. Upon His brow there shone a beauteous crown, which cast its splendour upon all who are in heaven and all who are on earth. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty cause.

4. Upon His shoulders there fell the locks of the spirit, like unto black musk upon white and lustrous pearls. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a transcendent cause.

5. On His right hand was a ring adorned with a pure and blessed gem. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty spirit.

6. Upon it was graven, in a secret and ancient script: “By God! A most noble Angel is this.” And the hearts of the inmates of the eternal realm cried out: “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with an ancient light.”

7. Upon His right cheek was a mark whose sight caused every man of understanding to waver in his faith. And they that dwell behind the veil of the Unseen exclaimed: “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty secret.”

8. This is the Point from which the knowledge of the former and the latter generations hath been unfolded. And the denizens of the Kingdom intoned: “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty knowledge.”

9. This, verily, is the Horseman of the Spirit Who circleth round the fount of everlasting life. And they that lie concealed in the retreats of the highest heaven cried out: “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty unveiling.”

10. He descended from the tabernacle of beauty till He stood, even as the sun in the midmost heaven, arrayed with a beauty at once peerless and transcendent. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with the most joyful tidings!

11. Standing in the midmost heaven, He shone forth like unto the sun in its meridian splendour, illumining the seat of divine beauty with His mighty Name. Whereupon the Crier cried out: “Rejoice! This is the Beauty of the Unseen, come with a mighty spirit.”

12. And the Maids of Heaven cried out from their celestial chambers: “Hallowed be the Lord, the most excellent of all creators!” And the nightingale sang sweetly: “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, Whose like the eyes of the favoured ones of Heaven have never beheld.”

13. And lo, the gates of Paradise were unlocked a second time with the key of His Great Name. “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty name.”

14. And the Maid of beauty shone forth even as the dawning sun above the horizon of a resplendent morn. Rejoice! This is the divine Maiden, come with surpassing beauty.

15. She came forth with such adorning as to seize with longing desire the minds of them that are nigh unto God. Rejoice! This is the Maid of Heaven, come with alluring charm.

16. Descending from the chambers of eternity, she sang in accents that entranced the souls of the sincere. Rejoice! This is the immortal Beauty, come with a mighty secret.

17. Suspended in the air, she let fall a single lock of her hair from beneath her luminous veil—Rejoice! This is the Maid of Heaven, come with a wondrous spirit—

18. Shedding the fragrance of that lock upon all creation. Whereupon the faces of the holy ones grew pale and the hearts of the ardent lovers were filled with the blood of anguish. Rejoice! This is the Maid of Heaven, come with the sweetest fragrance.

19. By God! Whosoever closeth his eyes to her beauty hath fallen prey to grave deception and manifest error. Rejoice! This is the immortal Beauty, come with a shining light.

20. She turned, and round her circled the inhabitants of both this world and the world to come. Rejoice! This is the Maid of Heaven, come with a mighty dispensation.

21. She advanced, arrayed with a rare and glorious adorning, till she stood face to face before the Youth. Rejoice! This is the immortal Beauty, come with enchanting grace.

22. From beneath her veil she drew forth her hand, golden tinged as a sunbeam falling upon the face of a stainless mirror. Rejoice! This is the immortal Beauty, come with a resplendent adorning.

23. Her incomparable ruby fingers seized the hem of the veil that hid the face of the Youth—Rejoice! This is the immortal Beauty, come with a mighty glance—

24. And drew it back, whereupon the pillars of the Throne on high were made to tremble. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty cause.

25. Then did the spirits of all created things part from their bodies. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty cause.

26. And the inmates of Paradise rent their garments asunder as they caught a lightning glimpse of His ancient and shining countenance. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with an effulgent light.

27. At that moment the Voice of the Eternal was heard from beyond the veil of clouds with a sweet and enchanting call. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty enchantment.

28. And from the source of God’s inscrutable decree the Tongue of the Unseen proclaimed: “By God! The like of this Youth the eyes of the former generations have never beheld.” Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty cause.

29. And the maids of holiness cried out from the chambers of exalted dominion. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with manifest sovereignty.

30. By God! This is that Youth Whose beauty is the ardent desire of the celestial Concourse. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty cause.

31. Then did the Youth lift up His head to the concourse of heavenly angels—Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty spirit—

32. And gave voice to a single word, whereupon the denizens of heaven arose, one and all, to a new life. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty trumpet blast.

33. He looked then upon the inhabitants of the earth with a wondrous glance. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty glance.

34. And with that glance He gathered them together each and all. Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with a mighty cause.

35. With another look He signalled to a chosen few, then repaired to His habitation in the everlasting Paradise. And this, truly, is a mighty cause!

36. The Herald of Eternity proclaimeth from his cloud-wrapped throne: O ye that wait expectant in the vale of patience and fidelity! O ye that long to soar in the atmosphere of nearness and reunion! The celestial Youth, hidden ere now within the inviolable treasuries of God, hath appeared, even as the Sun of Reality and the Eternal Spirit, from the Dawning-Place of changeless splendour, adorned with the ornament of the Almighty and the beauty of the All-Praised. He hath rescued all who are in heaven and on earth from the perils of death and extinction, clothed them in the garment of true and everlasting existence, and bestowed upon them a new life.

37. That concealed Word upon which the souls of all the Messengers of God and His Chosen Ones have ever depended hath manifested itself out of the invisible world into the visible plane. No sooner had this hidden Word shone forth from the Realm of inmost being and absolute singleness to illumine the peoples of the earth than a breeze of mercy wafted therefrom, purifying all things from the stench of sin and arraying the countless forms of existence and the reality of man with the vesture of forgiveness. So great was the wondrous grace which pervaded all things that through the utterance of the letters “B” and “E” the gems that lay hid within the repositories of this contingent world were brought forth and made manifest. Thus were the seen and the unseen joined in one garment, and the hidden and the manifest clothed in a single robe; thus did utter nothingness attain the realm of eternity, and pure evanescence gain admittance into the court of everlasting life.

38. Wherefore, O ye lovers of the beauty of the All-Glorious! O ye that ardently seek the court of the presence of the Almighty! This is the day of nearness and reunion, not the time for contention and idle words. If ye be sincere lovers, behold the beauty of the Best-Beloved shining clear and resplendent as the true morn. It behoveth you to be free from all attachment, whether to yourselves or to others; nay, ye should renounce existence and non-existence, light and darkness, glory and abasement alike. Sever your hearts from all transitory things, from all idle fancies and vain imaginings, that ye may, pure and unsullied, enter the realm of the spirit and partake with radiant hearts of the splendours of everlasting holiness.

39. O friends! The wine of eternal life is flowing. O lovers! The face of the Beloved is unveiled and unconcealed. O companions! The fire of the Sinai of love is burning bright and resplendent. Cast off the burden of love for this world and every attachment thereto, and, even as luminous, heavenly birds, soar in the atmosphere of the celestial Paradise and wing your flight to the everlasting nest. For devoid of this, life itself hath no worth, and bereft of the Beloved, the heart is of no account.

40. Yea, the moth-like lovers of the All-Glorious at every instant offer up their lives round the consuming flame of the Friend, occupying themselves with naught save Him. Yet not every bird can aspire to such heights. God, verily, guideth whom He willeth unto His mighty and exalted path.

41. Thus do We bestow upon the dwellers of the mystic realm that which shall draw them nigh unto the right hand of everlasting life and enable them to attain unto that station which hath been upraised in the heaven of holiness.

Provisional translation by John Walbridge (1996)

The corpus holds a complete provisional translation by John Walbridge, titled “Tablet of the Deathless Youth”, in [source]. It was made before the authorised translation and is not the text of the Writings. The page on bahai-library.com marks it “EnglishPermission” (translator’s permission) and notes that an authorised translation is in Days of Remembrance. A few short extracts show how it differs from the authorised text. Walbridge’s version of the refrain in the first stanza:

Rejoice! This is the Deathless Youth, come with a gushing spring.

The authorised version of the same refrain is “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with crystal waters” (`DOR 27.1`). The two differ in the translation of the title word (“Deathless”, “immortal”) and in the image of the closing words. Walbridge calls the female figure “the Deathless Maid” and “the Deathless Beauty”, where the authorised translation has “the Maid of Heaven” and “the immortal Beauty”.

Khávarí’s quotations

Ishráq Khávarí quotes the opening and part of the Persian conclusion in English in The Writings of Bahá’u’lláh, where the refrain reads “Glad-tidings! This is the Youth of Paradise.” These are quotations in a general work, not a translation of the whole Tablet, and are not used further here.

Original text

The Arabic and Persian original is not in the corpus. The Persian collection in [source] (line 540) lists the Tablet in its table of contents, as item 414 on page 672, without the text.

Themes and images

The text is a sequence of scenes, each followed by the refrain, and then a closing address to the reader. In order:

  • The Youth appears (stanzas 1 to 12). The gates of Paradise are unlocked and a Youth comes out. Stanzas 2 to 7 describe Him by detail: “a veil woven by the fingers of might and power”, “a beauteous crown”, “the locks of the spirit, like unto black musk upon white and lustrous pearls”, a ring graven with “By God! A most noble Angel is this”, and a mark on His right cheek. Stanza 8 says “This is the Point from which the knowledge of the former and the latter generations hath been unfolded”. Stanzas 10 and 11 set Him “even as the sun in the midmost heaven”. Different groups, among them the inmates of the eternal realm, the denizens of the Kingdom and the Maids of Heaven, answer in turn with the refrain.
  • The Maid of Heaven (stanzas 13 to 23). The gates open “a second time”. A Maid appears “as the dawning sun”, sings, lets fall “a single lock of her hair” whose fragrance reaches “all creation”, and advances until she stands “face to face before the Youth”. Stanza 19 says “Whosoever closeth his eyes to her beauty hath fallen prey to grave deception and manifest error.” Her hand then takes “the hem of the veil that hid the face of the Youth”.
  • The unveiling (stanzas 24 to 28). She draws the veil back, “the pillars of the Throne on high were made to tremble”, “the spirits of all created things part from their bodies”, and the inmates of Paradise “rent their garments asunder as they caught a lightning glimpse of His ancient and shining countenance”. The Voice of the Eternal and the Tongue of the Unseen proclaim that “the eyes of the former generations have never beheld” the like of Him.
  • The Youth speaks and looks (stanzas 29 to 35). He lifts His head, “gave voice to a single word”, and the denizens of heaven “arose, one and all, to a new life”. He looks on the inhabitants of the earth and gathers them with one glance; “with another look He signalled to a chosen few”, then returns to the everlasting Paradise.
  • The Herald’s address (stanzas 36 to 41). The refrain stops. The Herald of Eternity says that the “celestial Youth, hidden ere now within the inviolable treasuries of God, hath appeared”, and has “rescued all who are in heaven and on earth from the perils of death and extinction”. Stanza 37 says the “concealed Word” has “manifested itself out of the invisible world into the visible plane”, and that through “the letters ‘B’ and ‘E’” hidden gems “were brought forth and made manifest”. The volume’s notes do not gloss these letters here. (Note 13, which reads the letter “B” as that of “Bahá”, belongs to the Tablet of the Bell, DOR 26.6.)
  • The invitation (stanzas 38 to 41). The listeners are told that “This is the day of nearness and reunion, not the time for contention and idle words.” They should “be free from all attachment, whether to yourselves or to others”. Stanza 39 says “The wine of eternal life is flowing” and “The face of the Beloved is unveiled and unconcealed.” Stanza 40 says that the lovers “offer up their lives round the consuming flame of the Friend”, and that “not every bird can aspire to such heights”.

Who the figures are. The authorised text does not say. Walbridge reads the Youth as the Báb and the Maid of Heaven as “the personification of Bahá’u’lláh’s spirit of revelation”, and he notes that in a Baghdad-period group of allegorical Tablets Bahá’u’lláh uses figures such as the Maiden, the Deathless Youth and the Holy Mariner “to foreshadow His prophetic claims”. He also observes that the unveiling in stanzas 21 to 24 draws on a Middle Eastern wedding custom in which the husband lifts the bride’s veil, and that Bahá’u’lláh uses such imagery to express spiritual joy. These are scholarly readings. Walbridge says the Tablet ends by “hinting at Bahá’u’lláh’s own station”. Khávarí says that in its later part Bahá’u’lláh “refers to the coming of Him Who is the Desired One”.

Images recurring in the text: gates opening, a veil, a crown, a ring, a mark, the sun in the midst of heaven, crystal waters, black musk and white pearls, a single lock of hair and its fragrance, wine, fire on Sinai, moths and a flame, birds in flight.

Qur’ánic references in the volume’s notes: stanza 1 (“a serpent plain”) points to Qur’án 7:107, and stanza 6 (“A most noble Angel”) compares Qur’án 12:31.

What is not settled

  • When it was written. Sources give the occasion, the Declaration of the Báb, but not a date of revelation. The headnote’s “revealed in the year sixty” is read by Walbridge and Khávarí as referring to 1260 A.H. (1844). The authorised volume does not gloss it.
  • Where. Baghdad is stated by Walbridge (“certainly”) and Khávarí. Whether in the Garden of Riḍván is uncertain according to Walbridge; Khávarí says it was during the Riḍván festival, which is a statement about the time and not necessarily the Garden. The two sources do not agree on how firmly it is known.
  • For whom. Walbridge: not known whether it was written for a specific individual.
  • Who the Youth and the Maid are. No authoritative interpretation is in the corpus. The Báb and Bahá’u’lláh’s revelation are scholarly readings.
  • Form. The authorised volume does not call it verse; Walbridge calls the Arabic part rhymed prose.
  • The English title. “Immortal Youth” (authorised), “Deathless Youth” (Walbridge), “Youth of Paradise” (Khávarí, Taherzadeh’s index).
  • Taherzadeh’s account. Not available in the corpus; see above.
  • The original text. Not in the corpus, so the authorised English cannot be compared with it here.

For songwriting

Notes, not lyrics. All phrases below are from the authorised translation (© Bahá’í International Community; licence `ok-with-credit`: credit the work, and check the bahai.org terms of use before a commercial release). Cite as Days of Remembrance, Tablet of the Immortal Youth.

Singable exact phrases (5 to 15 words):

  • “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with crystal waters.” (`DOR 27.1`; the refrain, nine words)
  • “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with the most joyful tidings!” (`DOR 27.10`)
  • “Lo, the gates of Paradise were unlocked” (`DOR 27.1`)
  • “Upon His face was a veil woven by the fingers of might and power.” (`DOR 27.2`)
  • “like unto black musk upon white and lustrous pearls” (`DOR 27.4`)
  • “till He stood, even as the sun in the midmost heaven” (`DOR 27.10`)
  • “Rejoice! This is the Beauty of the Unseen” (`DOR 27.11`)
  • “Hallowed be the Lord, the most excellent of all creators!” (`DOR 27.12`)
  • “This is the day of nearness and reunion” (`DOR 27.38`)
  • “The wine of eternal life is flowing.” (`DOR 27.39`)
  • “The face of the Beloved is unveiled and unconcealed.” (`DOR 27.39`)
  • “The fire of the Sinai of love is burning bright and resplendent.” (`DOR 27.39`)
  • “soar in the atmosphere of the celestial Paradise” (`DOR 27.39`)
  • “bereft of the Beloved, the heart is of no account” (`DOR 27.39`)

Structure for a song. The Tablet already has a verse and refrain form: a scene, then “Rejoice! This is the immortal Youth, come with …”. The words after “come with” change each time (crystal waters, a mighty name, an ancient light, a mighty secret, the most joyful tidings), so a lyric can repeat the refrain and vary its last phrase from the text.

Concrete images: the opened gates, the veil, the crown, the ring, the sun in the midst of heaven, a lock of hair and its fragrance, the wine, the flame and the moths, the birds in flight.

Possible arc. The text itself moves from announcement (gates open), to appearance (the Youth, the sun), to the Maid and the lifting of the veil, to awe (pillars trembling, spirits parting from bodies), to gathering and new life (“a new life”), and ends with the invitation to detach and “soar”. A lyric can follow that order.

A lyric must not claim:

  • that the Tablet says the Youth is the Báb, or that the Maid is Bahá’u’lláh’s revelation; these are Walbridge’s readings;
  • that the Tablet was revealed in the Garden of Riḍván, or on a particular day;
  • that “the year sixty” is stated in the text to be 1844; the year and its link are given by other sources;
  • that Walbridge’s wording (“Deathless Youth”, “gushing spring”) belongs to the Writings; it needs the translator’s permission;
  • that the Tablet is “a poem” in the strict sense; it is rhymed prose in Arabic with a Persian prose ending.
