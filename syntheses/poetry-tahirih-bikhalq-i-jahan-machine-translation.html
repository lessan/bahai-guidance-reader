  • What the poem itself says: A Persian mathnaví excerpt in the mutaqárib meter (fa‘úlun fa‘úlun fa‘úlun fa‘al, rhyming aa bb cc dd ... ee ff gg), comprising 14 verse lines (7 couplets) divided into two parts by two editorial omission lines in the source (16 source lines in total). The first four couplets (lines 1–8) summon the Cup-Bearer (sáqí) to proclaim glad-tidings to the peoples of the world that the evening of sorrow has passed, the morn of joy has arrived, the Essence of the Eternal Manifestation (‘ayn-i-ẓuhúr-i-azal) has come, the Divine Beauty has been revealed, and the Exalted Lord (Rabb-i-‘Alá) rejoices at these tidings. After the source’s omission marker (lines 9–10), the remaining three couplets (lines 11–16) address one whom the ”Lord of the Bayán“ (Rabb-i-Bayán) has, out of grace, named ”the Fruit“ (Thamar), declaring that the purpose of the tree (shajar) is naught save the fruit, that through the fruit the tree becomes resplendent, and that through this figure the Bayán has been brought to perfection and the hidden mystery of God beheld. Any further historical details beyond the header and the text itself are not established.

Uncertain readings

VerseWord or phraseProblemAlternatives considered
2 (1b)که شد شام غم صبح عشرت رسید (kih shud shám-i-gham ṣubḥ-i-‘ishrat rasíd)In classical Persian verse, `شد` (shud) frequently retains its older sense of raft (”went / departed / passed away“), standing in direct antithesis to `رسید` (”arrived“). In later/modern Persian, `شد` means ”became“ (”the evening of sorrow became the morn of joy, [which] arrived“), which is syntactically awkward with `رسید`.(1) Read shud in its classical sense ”departed / passed away“ (chosen); (2) Read shud as ”turned into / became“.
5 (3a)عین ظهور ازل (‘ayn-i-ẓuhúr-i-azal)Polysemous Arabic-Persian theological construct: `عین` (‘ayn) can mean ”the very essence / self“ (”the very Essence of the Eternal Manifestation“), ”the eye“, or ”the wellspring / fount“. `ازل` (azal, Pre-Existence / Eternity) is both a theological term in Bábí writings and part of Bábí titles (the source header note cites the Persian Wikipedia claim that the poem was composed regarding ṭulú‘-i-Ṣubḥ-i-Azal).(1) Render as ”the Essence of the Eternal Manifestation / the very Essence of the Eternal Manifestation“ while noting the theological and historical ambiguity in the source header (chosen); (2) ”the Fount of the Manifestation of Eternity“; (3) ”the Eye of the Manifestation of Azal“.
8 (4b)خوش وقت رب علاست (khush-vaqt Rabb-i-‘Alást)`رب علا` (Rabb-i-‘Alá, literally ”Lord of Exaltation“ or ”Exalted Lord“) is a metrical shortening (for the mutaqárib cadence `u - -u -`) of the Báb’s primary title `رب اعلی` (Rabb-i-A‘lá, rendered by Shoghi Effendi as ”the Lord, the most exalted“, `kitab-i-iqan.md` line 552). `خوش وقت` (khush-vaqt) is a Sufi/classical compound meaning ”in a joyous spiritual state / gladdened / well-pleased“.(1) ”well-pleased / gladdened is the Lord, the Most Exalted“ (chosen); (2) ”rejoicing is the Lord of Exaltation“.
9–10........ تا آنجا که می‌گوید / :Editorial omission markers inserted by the Persian Wikipedia source (`........ تا آنجا که می‌گوید`, literally ”...up to where she/it says“, followed on the next line by a colon `:`). Per instructions, source ellipsis lines are rendered as `[omitted in the source]`.Kept strictly as `[omitted in the source]` on both lines.
11–12 (5a–5b)ثمر خواندت از لطف رب بیان (thamar khándat az luṭf Rabb-i-Bayán)The grammatical subject is `رب بیان` (”the Lord of the Bayán“, i.e. the Báb), and the enclitic object pronoun `-ت` (-at, ”thee“) in `خواندت` (”called thee“) refers to the addressee of the second excerpted section, whom the Lord of the Bayán named `ثمر` (Thamar / Thamarih, ”the Fruit“). Because lines 9–10 omit the intervening verses, the poem text does not explicitly name the addressee of lines 11–16 (whether a Bábí figure addressed as the ”Fruit“ of the Tree of the Bayán, as mentioned in the source header note, or the Báb Himself, Who is also titled Thamarih, cf. `selections-writings-bab.md` line 1470). Accordingly, lower-case ”thee/thy“ is used in lines 11–15 to avoid asserting an unestablished theological identification.(1) ”the Lord of the Bayán, out of grace, called thee ‘the Fruit’“ with lower-case ”thee“ (chosen); (2) Read `ثمر` as subject and `رب بیان` as predicate (unnatural in Persian word order and contradicted by lines 13–15, where the addressee perfects the Bayán as the fruit perfects the tree).
15 (7a)تکمیل گردیده شد (takmíl gardídih shud)Pleonastic passive construction combining the past participle `گردیده` (gardídih, ”become“) with the passive auxiliary `شد` (shud, ”was/became“) to satisfy the mutaqárib meter (`u - -u -`).(1) ”was brought to perfection / attained its consummation“ (chosen); (2) ”became completed“.
