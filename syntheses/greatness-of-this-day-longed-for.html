The Day the Prophets Longed For

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

The Writings say that this Day was announced by every Prophet and that every Prophet wished to see it. The texts below speak of two things: the announcing, in which each Messenger gave His followers the glad-tidings of a coming Day, and the longing, in which Messengers, saints and whole peoples waited for it. The Writings also say that the promise is now fulfilled. This page keeps to those three points. It does not go through the prophecies of each religion; that is done in The Day foretold. This page is one of a series; see The greatness of this Day.

The passages

Every Prophet thirsted for it and announced it

Bahá’u’lláh writes:

“Great indeed is this Day! The allusions made to it in all the sacred Scriptures as the Day of God attest its greatness. The soul of every Prophet of God, of every Divine Messenger, hath thirsted for this wondrous Day. All the divers kindreds of the earth have, likewise, yearned to attain it.”

(Gleanings VII.2)

The passage gives two reasons for calling the Day great. The first is how often the Scriptures mention it. The second is that the Messengers themselves, who are the source of those Scriptures, thirsted for it. The longing is also not limited to the holy: “all the divers kindreds of the earth” are included.

In a Tablet that Shoghi Effendi quotes in The Advent of Divine Justice, Bahá’u’lláh gives the same teaching from the side of the Messengers:

“Every Prophet hath announced the coming of this Day, and every Messenger hath groaned in His yearning for this Revelation—a revelation which, no sooner had it been revealed than all created things cried out saying, ‘The earth is God’s, the Most Exalted, the Most Great!’”

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

Here the Prophets do two things: they announce, and they yearn. The second half of the sentence says what happened on the Day itself: all created things responded.

In the same paragraph of The Advent of Divine Justice, Bahá’u’lláh says what the longing was for:

“The purpose underlying all creation is the revelation of this most sublime, this most holy Day, the Day known as the Day of God, in His Books and Scriptures—the Day which all the Prophets, and the Chosen Ones, and the holy ones, have wished to witness.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

The wish of the Prophets is tied to the purpose of creation. The Day is not only something earlier Messengers hoped for; it is what creation exists to bring about. The passage also names three groups who wished to witness it: the Prophets, the Chosen Ones and the holy ones.

Bahá’u’lláh also tells the believers to treat the promise as already kept. He writes in a Tablet that tells its reader to persevere in teaching:

“The Day, promised unto you in all the Scriptures, is now come.”

(Gleanings CXLIV.3)

The sentence is addressed to people who were taught from the Scriptures, and it tells them that what those Scriptures promised has arrived. Elsewhere He instructs another believer to carry the news:

“Arise thou to serve the Cause of thy Lord; then give the people the joyful tidings concerning this resplendent Light whose revelation hath been announced by God through His Prophets and Messengers.”

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.43)

The announcement made by the Messengers of the past becomes the content of the believer’s own teaching. The believer repeats the glad-tidings and now adds that the Light has come.

The Books of the past gave glad-tidings

In a Tablet to a woman believer, whom He addresses as His handmaiden and His leaf, Bahá’u’lláh writes:

“All the Books of the past and the Messengers of God have given the people the glad-tidings of this hallowed, this most exalted Day; nevertheless, the ungodly, the ignorant, and the evil ones have arisen with utmost cruelty to uproot the Holy Tree.”

(Additional Tablets and Extracts 3.2)

This passage adds a contrast. The announcement was universal, and the response to the One announced was cruelty. Having a promise in the Scriptures did not mean that those who held the Scriptures recognised its fulfilment.

Shoghi Effendi, describing the Revelation, gathers Bahá’u’lláh’s testimony about its scope. He quotes Him:

“All the Divine Books and Scriptures have predicted and announced unto men the advent of the Most Great Revelation. None can adequately recount the verses recorded in the Books of former ages which forecast this supreme Bounty, this most mighty Bestowal.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in God Passes By VI.24; source Tablet not identified)

Bahá’u’lláh says that the verses are too many to count. This is the reason the present page does not try to list them.

Messengers who would have recognised Him

Bahá’u’lláh also says what particular Messengers would have said had they lived to see this Day. In the Summons of the Lord of Hosts He writes:

“Had Muḥammad, the Apostle of God, attained this Day, He would have exclaimed: “I have truly recognized Thee, O Thou the Desire of the Divine Messengers!” Had Abraham attained it, He too, falling prostrate upon the ground, and in the utmost lowliness before the Lord thy God, would have cried: “Mine heart is filled with peace, O Thou Lord of all that is in heaven and on earth! I testify that Thou hast unveiled before mine eyes all the glory of Thy power and the full majesty of Thy law! I bear witness, moreover, that through Thy Revelation the hearts of the faithful are well assured and contented.” Had Moses Himself attained it, He, likewise, would have raised His voice saying: “All praise be to Thee for having lifted upon me the light of Thy countenance and enrolled me among them that have been privileged to behold Thy face!””

(The Summons of the Lord of Hosts 2.18)

The three speeches are in the conditional: they say what each would have said, not what each said. Muḥammad calls the One He recognises “the Desire of the Divine Messengers”, so the desire is shared by the Messengers as a body. Abraham speaks of peace and assurance in the hearts of the faithful. Moses speaks of seeing the face of God.

In the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Bahá’u’lláh puts a similar statement in the present tense:

“This is the Day in which He Who held converse with God hath attained the light of the Ancient of Days, and quaffed the pure waters of reunion from this Cup that hath caused the seas to swell.”

(The Kitáb-i-Aqdas 80)

He Who held converse with God is Moses, in the language of the passage. The verbs “hath attained” and “quaffed” are in the past tense, which makes this passage a statement of what has happened. It does not describe what would have happened. The same sentence appears in Gleanings CV, in the paragraphs that open “O Kings of the earth!” Bahá’u’lláh also writes, in another Tablet:

“The episode of Sinai hath been reenacted in this Revelation and He Who conversed upon the Mount is calling aloud: Verily, the Desired One is come, seated upon the throne of certitude, could ye but perceive it. He hath admonished all men to observe that which is conducive to the exaltation of the Cause of God and will guide mankind unto His Straight Path.”

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.60)

The call heard on Sinai is heard again. The Desired One has come, and the people addressed are told that they can perceive it.

In the same paragraph of Gleanings CV, Bahá’u’lláh says that Carmel hastened to this Day and that Zion cries out:

“Carmel hath, in this Day, hastened in longing adoration to attain His court, whilst from the heart of Zion there cometh the cry: “The promise is fulfilled. That which had been announced in the holy Writ of God, the most Exalted, the Almighty, the Best-Beloved, is made manifest.””

(Gleanings CV.3)

Here the longing belongs to places as well as persons, and the word “fulfilled” is stated directly.

The Báb: the Hour is at hand

The Báb writes of the Day before the Dayspring of the Gate has shone:

“O HOUR of the Dawn! Ere the resplendent glory of the divine Luminary sheddeth its radiance from the Dayspring of this Gate, call thou to mind that the appointed Day of God will indeed be at hand in less than the twinkling of an eye.”

(Selections from the Writings of the Báb 10.94)

The Báb speaks of the Day of God as near, and describes His own Gate as the place from which the radiance of the Luminary will first be seen. Bahá’u’lláh says that a John has cried out in the same spirit:

“O followers of the Son! We have once again sent John unto you, and He, verily, hath cried out in the wilderness of the Bayán: O peoples of the world! Cleanse your eyes! The Day whereon ye can behold the Promised One and attain unto Him hath drawn nigh!”

(The Summons of the Lord of Hosts 1.121)

Bahá’u’lláh addresses the followers of Christ and calls the crier “John” in “the wilderness of the Bayán”. The call is a forerunner’s call: the Day has “drawn nigh”, and the hearer is asked to clean his eyes.

The Báb also speaks as the One awaited. Shoghi Effendi, in God Passes By, records the words the Báb spoke at His examination in Tabríz:

“I am the One Whose name you have for a thousand years invoked, at Whose mention you have risen, Whose advent you have longed to witness, and the hour of Whose Revelation you have prayed God to hasten.”

(The Báb, quoted in God Passes By II.7)

This is the longing seen from the one longed for. The people addressed had invoked a name, risen at its mention, and prayed for the hour to be hastened.

Concealed from eternity

Bahá’u’lláh describes the Day as something kept ready before the creation of the world. In Days of Remembrance He writes:

“By My life! The Day is come which from all eternity was concealed in the treasuries of the might of your Lord. Rejoice in this blessed, this glorious and sublime Day. For He verily is Mine own Appearance amongst you, and whoso maketh the slightest distinction between Him and Me hath indeed strayed far from the straight path of truth.”

(Days of Remembrance 16.5)

The longing of the Prophets had a long history, and this passage says the Day had a longer one. It was kept from eternity. The last sentence is a limit on what can be said about the One who has come: He is called God’s own Appearance, and the believer is told not to make a distinction between Him and God. That sentence is about the station of the Manifestation; this page does not develop it.

In a passage from the Tablet of the Birth, Bahá’u’lláh speaks to the concourse of the seen and unseen:

“O concourse of the seen and the unseen! Rejoice with exceeding gladness in your hearts and souls, for the night hath come for the harvesting of the ages and the gathering up of past cycles, the night wherein all days and nights were called into being and the time preordained for this Revelation was fulfilled at the behest of Him Who is the Lord of might and power. All joy to the Concourse on high at the appearance of so glorious, so wondrous a Spirit!”

(Days of Remembrance 42.1)

The past cycles are “gathered up”. The time for this Revelation was “preordained” and it has now been “fulfilled”. In this passage the ages before are the field that is harvested on this night.

Beyond the saints of earlier ages

‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in a Tablet quoted by Shoghi Effendi, says that the Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh would overwhelm those who waited for it in earlier ages:

“The mere contemplation of the Dispensation inaugurated by the Blessed Beauty,” He furthermore affirms, “would have sufficed to overwhelm the saints of bygone ages—saints who longed to partake for one moment of its great glory.”

(‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted in God Passes By VI.23; Tablet not identified)

The saints of earlier ages are said to have longed for one moment of its glory. In a talk, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá speaks of the same longing in the language of nights spent awake:

”How many blessed souls have longed for this radiant century, their utmost hopes and desires centered upon the happiness and joy of one such day as this. Many the nights they passed sleepless and lamenting until the very morn in longing anticipation of this age, yearning to realize even an hour of this time.“

(‘Abdu’l-Bahá, The Promulgation of Universal Peace 76.9, in a talk recorded in notes)

This is a recorded talk, not a text written by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. It adds nothing to the point already made in the Tablet above, except for the picture of sleepless nights.

Shoghi Effendi: the promise redeemed

Shoghi Effendi writes about what will follow when the world commonwealth is established:

”Then will the promise enshrined in all the Books of God be redeemed, and all the prophecies uttered by the Prophets of old come to pass, and the vision of seers and poets be realized.“

(The Promised Day Is Come 301)

This passage looks ahead. Bahá’u’lláh says that the Day has come; Shoghi Effendi says that the promise of the Books of God will be redeemed in a future that follows from it. Both statements are in the sources, and they are about different things: the Day’s arrival, and the full realisation of what the Prophets announced.

How this facet connects

  • The King of Days gives the titles Bahá’u’lláh gives to the Day itself. The present page is about who waited for it; that page is about what the Day is called.
  • Envied by the Concourse on High takes the longing beyond the Prophets to the Concourse on high, whose joy is already in the Tablet of the Birth passage above.
  • The Day of God Himself treats the passages on the identity of the One who has come and on the Day as God’s own, which this page leaves aside except for one sentence in Days of Remembrance 16.5.
  • All Things Rejoice starts from the response of created things, which the Advent of Divine Justice passage here mentions in its last clause.
  • The Day foretold sets out what each tradition’s Scriptures said, which this page assumes without listing.
  • “Dearly Beloved Friends” is the article on the compilation in The Advent of Divine Justice from which the three ADJ 110 passages here are taken.
  • Promises of the Future covers what the Writings say will come about later, which bears on Shoghi Effendi’s sentence from The Promised Day Is Come.

What the sources do not settle

  • The three passages quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice (ADJ 110) are given without Tablet names. They are printed together in one paragraph of the book, and whether they come from one Tablet is not stated there. The same applies to the two passages quoted in God Passes By (VI.23, VI.24).
  • The Báb’s words at His examination in Tabríz are given in English by Shoghi Effendi. The original wording is not in this corpus.
  • The Summons passage on Muḥammad, Abraham and Moses says what they would have said. It does not report anything they said, and it should not be treated as a quotation of their words.
  • The Tablet to the woman believer (Additional Tablets 3.2) and the Tablet in Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17 are addressed to individuals; the corpus text does not state their occasion or date.
  • Bahá’u’lláh says that the Day was announced by every Prophet and that every Messenger yearned for it. The texts here do not list the Prophets, and no passage on this page says which of their words are the announcement.

  • ”in less than the twinkling of an eye“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb 10.94)

Concrete images

  • Thirst: the souls of the Prophets thirsting for the Day (Gleanings VII.2).
  • Sinai heard again, and the Desired One on a throne (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.60).
  • Carmel hastening toward Him; a cry from the heart of Zion (Gleanings CV.3).
  • Sleepless nights waiting for the morning (Promulgation 76.9, in a talk).
  • A voice in the wilderness calling people to wash their eyes (Summons 1.121).
  • Moses, Abraham and Muḥammad each speaking, in the conditional, on seeing the Day (Summons 2.18).
  • A harvest night, ages gathered up (Days of Remembrance 42.1).
  • A name invoked for a thousand years and prayers to hasten the hour (God Passes By II.7).

A possible emotional arc

  1. Longing: the Prophets thirst and the peoples yearn (Gleanings VII.2; ADJ 110).
  2. The long night of waiting: saints sleepless for one hour of it (Promulgation 76.9, in a talk; God Passes By VI.23).
  3. The call: the forerunner cries in the wilderness and the Hour of the Dawn is near (Summons 1.121; Selections 10.94).
  4. Arrival: ”The Day, promised unto you in all the Scriptures, is now come“ (Gleanings CXLIV.3), and Sinai is heard again (Tablets 17.60).
  5. Joy: the Messengers would have recognised Him, and the verse is sung in their voices (Summons 2.18; Days of Remembrance 16.5).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not present Muḥammad, Abraham or Moses as having said the words in Summons 2.18. The text says what they would have said had they attained this Day.
  • Do not attribute the Promulgation passage to ‘Abdu’l-Bahá as a written text; it is a recorded talk. Do not attribute the words in God Passes By VI.23 to Shoghi Effendi: he quotes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá.
  • Do not attribute the examination words (God Passes By II.7) to Bahá’u’lláh. They are the Báb’s.
  • Do not give a Tablet name to the passages in ADJ 110 or in God Passes By VI.23–24.
  • Do not make ”Mine own Appearance“ (Days of Remembrance 16.5) mean that Bahá’u’lláh is the Essence of God. The passage is about the Manifestation, and its limit is the instruction not to make a distinction between Him and God.
  • Do not turn Shoghi Effendi’s ”Then will the promise… be redeemed“ into a date or a claim that it has already been completed. He writes of a future state of the world.
  • Do not present the longing as sadness at a loss. In every passage here the Day has come or is near.
