The Greatness of This Day: The Facets, One Page Each

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this series is

Bahá’u’lláh returns again and again to the greatness of the Day in which He appeared. He says it in many ways: it is the Day every Prophet longed for, the King of Days, the Day of God Himself, a Day in which an atom is as the sun. This series sorts what the Writings and their authorised interpreters say about that greatness into twelve facets and gives each facet its own page. Each page sets out the passages, the Writings first, then Shoghi Effendi and the Universal House of Justice, and ends with notes for songwriting. Lessan and Melissa plan a series of songs, one for each facet.

The series starts from Shoghi Effendi’s group of thirty-one passages on “The Greatness of the Day of God” in The Advent of Divine Justice, traced in “Dearly Beloved Friends”, and goes beyond it into the whole authoritative corpus. The searches covered Gleanings, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, Days of Remembrance, the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, the Kitáb-i-Íqán, Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, Prayers and Meditations, the shorter compilations of Bahá’u’lláh’s Tablets, the Selections of the Báb and of ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, Some Answered Questions, the recorded talks, Tablets of the Divine Plan, five works of Shoghi Effendi, and the House of Justice’s books and messages. About 450 candidate passages were checked against the source files; 248 are kept in the passage bank (`kb/library/passage-bank/greatness-of-this-day.json`), each tagged with its facet.

The twelve facets

  1. The Day the Prophets Longed For. Every Prophet announced this Day and longed to see it, and the promise of past ages is now fulfilled. Bahá’u’lláh: “The soul of every Prophet of God, of every Divine Messenger, hath thirsted for this wondrous Day.” (Gleanings VII.2)
  1. The King of Days. This Day has no equal among days: past ages cannot rival it, all days reach their consummation in it, and it will not be followed by night. Shoghi Effendi reads “not followed by night” as the continuity of guidance through the Covenant. Bahá’u’lláh: “This is the Day that shall not be followed by night, nor shall it be bounded by any praise” (Summons of the Lord of Hosts 1.63).
  1. The Day of God Himself. This is the Day of God Himself, not of a lesser Messenger: the Beloved Himself now calls His lovers. The page also sets out what the texts do not claim. Bahá’u’lláh: “This is the Day of God Himself” (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.42).
  1. Envied by the Concourse on High. The Concourse on high and the holy ones of past ages look toward this Day and those alive in it. The texts draw from this a privilege and a responsibility. Bahá’u’lláh, of the one who defends the Cause: “the Concourse on high would envy his glory” (Gleanings CLIV.1).
  1. An Atom as the Sun. In this Day small acts weigh a great deal, and the deed asked for must be worthy of the Day. Bahá’u’lláh: “An atom, in these days, is as the sun, a drop as the ocean.” (quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice)
  1. The Creative Word. The Word revealed in this Day has creative power: it gives new life and new capacities to humankind and to the world. Bahá’u’lláh: “breathed a new life into every human frame, and instilled into every word a fresh potency” (Gleanings XLIII.2).
  1. The Divine Springtime. The coming of the Manifestation is the spiritual spring, and Bahá’u’lláh has renewed it. Bahá’u’lláh: “The Divine Springtime is come, O Most Exalted Pen” (Gleanings XIV.1).
  1. The Resurrection. This Day is the Day of Resurrection the Scriptures foretold: those dead in spirit are raised and a new creation begins. Bahá’u’lláh: “the people have come forth from their graves, and arising, are gazing around them” (Gleanings XVII.4).
  1. Grace Poured Out on All. God’s favours, mercy and forgiveness are poured out on all created things in this Day. Bahá’u’lláh: “This is the Day in which God’s most excellent favors have been poured out upon men” (Gleanings IV.1).
  1. All Things Rejoice. Creation answers the Day with joy: land and sea, the faculties of hearing and sight, the realm above. Bahá’u’lláh: the faculty of sight calls aloud, “Verily this is my Day” (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh 17.35).
  1. The Coming of Age of Humanity. This Day opens humanity’s coming of age: its maturity, its unity and a new World Order. Shoghi Effendi places the full coming of age in the future. Bahá’u’lláh: “One of the signs of the maturity of the world is that no one will accept to bear the weight of kingship.” (quoted in the notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, n. 194)
  1. The Day Can Be Missed. The greatness of the Day does not compel anyone to recognise it; the texts describe people asleep, veiled, and waiting at the gate. Bahá’u’lláh: the Celestial Youth stands “wondering what eye will recognize His glory” (Gleanings LIII.1).

Facets considered and merged or dropped

The run brief proposed starting points and asked that each facet have at least six strong passages, some of them from Bahá’u’lláh. These decisions were made from the passages found:

  • Spring and resurrection were proposed together and are kept as two facets. The texts use them differently: spring is renewal and growth, while resurrection is judgement and the raising of the dead in spirit. Each has more than a dozen passages.
  • The Day not followed by night is part of The King of Days. There are four passages for it (the Summons, two in God Passes By, and a message of the House of Justice of 14 March 1996), which is too few for a page of its own, and they belong with the passages on the Day’s place among all days.
  • The day of union, the ingathering of humankind is part of The Coming of Age of Humanity, where Shoghi Effendi and the House of Justice place it.
  • The hidden revealed, the veils lifted is part of The Day of God Himself: in the texts, what is unveiled is God’s own revelation of Himself.
  • The station of those who recognise and arise is divided between Envied by the Concourse on High (the privilege of being alive now) and An Atom as the Sun (the deed). The assurance of help for those who arise is the subject of Promises of Assistance and is not repeated here.
  • This century, “as a hundred other ages”, is mostly in ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s Tablets and talks, with few passages from Bahá’u’lláh. Its passages are placed in The Creative Word and The King of Days.
  • The warnings, the “grievous Day”, are dropped as a facet. They are a separate group in the Advent of Divine Justice compilation, already traced in “Dearly Beloved Friends”. Their side that concerns recognition is in The Day Can Be Missed.
  • The Day foretold, tradition by tradition (Isaiah, the Gospels, the Qur’án and other scriptures), is the subject of the companion series The Day Foretold and is kept out of these pages.

How the facets relate to the sequence of greatness, standard and assurance

On 2026-10-13 Lessan described what lifts him as a sequence: the greatness of the Day, then the standard expected, then the assurance of assistance for any step. The tracing in “Dearly Beloved Friends” found that sequence in Shoghi Effendi’s compilation, with the three parts mixed at every stage rather than laid end to end. The facets show the same mixing.

Most of the facets are about the greatness itself. Three carry it over into the other two parts:

  • An Atom as the Sun joins greatness to the standard. The same Day that makes an atom “as the sun” asks for a “matchless” deed (Gleanings XVI.1).
  • Envied by the Concourse on High joins greatness to responsibility. The Universal House of Justice, after quoting Bahá’u’lláh’s words on Mount Carmel to the believers gathered there, wrote: “Such a privilege carries with it an equally great responsibility” (24 May 2001).
  • The Day Can Be Missed supplies the urgency: the Day is great, and it can still pass a person by.

The assurance itself, the third part, is the subject of Promises of Assistance. The House of Justice joins the first part to action in one sentence: “How immensely blessed are those souls who, alive to the greatness of this Day and the significance of their actions, strive for the emergence of a society shaped by the divine teachings.” (30 December 2021)

What the series could not settle

  • Source Tablets. Many of Bahá’u’lláh’s passages on the greatness of the Day are available in English only as Shoghi Effendi quoted them in The Advent of Divine Justice, The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh and God Passes By. Their source Tablets are not identified in this corpus. The bank records them as “Tablet not identified” with the work that quotes them.
  • Three passages first credited to the wrong speaker. In “The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh” (The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, paragraph 6.31), Shoghi Effendi moves from Bahá’u’lláh’s words to ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s. The passages there on “ten thousand lives”, the holy ones who “yearned with tearful eyes” and the dwellers of Paradise who long “to return unto this world” are ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s. The bank and the pages attribute them to Him.
  • Commentary inside Turning Point. Parts II and III of Turning Point are not messages of the House of Justice; the book’s preface says Part II “should not be considered a statement of authoritative guidance”. Passages from those parts were left out.
  • Use in published songs. The texts come from the Bahá’í Reference Library. Its terms of use allow use with attribution and require prior permission for commercial use. Whether a song or video on a monetised channel counts as commercial use is not settled here; each bank entry carries a note saying so.
