The Creative Word

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

In the Writings, the Word that a Manifestation of God utters is an act of creation, not only a message. Bahá’u’lláh says that the Word of this Day has put a new life into every human frame, a fresh potency into every word, and capacities into every created thing that it did not have before. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá points to the widening of the sciences, arts and inventions of the age as an effect of that Word. Shoghi Effendi and the Universal House of Justice describe what has been released: creative energies that gave rise to the Administrative Order, and a creative force of unity whose potentialities are now free to act.

This page is one facet of the series The greatness of this Day. It sets out what the texts say the Word does. It does not describe what people should do in response; other pages in the series take that up.

The passages

A new life in every human frame

Bahá’u’lláh writes, in a passage that Shoghi Effendi later placed in the group on “The Regeneration of Human Life” in The Advent of Divine Justice:

Through the movement of Our Pen of glory We have, at the bidding of the omnipotent Ordainer, breathed a new life into every human frame, and instilled into every word a fresh potency. All created things proclaim the evidences of this worldwide regeneration.

(Gleanings XLIII.2)

The claim has two objects: human beings (“every human frame”) and speech (“every word”). The first sentence says what the Pen has done; the second says the result is visible in all created things, not only in believers. The act is attributed to the Pen “at the bidding of the omnipotent Ordainer”, so the source of the new life is God’s command, carried out through Bahá’u’lláh’s revelation.

In another Tablet Bahá’u’lláh observes that this new life is at work among people who do not recognise it:

A new life is, in this age, stirring within all the peoples of the earth; and yet none hath discovered its cause or perceived its motive.

(Gleanings XCVI.2)

Here the new life is not limited to those who accept the Revelation. It stirs “within all the peoples of the earth”, and its cause is unrecognised. The two passages together give the Bahá’í reading of a world in motion: the cause is the Word, and most of the world has not yet named it.

Potentialities carried by every creature

Bahá’u’lláh describes what the Word has put into creation:

Say: In this day, the fertilizing winds of the grace of God have passed over all things. Every creature hath been endowed with all the potentialities it can carry. And yet the peoples of the world have denied this grace! Every tree hath been endowed with the choicest fruits, every ocean enriched with the most luminous gems.

(The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, SLH 1.47)

The grace is given to every creature in the measure it can carry. The passage does not say that every creature has already borne fruit; it says each has been endowed. The denial belongs to the peoples, not to the grace.

For human beings, Bahá’u’lláh states the end of the process:

Through the Teachings of this Daystar of Truth every man will advance and develop until he attaineth the station at which he can manifest all the potential forces with which his inmost true self hath been endowed.

(Gleanings XXVII.5)

The verbs are future and progressive: “will advance and develop until”. The Teachings are the means, and the goal is that a person manifests the forces already present in the “inmost true self”. The Revelation does not add a second self; it enables the existing one to appear.

The First Word and a new creation

In a prayer, Bahá’u’lláh bears witness to the effect of the First Word of a Manifestation:

I testify that no sooner had the First Word proceeded, through the potency of Thy will and purpose, out of His mouth, and the First Call gone forth from His lips than the whole creation was revolutionized, and all that are in the heavens and all that are on earth were stirred to the depths. Through that Word the realities of all created things were shaken, were divided, separated, scattered, combined and reunited, disclosing, in both the contingent world and the heavenly kingdom, entities of a new creation, and revealing, in the unseen realms, the signs and tokens of Thy unity and oneness.

(Prayers and Meditations CLXXVIII.1)

The prayer describes a process in stages: shaking, division, separation, scattering, combination, reunion. The result is “entities of a new creation”, disclosed in both the visible and the spiritual worlds. A later paragraph of the same prayer adds a consequence that is easy to omit:

The forces of dissension were released, the meaning of the Word was unfolded, and every several atom in all created things acquired its own distinct and separate character.

(Prayers and Meditations CLXXVIII.1)

Dissension is named as one of the forces released along with the unfolding of the meaning. The creative Word first separates and distinguishes, and the passage does not say that the reunion is complete.

Bahá’u’lláh also speaks of the pace of the new creation. In the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf He quotes the Báb:

Ere nine will have elapsed from the inception of this Cause, the realities of the created things will not be made manifest. All that thou hast as yet seen is but the stage from the moist germ until We clothed it with flesh. Be patient, until thou beholdest a new creation.

(Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 222)

The Epistle introduces this with “And likewise, He saith”, in a sequence of sayings of the Primal Point. The passage compares the Cause to a development with stages. What has been seen so far is an early stage, and the “new creation” is promised, not yet complete. The passage as given does not say what “nine” counts.

The power of the letter and the word

Two passages that Shoghi Effendi quotes from Tablets he does not identify say what a single utterance carries. In The Advent of Divine Justice (ADJ 110, 111), the Guardian quotes Bahá’u’lláh:

This Day a different Sun hath arisen, and a different Heaven hath been adorned with its stars and its planets. The world is another world, and the Cause another Cause.

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 110; source Tablet not identified)

Every single letter proceeding from Our mouth is endowed with such regenerative power as to enable it to bring into existence a new creation—a creation the magnitude of which is inscrutable to all save God. He verily hath knowledge of all things.

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice, ADJ 111; source Tablet not identified)

The first passage states a change of kind (“another world”); the second locates the cause in the smallest unit of speech, a single letter, and says the magnitude of the result cannot be measured by anyone but God. A third passage, quoted in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, applies the same principle to ordinary words:

The word which the one true God uttereth in this day, though that word be the most familiar and commonplace of terms, is invested with supreme, with unique distinction.

(Bahá’u’lláh, quoted in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.18; source Tablet not identified)

The power is attributed to the speaker, not to the vocabulary. A common word spoken by God in this Day carries what no word carried before.

The Summons of the Lord of Hosts adds a statement of what God could do with a word, if He wished:

It is in Our power, should We wish it, to enable a speck of floating dust to generate, in less than the twinkling of an eye, suns of infinite, of unimaginable splendour, to cause a dewdrop to develop into vast and numberless oceans, to infuse into every letter such a force as to empower it to unfold all the knowledge of past and future ages.

(The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, paragraph 1.75)

This passage is a statement of power, in the conditional (“should We wish it”). It is not a report of what has happened. It sets the scale of what God can do through a letter.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá on the advances of the age

‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in a letter written for Riḍván to be read at a feast (the Selections give the occasion in the preceding paragraphs), looks at the age itself:

See how, in this day, the scope of sciences and arts hath widened out, and what wondrous technical advances have been made, and to what a high degree the mind’s powers have increased, and what stupendous inventions have appeared.

(Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá 73.5)

This age is indeed as a hundred other ages: should ye gather the yield of a hundred ages, and set that against the accumulated product of our times, the yield of this one era will prove greater than that of a hundred gone before. Take ye, for an example, the sum total of all the books that were ever written in ages past, and compare that with the books and treatises that our era hath produced: these books, written in our day alone, far and away exceed the total number of volumes that have been written down the ages. See how powerful is the influence exerted by the Daystar of the world upon the inner essence of all created things!

(Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá 73.6)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá gives two kinds of evidence, the technical and the written, and then names the cause in the last sentence: the influence of “the Daystar of the world upon the inner essence of all created things”. He does not treat the advances as separate from the Revelation. They are what the light of this Day does to the mind and to the world.

In another Tablet he writes of the Word acting on humankind itself:

In this wondrous Age, at this time when the Ancient Beauty, the Most Great Name, bearing unnumbered gifts, hath risen above the horizon of the world, the Word of God hath infused such awesome power into the inmost essence of humankind that He hath stripped men’s human qualities of all effect, and hath, with His all-conquering might, unified the peoples in a vast sea of oneness.

(Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá 7.2)

The Word acts on “the inmost essence of humankind”, and the effect named is oneness. The Universal House of Justice quotes this passage in Century of Light (see below).

Shoghi Effendi: creative energies

Shoghi Effendi uses the same vocabulary of creation when he describes the Will and Testament of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. He writes in God Passes By:

The creative energies unleashed by the Originator of the Law of God in this age gave birth, through their impact upon the mind of Him Who had been chosen as its unerring Expounder, to that Instrument, the vast implications of which the present generation, even after the lapse of twenty-three years, is still incapable of fully apprehending.

(God Passes By XXII.6)

The “Instrument” is the Will and Testament. In the paragraph before this one, Shoghi Effendi contrasts the Covenant of Bahá’u’lláh, instituted “solely through the direct operation of His Will and purpose”, with the Will and Testament, which he calls the offspring of Bahá’u’lláh, “Who had generated the forces of a God-given Faith”, and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, “the One Who had been made its sole Interpreter”. The creative energies of the Revelation, acting on ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s mind, produced an institution whose implications are still not fully understood.

The Universal House of Justice: new capacities, released potentialities

In Century of Light, the House of Justice describes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s journeys to the West and what He asked of the people He addressed:

In arising to undertake their mission, He promised that they would find unlocked in both themselves and others entirely new capacities with which God has in this Day endowed the human race:

(Century of Light III.24)

The passage ends with a colon because the House of Justice then quotes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s words, the passage from Selections 7.2 given above. The statement of the House of Justice adds a promise to those who undertake the mission: they will find the capacities in others as well as in themselves.

In a later chapter the House of Justice writes of the creative force of unity, which it has just described as “the primary influence driving the advancement of civilization”:

Through the Revelation of God to humanity’s coming of age, the full potentialities of this creative force have at last been released and the means necessary to the realization of the Divine purpose have been instituted.

(Century of Light IV.7)

The “creative force” in this sentence is unity, as the preceding paragraph states. The sentence names two things: potentialities that were not fully available before have been released, and the means to realise them have been set up, through the Will and Testament and the twin institutions of the Guardianship and the House of Justice, as the sentences that follow explain.

How this facet connects

  • The Resurrection treats the same Day under the image of rising to new life. This page adds what the Word produces in those who are raised: new life, new capacities, a new creation.
  • The Divine Springtime uses the image of a season. The texts here describe the same renewal in terms of creation and potentialities that creatures carry, rather than seasons.
  • The Coming of Age of Humanity sets out why capacities are released now. The House of Justice’s sentence in Century of Light IV.7 ties the release of potentialities to humanity’s coming of age.
  • An Atom as the Sun takes up the smallest creature made great. The passages here on the letter, the dewdrop and the speck of dust bear on it.
  • “Dearly Beloved Friends” traces the group of passages on the regeneration of human life that Shoghi Effendi compiled in The Advent of Divine Justice. Several passages above (ADJ 110, 111; Gleanings XLIII.2) come from that compilation, and the article gives what is known of their Tablets and of other translations.

What the sources do not settle

  • Three passages quoted by Shoghi Effendi (ADJ 110, ADJ 111, WOB 6.18) come from Tablets that are not identified in this corpus, so their occasion and addressee are not known here.
  • The prayer in Prayers and Meditations CLXXVIII refers to “the First Word” of “His mouth” without naming the Manifestation in the lines quoted. Which Manifestation and which First Word are meant is not stated in these passages, and this page does not supply it.
  • The quotation from the Báb in the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf says “Ere nine”; the passage does not say what is counted. The original wording is not in this corpus.
  • ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s account of advances in science, arts and books is made in a letter for a Riḍván feast. It does not say which advances it has in mind beyond those it names, and it does not claim that every advance is beneficial.
  • Gleanings XCVI.2 says the cause of the new life is undiscovered. The sources do not say when, or by whom, it will be discovered.

For songwriting

Singable phrases

  • “breathed a new life into every human frame” (Gleanings XLIII.2)
  • “instilled into every word a fresh potency” (Gleanings XLIII.2)
  • “A new life is, in this age, stirring” (Gleanings XCVI.2)
  • “the fertilizing winds of the grace of God” (SLH 1.47)
  • “Every tree hath been endowed with the choicest fruits” (SLH 1.47)
  • “every ocean enriched with the most luminous gems” (SLH 1.47)
  • “the whole creation was revolutionized” (Prayers and Meditations CLXXVIII.1)
  • “Be patient, until thou beholdest a new creation.” (ESW 222)
  • “The world is another world, and the Cause another Cause.” (ADJ 110)
  • “a different Sun hath arisen” (ADJ 110)
  • “Every single letter proceeding from Our mouth” (ADJ 111)
  • “a speck of floating dust” (SLH 1.75)
  • “a vast sea of oneness” (SWAB 7.2)

Concrete images

  • The Pen moving, and a new life entering a human frame.
  • Winds passing over all things; trees with fruit; oceans with gems.
  • A speck of dust, a dewdrop, one letter, each holding more than it seems to.
  • The germ developing to flesh, then “a new creation”.
  • A different sun and a different heaven with its own stars and planets.
  • The stack of books written in one era against the books of all earlier ages.
  • Peoples gathered into a sea.

A possible emotional arc

  1. A stirring that nobody can name: “A new life is, in this age, stirring” (Gleanings XCVI.2).
  2. The cause named: the Pen and the Word, and the new life in “every human frame” (Gleanings XLIII.2).
  3. What each creature carries: trees with fruit, oceans with gems, and the person who will “manifest all the potential forces” of the inmost self (SLH 1.47; Gleanings XXVII.5).
  4. The small made large: one letter, one common word, a speck of dust (ADJ 111; WOB 6.18; SLH 1.75).
  5. Patience and arrival: “Be patient, until thou beholdest a new creation” (ESW 222), ending on the sea of oneness (SWAB 7.2).

What a lyric must not claim

  • Do not say the new creation is complete. Bahá’u’lláh’s quotation from the Báb in ESW 222 describes stages and asks for patience, and the House of Justice says the potentialities “have at last been released”, not that they have all been realised.
  • Do not attribute ESW 222 to Bahá’u’lláh as his own words; he is quoting the Báb. Do not attribute ADJ 110 and 111 or WOB 6.18 to a named Tablet.
  • Do not attribute SWAB 7.2 to Bahá’u’lláh or to the House of Justice. It is ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s; the House of Justice quotes it.
  • Do not turn the passage on the age’s advances (SWAB 73.5–6) into a statement that all technology is good, or that the Faith is the same as scientific progress. The passage says the influence of the Daystar is behind the growth in the mind’s powers.
  • Do not turn the conditional of SLH 1.75 (“should We wish it”) into a report that dust has become suns.
  • Do not present the First Word of the prayer as identified. The text does not name the Manifestation in the lines quoted.
  • Do not turn “Ere nine” into a date or a count of years.
  • Do not use “forces of dissension were released” as a general warning against the Word; it describes one stage of the process.
