The Day Foretold: The Báb’s Promise of Him Whom God Shall Make Manifest

Last updated 17 October 2026

What this page covers

This page is part of the series gathered at the-day-foretold, one page for each tradition whose scripture looks ahead to the Day of Bahá’u’lláh. See also promises-of-the-future, the-hour-of-dawn, greatness-of-this-day-the-day-can-be-missed and the sibling pages the-day-foretold-hindu, the-day-foretold-jewish, the-day-foretold-zoroastrian, the-day-foretold-buddhist, the-day-foretold-christian and the-day-foretold-islamic.

For this tradition the scripture of the promise is the Báb’s own Writings, held in this corpus in the authorised translation (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, cited as SWB). No scripture from outside the corpus is needed. The Báb’s term for the One He foretold is “Him Whom God shall make manifest” (Persian Man yuẓhiruhu’lláh). In God Passes By, Shoghi Effendi’s list of the titles of Bahá’u’lláh includes “He Whom God will make manifest” (GPB VI.12). This page uses the Báb’s term when it reports what the Báb said, and “Bahá’u’lláh” where Bahá’u’lláh, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Shoghi Effendi or the Universal House of Justice identify Him.

The page keeps four kinds of source apart:

  • The Báb’s words (section 1): the authorised Selections. A few passages come from translations that are not authorised (Denis MacEoin’s Persian Bayán, E. G. Browne’s abstract); these are marked ”provisional translation“.
  • The Writings that state the fulfilment (section 2): Bahá’u’lláh, Shoghi Effendi, and the Notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas prepared by the Universal House of Justice. Bahá’u’lláh quotes the Báb often, and those passages are the Báb’s words in Bahá’u’lláh’s quotation.
  • History (section 4): Nabíl, Balyuzi, Taherzadeh, and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s A Traveller’s Narrative.
  • Commentary and scholarship (section 3): Bahá’í authors and academic scholars, quoted briefly and labelled. Their readings are theirs unless the Writings say the same.

Section 5 covers music, section 6 what the sources leave open, section 7 notes for songwriting, and section 8 the sources.

1. The promise: the Báb’s own words

The passages in this section are from the Báb, in the authorised translation, unless a line says ”provisional translation“. The Báb mentions Him Whom God shall make manifest throughout the Persian Bayán and in His other books and letters. The Selections gather only part of this.

Who He is and what His station is

The first tablet in the Selections is addressed to Him:

”This is an epistle from this lowly servant to the All-Glorious Lord—He Who hath been aforetime and will be hereafter made manifest. Verily He is the Most Manifest, the Almighty.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 1.1)

”This is an epistle from the letter Thá unto Him Who will be made manifest through the power of Truth…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 1.3)

The Báb writes of the weight of His mention:

”Know thou of a certainty that whenever thou makest mention of Him Whom God shall make manifest, only then art thou making mention of God.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.13)

and of His station:

”IT is not permissible to ask questions from Him Whom God will make manifest, except that which well beseemeth Him. For His station is that of the Essence of divine Revelation.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.63)

The Universal House of Justice records the rule that follows from this, in a note to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas: ”The Báb forbade His followers to ask questions of Him Whom God will make manifest (Bahá’u’lláh), unless their questions were submitted in writing and pertained to subjects worthy of His lofty station“ (Notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 146).

”Say, He Whom God shall make manifest is indeed the Primal Veil of God. Above this Veil ye can find nothing other than God, while beneath it ye can discern all things emanating from God. He is the Unseen, the Inaccessible, the Most Exalted, the Best-Beloved.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.7)

Many passages use the sun and the mirror. The Báb compares Him to the sun, and the believers to mirrors, and says that the sun does not need the mirrors:

”HE—glorified be His mention—resembleth the sun. Were unnumbered mirrors to be placed before it, each would, according to its capacity, reflect the splendor of that sun, and were none to be placed before it, it would still continue to rise and set, and the mirrors alone would be veiled from its light.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 14.15)

In the Qayyúmu’l-Asmá’, the first of His two chief books (GPB V.10), the Báb cries out to the “Remnant of God”. Bahá’u’lláh quotes this cry in the Kitáb-i-Íqán (KI 258), and Shoghi Effendi lists “the Remnant of God” among the titles by which the Báb extolled Bahá’u’lláh (GPB VI.21, section 2):

”O THOU Remnant of God! I have sacrificed myself wholly for Thee; I have accepted curses for Thy sake, and have yearned for naught but martyrdom in the path of Thy love. Sufficient witness unto me is God, the Exalted, the Protector, the Ancient of Days.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 10.59)

The Universal House of Justice notes that the Báb names Him “the Living Book”: ”These words contain an allusion to a statement of the Báb in the Persian Bayán about the “Living Book,” which He identifies as Him Whom God will make manifest. In one of His Tablets Bahá’u’lláh Himself states: “The Book of God hath been sent down in the form of this Youth.”” (Notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 155).

The Bayán as a gift and a preparation

In the second of the two tablets He addressed to Him, the Báb offers the Bayán and its people as a gift, and asks for a respite:

“…to affirm that the Bayán and such as bear allegiance to it are but a present from me unto Thee…” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 2.3)

“…do Thou grant a respite of nineteen years as a token of Thy favor so that those who have embraced this Cause may be graciously rewarded by Thee.…” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 2.3)

The laws and the Book are given for His sake:

“We have planted the Garden of the Bayán in the name of Him Whom God will make manifest, and have granted you permission to live therein until the time of His manifestation; then from the moment the Cause of Him Whom God will make manifest is inaugurated, We forbid you all the things ye hold as your own, unless ye may, by the leave of your Lord, be able to regain possession thereof.” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.23)

“But for the sole reason of His being present amongst this people, We would have neither prescribed any law nor laid down any prohibition.…” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.65)

In a prayer, the Báb asks that the whole Tree of the Bayán be made into a scroll and offered to Him:

“SEND down Thy blessings, O my God, upon the Tree of the Bayán, upon its root and its branch, its boughs, its leaves, its fruits and upon whatsoever it beareth or sheltereth. Cause this Tree then to be made into a magnificent Scroll to be offered to the presence of Him Whom Thou wilt make manifest on the Day of Judgment, that He may graciously allow the entire company of the followers of the Bayán to be restored to life and that He may, through His bounty, inaugurate a new creation.” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 15.4)

The Bayán is God’s balance until His Day, and a thousand readings do not equal one verse of His:

“THE Bayán shall constitute God’s unerring balance till the Day of Resurrection which is the Day of Him Whom God will make manifest.…” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.65)

“I SWEAR by the most holy Essence of God—exalted and glorified be He—that in the Day of the appearance of Him Whom God shall make manifest a thousand perusals of the Bayán cannot equal the perusal of a single verse to be revealed by Him Whom God shall make manifest.” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.69)

The Báb tells the believers to be trained in the Bayán so that no one holds to its outward form and loses Him (SWB 11.24), and that the sum of religion is to help Him:

“Take good heed of yourselves, for the sum total of the religion of God is but to help Him, rather than to observe, in the time of His appearance, such deeds as are prescribed in the Bayán.…” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.25)

The ring, and the Báb’s own place

The Báb says that He has attained the Day of the First Manifestation and will attain the Day of the Latter Manifestation:

“Verily I have attained the Day of the First Manifestation and by the bidding of the Lord and as a token of His grace, I shall attain the Day of the Latter Manifestation.…” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 3.6)

He lists the Days in order, up to “the One Who will appear after Him Whom God shall make manifest”:

“In the time of the First Manifestation the Primal Will appeared in Adam; in the day of Noah It became known in Noah; in the day of Abraham in Him; and so in the day of Moses; the day of Jesus; the day of Muḥammad, the Apostle of God; the day of the “Point of the Bayán”; the day of Him Whom God shall make manifest; and the day of the One Who will appear after Him Whom God shall make manifest.…” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 12.23)

The best-known statement of His own place is the image of the ring. Bahá’u’lláh repeats it in the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf (ESW 225), and Shoghi Effendi summarises it (GPB VI.21):

“O congregation of the Bayán, and all who are therein! Recognize ye the limits imposed upon you, for such a One as the Point of the Bayán Himself hath believed in Him Whom God shall make manifest, before all things were created. Therein, verily, do I glory before all who are in the kingdom of heaven and earth. Suffer not yourselves to be shut out as by a veil from God after He hath revealed Himself. For all that hath been exalted in the Bayán is but as a ring upon My hand, and I Myself am, verily, but a ring upon the hand of Him Whom God shall make manifest—glorified be His mention! He turneth it as He pleaseth, for whatsoever He pleaseth, and through whatsoever He pleaseth. He, verily, is the Help in Peril, the Most High.” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 14.58)

In another passage the Báb says that His aim in planting the Tree of the Bayán was that people should recognise Him, that He is the first to bow down, and that the Bayán bears fealty to Him Whom God shall make manifest. The phrase “He is I and I am He” is the Báb’s. Elsewhere He writes of the Manifestations that ”what shineth resplendent in each one of Them hath been and will ever remain the one and the same sun“ (SWB 12.23).

”IT behooveth you to await the Day of the appearance of Him Whom God shall manifest. Indeed My aim in planting the Tree of the Bayán hath been none other than to enable you to recognize Me. In truth I Myself am the first to bow down before God and to believe in Him. Therefore let not your recognition become fruitless, inasmuch as the Bayán, notwithstanding the sublimity of its station, beareth fealty to Him Whom God shall make manifest, and it is He Who beseemeth most to be acclaimed as the Seat of divine Reality, though indeed He is I and I am He. However, when the Tree of the Bayán attaineth its highest development, We shall bend it low as a token of adoration towards its Lord Who will appear in the person of Him Whom God shall make manifest. Perchance ye may be privileged to glorify God as it befitteth His august Self.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 14.56)

On the same Day, deeds done for the Point of the Bayán are not to be put in His place:

”AT the time of the appearance of Him Whom God shall make manifest, wert thou to perform thy deeds for the sake of the Point of the Bayán, they would be regarded as performed for one other than God, inasmuch as on that Day the Point of the Bayán is none other than Him Whom God shall make manifest.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.50)

The Day of Resurrection

The Báb says what He means by the Day of Resurrection:

”THE substance of this chapter is this, that what is intended by the Day of Resurrection is the Day of the appearance of the Tree of divine Reality, but it is not seen that anyone of the followers of Shí‘ih Islám hath understood the meaning of the Day of Resurrection; rather have they fancifully imagined a thing which with God hath no reality. In the estimation of God and according to the usage of such as are initiated into divine mysteries, what is meant by the Day of Resurrection is this, that from the time of the appearance of Him Who is the Tree of divine Reality, at whatever period and under whatever name, until the moment of His disappearance, is the Day of Resurrection.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.74)

He says that the Day can pass unnoticed, and also that it can come suddenly:

”THE Day of Resurrection is a day on which the sun riseth and setteth like unto any other day. How oft hath the Day of Resurrection dawned, and the people of the land where it occurred did not learn of the event. Had they heard, they would not have believed, and thus they were not told!“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.7)

”He will shine forth suddenly, and will pronounce judgment as He pleaseth.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.42)

He gives Paradise and the fire a meaning:

”And know thou of a certainty that by Paradise is meant recognition of and submission unto Him Whom God shall make manifest, and by the fire the company of such souls as would fail to submit unto Him or to be resigned to His good-pleasure.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.17)

and says that outward piety does not decide the matter:

”Among those to whom it will never occur that they might merit the displeasure of God, and whose pious deeds will be exemplary unto everyone, there will be many who will become the personification of the nethermost fire itself, when they fail to embrace His Cause…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.20)

He compares the coming of Him Whom God shall make manifest with the first years of the Qur’án and with His own day (SWB 11.18, 11.20). In His own day, He writes, the people who had longed for His advent were resting in their homes while He was confined in Máh-Kú:

”Likewise in the manifestation of the Point of the Bayán, the people stood up at the mention of His Name and fervently implored His advent night and day, and if they dreamt of Him they gloried in their dreams; yet now that He hath revealed Himself, invested with the mightiest testimony, whereby their own religion is vindicated, and despite the incalculable number of people who yearningly anticipate His coming, they are resting comfortably in their homes, after having hearkened to His verses; while He at this moment is confined in the mountain of Máh-Kú, lonely and forsaken.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.22)

Provisional translation by Denis MacEoin (Persian Bayán). The file says it is a partial translation done in haste and may contain errors.

”When his day comes, whoever enters into his book shall be saved from the fire“ (Persian Bayán, provisional translation by Denis MacEoin, Váhid 2, Báb 4)

The warning not to be veiled

The Báb repeats one warning more than any other: that the followers of the Bayán must not let the Bayán keep them from Him.

”O YE unto whom the Bayán is given! Be ye vigilant lest in the days of Him Whom God shall make manifest, while ye consider yourselves as seeking God’s pleasure, in reality ye persist in that which would only displease Him, even as did those who lived in the days of the Primal Point, to whom it never occurred that they were seeking things which ran counter to that which God had purposed.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.24)

”Take good heed of yourselves, O people of the Bayán, lest ye perform such deeds as to weep sore for His sake night and day, to stand up at the mention of His Name, yet on this Day of fruition—a Day whereon ye should not only arise at His Name, but seek a path unto Him Who personifies that Name—ye shut yourselves out from Him as by a veil.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 11.23)

”If ye seek God, it behooveth you to seek Him Whom God shall make manifest…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.8)

”God will cause oceans of living water to stream forth from the presence of Him Whom God shall make manifest, while ye will refuse to quench your thirst therefrom, notwithstanding that ye regard yourselves as the God-fearing witnesses of your Faith.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.40)

”That day whereon ye were, by God’s Will, initiated into the Bayán, did any of you know who were the Letters of the Living, or the Witnesses, or the Testimonies, or what were the names of the believers? Likewise doth God wish you to recognize Him Whom God shall make manifest on the Day of Resurrection.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.62)

The Báb also uses the name “Bahá” in one of these passages (section 3 gives how Bahá’í authors read it):

”WHEN the Daystar of Bahá will shine resplendent above the horizon of eternity it is incumbent upon you to present yourselves before His Throne. Beware lest ye be seated in His presence or ask questions without His leave. Fear ye God, O concourse of the Mirrors.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 14.49)

Two further passages ask for restraint. The first concerns anyone who claims a Cause:

”O ye who are invested with the Bayán! Should ye be apprised of a person laying claim to a Cause and revealing verses which to outward seeming are unlikely to have been revealed by anyone else save God, the Help in Peril, the Self-Subsisting, do not pass sentence against him, lest ye may inadvertently pass sentence against Him Whom God shall make manifest. Say, He Whom God shall make manifest is but one of you; He will make Himself known unto you on the Day of Resurrection. Ye shall know God when the Manifestation of His Own Self is made known unto you, that perchance ye may not stray far from His Path.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.50)

The second concerns His decree:

”Say, were He Whom God shall make manifest to pronounce a pious and truthful follower of the Bayán as false, it is incumbent upon you to submit to His decree, as this hath been affirmed by God in the Bayán; verily God is able to convert light into fire whenever He pleaseth; surely He is potent over all things.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.44)

Letters to Him

In His address to Sulaymán, a Muslim divine of Masqaṭ, the Báb writes:

”Wert thou to address a letter to Him Whom God shall make manifest, begging that it be delivered unto His presence, perchance He would graciously forgive thee and, at His behest, turn thy negation into affirmation. He is in truth the All-Bountiful, the Most Generous, He Whose grace is infinite.…“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 9.7)

He says that otherwise nothing will be gained ”by reason of thy failure to respond “yea, here am I.”” (SWB 9.7).

The Universal House of Justice notes that some Bábís rejected Bahá’u’lláh’s claim because of a line the Báb wrote on the back of the first tablet:

“In this verse and the ones which immediately follow it, Bahá’u’lláh confronts one of the reasons some of the Bábís rejected His claim to be the Promised One of the Bayán. Their rejection was based on a Tablet addressed by the Báb to “Him Who will be made manifest” on the reverse side of which the Báb had written: “May the glances of Him Whom God shall make manifest illumine this letter at the primary school.”…” (Notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 185, Universal House of Justice)

Bahá’u’lláh’s answer is in the Kitáb-i-Aqdas (KA 176, section 2).

The time: “the year nine” and “after Ḥín”

Shoghi Effendi gathers the Báb’s statements of the time in one passage of God Passes By:

”“In the year nine,” He, referring to the date of the advent of the promised Revelation, has explicitly written, “ye shall attain unto all good.” “In the year nine, ye will attain unto the presence of God.” And again: “After Ḥín (68) a Cause shall be given unto you which ye shall come to know.” “Ere nine will have elapsed from the inception of this Cause,” He more particularly has stated, “the realities of the created things will not be made manifest. All that thou hast as yet seen is but the stage from the moist germ until We clothed it with flesh. Be patient, until thou beholdest a new creation. Say: ‘Blessed, therefore, be God, the most excellent of Makers!’” “Wait thou,” is His statement to ‘Aẓím, “until nine will have elapsed from the time of the Bayán. Then exclaim: ‘Blessed, therefore, be God, the most excellent of Makers!’” “Be attentive,” He, referring in a remarkable passage to the year nineteen, has admonished, “from the inception of the Revelation till the number of Váḥid (19).” “The Lord of the Day of Reckoning,” He, even more explicitly, has stated, “will be manifested at the end of Váḥid (19) and the beginning of eighty (1280 A.H.).” “Were He to appear this very moment,” He, in His eagerness to insure that the proximity of the promised Revelation should not withhold men from the Promised One, has revealed, “I would be the first to adore Him, and the first to bow down before Him.”” (God Passes By, GPB II.23–24)

Bahá’u’lláh quotes the Báb on the same period in the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf. The first passage is addressed to ’Aẓím:

“This, verily, is the thing We promised thee, ere the moment We answered thy call. Wait thou until nine will have elapsed from the time of the Bayán. Then exclaim: ‘Blessed, therefore, be God, the most excellent of Makers!’ Say: This, verily, is an Announcement which none except God hath comprehended. Ye, however, will be unaware on that day.” In the year nine this Most Great Revelation arose (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 205)

“In the year nine ye will attain unto all good.” On another occasion He saith: “In the year nine ye will attain unto the Presence of God.” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 204)

The Báb also writes of “after Ḥín”. In an epistle to Muḥammad Sháh He writes: ”Praise be unto Him Who at this very moment perceiveth in this remote prison the goal of My desire. He is the One Who beareth witness unto Me at all times and beholdeth Me ere the inception of “after Ḥín.”” (SWB 5.5). The Research Department of the Universal House of Justice adds a compiler’s note to the Selections:

“The numerical value of the letters of the word Ḥín is 68. The year 1268 A.H. (1851–1852 A.D.) is the year preceding the birth of the Bahá’í Revelation.” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, compiler’s note to SWB 5.1, Research Department of the Universal House of Justice)

Nabíl’s chronicle, translated by Shoghi Effendi, records the Báb quoting a verse of poetry, which He calls “divinely inspired”, about “a Youth of sugar-tongue” who is to appear in Shíráz and whose breath “should agitate and upset Baghdád”, and then His comment: “The mystery enshrined within this verse is now concealed; it will be revealed in the year after Hin” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl, history).

Numbers in the Persian Bayán (provisional). The Persian Bayán also gives two numbers that count days: Ghiyáth (1511) and Mustagháth (2001). They are known in the Bahá’í literature. The authorised Writings in this corpus do not give them, so they are reported here only as scholarly material. E. G. Browne’s 1889 abstract of the Persian Bayán (edited by Moojan Momen, 1987) gives the Báb saying that “No name amongst the names of God is equal to Mustaghath”, and section 3 gives the readings of Terry, Velasco and Gonzales. Section 2 gives Nabíl’s account of how Bahá’u’lláh explained the Mustagháth.

2. The fulfilment in the Bahá’í Writings

Bahá’u’lláh is Him Whom God shall make manifest

The statement that the promise is fulfilled in Bahá’u’lláh comes from Shoghi Effendi. The list of titles in GPB VI.12 includes “He Whom God will make manifest.” In the same book he gathers what the Báb wrote about Him:

“The Báb had no less significantly extolled Him as the “Essence of Being,” as the “Remnant of God,” as the “Omnipotent Master,” as the “Crimson, all-encompassing Light,” as “Lord of the visible and invisible,” as the “sole Object of all previous Revelations, including The Revelation of the Qá’im Himself.” He had formally designated Him as “He Whom God shall make manifest,”…” (God Passes By, GPB VI.21)

In the same passage, Shoghi Effendi lists further statements of the Báb: that He was the “first servant to believe in Him”; that He bore Him allegiance “before all things were created”; that He was “but “a letter” of that “Most Mighty Book,””; that He had “covenanted with all created things” concerning Him ere His own covenant; and that “all that hath been exalted in the Bayán” was “a ring” on His hand. He ends:

”Finally, He had unequivocally prophesied: “Today the Bayán is in the stage of seed; at the beginning of the manifestation of Him Whom God shall make manifest its ultimate perfection will become apparent.” “Ere nine will have elapsed from the inception of this Cause the realities of the created things will not be made manifest. All that thou hast as yet seen is but the stage from the moist-germ until We clothed it with flesh. Be patient until thou beholdest a new creation. Say: Blessed, therefore, be God, the Most Excellent of Makers!”” (God Passes By, GPB VI.21)

Shoghi Effendi adds that Bahá’u’lláh alone was the object of the Báb’s two chief books:

“Bahá’u’lláh alone was the object and the center of the cryptic allusions, the glowing eulogies, the fervid prayers, the joyful announcements and the dire warnings recorded in both the Qayyúmu’l-Asmá’ and the Bayán, designed to be respectively the first and last written testimonials to the glory with which God was soon to invest Him.” (God Passes By, GPB V.10)

On the Báb’s successor:

“A successor or vicegerent the Báb never named, an interpreter of His teachings He refrained from appointing. So transparently clear were His references to the Promised One, so brief was to be the duration of His own Dispensation, that neither the one nor the other was deemed necessary.…” (God Passes By, GPB II.21)

Bahá’u’lláh writes in a prayer for the Fast that the Báb is the Announcer of the One whose name joins the letters B and E:

“…Whom Thou hast appointed as the Announcer of the One through Whose name the letter B and the letter E have been joined and united,…” (Bahá’u’lláh, a prayer for the Fast (Bahá’í Prayers))

In the Tablet of Aḥmad, Bahá’u’lláh says of Himself and the Bayán:

“Verily this is that Most Great Beauty, foretold in the Books of the Messengers, through Whom truth shall be distinguished from error and the wisdom of every command shall be tested. Verily He is the Tree of Life that bringeth forth the fruits of God, the Exalted, the Powerful, the Great.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Tablet of Aḥmad (Bahá’í Prayers))

“Say: O people be obedient to the ordinances of God, which have been enjoined in the Bayán by the Glorious, the Wise One.…” (Bahá’u’lláh, Tablet of Aḥmad (Bahá’í Prayers))

The Universal House of Justice, in its Notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, records two points on the Báb’s references to the Promised One:

“The passage of the Bayán which Bahá’u’lláh here quotes draws the attention of the believers to the imminence of the coming of “Him Whom God will make manifest.”…” (Notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 158, Universal House of Justice)

“Mírzá Yaḥyá was nominated by the Báb to serve as a figurehead for the Bábí community pending the imminent manifestation of the Promised One.” (Notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 190, Universal House of Justice)

Bahá’u’lláh to the people of the Bayán

Bahá’u’lláh addresses the Bábís directly in many Tablets. He appeals to them, reasons with them, warns them, and in places speaks severely. These are His words, quoted here without comment.

In the Kitáb-i-Aqdas, He quotes the Bayán’s Qiblih verse:

“O people of the Bayán! Fear ye the Most Merciful and consider what He hath revealed in another passage. He said: “The Qiblih is indeed He Whom God will make manifest; whenever He moveth, it moveth, until He shall come to rest.” Thus was it set down by the Supreme Ordainer when He desired to make mention of this Most Great Beauty.…” (The Kitáb-i-Aqdas, KA 137)

“O people of the Bayán! We, verily, set foot within the School of God when ye lay slumbering; and We perused the Tablet while ye were fast asleep. By the one true God! We read the Tablet ere it was revealed, while ye were unaware, and We had perfect knowledge of the Book when ye were yet unborn.…” (The Kitáb-i-Aqdas, KA 176)

In Gleanings:

“Say: O people of the Bayán! We have chosen you out of the world to know and recognize Our Self. We have caused you to draw nigh unto the right side of Paradise—the Spot out of which the undying Fire crieth in manifold accents: “There is none other God besides Me, the All-Powerful, the Most High!” Take heed lest ye allow yourselves to be shut out as by a veil from this Daystar that shineth above the dayspring of the Will of your Lord, the All-Merciful, and whose light hath encompassed both the small and the great.…” (Gleanings from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh, GWB LII.4)

In the Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh:

“Say, did ye not hearken to the Voice of the Crier, calling aloud in the wilderness of the Bayán, bearing unto you the glad-tidings of the coming of your Lord, the All-Merciful? Lo! He is come in the sheltering shadow of Testimony, invested with conclusive proof and evidence, and those who truly believe in Him regard His presence as the embodiment of the Kingdom of God. Blessed is the man who turneth towards Him, and woe betide such as deny or doubt Him.” (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 2.9)

“O followers of the Bayán! Fear ye the All-Merciful. This is the One Who hath been glorified by Muḥammad, the Apostle of God, and before Him by the Spirit and yet before Him by the One Who discoursed with God. This is the Point of the Bayán calling aloud before the Throne, saying: “By the righteousness of God, ye have been created to glorify this Most Great Announcement, this Perfect Way which lay hid within the souls of the Prophets, which was treasured in the hearts of the chosen ones of God and was written down by the glorious Pen of your Lord, the Possessor of Names.”” (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 8.4)

“Arise thou to serve the Cause and say: I swear by the righteousness of God! Verily this is the Primal Point, arrayed in His new attire and manifested in His glorious Name. He at present beholdeth everything from this Horizon. Indeed He is supreme over all things. Amongst the Concourse on High He is known as the Most Great Announcement and in the Realms of Eternity as the Ancient Beauty, and before the Throne by this Name which hath caused the footsteps of them that are endued with understanding to slip.” (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 12.4)

In the Kitáb-i-Íqán, Bahá’u’lláh asks the people of the Bayán to keep to the essentials of their own Book:

“And now, We beseech the people of the Bayán, all the learned, the sages, the divines, and witnesses amongst them, not to forget the wishes and admonitions revealed in their Book. Let them, at all times, fix their gaze upon the essentials of His Cause, lest when He, Who is the Quintessence of truth, the inmost Reality of all things, the Source of all light, is made manifest, they cling unto certain passages of the Book, and inflict upon Him that which was inflicted in the Dispensation of the Qur’án.…” (The Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 101)

Bahá’u’lláh quotes the Báb

Bahá’u’lláh answers the Bábís largely with the Báb’s own words. The passages below are the Báb’s words, in Bahá’u’lláh’s quotation. On recognising Him:

“In like manner, the Primal Point saith: “Behold ye Him with His own eyes. Were ye to behold Him with the eyes of another, ye would never recognize and know Him.” This referreth to naught else except this Most Great Revelation.…” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 220)

On the Báb’s purpose, in Bahá’u’lláh’s explanation:

“Be fair, O people! The purpose of the Most Exalted One (the Báb) was to insure that the proximity of the Revelation should not withhold men from the Divine and everlasting Law, even as the companions of John (the Baptist) were prevented from acknowledging Him Who is the Spirit (Jesus). Time and again He hath said: “Suffer not the Bayán and all that hath been revealed therein to withhold you from that Essence of Being and Lord of the visible and invisible.”…” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 246)

On fires and lights, and on His appearing:

“…How many the fires which God converteth into light through Him Whom God shall make manifest; and how numerous the lights which are turned into fire through Him! I behold His appearance even as the sun in the midmost heaven, and the disappearance of all even as that of the stars of the night by day.”…” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 250)

The same paragraph (ESW 222) gathers further sayings of the Báb, as Bahá’u’lláh quotes them:

“Beware, beware lest, in the days of His Revelation, the Váḥid of the Bayán (eighteen Letters of the Living) shut thee out as by a veil from Him, inasmuch as this Váḥid is but a creature in His sight. And beware, beware that the words sent down in the Bayán shut thee not out as by a veil from Him.” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 222, quoting the Báb)

“Look not upon Him with any eye except His own. For whosoever looketh upon Him with His eye, will recognize Him; otherwise he will be veiled from Him. Shouldst thou seek God and His Presence, seek thou Him and gaze upon Him.” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 222, quoting the Báb)

“Better is it for thee to recite but one of the verses of Him Whom God shall make manifest than to set down the whole of the Bayán, for on that Day that one verse can save thee, whereas the entire Bayán cannot save thee.” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 222, quoting the Báb)

Gleanings quotes the Báb in two places. On what He wrote of the One to come:

”He Who is the Heart and Center of the Bayán hath written: “The germ that holdeth within itself the potentialities of the Revelation that is to come is endowed with a potency superior to the combined forces of all those who follow Me.” And, again, He saith: “Of all the tributes I have paid to Him Who is to come after Me, the greatest is this, My written confession, that no words of Mine can adequately describe Him, nor can any reference to Him in My Book, the Bayán, do justice to His Cause.”” (Gleanings from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh, GWB VI.2)

and on what He asked of the Bábís:

““O people!” said He, “I have revealed Myself for His Manifestation, and have caused My Book, the Bayán, to descend upon you for no other purpose except to establish the truth of His Cause. Fear ye God, and contend not with Him as the people of the Qur’án have contended with Me. At whatever time ye hear of Him, hasten ye towards Him, and cleave ye to whatsoever He may reveal unto you. Naught else besides Him can ever profit you, no, not though ye produce from first to last the testimonies of all those who were before you.”” (Gleanings from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh, GWB LXXVI.6)

The year nine and the Síyáh-Chál

Shoghi Effendi states that the Báb’s “year nine” was the year in which Bahá’u’lláh’s Revelation began, in the Síyáh-Chál (“Black Pit”) of Ṭihrán:

”During nine years, as foretold by the Báb Himself, swiftly, mysteriously and irresistibly the embryonic Faith conceived by Him had been developing until, at the fixed hour, the burden of the promised Cause of God was cast amidst the gloom and agony of the Síyáh-Chál of Ṭihrán.…“ (God Passes By, GPB VI.6)

Bahá’u’lláh’s own testimony, quoted by Shoghi Effendi, is on the brevity of the interval:

”“That so brief an interval,” He, moreover has asserted, “should have separated this most mighty and wondrous Revelation from Mine own previous Manifestation is a secret that no man can unravel, and a mystery such as no mind can fathom. Its duration had been foreordained.”” (God Passes By, GPB VI.6)

“His imprisonment lasted for a period of no less than four months, in the middle of which the “year nine” (1269), anticipated in such glowing terms by the Báb, and alluded to as the year “after Ḥín” by Shaykh Aḥmad-i-Aḥsá’í, was ushered in” (God Passes By, GPB VII.1)

Shoghi Effendi quotes the words of Shaykh Aḥmad-i-Aḥsá’í, whom he calls the herald of the Bábí Dispensation:

“…The Mystery of this Cause must needs be made manifest, and the Secret of this Message must needs be divulged. I can say no more, I can appoint no time. His Cause will be made known after Ḥín (68)…” (God Passes By, GPB VI.19)

Shoghi Effendi quotes Bahá’u’lláh on the chains of that time, in a prayer revealed when He was ordered to leave Persia:

“The throat Thou didst accustom to the touch of silk Thou hast, in the end, clasped with strong chains, and the body Thou didst ease with brocades and velvets Thou hast at last subjected to the abasement of a dungeon. Thy decree hath shackled Me with unnumbered fetters, and cast about My neck chains that none can sunder.…” (God Passes By, GPB VII.12)

Riḍván 1863: the Declaration to the Bábís

Shoghi Effendi writes that Bahá’u’lláh’s Declaration in the Garden of Riḍván (1863) ended the delay between the birth of His Revelation and its announcement to the Báb’s disciples. He ties it to the Báb’s own reckoning:

“Through that solemn act the “delay,” of no less than a decade, divinely interposed between the birth of Bahá’u’lláh’s Revelation in the Síyáh-Chál and its announcement to the Báb’s disciples, was at long last terminated.…” (God Passes By, GPB IX.2)

“The nineteen years, constituting the first “Váḥid,” preordained in the Persian Bayán by the pen of the Báb, had been completed.…” (God Passes By, GPB IX.2)

“The Báb’s own prophecy regarding the “Riḍván,” the scene of the unveiling of Bahá’u’lláh’s transcendent glory, had been literally fulfilled.” (God Passes By, GPB IX.2)

Nabíl records how Bahá’u’lláh explained the Mustagháth. Years later, in ‘Akká, Jináb-i-Muballigh wrote from Shíráz and asked Bahá’u’lláh to unravel the Báb’s Tablet to Dayyán, the Lawḥ-i-Ḥurúfát:

“Bahá’u’lláh adduced from the statements of the Báb irrefutable evidence proving that the appearance of the Man-Yuzhiruhu’llah must needs occur no less than nineteen years after the Declaration of the Báb. The mystery of the Mustagháth had long baffled the most searching minds among the people of the Bayán and had proved an unsurmountable obstacle to their recognition of the promised One. The Báb had Himself in that Tablet unravelled that mystery; no one, however, was able to understand the explanation which He had given. It was left to Bahá’u’lláh to unveil it to the eyes of all men.” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl, history)

The Dawn-Breakers glossary adds: “Mustagháth - “He who is invoked”; the numerical value of which has been assigned by the Báb as the limit of the time fixed for the advent of the promised Manifestation.” Neither Bahá’u’lláh’s reply to Jináb-i-Muballigh nor the Lawḥ-i-Ḥurúfát is in this corpus.

Adib Taherzadeh (history) writes that in His ten years in Iraq Bahá’u’lláh “had never designated Himself as ‘Him Whom God shall make manifest’”, and that in the Garden of Riḍván “He unequivocally did so” (The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1, p. 262).

In Adrianople, Shoghi Effendi writes, Bahá’u’lláh informed Mírzá Yaḥyá. The Tablet, the Súriy-i-Amr, is not in this corpus. Only Shoghi Effendi’s summary is:

“The moment had now arrived for Him Who had so recently, both verbally and in numerous Tablets, revealed the implications of the claims He had advanced, to acquaint formally the one who was the nominee of the Báb with the character of His Mission.…” (God Passes By, GPB X.7)

To the followers of the Son

In the Súriy-i-Haykal, in the passage addressed to Pope Pius IX, Bahá’u’lláh speaks of the same promise in terms that Christians know, and uses the name of the Báb’s Book, the Bayán:

“O followers of the Son! We have once again sent John unto you, and He, verily, hath cried out in the wilderness of the Bayán: O peoples of the world! Cleanse your eyes! The Day whereon ye can behold the Promised One and attain unto Him hath drawn nigh! O followers of the Gospel! Prepare the way! The Day of the advent of the Glorious Lord is at hand! Make ready to enter the Kingdom. Thus hath it been ordained by God, He Who causeth the dawn to break.” (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, SLH 1.121)

3. How Bahá’í authors and scholars have read the promise

This section is commentary and scholarship. It is not authoritative. Each reading is labelled with its author. The section says where a reading rests on a text above.

The Bayán as preparation (Afnan, Velasco, Taherzadeh)

El-Ham Afnan, in the article “A Twofold Mission” (bahai-library.com), writes that ”The second purpose is to herald the coming of Bahá’u’lláh, the Promised One designated by the Báb as Him Whom God shall make manifest“. Afnan reads the laws as preparation: the law of repeating Alláh’u’Abhá ninety-five times a day ”was meant to enable the one reciting it to attain to divine guidance so that he would recognize the Promised One when He appeared“, and the Báb ”made the implementation of His laws subject to the sanction of Him Whom God shall make manifest, while at the same time making it clear that His advent was near at hand“. These readings rest on the Báb’s statements that the laws are given for His sake (SWB 13.23, 13.65 above).

Ismael Velasco, in “Fixing the Gaze”, reads the Bayán’s nazm (order), which is built on the numbers four and nineteen, as meant ”to prepare the Babí community to recognise Him Whom God shall make manifest“. Velasco translates Persian Bayán 7:10 as ”For only God knows how long it will be from the beginning of a Manifestation until another, but if God please, it will not be more than the number of Mustagháth“, and takes this as the longest interval between Manifestations.

Adib Taherzadeh (history and commentary) writes: ”The laws revealed in the Bayán by the Báb were designed to be short-lived. Some of them were incomplete, being either directly or by implication dependent upon the advent of ‘Him Whom God shall make Manifest’“ (The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1, p. 278, as quoted in the Wilmette Institute notes). The Universal House of Justice makes the same point about one Bayán law (note 158, above).

The mystic bond (James B. Thomas)

James B. Thomas, in ”Seeds of Revelation and the Mystic Bond between The Báb and Bahá’u’lláh“, notes what is unusual about the Báb’s promise. Earlier Founders told their followers of another to come ”in the fullness of time“, with ”significant spans of time“ between them. In the Báb’s case “the one to follow” lived at the same time. Thomas writes that the Báb ”constantly reminded His followers of “Him Whom God Shall make manifest” but never seemed to mention Him by name”.

The numbers (Terry, Gonzales, MacEoin)

Peter Terry, in the analysis summary of the Persian Bayán (bahai-library.com), writes that the Báb “does not make specific predictions, except with unexplained numbers and terms (such as al-Mustaghath) regarding the exact time of the declaration of the next Manifestation”. From the Báb’s text on the Mustaghath Terry translates: “but it will not be longer than the name Mustaghath if God is willing.”

Grover Gonzales, in “He Whom God Shall Make Manifest: Some Notes (Gematria, Tetractys…)”, reads Persian Bayán 2:16, “Should HWGSMM come in the number of Ghiyáth [1511 days] all men shall enter in, not one shall remain in the Fire; and if he comes to Mustagháth [2001 days]…” as a count of days. Gonzales writes: “The starting point of the Bábí Calendar is His Declaration on 5 Jumádá I 1260 (May 22, 1844). The events that occurred after 1511 and 2001 days respectively are: The Conference of Badasht on mid-1848 and the Martyrdom of the Báb on mid-1850.” This is Gonzales’s reading. The Writings do not make it, and this page has not checked the arithmetic.

A note to the article quotes a letter of 28 May 2004, which says that the Research Department of the Universal House of Justice “has not, to date, been able to locate an authoritative interpretation of the passage from the Persian Bayán in the Writings”, with reference to Persian Bayán 2:16 and the Mustaghath. This page knows the letter only as Gonzales quotes it.

Gonzales also offers readings by gematria (the numerical value of letters). They are Gonzales’s own, and they are not in the Writings:

  • On the year nine: “The expected Year Nine (BaHA’) is 1269 A.H.; by applying gematria to this year: (1+2+6+9=18), 1+8=9, The year 1269 is the year of BaHA’”.
  • On the number nineteen: “Nineteen (19), means: The Manifest Alláh (1) and the Occult Bahá’ (9), i.e. Bahá’Alláh.”

The academic scholar Denis MacEoin, answering a question about this kind of reckoning on a mailing list, wrote: “man yuzhiruhu ’llah comes out as 1276 and Baha’ Allah as 75”. That is a scholar’s computation of the two phrases. This page does not decide what follows from it.

“Bahá” and the Crimson Ark (Taherzadeh)

SWB 14.49 (section 1) has the Báb write “the Daystar of Bahá”. Taherzadeh writes that the Báb ”has made many wonderful references to the name ‘Bahá’“, and gives a scroll ”in the form of a pentacle, three hundred and sixty derivatives of the word“ as an example (The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1, p. 117; section 4 compares the accounts of that scroll). Taherzadeh also writes that in the first chapter of the Qayyúmu’l-Asmá’ the Báb called the people of Bahá ”companions of the Crimson-Coloured Ark“ (vol. 1, p. 280). Bahá’u’lláh refers to the Qayyúm-i-Asmá in the same terms: ”enter, then, the Crimson Ark which God hath ordained in the Qayyúm-i-Asmá for the people of Bahá“ (ESW 203), and Shoghi Effendi writes that the “companions of the Crimson Colored Ark” had emerged by Riḍván (GPB IX.2, above).

The year nine and the Riḍván (Taherzadeh)

Taherzadeh writes: ”The ‘year nine’ was 1269 A.H., which opened about the middle of October“ (The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1, p. 298). Taherzadeh adds that the Báb ”had alluded in His Writings to the scene of Bahá’u’lláh’s Declaration in ‘Ridvan’ and to the wafting of the breezes of His Revelation from ’Baghdad’“ (vol. 1, p. 280). Shoghi Effendi states the prophecy regarding the “Riḍván” (GPB IX.2, above).

The law of the sword, and the culture of awaiting (Saiedi)

Jack McLean, in the review of Nader Saiedi’s Gate of the Heart, summarises Saiedi’s argument: “At Bayán (8:15), the Báb states that the implementation of the severe laws of the Bayán should be delayed until “the exaltation of the Cause of God in the Day of Him Whom God shall make manifest.”” On McLean’s account, Saiedi holds that Bahá’u’lláh “formally abolished” offensive jihad ”on the first day of His arrival in the Garden of Ridvan in Baghdad in 1863“. This is the reviewer’s summary of Saiedi. It is not stated in the passages of the Writings quoted on this page.

Notes of Saiedi’s talks, not checked by the speaker. Notes taken at talks by Nader Saiedi on the Writings of the Báb (bahai-library.com) record that the Báb ”created a new culture of awaiting“: if someone falsely claims to be Him, ”he is to be treated well“. Compare SWB 13.50 above. The notes also record that the Báb reads the nineteen letters of the Bismillah as ”the first 19 realities of His own Dispensation. The B refers to Him and the subsequent 18 letters are the Letters of the Living.“

An outside scholar (E. G. Browne)

E. G. Browne, in the article on Bábism (bahai-library.com copy), wrote of the Báb that ”his chief anxiety was not for his own fate, but for the reception which should be accorded by his followers to “Him whom God shall manifest.”” Browne adds that the Báb entreated them “not to behave in the next manifestation as the Muhammadans have behaved in this”. This is a scholar’s description of the Báb’s books, and not a Bahá’í source. It matches the warnings in section 1.

Gonzales also writes: “Apart from a few, the great majority of the Bábís accepted His proclamation.” This is Gonzales’s claim. Section 6 says what the sources gathered here do and do not show.

4. Those who recognised Him

These accounts are history, not the Writings. Each is labelled with its author.

The Báb prepared some of His disciples

Shoghi Effendi lists them in God Passes By:

“Some of His disciples the Báb assiduously prepared to expect the imminent Revelation. Others He orally assured would live to see its day. To Mullá Báqir, one of the Letters of the Living, He actually prophesied, in a Tablet addressed to him, that he would meet the Promised One face to face. To Sayyáḥ, another disciple, He gave verbally a similar assurance. Mullá Ḥusayn He directed to Ṭihrán, assuring him that in that city was enshrined a Mystery Whose light neither Ḥijáz nor Shíráz could rival. Quddús, on the eve of his final separation from Him, was promised that he would attain the presence of the One Who was the sole Object of their adoration and love. To Shaykh Ḥasan-i-Zunúzí He declared while in Máh-Kú that he would behold in Karbilá the countenance of the promised Ḥusayn. On Dayyán He conferred the title of “the third Letter to believe in Him Whom God shall make manifest,” while to ‘Aẓím He divulged, in the Kitáb-i-Panj-Sha‘n, the name, and announced the approaching advent, of Him Who was to consummate His own Revelation.” (God Passes By, GPB II.20)

In the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, Bahá’u’lláh quotes the Báb’s words to Mullá Báqir:

“Likewise, He saith to the Letter of the Living, Mullá Báqir—upon him be the glory of God and His loving-kindness: “Haply thou mayest in eight years, in the day of His Revelation, attain unto His Presence.”” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 251)

Mullá Ḥusayn in Ṭihrán: the Mystery, the scroll, the sugar and the tea

Nabíl gives the chain of narration (The Dawn-Breakers, history). Mírzá Músá, Áqáy-i-Kalím, the brother of Bahá’u’lláh, told Nabíl that he had heard it from Mullá Muḥammad-i-Mu’allim, a native of Núr. Mullá Muḥammad was a disciple of the Shaykhí teacher Ḥájí Mírzá Muḥammad and lived in the same school in Ṭihrán, where Mullá Ḥusayn had taken a room.

Mullá Ḥusayn asked the student about the family of the late Mírzá Buzurg-i-Núrí, and about one of his sons:

““What is His occupation?” he asked me. “He cheers the disconsolate and feeds the hungry,” I replied. “What of His rank and position?” “He has none,” I said, “apart from befriending the poor and the stranger.” “What is His name?” “Husayn-‘Alí.”” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl, from the account of Mullá Muḥammad-i-Mu’allim)

“He then gave me a scroll wrapped in a piece of cloth, and requested me to hand it to Him the next day at the hour of dawn.” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl)

The student gave the scroll to Mírzá Músá at the gate, and Mírzá Músá laid it before Bahá’u’lláh:

“Unfolding the scroll, He glanced at its contents and began to read aloud to us certain of its passages. I sat enraptured as I listened to the sound of His voice and the sweetness of its melody.” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl)

““Músá, what have you to say? Verily I say, whoso believes in the Qur’án and recognises its Divine origin, and yet hesitates, though it be for a moment, to admit that these soul-stirring words are endowed with the same regenerating power, has most assuredly erred in his judgment and has strayed far from the path of justice.” He spoke no more.” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl)

He sent a gift back with the student:

“He charged me to take to Mullá Husayn, as a gift from Him, a loaf of Russian sugar and a package of tea, and to convey to him the expression of His appreciation and love.” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl)

Mullá Ḥusayn, the student says, received it with bowed head and kissed it. The student could not understand “the nature of the bond that unites these two souls”. Nabíl records a second account from another side. The Báb’s maternal uncle described the night on which a letter from Mullá Ḥusayn reached the Báb, the night before the twenty-seventh of Ramaḍán. Quddús was the only companion with Him:

“That night I saw such evidences of joy and gladness on the faces of the Báb and of Quddús as I am unable to describe. I often heard the Báb, in those days, exultingly repeat the words, “How marvellous, how exceedingly marvellous, is that which has occurred between the months of Jamádi and Rajab!”” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl, from the Báb’s maternal uncle, as told by Mírzá Aḥmad)

Mírzá Aḥmad then asked Mullá Ḥusayn in Shíráz. Mullá Ḥusayn said only that between those months he had been in Ṭihrán:

“This was sufficient, however, to convince me that in the city of Tihran there lay hidden a Mystery which, when revealed to the world, would bring unspeakable joy to the hearts of both the Báb and Quddús.” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl, from Mírzá Aḥmad)

Shoghi Effendi writes that the Báb decided on His pilgrimage only after receiving “the eagerly anticipated letter of Mullá Ḥusayn, His trusted and beloved lieutenant, communicating the joyful tidings of his interview with Bahá’u’lláh” (GPB I.10). The Báb had directed Mullá Ḥusayn to Ṭihrán, “assuring him that in that city was enshrined a Mystery Whose light neither Ḥijáz nor Shíráz could rival” (GPB II.20, above).

H. M. Balyuzi (history, The Báb: The Herald of the Day of Days) writes that when Mullá Ḥusayn entered Ṭihrán, “there lay the ’Mystery’ which the Báb had mentioned”, and that Mullá Ḥusayn “recounted what had transpired in Ṭihrán, culminating in the gracious response of the nobleman of Núr”.

“a scroll of blue paper, of the most delicate texture, on which the Báb, in His own exquisite handwriting, which was a fine shikastih script, had penned, in the form of a pentacle, what numbered about five hundred verses, all consisting of derivatives from the word “Bahá.”” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl)

Quddús and Badasht

Quddús, Nabíl writes, was admitted into the presence of Bahá’u’lláh in Ṭihrán (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl). Áqáy-i-Kalím, Bahá’u’lláh’s brother, described Quddús there: “The charm of his person, his extreme affability, combined with a dignity of bearing, appealed to even the most careless observer.” Shoghi Effendi records that the Báb promised Quddús, “on the eve of his final separation from Him”, that “he would attain the presence of the One Who was the sole Object of their adoration and love” (GPB II.20).

At Badasht, four years after the Báb’s declaration (GPB II.31), Shoghi Effendi writes that Bahá’u’lláh rented three gardens and gave names:

“The scene of such a challenging and far-reaching proclamation was the hamlet of Badasht, where Bahá’u’lláh had rented, amidst pleasant surroundings, three gardens, one of which He assigned to Quddús, another to Ṭáhirih, whilst the third He reserved for Himself. The eighty-one disciples who had gathered from various provinces were His guests from the day of their arrival to the day they dispersed. On each of the twenty-two days of His sojourn in that hamlet He revealed a Tablet, which was chanted in the presence of the assembled believers. On every believer He conferred a new name, without, however, disclosing the identity of the one who had bestowed it. He Himself was henceforth designated by the name Bahá.…” (God Passes By, GPB II.28)

Shoghi Effendi writes that “it was Bahá’u’lláh Who steadily, unerringly, yet unsuspectedly, steered the course of that memorable episode” (GPB II.29), and that “the primary purpose of that gathering was to implement the revelation of the Bayán by a sudden, a complete and dramatic break with the past” (GPB II.27). Taherzadeh (history) writes that at Badasht Bahá’u’lláh “was designated Jinab-i-Baha, an appellation which the pen of the Báb later confirmed” (The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1, p. 8).

Of Ṭáhirih, Taherzadeh (history) writes that “long before Bahá’u’lláh’s imprisonment in the Siyah-Chal, … became fully aware of His station, and wrote some of her most beautiful poetry extolling Him as her Lord” (The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1). When some at Badasht tried to damage her name, Nabíl writes that the Báb replied: “What am I to say regarding her whom the Tongue of Power and Glory has named Táhirih [the Pure One]?” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl).

Shaykh Ḥasan-i-Zunúzí in Karbilá

The Báb told Shaykh Ḥasan-i-Zunúzí in Máh-Kú that he would behold in Karbilá “the countenance of the promised Ḥusayn” (GPB II.20). Nabíl writes:

“It was during that visit to Karbilá that Bahá’u’lláh encountered, as He was walking through the streets, Shaykh Hasan-i-Zunúzí, to whom He confided the secret He was destined to reveal at a later time in Baghdád.” (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl)

Mírzá Áqá Ján of Káshán

H. M. Balyuzi, in Bahá’u’lláh: The King of Glory (history), writes that Bahá’u’lláh disclosed His station to one Bábí youth first:

“He was a Babi youth of Kashan named Mirza Aqa Jan. This youth had a dream, in which the Bab appeared, and then he came upon some of the writings of Bahá’u’lláh.” (Balyuzi, Bahá’u’lláh: The King of Glory)

Balyuzi adds that, whatever his later conduct, “this distinction remains his, that he was the first to recognize in the person of Bahá’u’lláh the Promised One of the Bayan.” Shoghi Effendi (God Passes By, GPB VII.27) describes him as “a Bábí youth, aflame with devotion” who “precipitately forsaken his home in Káshán and traveled to ‘Iráq, in the hope of attaining His presence.”

Mírzá Áqá Ján later told Nabíl of his first night in Karbilá. Shoghi Effendi quotes him (God Passes By, GPB VII.27; Mírzá Áqá Ján’s account, recorded by Nabíl):

“Whereupon He began to chant and pace back and forth. How shall I ever describe that voice and the verses it intoned, and His gait, as He strode before me!”

He also records what Bahá’u’lláh said to him that night, as Mírzá Áqá Ján reported it:

“Hear Me, My son. By God, the True One! This Cause will assuredly be made manifest. Heed thou not the idle talk of the people of the Bayán, who pervert the meaning of every word.” (God Passes By, GPB VII.27, quoting Mírzá Áqá Ján)

Years later, before the Declaration, Nabíl writes that Mírzá Áqá Ján would gather Bahá’u’lláh’s companions at night, “chant aloud to them the newly revealed odes and Tablets in his possession,” and that they would “suddenly discover that night had become day” (God Passes By, GPB IX.5, quoting Nabíl).

Dayyán

Shoghi Effendi writes that the Báb gave Dayyán the title of “the third Letter to believe in Him Whom God shall make manifest” (God Passes By, GPB II.20). Bahá’u’lláh quotes the Báb’s words to him in the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf:

“And likewise He addresseth Dayyán, who was wronged and suffered martyrdom, saying: “Thou shalt recognize thy worth through the words of Him Whom God shall make manifest.” He, likewise, hath pronounced him to be the third Letter to believe in Him Whom God shall make manifest, through these words: “O thou who art the third Letter to believe in Him Whom God shall make manifest!”…” (Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, ESW 253)

Nabíl (history) writes that Dayyán, Mírzá Asadu’lláh, was “a prominent official of high literary ability” whose “vehement denunciations of His Message had baffled those who had endeavoured to convert him”. He dreamed a dream, told no one, and asked the Báb to reveal it; “The accuracy of that reply brought about a sudden conversion”. The Báb “bestowed on him the name Dayyán and revealed in his honour the Lawh-i-Hurúfat”. Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Epistle that Dayyán and others “suffered martyrdom through the decree pronounced by Mírzá Yaḥyá” (ESW 253).

Adib Taherzadeh (scholarship and history, The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1) writes of Dayyán’s meeting with Bahá’u’lláh in His house in Baghdád: “There he witnessed the fulfilment of the Báb’s promise to him that he would believe in ‘Him Whom God shall make manifest’. He recognized the station of Bahá’u’lláh, acknowledged His Faith and prostrated himself at His feet.”

Nabíl and the Bábís who read the Writings

Taherzadeh (history) writes that Nabíl first met Bahá’u’lláh in Ṭihrán around 1850 and “did not appreciate the loftiness of His station.” Taherzadeh writes that when the Bábí community seemed leaderless, Nabíl himself claimed to be Him Whom God shall make manifest; that on coming to Baghdád “his inner eyes beheld the glory of His Revelation”; and that he “prostrated himself at His feet and begged forgiveness for his presumption” (The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1). Nabíl then became the chronicler of the Faith.

Of Ḥájí Mírzá Kamálu’d-Dín, Taherzadeh writes that on reading a Tablet “he found the Source of all knowledge and recognized the station of Bahá’u’lláh as ‘Him Whom God shall make manifest’” (vol. 1, p. 56). Taherzadeh writes of the wider movement of Bábís to Baghdád:

“a few who were endowed with spiritual insight, through the mere perusal of these Writings, recognized in Him the One designated by the Báb as ‘Him Whom God shall make manifest’.” (Taherzadeh, The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1, p. 84)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s Memorials of the Faithful (writings) gives individual cases. Of the scholar Áqá Muḥammad of Qá’in, Nabíl-i-Akbar, who spread the Faith in Qá’in after leaving Baghdád, and of Mullá Muḥammad-‘Alí, who had met Bahá’u’lláh in Ṭihrán and accepted the Báb on hearing His name from Nabíl-i-Akbar:

“The moment Mullá Muḥammad-‘Alí heard the name of the Blessed Beauty, he immediately accepted the Báb. “I had the honor,” he said, “of meeting the Blessed Beauty in Ṭihrán. The instant I saw Him, I became His slave.”” (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Memorials of the Faithful, MF 17.3)

Mírzá Yaḥyá, the figurehead

The Báb nominated Mírzá Yaḥyá, Shoghi Effendi writes, “who would act solely as a figurehead pending the manifestation of the Promised One, thus enabling Bahá’u’lláh to promote, in relative security, the Cause so dear to His heart” (GPB II.21, where he cites ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s A Traveller’s Narrative). The Notes to the Kitáb-i-Aqdas say that Mírzá Yaḥyá “betrayed the trust of the Báb, claimed to be His successor, and intrigued against Bahá’u’lláh”, and that when Bahá’u’lláh “formally declared His Mission to him in Adrianople, Mírzá Yaḥyá responded by going to the length of putting forward his own claim” (note 190). This is the Universal House of Justice’s statement. The corpus does not give the views of the Azalís in their own words.

The corpus itself shows that chanting and reading aloud were part of Bábí gatherings. Shoghi Effendi quotes Mírzá Áqá Ján on hearing Bahá’u’lláh chant in the moonlight in Karbilá, and Nabíl on Mírzá Áqá Ján chanting newly revealed odes and Tablets through the night (both in section 4). In the account of the scroll in Ṭihrán, the student writes that he ”sat enraptured as I listened to the sound of His voice and the sweetness of its melody“ (The Dawn-Breakers, Nabíl). None of these passages describes a melody or a style. A songwriter who wants an authentic sound will need a source beyond this knowledge base.

6. What the sources do not settle

  • How “nine” and “nineteen” are reckoned. Shoghi Effendi presents the “year nine” (1269 A.H., the Síyáh-Chál) and the nineteen years of the first Váḥid (completed at Riḍván 1863) as two points in the Báb’s promise (GPB VI.6, IX.2). In the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, Bahá’u’lláh quotes the Báb as saying ”Wait thou until nine will have elapsed from the time of the Bayán“ (ESW 205) and ”Ere nine will have elapsed from the inception of this Cause“ (ESW 222). Nabíl records that Bahá’u’lláh showed from the Báb’s statements that the appearance must come ”no less than nineteen years after the Declaration“. The sources do not say how the two phrases for “nine” are counted or how they relate to the nineteen years. This page does not compute dates.
  • What “Ḥín (68)” means. The Research Department’s compiler’s note gives the numerical value 68 for the letters of Ḥín and says 1268 A.H. is the year before the birth of the Bahá’í Revelation. Shoghi Effendi calls the year nine (1269 A.H.) the year “after Ḥín” (GPB VII.1). This settles the number and the year. The sources do not give a longer explanation of the reckoning.
  • The 1511 and 2001 days. The numbers are in Browne’s abstract and in the Bahá’í literature (Terry, Velasco, Gonzales). The Research Department wrote on 28 May 2004 that it had not located an authoritative interpretation, a letter known here only through Gonzales. Gonzales’s dates (Badasht 1848, the Báb’s martyrdom 1850) are Gonzales’s reading.
  • The pen-case, seals and scroll. The three accounts differ (section 4). The sources do not reconcile them.
  • How the Bábí community received the claim. The Báb’s warnings are given in section 1 and the history of those who responded in section 4. Taherzadeh writes of Bábís who recognised Bahá’u’lláh on reading His Writings, and Gonzales writes that ”the great majority of the Bábís accepted His proclamation“. The sources gathered here give no figures and do not tell how the rest understood the Báb’s promise. This page does not describe the views of Bábís who did not accept the claim, because the corpus does not give them in their own words.
  • Which Writings of the Báb were addressed to which disciple. Shoghi Effendi names the disciples to whom assurances were given. The Epistle to the Son of the Wolf supplies the words to Mullá Báqir (ESW 251) and Dayyán (ESW 253). The text of the assurances to Sayyáḥ, to Quddús and to Shaykh Ḥasan-i-Zunúzí is not in the corpus.
  • Tablets named but not held. The Lawḥ-i-Ḥurúfát, Bahá’u’lláh’s reply to Jináb-i-Muballigh, and the Súriy-i-Amr (GPB X.7) are not in the corpus.
  • Provisional translations. MacEoin’s Persian Bayán is a partial translation done in haste. The Arabic Bayán in Peter Terry’s file is damaged by OCR in places, so no passage from it is quoted. Browne’s abstract is an 1889 summary. This page quotes from them only where the words are clean and an authorised passage says the same.
  • Scholarship outside the Bahá’í community. The scholarly sources are mostly Bahá’í authors. Browne and MacEoin are the exceptions here, and neither wrote a study of the Bahá’í reading.

Concrete images

  • An upper chamber in Shíráz, an hour before sunset on 22 May 1844 (Balyuzi, history).
  • A scroll wrapped in a piece of cloth, carried at dawn to a house in Ṭihrán and read aloud in a voice with ”the sweetness of its melody“, and a loaf of Russian sugar and a package of tea sent back (Nabíl, The Dawn-Breakers).
  • A night when the Báb and Quddús rejoice over a letter: ”How marvellous, how exceedingly marvellous“ (Nabíl).
  • A blue paper scroll in the form of a pentacle, written so finely that it looks like a wash of ink (Nabíl; Taherzadeh; A Traveller’s Narrative says ”in the shape of a man“. The sources differ on the number of verses and on who carried it).
  • Three gardens at Badasht, a Tablet each day, and new names given to eighty-one people (GPB II.28).
  • The Síyáh-Chál: stocks on the feet and a heavy chain on the neck (Taherzadeh, history), in the year nine; and ”chains that none can sunder“ (Bahá’u’lláh, GPB VII.12).
  • A roof in Karbilá in summer, moonlight, a voice chanting, a young man who has just risen to pray (Mírzá Áqá Ján’s account, GPB VII.27).
  • A room with candles lit at night, a believer chanting newly revealed Tablets until the night “had become day” (Nabíl, GPB IX.5).
  • A letter addressed to Him Whom God shall make manifest, asking that it be delivered into His presence (SWB 9.7).
  • A ring turned on a hand (SWB 14.58). A Tree of the Bayán made into a scroll and offered (SWB 15.4).
  • A day that is like any other day, when ”the sun riseth and setteth“ (SWB 11.7).
  • The Garden of Riḍván, spring 1863 (GPB IX.2).

Possible emotional arcs

  1. Waiting: the Báb names Him but gives no name, and says ”await the Day of the appearance.“
  2. Fear of being veiled: ”Suffer not yourselves to be shut out.“
  3. The dark hour: the Síyáh-Chál, the year nine, a Cause that appears to be ending.
  4. A person who sees: the young man on the roof in Karbilá.
  5. The garden: the Declaration, and the end of the waiting.

A shorter arc follows the sugar and the tea: a scroll goes out at dawn, a gift comes back by the same hand, and the man who sent the scroll kisses the gift. The song need say no more than the sources do.

What a lyric must not claim

  • That the Báb’s passages use the name Bahá’u’lláh. Their term is ”Him Whom God shall make manifest“. Shoghi Effendi states the identification, and writes that the name Bahá’u’lláh is ”an appellation specifically recorded in the Persian Bayán“ (GPB VI.12).
  • That all Bábís recognised Him. The sources describe those who did.
  • That the date arithmetic (“nine”, “nineteen”, “Ḥín”, 1511, 2001) is settled beyond what the texts state, or that the gematria readings (Gonzales) are in the Writings.
  • That the Báb’s followers who did not accept Bahá’u’lláh were unbelievers or enemies. Quote the Báb’s warnings as warnings; do not apply them to named groups.
  • That Mírzá Áqá Ján or any other person is a model of faithfulness throughout his life. Balyuzi notes that Mírzá Áqá Ján later broke the Covenant; the song can use the one night in Karbilá without making a claim about the rest.
  • That the pentacle scroll held a set number of verses. The sources differ.
