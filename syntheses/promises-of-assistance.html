Promises of Assistance: The Assurance That Effort Will Be Confirmed

Last updated 13 October 2026

What this page covers

Many readers recognise a sequence when they read the Writings. First come the passages on the greatness of this Day. Then come the passages on the standard expected of those who believe, which can leave a reader feeling that he falls short. Then comes the assurance that holds the two together: even though what is expected is overwhelming, a small step in the right direction will be assisted. This page gathers the third part: the promises, made by Bahá’u’lláh, the Báb, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, Shoghi Effendi and the Universal House of Justice, that those who arise to serve will be helped.

The page is arranged by theme, not by author:

  1. One address that contains all three parts — the Báb’s parting words to the Letters of the Living.
  2. The promise in Bahá’u’lláh’s words — including two paragraphs of the Kitáb-i-Aqdas addressed to the time after His passing.
  3. The hosts of the Concourse on high — what the texts say this help is, and who gives it.
  4. The small made great — the gnat and the eagle, the ant and the elephant, the drop and the sea, and the passages that speak of humble efforts and small shares.
  5. Against preoccupation with one’s own weakness — the Guardian’s letters to individuals who felt unequal to the work.
  6. The conditions — arising first, detachment, purity of motive, steadfastness in the Covenant and in love, perseverance — and the one place where the conditions appear to pull against the promise.
  7. The standard and the promise given together — passages where the demand is stated at full height and the assurance follows in the same breath.
  8. Prayers for assistance — the prayers ‘Abdu’l-Bahá asked teachers to recite every morning, and the Báb’s “Remover of difficulties”.
  9. What the promise does not say — it promises confirmation, not an easy path or a particular outcome.
  10. Short passages for daily reading or for songs, and notes on sources.

Every quotation is taken from a text held in this knowledge base and has been checked against it. Where a passage reaches us second-hand — Shoghi Effendi quoting ‘Abdu’l-Bahá quoting the Báb, for example — the chain is named. The prose around the quotations is editorial.

One address that contains all three parts

Before the Báb sent the eighteen Letters of the Living out from Shíráz, He gave them a parting address. Nabíl recorded it in The Dawn-Breakers, the narrative Shoghi Effendi translated and edited. It is the Báb’s utterance as Nabíl preserved it, not a Tablet in the Báb’s own hand, but the Research Department of the Universal House of Justice placed its closing lines in the compilation The Power of Divine Assistance (1981, revised 1990) under “the Writings and Utterances of the Báb”. It is placed first here because it follows these three steps in order.

The greatness of the Day:

“O My Letters! Verily I say, immensely exalted is this Day above the days of the Apostles of old. Nay, immeasurable is the difference! You are the witnesses of the Dawn of the promised Day of God.”

The standard:

“The days when idle worship was deemed sufficient are ended. The time is come when naught but the purest motive, supported by deeds of stainless purity, can ascend to the throne of the Most High and be acceptable unto Him.”

“You are the lowly, of whom God has thus spoken in His Book: “And We desire to show favour to those who were brought low in the land, and to make them spiritual leaders among men, and to make them Our heirs.’ You have been called to this station; you will attain to it, only if you arise to trample beneath your feet every earthly desire, and endeavour to become those ‘honoured servants of His who speak not till He hath spoken, and who do His bidding.’“

The assurance:

”Heed not your weaknesses and frailty; fix your gaze upon the invincible power of the Lord, your God, the Almighty. Has He not, in past days, caused Abraham, in spite of His seeming helplessness, to triumph over the forces of Nimrod? Has He not enabled Moses, whose staff was His only companion, to vanquish Pharaoh and his hosts? Has He not established the ascendancy of Jesus, poor and lowly as He was in the eyes of men, over the combined forces of the Jewish people? Has He not subjected the barbarous and militant tribes of Arabia to the holy and transforming discipline of Muḥammad, His Prophet? Arise in His name, put your trust wholly in Him, and be assured of ultimate victory.“

(The Dawn-Breakers, the Báb’s farewell to the Letters of the Living; the final paragraph as given in The Power of Divine Assistance, which cites p. 94.)

Three things in this address recur in almost every text gathered below. The hearers are told plainly that they are ”the lowly“; their weakness is not denied. The standard is not lowered to fit that weakness — ”naught but the purest motive“ is the bar. And the instruction for handling the gap between the two is about where to look: away from ”your weaknesses and frailty“ and toward ”the invincible power of the Lord“. The examples the Báb gives are all of one figure with almost nothing — Moses with a staff, Jesus ”poor and lowly“ — prevailing against much larger forces.

The promise in Bahá’u’lláh’s words

Addressed to the time after His passing

Two paragraphs of the Kitáb-i-Aqdas speak to the believers about the period after Bahá’u’lláh is no longer among them. They are therefore addressed directly to every later generation, including this one.

”Be not dismayed, O peoples of the world, when the daystar of My beauty is set, and the heaven of My tabernacle is concealed from your eyes. Arise to further My Cause, and to exalt My Word amongst men. We are with you at all times, and shall strengthen you through the power of truth. We are truly almighty. Whoso hath recognized Me will arise and serve Me with such determination that the powers of earth and heaven shall be unable to defeat his purpose.“ (Kitáb-i-Aqdas ¶38; also Gleanings LXXI)

”Say: Let not your hearts be perturbed, O people, when the glory of My Presence is withdrawn, and the ocean of My utterance is stilled. In My presence amongst you there is a wisdom, and in My absence there is yet another, inscrutable to all but God, the Incomparable, the All-Knowing. Verily, We behold you from Our realm of glory, and shall aid whosoever will arise for the triumph of Our Cause with the hosts of the Concourse on high and a company of Our favored angels.“ (Kitáb-i-Aqdas ¶53; also Gleanings LXXII)

The structure of both is the same: a command (”Arise“) and a promise (”We are with you at all times“; ”shall aid whosoever will arise“). The promise in ¶53 is not limited to the gifted or the advanced. Its only qualifier is ”whosoever will arise“.

”Whoso openeth his lips“

The passage most often quoted on this subject is in Gleanings CXXIX:

”By the righteousness of God! Whoso openeth his lips in this Day and maketh mention of the name of his Lord, the hosts of Divine inspiration shall descend upon him from the heaven of My name, the All-Knowing, the All-Wise. On him shall also descend the Concourse on high, each bearing aloft a chalice of pure light. Thus hath it been foreordained in the realm of God’s Revelation, by the behest of Him Who is the All-Glorious, the Most Powerful.“ (Gleanings CXXIX)

The act named here is small: to open one’s lips and mention the name of one’s Lord. The promise attached to it is large. Shoghi Effendi placed this passage twice in The Advent of Divine Justice (1938) — once under the heading ”Assurances of Aid“ and once among the passages on ”The Paramount Importance of Teaching“. The Universal House of Justice quoted it at Riḍván 1995, to answer feelings of inadequacy, and again at Riḍván 1996, in describing what the individual believer can draw on (both below).

The same Tablet, a few lines earlier, addresses the person who acts alone:

”Be not grieved if thou performest it thyself alone. Let God be all-sufficient for thee. Commune intimately with His Spirit, and be thou of the thankful.“ (Gleanings CXXIX)

Those who leave home to teach

”They that have forsaken their country for the purpose of teaching Our Cause—these shall the Faithful Spirit strengthen through its power. A company of Our chosen angels shall go forth with them, as bidden by Him Who is the Almighty, the All-Wise. How great the blessedness that awaiteth him that hath attained the honor of serving the Almighty!“ (Gleanings CLVII)

The same section goes on to state the conditions for this kind of service, discussed under ”The conditions“ below.

”Should a man, all alone, arise“

In The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh (1934), Shoghi Effendi quotes two passages from Tablets he does not name, introducing them as Bahá’u’lláh ”wishing to reveal the full potency of His invincible power“:

”should a man, all alone, arise in the name of Bahá and put on the armor of His love, him will the Almighty cause to be victorious, though the forces of earth and heaven be arrayed against him.“ ”By God besides Whom is none other God! Should any one arise for the triumph of our Cause, him will God render victorious though tens of thousands of enemies be leagued against him. And if his love for Me wax stronger, God will establish his ascendancy over all the powers of earth and heaven.“ (quoted in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, ”The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh“)

The first passage names a single person acting alone. The second adds a measure: the help grows as ”his love for Me wax stronger“. Love is the condition named here, not skill or numbers.

Reciprocity

Among the passages Shoghi Effendi gathered in 1938 from what he called ”the great mass of Bahá’u’lláh’s unpublished and untranslated writings“, under the heading ”The High Station of the People of Bahá“, is this one:

”He, verily, will aid everyone that aideth Him, and will remember everyone that remembereth Him.“ (quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice)

The source Tablet is not named in The Advent of Divine Justice, and this knowledge base does not identify it.

A short line in the Tablet Words of Wisdom (Aṣl-i-Kullu’l-Khayr) states where courage itself comes from:

”The source of courage and power is the promotion of the Word of God, and steadfastness in His Love.“ (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, Words of Wisdom)

The Báb

The Báb gave the same assurance to His own followers:

”Say, verily any one follower of this Faith can, by the leave of God, prevail over all who dwell in heaven and earth and in whatever lieth between them; for indeed this is, beyond the shadow of a doubt, the one true Faith. Therefore fear ye not, neither be ye grieved.“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, Excerpts from Various Writings)

The passage continues in terms of proportion: God has ”taken upon Himself the task of ensuring the ascendancy of anyone of the followers of the Truth, over and above one hundred other souls“.

The hosts of the Concourse on high

The promises speak of ”the hosts of the Concourse on high“, ”a company of Our favored angels“, ”the cohorts of the Kingdom“, ”the invisible battalions“. A reader may reasonably ask what this help is.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá gives one answer in the Tablets of the Divine Plan. Explaining the title ”Lord of Hosts“, He says the hosts are also souls in this world who have become entirely devoted:

”THE blessed Person of the Promised One is interpreted in the Holy Book as the Lord of Hosts—the heavenly armies. By heavenly armies those souls are intended who are entirely freed from the human world, transformed into celestial spirits and have become divine angels.“

”These souls are the armies of God and the conquerors of the East and the West. Should one of them turn his face toward some direction and summon the people to the Kingdom of God, all the ideal forces and lordly confirmations will rush to his support and reinforcement. He will behold all the doors open and all the strong fortifications and impregnable castles razed to the ground.“ (Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet to the Bahá’ís of the United States and Canada, revealed 19–22 April 1916)

So the image works in two directions. There are unseen forces that come to the aid of the one who arises. And the one who arises, by becoming detached and devoted, becomes one of those hosts — ”the Apostle of Bahá’u’lláh“, as the same Tablet goes on to say.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá describes these hosts as waiting for someone to act:

”The triumphant hosts of the Celestial Concourse, arrayed and marshaled in the Realms above, stand ready and expectant to assist and assure victory to that valiant horseman who with confidence spurreth on his charger into the arena of service.“ (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá §208)

Shoghi Effendi, through his secretary, used the same picture in 1953:

”Today, as never before, the magnet which attracts the blessings from on high is teaching the Faith of God. The Hosts of Heaven are poised between heaven and earth, just waiting, and patiently, for the Bahá’í to step forth, with pure devotion and consecration, to teach the Cause of God, so they may rush to his aid and assistance.“ (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 28 March 1953, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

The Universal House of Justice has repeated the assurance to the whole community at the close of its messages:

”Rest assured, dear friends, that the hosts of the Abhá Kingdom stand ready to rush to the support of anyone who will arise to offer his or her acts of service to the unfolding, spiritual drama of these momentous days.“ (Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 1997)

The common feature of all four is timing. The hosts are ”ready“, ”expectant“, ”waiting“. What they wait for is the person stepping forward. This is taken up again under ”Arising comes first“.

The small made great

These are the passages that answer the idea of ”a small step“ most directly. They take something tiny or weak and say what it becomes when it is confirmed.

The gnat, the sparrow, the ant, the moth

”If in this day a soul shall act according to the precepts and the counsels of God, he will serve as a divine physician to mankind, and like the trump of Isráfíl, he will call the dead of this contingent world to life; for the confirmations of the Abhá Realm are never interrupted, and such a virtuous soul hath, to befriend him, the unfailing help of the Company on high. Thus shall a sorry gnat become an eagle in the fullness of his strength, and a feeble sparrow change to a royal falcon in the heights of ancient glory.“ (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá §8)

(Isráfíl: ”Believed to be the angel appointed to sound the trumpet on the Day of Resurrection to raise the dead at the bidding of the Lord“ — footnote in The Power of Divine Assistance.)

The condition in this passage is to ”act according to the precepts and the counsels of God“. The result is stated without qualification: ”the confirmations of the Abhá Realm are never interrupted“.

”Rest ye assured that if a soul ariseth in the utmost perseverance and raiseth the Call of the Kingdom and resolutely promulgateth the Covenant, be he an insignificant ant he shall be enabled to drive away the formidable elephant from the arena, and if he be a feeble moth he shall cut to pieces the plumage of the rapacious vulture.“ (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá §184)

The drop and the sea

”Do not take into consideration your own aptitudes and capacities, but fix your gaze on the consummate bounty, the divine bestowal and the power of the Holy Spirit—the power that converteth the drop into a sea and the star into a sun.“ (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá §68)

Bahá’u’lláh uses the same image of God’s power, among the passages Shoghi Effendi gathered under ”The Regeneration of Human Life“:

”It is in Our power, should We wish it, to enable a speck of floating dust to generate, in less than the twinkling of an eye, suns of infinite, of unimaginable splendor, to cause a dewdrop to develop into vast and numberless oceans, to infuse into every letter such a force as to empower it to unfold all the knowledge of past and future ages.“ (quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice)

A letter written on Shoghi Effendi’s behalf in 1947 applies it to discouragement over one’s own failures:

”Each one of us, if we look into our failures, is sure to feel unworthy and despondent, and this feeling only frustrates our constructive efforts and wastes time. The thing for us to focus on is the glory of the Cause and the Power of Bahá’u’lláh which can make of a mere drop a surging sea!“ (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 13 October 1947, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

The Universal House of Justice used it in two letters to the Bahá’ís of Iran. In 2008 it attached the image to a single sincere step:

”Be confident that every step taken in the path of God with sincerity will attract heavenly confirmations, for He “imparteth to the drop the power of the sea, and turneth the atom into a very sun.”” (Universal House of Justice, 31 October 2008, to the Believers in the Cradle of the Faith)

The letter does not name the source of the words it quotes, and they could not be traced in this knowledge base. The letter was written in Persian (its Persian original reads “قطره را حکم دریا دهد و ذرّه را آفتاب کند”); ‘Abdu’l-Bahá uses closely related wording, as in Selections §68 above.

In 2022, looking back on the century since ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s passing, the House of Justice described what had already happened to a small and grieving community:

“One marvels at how, after the departure of their kind Master, His followers throughout the world—that handful of feeble ones devoted to their peerless Beloved, those lovers who were burning in the fire of separation from Him, mournful and anxious in His absence—succeeded through unshakeable determination and matchless sacrifice to carry out the admonitions of their far-sighted Master contained in His Tablets of the Divine Plan, and were enabled to establish the Cause of God upon so strong a foundation. Assuredly, confirmations made the drops to become seas, and endowed the atoms with the splendour of the sun.” (Universal House of Justice, Naw-Rúz 179 [2022], to the followers of Bahá’u’lláh in Iran; authorized translation from the Persian)

This is the House of Justice stating that the promise has been fulfilled in the history of the Faith’s first century, not only that it will be.

The tiny ant, and Peter

In The Advent of Divine Justice Shoghi Effendi argues from the lesser to the greater. He quotes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, who is Himself quoting the Báb:

““The Báb,” ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, moreover, has written, “hath said: ‘Should a tiny ant desire, in this day, to be possessed of such power as to be able to unravel the abstrusest and most bewildering passages of the Qur’án, its wish will no doubt be fulfilled, inasmuch as the mystery of eternal might vibrates within the innermost being of all created things.‘ If so helpless a creature can be endowed with so subtle a capacity, how much more efficacious must be the power released through the liberal effusions of the grace of Bahá’u’lláh!”” (The Advent of Divine Justice)

Just before it, he recalls ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s account of Peter, who could not keep count of the days of the week and tied his food into seven parcels so that he would know when the Sabbath came. Shoghi Effendi then draws the conclusion:

“If the Son of Man was capable of infusing into apparently so crude and helpless an instrument such potency as to cause, in the words of Bahá’u’lláh, “the mysteries of wisdom and of utterance to flow out of his mouth,” and to exalt him above the rest of His disciples, and render him fit to become His successor and the founder of His Church, how much more can the Father, Who is Bahá’u’lláh, empower the most puny and insignificant among His followers to achieve, for the execution of His purpose, such wonders as would dwarf the mightiest achievements of even the first apostle of Jesus Christ!” (The Advent of Divine Justice)

The phrase “the most puny and insignificant among His followers” is the Guardian’s. The promise is addressed to them specifically.

Small numbers, small shares, one teaching

‘Abdu’l-Bahá, writing to the believers of Canada in 1917, when they were very few:

“O ye believers of God! Be not concerned with the smallness of your numbers, neither be oppressed by the multitude of an unbelieving world. Five grains of wheat will be endued with heavenly blessing, whereas a thousand tons of tares will yield no results or effect.” (Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet to the Bahá’ís of Canada and Greenland, 21 February 1917)

Bahá’u’lláh, in a passage Shoghi Effendi placed among those on the importance of teaching, speaks of a person whose share is small:

“If any man were to arise to defend, in his writings, the Cause of God against its assailants, such a man, however inconsiderable his share, shall be so honored in the world to come that the Concourse on high would envy his glory.” (quoted in The Advent of Divine Justice)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá describes the slow work of one friendship. The passage begins with teaching under restriction (“If the work cannot proceed openly, then let them teach in private”):

“If, for example, each and every one of the believers would become a true friend to one of the unheeding, and, conducting himself with absolute rectitude, associate with this soul, treat him with the utmost kindness, himself exemplify the divine instructions he hath received, the good qualities and behavior patterns, and at all times act in accord with the admonitions of God—it is certain that little by little he will succeed in awakening that previously heedless individual, and in changing his ignorance to knowledge of the truth.” (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá §209)

Shoghi Effendi, in a letter to the Bahá’ís of Persia in 1923, recalls something ‘Abdu’l-Bahá often said, about the effect of carrying out a single teaching fully:

“How often the beloved Master was heard to say: Should each one of the friends take upon himself to carry out, in all its integrity and implications, only one of the teachings of the Faith, with devotion, detachment, constancy and perseverance and exemplify it in all his deeds and pursuits of life, the world would become another world and the face of the earth would mirror forth the splendours of the Abhá Paradise.” (letter of Shoghi Effendi to the Bahá’ís of Persia, 12 January 1923, translated from the Persian; in Living the Life)

This is the Guardian’s report of the Master’s spoken words, not a Tablet. Its point fits the idea of a small step closely: the unit of change named is one teaching, carried out completely.

The Universal House of Justice has repeatedly invited each believer to give a share “no matter how humble”. On the centenary of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s journeys to the West:

“let each and every one of His faithful lovers arise and act in His Name. Let them offer their share, no matter how humble, to the progress of the Plan He authored—that priceless and everlasting bequest.” (Universal House of Justice, 29 August 2010, to the Bahá’ís of the World)

And at Riḍván 2016, about the work of a five-year Plan in each cluster:

“The sustained effort required will be arduous. But the outcome has the potential to be profoundly significant, even epoch making. Small steps, if they are regular and rapid, add up to a great distance travelled.” (Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 2016)

In context this sentence is advice on pacing the collective work of a cluster cycle by cycle, not a statement about divine assistance. It is included because it names the same arithmetic: a large distance is covered by small steps taken regularly.

Witnesses

The texts also point to people. In the Tablets of the Divine Plan ‘Abdu’l-Bahá names two women who went out alone:

“Consider ye, that Miss Agnes Alexander, the daughter of the Kingdom, the beloved maidservant of the Blessed Perfection, traveled alone to the Hawaiian Islands, to the Island of Honolulu, and now she is gaining spiritual victories in Japan! Reflect ye how this daughter was confirmed in the Hawaiian Islands. She became the cause of the guidance of a gathering of people.”

“Likewise Miss Knobloch traveled alone to Germany. To what a great extent she became confirmed! Therefore, know ye of a certainty that whosoever arises in this day to diffuse the divine fragrances, the cohorts of the Kingdom of God shall confirm him and the bestowals and the favors of the Blessed Perfection shall encircle him.” (Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet to the Bahá’ís of the United States and Canada, 11 April 1916)

In the same Tablet He points to Himself:

“You have observed that while ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá was in the utmost bodily weakness and feebleness, while he was indisposed, and had not the power to move—notwithstanding this physical state he traveled through many countries, in Europe and America, and in churches, meetings and conventions was occupied with the promotion of the divine principles and summoned the people to the manifestation of the Kingdom of Abhá. You have also observed how the confirmations of the Blessed Perfection encompassed all.”

The Universal House of Justice, at Riḍván 2015, addressed those whose surroundings show little progress:

“Have hope. It will not always be so. Is not the history of our Faith filled with accounts of inauspicious beginnings but marvellous results? How many times have the deeds of a few believers—young or old—or of a single family, or even of a lone soul, when confirmed by the power of divine assistance, succeeded in cultivating vibrant communities in seemingly inhospitable climes? Do not imagine that your own case is inherently any different.” (Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 2015)

Against preoccupation with one’s own weakness

A large share of the Guardian’s correspondence on this subject was with individuals who said they could not teach or serve because they were not capable enough. His answers, written by his secretaries on his behalf and gathered in The Power of Divine Assistance and Living the Life, do not tell these people that they are more capable than they think. They tell them to stop measuring the work by their own capacity.

“Perhaps the reason why you have not accomplished so much in the field of teaching is the extent you looked upon your own weaknesses and inabilities to spread the message. Bahá’u’lláh and the Master have both urged us repeatedly to disregard our own handicaps and lay our whole reliance upon God. He will come to our help if we only arise and become an active channel for God’s grace. Do you think it is the teachers who make converts and change human hearts? No, surely not. They are only pure souls who take the first step, and then let the spirit of Bahá’u’lláh move them and make use of them.”

“Stop being conscious of your frailties, therefore; have a perfect reliance upon God; let your heart burn with the desire to serve His mission and proclaim His call; and you will observe how eloquence and the power to change human hearts will come as a matter of course.”

“Shoghi Effendi will surely pray for your success if you should arise and start to teach. In fact the mere act of arising will win for you God’s help and blessings.” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 31 March 1932, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

“Pure souls who take the first step” is the closest any text in this collection comes to the phrase “a small step”. The work described is not the teacher’s achievement; the teacher’s part is the first step and the willingness to be used.

“You should never look at your own limitations, much less allow them to deter you from promoting the Message. For the believers, whether capable or not, whether poor or rich, and whether influential or obscure, are after all but mere channels through which God carries His message to mankind. They are instruments whereby He communicates His will to His people. The friends, therefore, must cease looking at their own deficiencies in a way that would kill in them the spirit of initiative and of service.” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 18 March 1934, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

The qualifier “in a way that would kill in them the spirit of initiative and of service” matters. Elsewhere the Writings ask believers to know their own faults. What is ruled out here is self-examination that ends in doing nothing.

“If the friends always waited until they were fully qualified to do any particular task, the work of the Cause would be almost at a standstill! But the very act of striving to serve, however unworthy one may feel, attracts the blessings of God and enables one to become more fitted for the task.” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 4 May 1942, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

“At all times we must look at the greatness of the Cause, and remember that Bahá’u’lláh will assist all who arise in His service. When we look at ourselves, we are sure to feel discouraged by our shortcomings and insignificance!” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 12 December 1950, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

This last letter describes the movement of attention that this sequence describes. Looking at the greatness of the Cause comes first; looking at oneself produces discouragement; the remedy is to remember the promise, not to look away from the standard.

To a believer who had written of hindrances to service, the Guardian’s secretary wrote that such hindrances “can and should be effectively overcome through the combined and sustained power of prayer and of determined and continued effort”, and went on:

“For have not Bahá’u’lláh and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá both repeatedly assured us that the Divine and unseen hosts of victory will ever reinforce and strengthen those who valiantly and confidently labour in their name? This assurance should indeed enable you to overcome any feeling of unworthiness, of incapacity to serve, and any inner or outer limitations which threaten to handicap your labours for the Cause. You should therefore arise, and with a heart filled with joy and confidence endeavour to contribute any share that is in your power toward the wider diffusion and greater consolidation of our beloved Faith.” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 6 February 1939, in Living the Life)

“Any share that is in your power” sets the measure at what the person can actually do, which for someone who is ill may be small.

To the believers of India in 1941, on pioneering:

“Let them dedicate themselves—young and old, men and women alike—and go forth and settle in new districts, travel, and teach in spite of lack of experience, and be assured that Bahá’u’lláh has promised to aid all those who arise in His Name. His strength will sustain them; their own weakness is unimportant.” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to the National Spiritual Assembly of India, 29 June 1941, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá said the same in Paris, pointing to His own condition:

“Remember not your own limitations; the help of God will come to you. Forget yourself. God’s help will surely come!”

“Look at me: I am so feeble, yet I have had the strength given me to come amongst you: a poor servant of God, who has been enabled to give you this message! I shall not be with you long! One must never consider one’s own feebleness; it is the strength of the Holy Spirit of Love, which gives the power to teach. The thought of our own weakness could only bring despair.” (Paris Talks, ch. 9, “The Universal Love”, 24 October 1911)

Paris Talks records ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s spoken addresses as they were noted down at the time; it is not a text He revised, and it is quoted here alongside His written words, not in place of them.

The Universal House of Justice addressed the whole Bahá’í world in the same terms at Riḍván 1995:

“Let no excessive self-criticism or any feelings of inadequacy, inability or inexperience hinder you or cause you to be afraid. Bury your fears in the assurances of Bahá’u’lláh. Has He not asserted that upon anyone who mentions His Name will descend the “hosts of Divine inspiration” and that on such a one will also descend the “Concourse on high, each bearing aloft a chalice of pure light”? Step forth, then, into the arena where all His loved ones are equally summoned, equally challenged and abundantly blessed.” (Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 1995)

“Equally summoned, equally challenged and abundantly blessed” puts the standard and the promise in one phrase, and applies both to everyone.

The conditions

The promises are not unconditional. The texts link assistance to particular things, and they are consistent about what those things are.

Arising comes first

Almost every promise above is attached to a verb of action: “arise”, “openeth his lips”, “turn his face”, “step forth”, “start out”. The texts are explicit that the help follows the step and does not come before it.

“It is clear that in this day, confirmations from the unseen world are encompassing all those who deliver the divine Message. Should the work of teaching lapse, these confirmations would be entirely cut off, since it is impossible for the loved ones of God to receive assistance unless they teach.” (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá §209)

“However, when the tongue of the teacher is engaged in teaching, he will naturally himself be stimulated, will become a magnet attracting the divine aid and bounty of the Kingdom, and will be like unto the bird at the hour of dawn, which itself becometh exhilarated by its own singing, its warbling and its melody.” (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá §211)

The second passage describes something many people will recognise: the act of teaching changes the teacher’s own state, as the bird’s own singing exhilarates it.

Shoghi Effendi stated the order most sharply in 1954, writing to the American Bahá’ís:

“The unseen legions, standing rank upon rank, and eager to pour forth from the Kingdom on high the full measure of their celestial strength on the individual participants of this incomparably glorious Crusade, are powerless unless and until each potential crusader decides for himself, and perseveres in his determination, to rush into the arena of service ready to sacrifice his all for the Cause he is called upon to champion.” (Shoghi Effendi, 28 July 1954, in Citadel of Faith)

“Powerless unless and until” is a strong phrase. The help exists and is “eager”; the decision that releases it belongs to the individual. In 1945 his secretary wrote to the British national assembly:

“Once the friends start out to win the goals set in their Plan, they will find the Divine confirmations sustaining them and hastening its consummation....” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to the National Spiritual Assembly of the British Isles, 9 August 1945, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

And in a postscript in his own hand in 1939, he invited the doubter to test the claim by acting on it:

“The powers of heaven and earth mysteriously assist in its execution. Such an opportunity is irreplaceable. Let the doubter arise, and himself verify the truth of such assertions. To try, to persevere, is to ensure ultimate and complete victory.” (Shoghi Effendi, postscript to a letter of 28 January 1939 written on his behalf to the National Spiritual Assembly of the United States and Canada, in The Power of Divine Assistance; also in Messages to America and This Decisive Hour no. 46)

The same letter quotes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá on the quality the action needs:

“The Kingdom of God is possessed of limitless potency. Audacious must be the army of life if the confirming aid of that Kingdom is to be repeatedly vouchsafed unto it.” (‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted in the same letter of 28 January 1939)

The Guardian also stated the reverse, in the same 1954 letter as “powerless unless and until”:

“The sustaining strength of Bahá’u’lláh Himself, the Founder of the Faith, will be withheld from every and each individual who fails in the long run to arise and play his part.” (Shoghi Effendi, 28 July 1954, in Citadel of Faith)

The words “in the long run” are part of the sentence. What is described is a settled pattern of not arising, not a single missed opportunity or a bad season. The Universal House of Justice quoted this sentence at Riḍván 1996 in the course of explaining why individual initiative is necessary.

Detachment and trust

In the same section of Gleanings that promises angels to those who leave home to teach, Bahá’u’lláh names what is required of them:

“Whoso ariseth to teach Our Cause must needs detach himself from all earthly things, and regard, at all times, the triumph of Our Faith as his supreme objective. This hath, verily, been decreed in the Guarded Tablet. And when he determineth to leave his home, for the sake of the Cause of his Lord, let him put his whole trust in God, as the best provision for his journey, and array himself with the robe of virtue.”

“If he be kindled with the fire of His love, if he forgoeth all created things, the words he uttereth shall set on fire them that hear him.” (Gleanings CLVII)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in a Tablet published in an early English translation:

“By the Lord of the Kingdom! If one arise to promote the Word of God with a pure heart, overflowing with the love of God and severed from the world, the Lord of Hosts will assist him with such a power as will penetrate the core of the existent beings.” (Tablets of Abdul-Baha Abbas, vol. 2, p. 348, as given in The Power of Divine Assistance)

The Báb’s prayer for those who serve asks for help specifically for the detached:

“O Lord! Assist those who have renounced all else but Thee, and grant them a mighty victory. Send down upon them, O Lord, the concourse of the angels in heaven and earth and all that is between, to aid Thy servants, to succor and strengthen them, to enable them to achieve success, to sustain them, to invest them with glory, to confer upon them honor and exaltation, to enrich them and to make them triumphant with a wondrous triumph.” (Selections from the Writings of the Báb, Prayers and Meditations)

Purity of motive

The Báb’s standard for the Letters of the Living was “naught but the purest motive”. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in the Tablets of the Divine Plan, sets out conditions for attaining the station of the “Apostles of Bahá’u’lláh” — “The first condition is firmness in the Covenant of God”; “The second condition: Fellowship and love amongst the believers”; “The third condition: Teachers must continually travel” — and then sums up what is required of the teacher:

“The aim is this: The intention of the teacher must be pure, his heart independent, his spirit attracted, his thought at peace, his resolution firm, his magnanimity exalted and in the love of God a shining torch. Should he become as such, his sanctified breath will even affect the rock; otherwise there will be no result whatsoever.” (Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet to the Bahá’ís of the United States and Canada, revealed 19–22 April 1916)

The Guardian’s 1932 letter, quoted above, names the most common failure of motive — taking credit:

“If any one of them should even for a second consider his achievements as due to his own capacities, his work is ended and his fall starts. This is in fact the reason why so many competent souls have after wonderful services suddenly found themselves absolutely impotent and perhaps thrown aside by the Spirit of the Cause as useless souls. The criterion is the extent to which we are ready to have the will of God operate through us.” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 31 March 1932, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

The same letter that tells the discouraged believer to stop dwelling on his frailties warns the capable believer against relying on his strengths. The principle is the same in both cases: the work is done through the person, not by the person.

Steadfastness in the Covenant, and love

Firmness in the Covenant is ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s first condition in the Tablet above, and it appears in the promise of the ant and the elephant (“resolutely promulgateth the Covenant”). In an early Tablet He wrote:

“Be ye assured with the greatest assurance that, verily, God will help those who are firm in His Covenant in every matter, through His confirmation and favor, the lights of which will shine forth unto the east of the earth, as well as the west thereof.” (Tablets of Abdul-Baha Abbas, vol. 1, p. 83, as given in The Power of Divine Assistance)

Love appears as the measure in Bahá’u’lláh’s words quoted in The Dispensation: “if his love for Me wax stronger”. The Words of Wisdom name “steadfastness in His Love” as the source of courage.

Perseverance and daily effort

The promise is not described as a single intervention. It is described as proportionate to continuing effort.

“The harder you strive to attain your goal, the greater will be the confirmations of Bahá’u’lláh, and the more certain you can feel to attain success. Be cheerful, therefore, and exert yourself with full faith and confidence. For Bahá’u’lláh has promised His Divine assistance to everyone who arises with a pure and detached heart to spread His holy Word, even though he may be bereft of every human knowledge and capacity, and notwithstanding the forces of darkness and of opposition which may be arrayed against him.” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 3 February 1937, in The Power of Divine Assistance)

“However severe the challenge, however multiple the tasks, however short the time, however somber the world outlook, however limited the material resources of a hard-pressed adolescent community, the untapped sources of celestial strength from which it can draw are measureless, in their potencies, and will unhesitatingly pour forth their energizing influences if the necessary daily effort be made and the required sacrifices be willingly accepted.” (Shoghi Effendi, 5 July 1950, in Citadel of Faith)

The 1950 passage names the condition as “the necessary daily effort”. The word is daily, not heroic. (The Power of Divine Assistance gives this passage with the date 18 December 1945; the text in Citadel of Faith places it in the letter of 5 July 1950. The Citadel date is used here.)

Where the conditions seem to pull against the promise

A careful reader will notice a tension. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá says the teacher’s intention “must be pure”, his heart “independent”, his resolution “firm”, and that “otherwise there will be no result whatsoever”. The same Tablet asks, “As long as a soul is not perfected, how can he efface the defects of others?” Yet the Guardian’s letters tell people to serve “however unworthy one may feel”, “in spite of lack of experience”, and “even though he may be bereft of every human knowledge and capacity”.

These do not contradict each other. The conditions in ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s Tablet concern the direction of the heart — purity of intention, detachment, reliance on God, love — not prior competence or a finished character. The Guardian’s letters relieve people of the requirement to be competent or complete first. They never relieve them of the requirement to rely on God rather than on themselves; the 1932 letter makes that the “criterion”. And the 1942 letter shows how the two connect over time: “the very act of striving to serve, however unworthy one may feel, attracts the blessings of God and enables one to become more fitted for the task”. The fitness ‘Abdu’l-Bahá describes is something that grows through serving. It is not an entrance requirement.

The standard and the promise given together

The sequence of greatness, standard and assurance is not an arrangement imposed on the texts. In many places the authoritative texts state the demand at its full height and then, in the same passage, give the promise. Neither is softened to fit the other. Four examples follow.

‘Abdu’l-Bahá, February 1917

In the Tablet to the Southern States of 3 February 1917, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá states what is asked:

“At this time and at this period we must avail ourselves of this most great opportunity. We must not sit inactive for one moment; we must sever ourselves from composure, rest, tranquillity, goods, property, life and attachment to material things. We must sacrifice everything to His Highness, the Possessor of existence, so that the powers of the Kingdom may show greater penetration and the brilliant effulgence in this New Cycle may illumine the worlds of minds and ideals.”

A few paragraphs later comes the assurance, followed by more requirements:

“Therefore, rest ye assured in the confirmations of the Merciful and the assistance of the Most High; become ye sanctified above and purified from this world and the inhabitants thereof; suffer your intentions to work for the good of all; cut your attachment to the earth and like unto the essence of the spirit become ye light and delicate.”

And then a prayer for the reader to say, which begins by admitting the gap:

“O my God! O my God! Thou seest me in my lowliness and weakness, occupied with the greatest undertaking, determined to raise Thy word among the masses and to spread Thy teachings among Thy peoples. How can I succeed unless Thou assist me with the breath of the Holy Spirit, help me to triumph by the hosts of Thy glorious kingdom, and shower upon me Thy confirmations, which alone can change a gnat into an eagle, a drop of water into rivers and seas, and an atom into lights and suns?” (Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet to the Bahá’ís of the Southern States, 3 February 1917)

The prayer gives the believer words for the three things this page is about: “lowliness and weakness” (the honest self-assessment), “the greatest undertaking” (the standard), and “Thy confirmations, which alone can change a gnat into an eagle” (the assurance).

Shoghi Effendi, The Advent of Divine Justice (1938)

Under the heading “Universal Participation”, Shoghi Effendi sets out a demand that leaves no one out:

“All must participate, however humble their origin, however limited their experience, however restricted their means, however deficient their education, however pressing their cares and preoccupations, however unfavorable the environment in which they live.”

and follows it with the historical evidence that such people succeed:

“How often—and the early history of the Faith in the land of its birth offers many a striking testimony—have the lowliest adherents of the Faith, unschooled and utterly inexperienced, and with no standing whatever, and in some cases devoid of intelligence, been capable of winning victories for their Cause, before which the most brilliant achievements of the learned, the wise, and the experienced have paled.” (The Advent of Divine Justice, “Universal Participation”)

The passages on Peter and the tiny ant follow directly. The same book contains the Guardian’s most demanding statements of the standard of rectitude, chastity and freedom from prejudice, and closes with the compilation of Bahá’u’lláh’s words that many readers treat as an anchor. Shoghi Effendi’s introduction to that compilation lists its contents and its intended effect together: the passages contain “the counsels He imparts, the warnings He utters, the vistas He discloses, and the assurances and promises He gives”, and they

“cannot fail to produce on the minds and hearts of any one of its members, who approaches them with befitting humility and detachment, such powerful reactions as to illuminate his entire being and intensify tremendously his daily exertions.” (The Advent of Divine Justice, “Passages from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh to Aid in Discharging Multiple Tasks”)

The purpose he names is daily exertion, increased by reading.

Shoghi Effendi, 19 July 1956

In a letter to the American Bahá’ís, under the heading “The Individual Bahá’í Must Arise”, the Guardian describes the inner work required of each person without softening it:

“The individual alone must assess its character, consult his conscience, prayerfully consider all its aspects, manfully struggle against the natural inertia that weighs him down in his effort to arise, shed, heroically and irrevocably, the trivial and superfluous attachments which hold him back, empty himself of every thought that may tend to obstruct his path”

“The natural inertia that weighs him down in his effort to arise” is a plain acknowledgment of the ordinary difficulty of contending with the insistent self. Two paragraphs later comes the promise:

“Delicate and strenuous though the task may be, however arduous and prolonged the effort required, whatsoever the nature of the perils and pitfalls that beset the path of whoever arises to revive the fortunes of a Faith struggling against the rising forces of materialism, nationalism, secularism, racialism, ecclesiasticism, the all-conquering potency of the grace of God, vouchsafed through the Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, will, undoubtedly, mysteriously and surprisingly, enable whosoever arises to champion His Cause to win complete and total victory.” (Shoghi Effendi, 19 July 1956, in Citadel of Faith)

The Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 2008

The House of Justice’s message at Riḍván 2008 ends with a paragraph of demands and a sentence of assurance:

“So free must be your thoughts and actions of any trace of prejudice—racial, religious, economic, national, tribal, class, or cultural—that even the stranger sees in you loving friends. So high must be your standard of excellence and so pure and chaste your lives that the moral influence you exert penetrates the consciousness of the wider community. Only if you demonstrate the rectitude of conduct to which the writings of the Faith call every soul will you be able to struggle against the myriad forms of corruption, overt and subtle, eating at the vitals of society.”

“As you press ahead, be confident that the Concourse on high is marshalling its forces and stands ready to come to your aid. Our continued prayers will surround you.” (Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 2008)

What the pairing shows

In each case the order is the same as in the Báb’s address to the Letters of the Living. The standard is not reduced to what the reader can already manage. The promise is not made conditional on first reaching the standard. It is attached to “pressing ahead”, “arising”, “striving”. The texts treat a person who is far from the standard but moving toward it as someone the help is meant for.

At Riḍván 1996 the Universal House of Justice listed what the individual draws on in this work. The promise of assistance is one item on a list that also includes study, prayer and the effort to live by the laws:

“To optimize the use of these capacities, the individual draws upon his love for Bahá’u’lláh, the power of the Covenant, the dynamics of prayer, the inspiration and education derived from regular reading and study of the Holy Texts, and the transformative forces that operate upon his soul as he strives to behave in accordance with the divine laws and principles. In addition to these, the individual, having been given the duty to teach the Cause, is endowed with the capacity to attract particular blessings promised by Bahá’u’lláh.” (Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 1996)

Prayers for assistance

Prayers from the Tablets of the Divine Plan

‘Abdu’l-Bahá did not only promise help; He gave the words with which to ask for it, and instructed that some be said daily. In the Tablet to the Central States (8 February 1917) He wrote: “Let the spreaders of the fragrances of God recite this prayer every morning”. The prayer includes:

“O Lord! I am weak, strengthen me with Thy power and potency. My tongue falters, suffer me to utter Thy commemoration and praise. I am lowly, honor me through admitting me into Thy kingdom. I am remote, cause me to approach the threshold of Thy mercifulness. O Lord! Make me a brilliant lamp, a shining star and a blessed tree, adorned with fruit, its branches overshadowing all these regions.” (Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet to the Bahá’ís of the Central States, 8 February 1917)

Each sentence pairs a weakness with a request: weak / strengthen; falters / utter; lowly / honor; remote / approach.

From the Tablet to the Western States (15 February 1917):

“O God! O God! This is a broken-winged bird and his flight is very slow—assist him so that he may fly toward the apex of prosperity and salvation, wing his way with the utmost joy and happiness throughout the illimitable space, raise his melody in Thy Supreme Name in all the regions, exhilarate the ears with this call, and brighten the eyes by beholding the signs of guidance.”

“O Lord! I am single, alone and lowly. For me there is no support save Thee, no helper except Thee and no sustainer beside Thee. Confirm me in Thy service, assist me with the cohorts of Thine angels, make me victorious in the promotion of Thy Word and suffer me to speak out Thy wisdom amongst Thy creatures. Verily, Thou art the helper of the weak and the defender of the little ones, and verily Thou art the Powerful, the Mighty and the Unconstrained.” (Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet to the Bahá’ís of the Western States, 15 February 1917)

From the last Tablet (8 March 1917), a prayer that recalls what confirmation has done for others:

“O God! O God! Thou seest my weakness, lowliness and humility before Thy creatures; nevertheless, I have trusted in Thee and have arisen in the promotion of Thy teachings among Thy strong servants, relying on Thy power and might.”

“O Lord! Should the breath of the Holy Spirit confirm the weakest of creatures, he would attain all to which he aspireth and would possess anything he desireth. Indeed, Thou hast assisted Thy servants in the past and, though they were the weakest of Thy creatures, the lowliest of Thy servants and the most insignificant of those who lived upon the earth, through Thy sanction and potency they took precedence over the most glorious of Thy people and the most noble of mankind. Whereas formerly they were as moths, they became as royal falcons, and whereas before they were as brooks, they became as seas, through Thy bestowal and Thy mercy.” (Tablets of the Divine Plan, Tablet to the Bahá’ís of the United States and Canada, 8 March 1917)

The word “nevertheless” in the first line states the subject of this page: weakness is admitted, and the person has arisen anyway, “relying on Thy power and might”.

“Is there any Remover of difficulties save God?”

The Báb’s short prayer is printed among the prayers for tests and difficulties:

“Is there any Remover of difficulties save God? Say: Praised be God! He is God! All are His servants, and all abide by His bidding!” (the Báb, Bahá’í Prayers, Tests and Difficulties)

Shoghi Effendi records, in God Passes By, an occasion when Bahá’u’lláh Himself prescribed it. During the years in Baghdad, before His withdrawal to the mountains of Kurdistan, when the Bábí community was in a low and divided state, Mírzá Áqá Ján reported (as related by Nabíl) that Bahá’u’lláh said:

“Bid them recite: “Is there any Remover of difficulties save God? Say: Praised be God! He is God! All are His servants, and all abide by His bidding!” Tell them to repeat it five hundred times, nay, a thousand times, by day and by night, sleeping and waking, that haply the Countenance of Glory may be unveiled to their eyes, and tiers of light descend upon them.” (God Passes By, ch. 7)

This account is Shoghi Effendi’s citation of Nabíl’s narrative of Mírzá Áqá Ján’s testimony, so it is reported speech at two removes. It shows the prayer used for a community in difficulty and low spirits, not only for individual hardship.

What the promise does not say

Three limits are worth stating so that the promise is not misread.

It does not promise an easy path. The promises are mostly given to people who are about to face opposition: “though the forces of earth and heaven be arrayed against him”; “though tens of thousands of enemies be leagued against him”. At Riḍván 2022 the House of Justice wrote that the more the friends do, the more resistance they should expect:

“we are conscious that the greater the friends’ efforts to promote the Word of God, the stronger the countervailing forces they will encounter, sooner or later, from various quarters. They must fortify their minds and spirits against the tests that are sure to come, lest these impair the integrity of their endeavours.” (Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 2022)

The same paragraph then names what carries the community through: “The confirmations of the Almighty are the gusts that fill its sails and propel it towards its destination.”

It does not promise a particular result on a particular day. The Guardian’s secretary wrote to a believer facing many obstacles:

“While he would urge you to courageously meet and overcome the many obstacles that stand in your way, he would at the same time advise you that in case of failure and no matter what befalls you, you should remain radiantly content at, and entirely submissive to, the Divine will.” (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, 28 April 1936, in Living the Life)

The promise of “victory” in these texts often refers to the long-run outcome (“ultimate victory”, “in the fullness of time”), and Shoghi Effendi describes the help as working “mysteriously and surprisingly” — that is, not on a schedule the believer sets.

It does not depend on visible success to be worth acting on. At Riḍván 2015, after its words to those in places where progress is slow, the House of Justice added:

“Beyond this, and whatever its immediate effects, service to the Beloved is, in itself, a source of abiding joy to the spirit.” (Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 2015)

This connects to the question of what lifts the spirit. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s image of the bird “exhilarated by its own singing” (§211) and this sentence say the same thing from two directions: the act of service has its own effect on the person who performs it, whatever its outward results.

Notes on sources and what could not be verified

  • The Báb’s farewell address is Nabíl’s record in The Dawn-Breakers, not an authenticated Tablet. It is quoted from the corpus copy of The Dawn-Breakers ([source]), whose OCR omits some diacritics; the final paragraph is given as it appears in The Power of Divine Assistance, which has “Muḥammad”.
  • Passages Shoghi Effendi quoted from untranslated Tablets (“He, verily, will aid everyone that aideth Him”; the speck of dust and the dewdrop; “however inconsiderable his share”; the two passages in The Dispensation) are given as he quoted them. Their source Tablets are not named in his text and were not identified in this knowledge base.
  • The Báb’s “tiny ant” reaches us through three hands: the Báb, quoted by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, quoted by Shoghi Effendi. The original Tablets of the Báb and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá were not located.
  • “imparteth to the drop the power of the sea, and turneth the atom into a very sun” (Universal House of Justice, 31 October 2008) is a quotation whose source the letter does not give. It could not be traced. Closely related Persian wording (“the drop a sea and the atom a sun”) appears in two Persian works attributed to ‘Abdu’l-Bahá in this knowledge base ([source], [source]), but not this exact phrase.
  • Letters written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to individuals are quoted from the Research Department compilations The Power of Divine Assistance and Living the Life as held on the Bahá’í Reference Library. The original letters are not in the corpus.
  • Early translations of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s Tablets (Tablets of Abdul-Baha Abbas, 1909–1916) are used only where the Research Department included them in The Power of Divine Assistance. Their English is older and not the current authorised style.
  • Date discrepancy: The Power of Divine Assistance (no. 59) dates “the untapped sources of celestial strength” passage to 18 December 1945; Citadel of Faith prints it in the letter of 5 July 1950. The Citadel date is used.
  • Text variants: small wording differences exist between The Power of Divine Assistance and the source books (for example “spurs on” in the compilation, “spurreth on” in Selections §208; “racialism and ecclesiasticism” in the compilation, “racialism, ecclesiasticism” in Citadel of Faith; “ensure” in the compilation, “insure” in the This Decisive Hour copy). Quotations here follow the source book where it is in the corpus.
  • Paris Talks records ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s spoken words as noted at the time; it is quoted alongside His Tablets and is not used as the only support for any point.
