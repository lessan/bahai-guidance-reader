The Tablet of the Holy Mariner (Lawḥ-i-Malláḥu’l-Quds)

Last updated 17 October 2026

The Tablet of the Holy Mariner is a Tablet of Bahá’u’lláh in two parts, one in Arabic and one in Persian. The Arabic part is a narrative in short lines, each followed by the refrain “Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!” It tells of a ship, its passengers, their ascent and expulsion, and the grief of a heavenly Maid at the unfaithfulness she finds. Shoghi Effendi describes the Tablet as one “in which Bahá’u’lláh prophesies the severe afflictions that are to befall Him”. The Arabic part has an authorised English translation, printed in full below. The Persian part has no authorised translation; one provisional translation is quoted in short extracts. This page is one of a series; the hub is Poetry in the Writings and by Bahá’í poets.

The poem

When and where. Shoghi Effendi writes that it was “on the fifth of Naw-Rúz (1863)”, while Bahá’u’lláh was keeping the festival at the Mazra‘iy-i-Vashshásh on the outskirts of Baghdád, that an emissary of Námiq Páshá arrived with a request for an interview. Bahá’u’lláh “had just revealed” the Tablet, “whose gloomy prognostications had aroused the grave apprehensions of His Companions” (God Passes By, GPB VIII.39). He lists the Tablet among the writings of the Baghdád years (GPB VIII.29) and, later in the same chapter, refers to “the Tablet of the Holy Mariner” as an anticipation of those who “were to raise the standard of rebellion and precipitate the gravest crisis in the history of the Faith” (GPB VIII.42).

The occasion, from the historians. Adib Taherzadeh (scholarship, not authority) gives the setting: Bahá’u’lláh had pitched His tent in a field outside Baghdad known as the Mazra’iy-i-Vashshash, rented by His brother Mírzá Músá, and was keeping the festival there with companions who also lived in tents. On the fifth day of Naw-Rúz, he writes, Mírzá Áqá Ján, Bahá’u’lláh’s amanuensis, came out of the tent and chanted the Tablet to the believers (The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, vol. 1, pp. 228–229). Taherzadeh quotes Nabíl, who was present, that the Tablet was “read aloud to them”, that “the chapter of Baghdad was about to be closed”, and that Bahá’u’lláh then ordered the tents folded and the companions to return to the city. Before the tents were gone, the governor’s emissary arrived (GPB VIII.39–40, quoting Nabíl). Shoghi Effendi records that the next day the Deputy-Governor delivered ‘Álí Páshá’s letter inviting Bahá’u’lláh to Constantinople (GPB VIII.41), and that the news overwhelmed the exiles.

What ‘Abdu’l-Bahá says. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá wrote: “Study the Tablet of the Holy Mariner that ye may know the truth, and consider that the Blessed Beauty hath fully foretold future events. Let them who perceive, take warning!” In Bahá’í Prayers these words stand before the Tablet as an epigraph (Bahá’í Prayers, “Tablet of the Holy Mariner”, lines 2487–2489 of the corpus file). A version of the same sentence, ending “Verily in this is a bounty for the sincere!”, is in Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá (SWAB 233.15).

Languages and form. The Arabic part opens “He is the Gracious, the Well-Beloved!” (in Arabic هو العزيز المحبوب) and the Persian part opens with a different invocation, in Persian هو العجمّى الفارسى العراقى. The saved Arabic text (from the Má’idiy-i-Ásmání edition, as published at oceanoflights.org, which gives vol. 5, p. 335; Cole’s note gives vol. 4, pp. 335–341) is a single paragraph in which each clause ends with the refrain فسبحان ربّي الأبهى, and many clauses end in the same long vowel followed by hamza (for example العماء, البيضاء, الإنشاء). That is rhymed prose with a refrain. The sources used here do not name a metre. The saved Persian text has no refrain. Bahá’í Library files the Tablet under “Bahá’u’lláh, Poetry of”.

Structure. The Arabic part has three movements. First, the Holy Mariner is told to launch the ark and bring its passengers to a station where God appeared in the deathless tree. They ascend, then try to rise to a station beyond their own, are cast out by “the burning meteor”, and are returned to the world below. Second, the Maid of Heaven appears, declares that no one without love for the Youth can ascend, and sends a handmaiden down to find the fragrance of faithfulness in those who claim it. Third, the handmaiden finds none, reports that the Youth is “lone and forlorn in the land of exile”, and dies; the maids of heaven gather and mourn. The Persian part retells the voyage in plainer terms and ends with direct counsel to the reader (see below).

What the translators and commentators say (scholarship). Taherzadeh writes that “The theme of this Tablet is the story of the Covenant and man’s unfaithfulness to it”, that it “is revealed in symbolic language”, and that the Persian part is “for the most part, similar in content to the Arabic Tablet” with added exhortations. In his reading the Holy Mariner is Bahá’u’lláh, the dwellers are the believers, and the ark is the Cause and the Covenant. Michael Sours (Wilmette Institute notes, 1999) compares the Tablet’s outline with that of the Kitáb-i-Íqán. Juan Cole, in a note to his provisional translation, links the mariner to Noah and to the spiritual journey of ʿAṭṭár’s Parliament of the Birds. These are readings by scholars. None of them is in the text of the Writings.

The text in English

The Arabic part: authorised translation (in full)

Authorised translation, as printed in Bahá’í Prayers (address BPRY Tablet of the Holy Mariner 1; corpus file [source], lines 2491–2722). Licence: ok-with-credit (© Bahá’í International Community; check the bahai.org terms before a commercial release). The corpus file names no translator. Bahaipedia and Bahá’í Library attribute the translation to Shoghi Effendi. The text below includes the refrain after each line, as printed.

He is the Gracious, the Well-Beloved!

O Holy Mariner! Bid thine ark of eternity appear before the Celestial Concourse, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Launch it upon the ancient sea, in His Name, the Most Wondrous, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And let the angelic spirits enter, in the Name of God, the Most High. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Unmoor it, then, that it may sail upon the ocean of glory, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Haply the dwellers therein may attain the retreats of nearness in the everlasting realm. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Having reached the sacred strand, the shore of the crimson seas, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Bid them issue forth and attain this ethereal invisible station, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

A station wherein the Lord hath in the Flame of His Beauty appeared within the deathless tree; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Wherein the embodiments of His Cause cleansed themselves of self and passion; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Around which the Glory of Moses doth circle with the everlasting hosts; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Wherein the Hand of God was drawn forth from His bosom of Grandeur; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Wherein the ark of the Cause remaineth motionless even though to its dwellers be declared all divine attributes. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

O Mariner! Teach them that are within the ark that which we have taught thee behind the mystic veil. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Perchance they may not tarry in the sacred snow-white spot, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

But may soar upon the wings of the spirit unto that station which the Lord hath exalted above all mention in the worlds below, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

May wing through space even as the favored birds in the realm of eternal reunion; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

May know the mysteries hidden in the Seas of light. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

They passed the grades of worldly limitations and reached that of the divine unity, the center of heavenly guidance. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

They have desired to ascend unto that state which the Lord hath ordained to be above their stations. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Whereupon the burning meteor cast them out from them that abide in the Kingdom of His Presence, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And they heard the Voice of Grandeur raised from behind the unseen pavilion upon the Height of Glory: Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“O guardian angels! Return them to their abode in the world below, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“Inasmuch as they have purposed to rise to that sphere which the wings of the celestial dove have never attained; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“Whereupon the ship of fancy standeth still which the minds of them that comprehend cannot grasp.” Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Whereupon the maid of heaven looked out from her exalted chamber, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And with her brow signed to the Celestial Concourse, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Flooding with the light of her countenance the heaven and the earth, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And as the radiance of her beauty shone upon the people of dust, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

All beings were shaken in their mortal graves. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

She then raised the call which no ear through all eternity hath ever heard, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And thus proclaimed: “By the Lord! He whose heart hath not the fragrance of the love of the exalted and glorious Arabian Youth, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“Can in no wise ascend unto the glory of the highest heaven.” Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Thereupon she summoned unto herself one maiden from her handmaidens, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And commanded her: “Descend into space from the mansions of eternity, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“And turn thou unto that which they have concealed in the inmost of their hearts. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“Shouldst thou inhale the perfume of the robe from the Youth that hath been hidden within the tabernacle of light by reason of that which the hands of the wicked have wrought, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“Raise a cry within thyself, that all the inmates of the chambers of Paradise, that are the embodiments of the eternal wealth, may understand and hearken; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“That they may all come down from their everlasting chambers and tremble, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“And kiss their hands and feet for having soared to the heights of faithfulness; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“Perchance they may find from their robes the fragrance of the Beloved One.” Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Thereupon the countenance of the favored damsel beamed above the celestial chambers even as the light that shineth from the face of the Youth above His mortal temple; Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

She then descended with such an adorning as to illumine the heavens and all that is therein. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

She bestirred herself and perfumed all things in the lands of holiness and grandeur. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

When she reached that place she rose to her full height in the midmost heart of creation, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And sought to inhale their fragrance at a time that knoweth neither beginning nor end. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

She found not in them that which she did desire, and this, verily, is but one of His wondrous tales. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

She then cried aloud, wailed and repaired to her own station within her most lofty mansion, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And then gave utterance to one mystic word, whispered privily by her honeyed tongue, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And raised the call amidst the Celestial Concourse and the immortal maids of heaven: Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“By the Lord! I found not from these idle claimants the breeze of Faithfulness! Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

“By the Lord! The Youth hath remained lone and forlorn in the land of exile in the hands of the ungodly.” Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

She then uttered within herself such a cry that the Celestial Concourse did shriek and tremble, Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

And she fell upon the dust and gave up the spirit. It seemeth she was called and hearkened unto Him that summoned her unto the Realm on High. Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Glorified be He that created her out of the essence of love in the midmost heart of His exalted paradise! Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!

Thereupon the maids of heaven hastened forth from their chambers, upon whose countenances the eye of no dweller in the highest paradise had ever gazed. Glorified be our Lord, the Most High!

They all gathered around her, and lo! they found her body fallen upon the dust; Glorified be our Lord, the Most High!

And as they beheld her state and comprehended a word of the tale told by the Youth, they bared their heads, rent their garments asunder, beat upon their faces, forgot their joy, shed tears and smote with their hands upon their cheeks, and this is verily one of the mysterious grievous afflictions— Glorified be our Lord, the Most High!

—Bahá’u’lláh

The Persian part: provisional translation (short extracts)

Provisional translation by Mehran Ghasempour (posted to Tarjuman, 2002; web edition by Mehdi Wolf at bahai-library.com). It is not the text of the Writings and is not authorised. Licence: permission-needed.

The Persian part first retells the voyage: the dwellers in the Ark are stopped at a station, the Mariner is commanded to give them one letter of the concealed Word, they fly, they are served ruby wine by the immortal youths and the cup-bearer, they stay for ages, and then they are tested, turn to the cup-bearer instead of the Friend, try to rise to the Greatest Name, and are forbidden entry because they “inhaled not the fragrance of the mystic Youth”. It then turns to the reader. Three sentences from that address:

“Barter not nearness unto the beauty of the Friend for both worlds.”

“The lips are for the mention of the Beloved; defile them not with foul water. The heart is the abode of the immortal mysteries; busy it not with attention unto ephemeral things.”

Ghasempour’s notes explain that the Persian word for the ship (fulk), with different vowel points, means “heaven, sphere, firmament”. His first line is “He is the Ajami, the Persian, the ‘Iráqí!”, and his note says that “Iraqí” is also translated “Arabian” in the Arabic part.

Themes and images

The ark and the sea. The Holy Mariner is told to “Launch it upon the ancient sea” and to unmoor the ark “that it may sail upon the ocean of glory”. Taherzadeh reads the ark as the Cause of God and the Covenant. Those within it are, in the Tablet’s words, “the dwellers therein”.

A station and a limit. The passengers reach a station where “the Lord hath in the Flame of His Beauty appeared within the deathless tree” and “the Glory of Moses doth circle with the everlasting hosts”. At that station “the ark of the Cause remaineth motionless”. The Mariner is told to teach them “that which we have taught thee behind the mystic veil”, so that they may “wing through space even as the favored birds in the realm of eternal reunion” and “know the mysteries hidden in the Seas of light”.

Ascent beyond one’s station, and expulsion. The passengers “have desired to ascend unto that state which the Lord hath ordained to be above their stations”. The “burning meteor” casts them out, and a voice orders the guardian angels to return them “to their abode in the world below”. Taherzadeh reads this as a prophecy of those who would break the Covenant; Shoghi Effendi’s statement at GPB VIII.42 refers to the Tablet in connection with the later rebellion.

The Maid of Heaven and the Youth. The Maid’s brow signs to the Concourse, and “All beings were shaken in their mortal graves”. Her cry is the test: “He whose heart hath not the fragrance of the love of the exalted and glorious Arabian Youth” can “in no wise ascend unto the glory of the highest heaven”. Taherzadeh writes that the Maid is an allegorical term that in Bahá’u’lláh’s Writings can refer to Himself, to an attribute of God, or to the Most Great Spirit, and that the Youth is Bahá’u’lláh.

Fragrance as the sign of faithfulness. The handmaiden is told to “inhale the perfume of the robe from the Youth”, and the inmates of Paradise are told they “may find from their robes the fragrance of the Beloved One”. The handmaiden’s report is the turning point: “She found not in them that which she did desire”.

Grief. The Youth is “lone and forlorn in the land of exile in the hands of the ungodly”. The Maid “fell upon the dust and gave up the spirit”, and the maids of heaven “bared their heads, rent their garments asunder, beat upon their faces”. The last refrain changes from “my Lord, the All-Glorious” to “our Lord, the Most High”.

Inward and outward unity (Persian part). The Persian ends with an appeal, in the provisional translation, that “the inward mysteries not oppose the outward deeds, nor the outward deeds turn away from the inward mysteries”. Taherzadeh gives the same point: that conduct “faithfully reflect the secrets of their hearts”. Cole’s note states it as a hope for “inward and outward unity” followed by a lament that the addressees will choose otherwise.

What is not settled

  • The day. Shoghi Effendi gives “the fifth of Naw-Rúz (1863)”. Taherzadeh’s note says Naw-Rúz fell on 22 March in 1863, and Cole’s note gives 26 March; both follow if 22 March is the first day. The sources here do not give a calendar date beyond these two scholars’ notes.
  • The translator and first printing of the Arabic part. Bahá’í Prayers does not name the translator. Bahaipedia says Shoghi Effendi’s translation was first published in Star of the West. The Leiden List, as Cole quotes it, calls the Star of the West version an earlier translation with misprints. The Star of the West text differs from the present authorised text at least in one phrase: “Flame of His Bounty” (SW 13:4, line 969) against “Flame of His Beauty” (Bahá’í Prayers). The sources here do not say how the two relate.
  • “Arabian” or “Iraqi”. The authorised text reads “Arabian Youth”. The saved Arabic text (oceanoflights.org) reads العراقي (“Iraqi”) at that place, and the Persian invocation also has “Iraqi”. Ghasempour’s note says “Iraqí” is also translated “Arabian”. Which manuscript reading lies behind the authorised wording is not stated in the sources used.
  • The name that stopped the ark (Persian part). Ghasempour notes that the sentence about the “Name which dwelt therein” was “most difficult”, and records Christopher Buck’s suggestion that it alludes to Mírzá Yahyá. That is one scholar’s reading, reported in a translator’s note.
  • Who the figures are. The Tablet is allegorical. Taherzadeh identifies the Mariner and the Youth with Bahá’u’lláh and applies the expulsion to Covenant-breakers; the text itself names no one. A lyric should not name anyone the text does not.
  • Authorised Persian text. The Persian part has no authorised English translation; Taherzadeh’s volume says only the Arabic part had been rendered into English when he wrote.
  • The saved originals. Both originals were taken from a web edition of Má’idiy-i-Ásmání, not from a manuscript or from the Bahá’í Reference Library, and the Persian file is in an older orthography with irregular spacing. They have not been collated.

For songwriting

Notes only, not lyrics. Phrases below are exact, from the authorised translation (licence: ok-with-credit, source `BPRY Tablet of the Holy Mariner 1`).

Singable exact phrases (5–15 words)

  • “Glorified be my Lord, the All-Glorious!” (the refrain; it suits a repeated sung line)
  • “Bid thine ark of eternity appear before the Celestial Concourse”
  • “Launch it upon the ancient sea, in His Name, the Most Wondrous”
  • “Unmoor it, then, that it may sail upon the ocean of glory”
  • “Teach them that are within the ark”
  • “May wing through space even as the favored birds in the realm of eternal reunion”
  • “Flooding with the light of her countenance the heaven and the earth”
  • “Perchance they may find from their robes the fragrance of the Beloved One”
  • “She found not in them that which she did desire”
  • “The Youth hath remained lone and forlorn in the land of exile”
  • “Glorified be our Lord, the Most High!” (the closing refrain)

Concrete images. An ark, an ancient sea, a crimson shore, a tree that does not die, a burning meteor, a Maid at a high window whose face lights heaven and earth, a robe with a scent on it, a body fallen on the dust, maids with bared heads and torn garments.

A possible arc. Launch (the ark and the sea), arrival (the station of the tree), the ascent that goes too far, the fall, the Maid’s search for fragrance, her cry, the mourning. The refrain can stay constant through all of it and change only in the last three verses, as the printed text does. The Persian part supplies a different ending: an appeal to keep the heart for the Beloved and to make the outward match the inward.

What a lyric must not claim.

  • That the Tablet names a person, a date or an event. Shoghi Effendi calls it a prophecy of “severe afflictions” and links it to the later rebellion; the Tablet itself does not name them.
  • That the Maid or the Youth is any particular person, unless it says plainly that this is Taherzadeh’s reading.
  • That a provisional or machine-drafted translation of the Persian part is the text of the Writings, or that Cole’s or Ghasempour’s wording may be printed without permission.
  • That the refrain changes anywhere except where the authorised text changes it.
  • That the Tablet is a hymn Bahá’ís have sung to a fixed tune. The sources used here describe it as chanted (Taherzadeh) and as printed with a chorus (Star of the West), and say nothing of a melody.
