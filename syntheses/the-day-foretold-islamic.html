The Day Foretold in Islám: the Day of God, the Qá’im and the Return of Jesus

Last updated 16 October 2026

This page is part of the series the-day-foretold. Related pages: promises-of-the-future, the-day-foretold-hindu, the-day-foretold-jewish, the-day-foretold-zoroastrian, the-day-foretold-buddhist, the-day-foretold-christian, the-day-foretold-babi.

What this page covers

The Qur’án speaks of a Day of God, an Hour, a Resurrection and a Judgment. Shí‘ih Muslims await the Qá’im (He who ariseth) and the return of the Imám Ḥusayn. Sunní Muslims await the Mihdí and the return of Jesus. Shoghi Effendi names what Bahá’u’lláh’s coming is to each:

“…to Christendom Christ returned ‘in the glory of the Father,’ to Shí‘ah Islám the return of the Imám Ḥusayn; to Sunní Islám the descent of the ‘Spirit of God’ (Jesus Christ)…” (Shoghi Effendi, God Passes By, GPB VI.11)

He names the Báb, in the same book, as “the promised Qá’im (He who ariseth), the Ṣáḥibu’z-Zamán (the Lord of the Age)” (GPB I.2) and as “the ‘Qá’im’ (He Who ariseth) promised to the Shí‘ahs, the ‘Mihdí’ (One Who is guided) awaited by the Sunnís” (GPB IV.17). The fulfilment is therefore twofold, through the Báb and through Bahá’u’lláh. Shoghi Effendi quotes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá: “All the peoples of the world are awaiting two Manifestations, Who must be contemporaneous; all wait for the fulfillment of this promise” (GPB VI.7).

The page has separate sections for the Shí‘ih and Sunní expectations. Three kinds of source are kept apart: the Qur’án in Rodwell’s translation (1861), the Bahá’í Writings and Shoghi Effendi, and history and scholarship, each labelled where it appears.

1. The promise: the Qur’án on the Day of God

The quotations below are from J. M. Rodwell’s translation (1861), saved at [source]. Rodwell’s text has no verse numbers; the references use the standard numbering, located by content. Each is followed by what the Writings say about the same theme.

The Day of God and the coming of the Lord

“What can such expect but that God should come down to them overshadowed with clouds, and the angels also, and their doom be sealed? And to God shall all things return.” (Qur’án 2:210, Rodwell)

“…thy Lord shall come and the angels rank on rank” (Qur’án 89:22, Rodwell)

Bahá’u’lláh quotes the first of these verses in the Kitáb-i-Íqán, and also says how the Imáms read it:

“In like manner, the Imáms, those unquenchable lights of divine guidance, have interpreted the verse: ‘What can such expect but that God should come down to them overshadowed with clouds,‘—a sign which they have unquestionably regarded as one of the features of the Day of Resurrection—as referring to the Qá’im and His manifestation.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 152)

Shoghi Effendi lists these verses among those in which, in the Bahá’í understanding, Muḥammad alluded to Bahá’u’lláh’s Day:

“To Him Muḥammad, the Apostle of God, had alluded in His Book as the ‘Great Announcement,’ and declared His Day to be the Day whereon ‘God’ will ‘come down’ ‘overshadowed with clouds,’ the Day whereon ‘thy Lord shall come and the angels rank on rank,’…” (Shoghi Effendi, God Passes By, GPB VI.17)

The trumpet and the light of the Lord

“And there shall be a blast on the trumpet, and all who are in the Heavens and all who are in the Earth shall expire, save those whom God shall vouchsafe to live. Then shall there be another blast on it, and lo! arising they shall gaze around them: And the earth shall shine with the light of her Lord, and the Book shall be set, and the prophets shall be brought up, and the witnesses; and judgment shall be given between them with equity; and none shall be wronged” (Qur’án 39:68–69, Rodwell)

The Íqán gives its own rendering of a trumpet verse and explains it:

“‘And there was a blast on the trumpet—lo! it is the threatened Day! And every soul is summoned to a reckoning—with him an impeller and a witness.‘” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 122)

“Nay, by ‘trumpet’ is meant the trumpet-call of Muḥammad’s Revelation, which was sounded in the heart of the universe, and by ‘resurrection’ is meant His own rise to proclaim the Cause of God.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 123)

Bahá’u’lláh adds that “the Day of Resurrection was ushered in through the Revelation of Muḥammad” (KI 124), and writes:

“It hath been demonstrated and definitely established, through clear evidences, that by ‘Resurrection’ is meant the rise of the Manifestation of God to proclaim His Cause, and by ‘attainment unto the divine Presence’ is meant attainment unto the presence of His Beauty in the person of His Manifestation.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 182)

Shoghi Effendi lists “the second ‘Trumpet blast’” and the Day “when the earth shall shine with the light of her Lord” among the Qur’ánic titles and descriptions of the Day of Bahá’u’lláh (GPB VI.17). Bahá’u’lláh writes of the same Day:

“This is indeed the Day of which it hath been written: ‘Whose shall be the Kingdom this Day?‘” (Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, GWB LXXVIII.3)

In the Tablet to Pope Pius IX He writes: “The Hour that was concealed within the knowledge of God hath struck” (Bahá’u’lláh, The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, SLH 1.107). This echoes the verse in which the Hour is known to God alone:

“They will ask thee of the Hour-for what time is its coming fixed? SAY: The knowledge of it is only with my Lord… not otherwise than on a sudden will it come on you.” (Qur’án 7:187, Rodwell)

The days of God

“Of old did we send Moses with our signs: and said to him, ‘Bring forth thy people from the darkness into the light, and remind them of the days of God.‘” (Qur’án 14:5, Rodwell)

The Báb uses the same phrase when He writes of the Revelation He brings: “My Revelation is indeed far more bewildering than that of Muḥammad, the Apostle of God, if thou dost but pause to reflect upon the days of God” (the Báb, Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.35).

The heavens, the earth and the Revelation

The Qur’án describes the heaven cleaving and the earth changing (for example 82:1, “WHEN the Heaven shall CLEAVE asunder”, Rodwell). Bahá’u’lláh explains these terms in the Íqán:

“By ‘cloven asunder’ is meant that the former Dispensation is superseded and annulled.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 46)

“…by the term ‘earth’ is meant the earth of understanding and knowledge, and by ‘heavens’ the heavens of divine Revelation.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 51)

Of the terms “sovereignty, wealth, life, death, judgment and resurrection”, He writes that they “are not what this generation hath conceived and vainly imagined” (KI 114).

The seal of the prophets

“Muhammad is not the father of any man among you, but he is the Apostle of God, and the seal of the prophets: and God knoweth all things.” (Qur’án 33:40, Rodwell)

The Writings address this phrase directly. Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Íqán:

“Hath not Muḥammad, Himself, declared: ‘I am all the Prophets’? Hath He not said as We have already mentioned: ‘I am Adam, Noah, Moses, and Jesus’? Why should Muḥammad, that immortal Beauty, Who hath said: ‘I am the first Adam’ be incapable of saying also: ‘I am the last Adam’?” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 172)

He continues that “the ‘Seal of the Prophets’ is also applicable unto that Divine Beauty”, and that “being the ‘First of the Prophets,’ He likewise is their ‘Seal.’” In another passage He writes of “veils of glory” and names “such terms as the ‘Seal of the Prophets’ and the like” among them (KI 178). Of the Manifestations of God in general, He writes: “And were they all to proclaim: ‘I am the Seal of the Prophets,‘ they verily utter but the truth, beyond the faintest shadow of doubt.” (KI 196) This page records these statements as the Bahá’í understanding and goes no further.

2. The Shí‘ih expectation: the Qá’im and the return of the Imám Ḥusayn

The Qá’im is promised in Shí‘ih tradition (hadith), not in the Qur’án. Bahá’u’lláh quotes these traditions in the Íqán. Two of them:

“Out of Baní-Háshim there shall come forth a Youth Who shall reveal new laws. He shall summon the people unto Him, but none will heed His call.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 271, quoting a tradition)

“He shall manifest the perfection of Moses, the splendor of Jesus, and the patience of Job. His chosen ones shall be abased in His day.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 273, quoting the tradition of Jábir in the “Káfí”)

Of the second tradition Bahá’u’lláh writes, “Consider, not a single letter of this tradition hath remained unfulfilled.” He also quotes the saying of Imám Ṣádiq: “Knowledge is twenty and seven letters. All that the Prophets have revealed are two letters thereof… But when the Qá’im shall arise, He will cause the remaining twenty and five letters to be made manifest.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 272; also quoted by Shoghi Effendi in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 2.23). Another tradition He cites: “When the Qá’im riseth, that day is the Day of Resurrection” (KI 152).

On the sovereignty attributed to the Qá’im, Bahá’u’lláh writes: “Yea, the sovereignty attributed to the Qá’im and spoken of in the scriptures is a reality, the truth of which none can doubt. This sovereignty, however, is not the sovereignty which the minds of men have falsely imagined” (KI 113).

The Báb as the Qá’im

The Báb states His station in His own words:

“Indeed observe how He Who representeth the origin of creation… identified Himself as the Gate [Báb] for the advent of the promised Qá’im, a descendant of Muḥammad” (the Báb, Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 12.4)

In this passage the Báb explains that He first identified Himself as the Gate. In the next He states that the Bayán was revealed “unto Him Who is your Qá’im”:

“…hath likewise revealed the Bayán, in the manner ye have been promised, unto Him Who is your Qá’im, your Guide, your Mihdí, your Lord” (the Báb, Selections from the Writings of the Báb, SWB 13.34)

Shoghi Effendi writes that Bahá’u’lláh “has acclaimed in the Kitáb-i-Íqán” the Báb as “that promised Qá’im” (WOB 6.9).

The return of the Imám Ḥusayn

“In the name He bore He combined those of the Imám Ḥusayn, the most illustrious of the successors of the Apostle of God… and of the Imám ‘Alí, the Commander of the Faithful” (Shoghi Effendi, God Passes By, GPB VI.12)

Shoghi Effendi also records the testimony of the Imám Ḥusayn: “To the transcendent character of His mission the Imám Ḥusayn had, again according to Bahá’u’lláh, borne witness as a ‘Revelation whose Revealer will be He Who revealed’ the Apostle of God Himself” (GPB VI.18).

Shaykh Aḥmad and Siyyid Káẓim

Shoghi Effendi quotes Siyyid Káẓim-i-Rashtí, successor of Shaykh Aḥmad-i-Aḥsá’í, who told his disciples near the end of his life:

“Verily, I say, after the Qá’im the Qayyúm will be made manifest. For when the star of the former has set the sun of the beauty of Ḥusayn will rise and illuminate the whole world.” (Siyyid Káẓim-i-Rashtí, quoted in God Passes By, GPB VI.20)

Of Shaykh Aḥmad, Shoghi Effendi writes that he “laid stress upon ‘the twin Revelations which are to follow each other in rapid succession’” (GPB VI.7).

3. The Sunní expectation: the Mihdí and the return of Jesus

Shoghi Effendi’s title for Bahá’u’lláh among the Sunnís is “the descent of the ‘Spirit of God’ (Jesus Christ)” (GPB VI.11). The Mihdí, “One Who is guided”, is named in the Báb’s fulfilment (GPB IV.17, above).

The Qur’án verses most often read in this connection are these two, in Rodwell’s translation:

“And he shall be a sign of the last hour” (Qur’án 43:61, Rodwell)

“There shall not be one of the people of the Book but shall believe in Him before his death, and in the day of resurrection, He will be a witness against them.” (Qur’án 4:159, Rodwell)

The passages gathered here do not discuss these two verses. They state the principle on which the return of a Manifestation rests. Bahá’u’lláh writes in the Íqán:

“Muḥammad, Himself, declared: ‘I am Jesus.’ … Thus it is that Jesus, Himself, declared: ‘I go away and come again unto you.’ Consider the sun. Were it to say now, ‘I am the sun of yesterday,‘ it would speak the truth.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 20)

Bahá’u’lláh addresses Jews, Christians and Muslims in turn:

“O JEWS! If ye be intent on crucifying once again Jesus, the Spirit of God, put Me to death, for He hath once more, in My person, been made manifest unto you.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, GWB XLVII)

“Followers of Muḥammad! If it be your wish to riddle with your shafts the breast of Him Who hath caused His Book the Bayán to be sent down unto you, lay hands on Me and persecute Me, for I am His Well-Beloved, the revelation of His own Self, though My name be not His name. I have come in the shadows of the clouds of glory, and am invested by God with invincible sovereignty.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, GWB XLVII)

The phrase “in the shadows of the clouds of glory” connects this passage to Qur’án 2:210 above. An anonymous Bahá’í study, Some Notes on Bahá’í Proofs Based on the Qur’an (scholarship, not authoritative interpretation), reads the return of Jesus in Qur’án 4:158 and 43:61 as “the advent of the promised Apostle of God with the same qualities as Jesus evinced during His mission.”

4. The fulfilment in the Writings: the Day

Shoghi Effendi lists the Qur’ánic titles of the Day in GPB VI.17: the “Great Day,” the “Last Day,” the “Day of God,” the “Day of Judgment,” the “Day of Meeting,” among others. Bahá’u’lláh writes of this Day:

“Attainment unto such presence is possible only in the Day of Resurrection, which is the Day of the rise of God Himself through His all-embracing Revelation.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 151)

“…the promised hour is now come.” (Gleanings, GWB XII)

“These holy ones have, moreover, announced that when He Who is the Dayspring of the manifold grace of God manifesteth Himself, all the Prophets and Messengers, including the Qá’im, will gather together beneath the shadow of the sacred Standard which the Promised One will raise. That hour is now come.” (Bahá’u’lláh, Gleanings, GWB IX)

“Had Muḥammad, the Apostle of God, attained this Day, He would have exclaimed: ‘I have truly recognized Thee, O Thou the Desire of the Divine Messengers!‘” (Bahá’u’lláh, Súriy-i-Ra’ís, in The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, SLH 2.18)

Of the water of His Revelation He writes: “This is the flowing water ye were promised in the Qur’án, and later in the Bayán” (GWB XVIII.8).

5. Those who recognised it (history)

The following is from history and scholarship, not the Writings.

Mullá Ḥusayn. Nabíl’s narrative, The Dawn-Breakers, translated and edited by Shoghi Effendi, records that Siyyid Káẓim’s disciples told Mullá Ḥusayn what their teacher had asked of them:

“…quit their homes, scatter far and wide, purge their hearts from every idle desire, and dedicate themselves to the quest of Him to whose advent he had so often alluded.” (Nabíl, The Dawn-Breakers)

Mullá Ḥusayn’s own account, as recorded by Nabíl, tells of the evening of 22 May 1844 in Shíráz. He had come, he says, “in quest of the promised Beloved.” The Báb described to him the signs of the promised One and then said: “Behold, all these signs are manifest in Me!” Mullá Ḥusayn tells of the night:

“‘I sat spellbound by His utterance, oblivious of time and of those who awaited me. Suddenly the call of the muadhdhín, summoning the faithful to their morning prayer, awakened me from the state of ecstasy into which I seemed to have fallen.’” (Nabíl, The Dawn-Breakers, quoting Mullá Ḥusayn)

“O thou who art the first to believe in Me! Verily I say, I am the Báb, the Gate of God” (the Báb to Mullá Ḥusayn, in The Dawn-Breakers)

Shoghi Effendi summarises the account: Mullá Ḥusayn received “conclusive replies” that “had established beyond the shadow of a doubt the validity of His claim to be the promised Qá’im” (GPB I.4). The historian H. M. Balyuzi (Bahá’u’lláh: The King of Glory) writes that Siyyid Káẓim had told his students “to keep watch, because the advent of the Sahibu’z-Zaman (the Lord of the Age), the Qa’im of the House of Muhammad, was close at hand,” and that they had come together from Karbilá to Shíráz.

The pilgrimage and the declaration. Shoghi Effendi writes that in the month of Sha‘bán 1260 A.H. (September 1844) the Báb, “of the seed of the illustrious Fáṭimih” and “a descendant of the Imám Ḥusayn,” “proceeded, in fulfillment of Islamic traditions, to visit the Kaaba” (GPB I.10). He records the Báb’s “formal assumption… of the authority of the promised Qá’im, in such dramatic circumstances and in so challenging a tone, before a distinguished gathering of eminent Shí‘ah ecclesiastics” (GPB III.3).

6. The sacred music and poetry of Islám

These are descriptions from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), saved under `sources/web/the-day-foretold/islamic/`, so a songwriter can choose an idiom with care.

  • The adhan (call to prayer), recited five times a day. According to the Wikipedia description, the phrases are repeated, the first statement is less ornamented and the repetition has melismas, with a range that can pass an octave. It moves between maqams; Medina uses Bayati and Mecca uses Hijaz. In the Sunní fajr (dawn) adhan the phrase “Prayer is better than sleep” is included; the Shí‘ih formulation includes “Come to the best of deeds” instead. Mullá Ḥusayn’s account in section 5 ends with the muadhdhín’s call to the morning prayer.
  • Qira’at, the modes of recitation of the Qur’án; ten canonical readings were set by scholars in the early centuries.
  • Ta’ziyeh, the Iranian Shí‘ih passion play about Karbalá, with sung dialogue in classical Persian modes and instrumental music of drums and trumpets.
  • Rowzeh-khani and noha: the recitation of the sufferings of Ḥusayn and his family, and the elegy about the martyrdom at Karbalá.

The Shí‘ih forms are mourning forms for Ḥusayn and belong to that devotion.

7. What the sources do not settle

  • The Qur’án and the Qá’im. The Qá’im and the return of Ḥusayn are in Shí‘ih tradition, not in the Qur’án. The Qur’án verses above speak of the Day of God, the Hour and Jesus. How Muslim commentators read them is not in the sources gathered here.
  • The return of Jesus in Qur’án 43:61 and 4:159. The Writings cited here state the principle of the return of a Manifestation and Bahá’u’lláh says He is Jesus, “the Spirit of God”, “once more… made manifest”. They do not set out a verse-by-verse reading of these two verses. The reading in section 3 is from a Bahá’í study, not an authoritative text.
  • Which title belongs to whom. Shoghi Effendi gives the titles of fulfilment as above. The same Qur’ánic Day is referred in different passages to the Báb’s coming and to Bahá’u’lláh’s. GPB VI.7 and VI.12 are the passages that combine them.
  • Muslim acceptance. Nothing here shows that Muslim scholars or institutions accept the Bahá’í reading.

Concrete images from the sources. The upper room in Shíráz, the hour after sunset, 22 May 1844. The Ethiopian servant who serves dinner. The muadhdhín’s call at the end of the night. The pen writing the Súrih of Mulk (the Báb’s commentary on the Súrih of Joseph) while a voice chants. The students from Karbilá who left their homes at Siyyid Káẓim’s request. Clouds, trumpet, light on the earth. The pilgrimage to the Kaaba in September 1844.

A possible emotional arc. Waiting (the students told to watch for the Qá’im), then the door opened at dawn, then the astonishment of the first to recognise, then the promise carried onward (“Arise, scatter far and wide”).

What a lyric must not claim.

  • It must not say that Muslims have failed to see or have rejected the Day; the Writings’ words to the clergy are the Báb’s and Bahá’u’lláh’s and are not to be put in a singer’s voice as a charge.
  • It must not say that the Qur’án itself names the Qá’im or Ḥusayn’s return.
  • It must not say Islám’s scholars accept the Bahá’í reading.
  • It must not claim that the Writings reinterpret “seal of the prophets” beyond what is quoted in section 1.
  • Shí‘ih mourning forms (noha, ta’ziyeh) should be used with care and not as ornament.
