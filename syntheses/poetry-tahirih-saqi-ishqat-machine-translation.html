About the text

  • Source title: ساقی عشقت ای صنم (Sáqí-i-‘ishqat ay ṣanam) (Ṭáhirih, attributed) — original Persian
  • Author field in source header: Ṭáhirih (Qurrat al-‘Ayn), attributed
  • Edition in source header: Persian Wikipedia article ‘اشعار طاهره قرةالعین’ (oldid 43809681), Persian text as shown (retrieved 2026-10-17)
  • Source notes (copied unchanged from header): Persian Wikipedia, ”اشعار طاهره قرةالعین”. Version of the same ghazal with a different opening, given there as a mukhammas by Mirza Vahid Qazvini (Majlis MS 8841, p. 576, as cited by Mohit Tabatabai). The article notes the closing takhallus ”Ṭáhirá” and says this mukhammas is a tazmin of an earlier poet, so attribution to Ṭáhirih is disputed on the page.
  • What the poem itself says: A Persian mukhammas (five-line strophic poem) consisting of 3 stanzas of 5 hemistichs each (15 lines in the source file). Each stanza consists of three lines sharing a stanzaic rhyme (-ú in Stanza 1, -ádih-am in Stanza 2, -á in Stanza 3) followed by two lines incorporating couplets 1, 2, and 7 (with textual variants) of the ghazal `Gar bi-tú uftadam naẓar` (taḍmín). In line 14, the pen-name appears as `طاهرا` (Ṭáhirá), and the object beheld within the heart is `وفا` (”faithfulness“). Any further historical circumstances of composition are not established.

Literal translation

(3 five-line mukhammas stanzas = 15 hemistichs, numbered line by line to match the 15 poem lines of the source file.)

1 (Stanza 1, line 1).

ساقی عشقت ای صنم! زهر ستم سبو سبو

The Cup-Bearer of Thy love, O Idol!—the poison of tyranny, pitcher by pitcher,

2 (Stanza 1, line 2).

ریخت به ساغر دلم با می‌غم کدو کدو

hath poured into the goblet of my heart, together with the wine of sorrow, gourd by gourd.

3 (Stanza 1, line 3).

چند دوم من از غمت گوشه به گوشه سو به سو

How long must I run from sorrow for Thee, corner to corner, side to side?

4 (Stanza 1, line 4).

گر به تو افتدم نظر چشم به چشم و رو به رو

If my gaze should fall upon Thee, eye to eye and countenance to countenance,

5 (Stanza 1, line 5).

شرح کنم غم تو را نکته به نکته مو به مو

I would recount my sorrow for Thee, point by point, hair by hair.

6 (Stanza 2, line 1).

تنا به ره محبتت پای طلب نهاده‌ام

Ever since [printed: taná, “O body”] upon the path of Thine affection I have set the foot of search,

7 (Stanza 2, line 2).

بر رخ دل در الم از ستمت گشاده‌ام

before the face of my heart I have flung open the door of anguish from Thy tyranny;

8 (Stanza 2, line 3).

تا قدمم به سر نهی خاکنشین چو جاده‌ام

so that Thou mayest set Thy foot upon my head, I sit in the dust like the road.

9 (Stanza 2, line 4).

از پی دیدن رخت همچو صبا فتاده‌ام

In pursuit of beholding Thy visage, like the morning breeze I have been cast wandering,

10 (Stanza 2, line 5).

خانه به خانه در به در کوُچه به کوچه کو به کو

house to house, door to door, lane to lane, street to street.

11 (Stanza 3, line 1).

تا شده استخوان من با سگ کویت آشنا

Ever since my bones have become familiar with the dog of Thy street,

12 (Stanza 3, line 2).

محض وفا تویی، مرا غیر تو نیست مدعا

sheer faithfulness art Thou; for me there is no desire save Thee.

13 (Stanza 3, line 3).

مانده به زیر بال غم کگردن مطلب هما

Beneath the wing of sorrow hath remained the neck [printed: kgardan, for gardan] of the quest of the Humá;

14 (Stanza 3, line 4).

در دل خویش طاهرا گشت و ندید جز وفا

within his/her own heart Ṭáhirá searched, and saw naught save faithfulness,

15 (Stanza 3, line 5).

صفحه به صفحه سر به سر پرده به پرده تو به تو

page by page, from end to end, veil by veil, layer by layer.

Readable translation

1 (Stanza 1, line 1). The Cup-Bearer of Thy love, O Beloved, hath poured the venom of tyranny, pitcher on pitcher,

2 (Stanza 1, line 2). Into the goblet of my heart, mingled with the wine of grief, flagon on flagon.

3 (Stanza 1, line 3). How long must I hasten in sorrow for Thee, from corner to corner, from side to side?

4 (Stanza 1, line 4). Should e’er my gaze alight on Thee, eye unto eye and countenance to countenance,

5 (Stanza 1, line 5). I would recount my grief for Thee, point after point and hair by hair.

6 (Stanza 2, line 1). Since on the pathway of Thy love I planted first the foot of quest,

7 (Stanza 2, line 2). Before the face of my own heart I have flung wide the door of anguish through Thy cruelty;

8 (Stanza 2, line 3). That Thou mayest set Thy foot upon my head, I lie in the dust even as the roadway.

9 (Stanza 2, line 4). In quest of gazing on Thy visage, even as the morning breeze I roam,

10 (Stanza 2, line 5). From house to house, from door to door, from lane to lane, from street to street.

11 (Stanza 3, line 1). Since my worn bones have grown familiar with the watch-dog of Thy lane,

12 (Stanza 3, line 2). Thou art pure faithfulness itself; no other goal or heart’s desire have I save Thee.

13 (Stanza 3, line 3). Beneath the wing of grief the royal phoenix of aspiration hath bowed its neck;

14 (Stanza 3, line 4). Within the heart Ṭáhirá searched, and beheld naught therein save faithfulness,

15 (Stanza 3, line 5). Page after page, from end to end, veil within veil and fold on fold.

Uncertain readings

VerseWord or phraseProblemAlternatives considered
2با می‌غم کدو کدو (bá may-i-gham kadú kadú)Orthography in the source joins `می` (may, wine) and `غم` (gham, sorrow) with a zero-width non-joiner (`می‌غم`), making `می` look like the verbal prefix mí-. `کدو` (kadú) literally means ”gourd“, used in classical Persian for a hollowed gourd wine-vessel or flagon, parallel to `سبو` (sabú, pitcher/jar) in line 1.(1) Read `می‌غم` as may-i-gham (”wine of sorrow“) and `کدو کدو` as ”gourd by gourd / flagon on flagon“ (chosen); (2) Read `می` as verbal prefix (ungrammatical here since `غم` is a noun and the verb `ریخت` is already at the start of line 2).
3چند دوم (chand davam)Unvocalized `دوم` is homographic between davam (1st person singular present of davídan, ”to run/hasten“) and duvvum (”second“). Meter (- u u - u - u -, chand davam man az ghamat) and syntax require the verb davam.(1) ”How long must I run / hasten“ (chand davam, chosen); (2) duvvum (”second“, impossible in syntax and meter).
6تنا به ره محبتت (taná bi-rah-i-maḥabbatat)Probable typographical/OCR error in the Wikipedia source text. Every line of the poem is in ramal-i-muthamman-i-maḥdhúf (`- u u -u - u -- u u -u - u -` with khabn in the first foot); `تا به ره محبتت` (tá bi-rah-i-maḥabbatat, ”since upon the path of Thine affection“) scans perfectly (`- u u -u - u -`), matching the anaphoric `تا` at the start of lines 8 and 11, whereas `تنا` (taná, `u -`) violates the meter.(1) Emend `تنا` to `تا` (tá, ”ever since / since“) while noting the printed `تنا` (chosen); (2) Read `تنا` literally as vocative tan-á (”O body!“) or as a corruption of `تنها` (tanhá, ”alone“).
7بر رخ دل در الم (bar rukh-i-dil dar-i-alam)Unvocalized `در الم` can be read with iḍáfih as dar-i-alam (”the door of anguish“, object of `گشاده‌ام`, in the idiom dar bar rukh-i-kasí gushádan, ”to open a door before someone’s face“) or without iḍáfih as dar alam (”in anguish“, taking `رخ دل` as object). The meter (`bar rukh-i-dildar-i-alam`, `- u u -u - u -`) requires the short iḍáfih vowel after `در`.(1) dar-i-alam (”the door of anguish“, chosen); (2) dar alam (”in anguish“, metrically deficient by one short syllable unless scanned with license).
8تا قدمم به سر نهی (tá qadamam bi-sar nahí)Poetic displacement of the 1st-person enclitic pronoun -am: attached to `قدم` (qadam, foot) instead of `سر` (sar, head), standing for `تا قدم به سرم نهی` (”that Thou mayest set Thy foot upon my head“, matching the image of the speaker lying in the dust like a road for the Beloved to tread upon).(1) ”that Thou mayest set Thy foot upon my head“ (chosen); (2) ”that Thou mayest set my foot upon the head“.
13کگردن مطلب هما (kgardan-i-maṭlab-i-humá)Clear typographical error in the source text (`کگردن` with a stray initial `ک`). Removing the extra `ک` yields `گردن مطلب هما` (gardan-i-maṭlab-i-humá, `- u u -u - u -`), which restores the exact meter. Even emended, the iḍáfih chain `گردن مطلب هما` is dense: literally ”the neck of the quest of the Humá“ (either the Humá-bird of high aspiration tucking its neck of desire beneath the wing of sorrow, or the neck of the quest for the royal Humá remaining beneath the wing of grief).(1) Emend `کگردن` to `گردن` (gardan, ”neck“) and read gardan-i-maṭlab-i-humá as ”the neck of the quest of the Humá / the royal phoenix of aspiration“ (chosen); (2) Emend `کگردن` to `کردن` (kardan, verbal noun ”the making of the quest of the Humá“).
14–15طاهرا ... سر به سر (Ṭáhirá ... sar bi-sar)In line 14, the pen-name is `طاهرا` (Ṭáhirá, either the masculine/poetic pen-name Ṭáhirá or vocative ”O Ṭáhir!“, as noted in the source header regarding disputed attribution). In line 15, `سر به سر` replaces Poem 1’s `لا به لا`: read as Persian sar bi-sar (”from end to end / wholly“), or as Arabic-Persian sirr bi-sirr (”secret by secret / mystery by mystery“).(1) Transliterate `طاهرا` as Ṭáhirá without gendering the pronoun in the readable rendering, and render sar bi-sar as ”from end to end“ (chosen); (2) Render sirr bi-sirr as ”secret by secret“.
