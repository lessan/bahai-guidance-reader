The Day Can Be Missed

Last updated 16 October 2026

What this facet is

The Writings describe this Day as the greatest of days, and they also say that most people do not notice it. The greatness does not force itself on anyone. The Báb says a Day of Resurrection can rise and set like any other day. Bahá’u’lláh says the Chalice is held out and its Bearer waits to see who will take it. Those who are asleep, veiled, tarrying at the gate, or learned in everything but this, miss it. This page keeps to that one point: the heedlessness of individuals and of the age toward the Day. The warnings of calamity to the world are traced in “Dearly Beloved Friends”: The Advent of Divine Justice Compilation, Traced and are not repeated here. The series hub is The greatness of this Day.

The passages

A Day that rises and sets like any other

The Báb, in the Selections, writes:

”THE Day of Resurrection is a day on which the sun riseth and setteth like unto any other day. How oft hath the Day of Resurrection dawned, and the people of the land where it occurred did not learn of the event. Had they heard, they would not have believed, and thus they were not told!“

(Selections from the Writings of the Báb 11.7)

The sentence separates the occurrence of the Day from knowledge of it. The sun keeps its ordinary course, and a people can live through the Day without being told. The last clause gives a reason for their not being told: they would not have believed.

Bahá’u’lláh says the same of the Promised One. He writes:

”BEHOLD, how the divers peoples and kindreds of the earth have been waiting for the coming of the Promised One. No sooner had He, Who is the Sun of Truth, been made manifest, than, lo, all turned away from Him, except them whom God was pleased to guide.“

(Gleanings VI.1)

A long, shared expectation did not produce recognition. In another passage He writes of ”the Day when all except a few who were endued with insight have broken away from Him“ (Gleanings XV.2). The few are those ”endued with insight“, which is a matter of perception, not of rank or learning.

The Báb applies the point to His own followers. Writing of the people of the Bayán, He warns:

”Take good heed of yourselves, O people of the Bayán, lest ye perform such deeds as to weep sore for His sake night and day, to stand up at the mention of His Name, yet on this Day of fruition—a Day whereon ye should not only arise at His Name, but seek a path unto Him Who personifies that Name—ye shut yourselves out from Him as by a veil.“

(Selections from the Writings of the Báb 11.23)

The source file places this in a passage that sets the reception of the Apostle of God beside the reception of the Báb: people who had longed for Him night and day were the ones who failed Him when He came. Longing, ritual respect and devotion to the Name are not the same as seeking the Person who bears it.

The Chalice and the One who waits

Bahá’u’lláh writes to a believer, Naṣír:

”The Celestial Youth hath, in this Day, raised above the heads of men the glorious Chalice of Immortality, and is standing expectant upon His seat, wondering what eye will recognize His glory, and what arm will, unhesitatingly, be stretched forth to seize the Cup from His snow-white Hand and drain it.“

(Gleanings LIII.1)

The Chalice is held up and the Celestial Youth waits. He does not press it on anyone. The passage asks two things of the one who takes it: an eye that recognizes, and an arm that is stretched forth ”unhesitatingly“. Recognition and prompt action are both named. The sentence leaves open how many will do either.

Bahá’u’lláh also writes:

”This is the Day in which the earth shineth with the effulgent light of thy Lord, but the people are lost in error and have been shut out as by a veil.“

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 17.51)

The light is described as already present on the earth. The veil is on the people. This is the pattern throughout the passages below: the Day is not hidden, and the obstruction lies in those who look.

Asleep, heedless, tarrying at the gate

‘Abdu’l-Bahá writes to a woman believer, a ”lady of the Kingdom“:

”Praise thou God that in this age, the age of the dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh, thou hast been awakened, hast been made aware of the Manifestation of the Lord of Hosts. All the people of the world are buried in the graves of nature, or are slumbering, heedless and unaware.“

(Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá 168.1)

Being awake is treated here as a cause for thanks, not for pride. The recipient is told to praise God because she has been ”awakened“ and ”made aware“; the passage does not credit her with having woken herself.

Bahá’u’lláh addresses the Christians twice over in the Summons of the Lord of Hosts. First He says:

”Say: O concourse of Christians! We have, on a previous occasion, revealed Ourself unto you, and ye recognized Me not. This is yet another occasion vouchsafed unto you. This is the Day of God; turn ye unto Him.“

(The Summons of the Lord of Hosts 1.127)

Then:

”The King of Glory proclaimeth from the tabernacle of majesty and grandeur His call, saying: O people of the Gospel! They who were not in the Kingdom have now entered it, whilst We behold you, in this day, tarrying at the gate. Rend the veils asunder by the power of your Lord, the Almighty, the All-Bounteous, and enter, then, in My name My Kingdom. Thus biddeth you He Who desireth for you everlasting life.“

(The Summons of the Lord of Hosts 1.128)

Two features stand out. Missing the Day once does not close the matter: ”This is yet another occasion vouchsafed unto you.“ And the people addressed are not outside the door in hostility; they are ”tarrying at the gate“, already near it. The call asks them to rend the veils and enter. Others who were ”not in the Kingdom“ have already entered, which shows the gate can be passed.

Bahá’u’lláh also names what keeps people back:

”O ye heedless ones! By reason of a droplet ye have deprived yourselves of the ocean of heavenly verses and for the sake of an insignificant atom ye have shut yourselves out from the splendors of the Daystar of Truth.“

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 6.50)

”How sad if any man were, in this Day, to rest his heart on the transitory things of this world!“

(Gleanings CXLVII.1)

The cause given is a trade: something small taken in place of something vast. The heart rests on ”the transitory things of this world“, and the Day goes unseen. The warning is addressed to individuals who are still free to choose otherwise.

The learned who do not recognize it

Bahá’u’lláh writes, in a Tablet that opens by addressing a handmaid of God:

”In this Day the Straight Path is made manifest, the Balance of divine justice is set and the light of the sun of His bounty is resplendent, yet the oppressive darkness of the people of tyranny hath, even as clouds, intervened and caused a grievous obstruction between the Daystar of heavenly grace and the people of the world. Blessed is he who hath rent the intervening veils asunder and is illumined by the radiant light of divine Revelation. Consider how numerous were those who accounted themselves among the wise and the learned, yet in the Day of God were deprived of the outpourings of heavenly bounties.“

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 17.87)

The passage names two obstructions: clouds raised by ”the people of tyranny“, and the self-regard of those who ”accounted themselves among the wise and the learned“. Learning is not what is condemned. What is described is learning that stood in the way of receiving the Day.

In the Kitáb-i-Íqán, Bahá’u’lláh puts the same point as a test of the word ”learned“:

”Consider, how can he that faileth in the day of God’s Revelation to attain unto the grace of the “Divine Presence” and to recognize His Manifestation, be justly called learned, though he may have spent aeons in the pursuit of knowledge, and acquired all the limited and material learning of men?”

(The Kitáb-i-Íqán 154)

Bahá’u’lláh here defines learning by what a person recognizes, not by what a person has accumulated. He then names the divines as those who kept the people back:

“The glory with which this Day is invested hath been explicitly mentioned and clearly set forth in most heavenly Books and Scriptures. However, the divines of the age have debarred men from this transcendent station, and have kept them back from this Pinnacle of Glory, this Supreme Goal.”

(Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, TB 17.100)

The Day was foretold in the Scriptures, yet the people who held those Scriptures in trust did not lead others to it. The failure is of guidance, and it falls on followers as well as on the guides.

In a Tablet addressed to high priests, Bahá’u’lláh also writes:

“Great is the station of him who hath in this Day perceived and become aware of the truth, and wretched is he who hath failed to comprehend the utterances of the Lord of wisdom and to recognize the newly arrived Friend in His new attire.”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Additional Tablets and Extracts, ATE 71.6)

The text immediately before it, in the source file, rebukes the high priests and tells them to shake off their slumber. “In His new attire” names the difficulty: He is the same Friend, and the unfamiliar form is what people fail to recognize.

Few who awaken others

In another passage Bahá’u’lláh adds that recognizing the Day is not the only thing that is scarce:

“The truth is manifest, the kingdom of utterance is resplendent, and the Day of God is clearly revealed! And yet souls who would, wholly for the sake of God, awaken the people and guide them to the True One are as scarce as the philosopher’s stone!”

(Bahá’u’lláh, Additional Tablets and Extracts, ATE 27.1)

The Day is plain, and the shortage He names is of people who will work purely for God to wake others. The existing article The Few Who Rise takes up those few.

Shoghi Effendi

In the Kitáb-i-Aqdas Bahá’u’lláh addresses the rulers of America and the presidents of the republics therein. Shoghi Effendi quotes the passage in Citadel of Faith, in the section headed “Bahá’u’lláh’s Summons to the Western Hemisphere”:

“The Promised One hath appeared in this glorified Station, whereat all beings, both seen and unseen, have rejoiced. Take ye advantage of the Day of God.”

(The Kitáb-i-Aqdas, KA 88; quoted in Citadel of Faith 6.35)

Here the rejoicing of “all beings, both seen and unseen” is set beside an instruction to “take advantage” of the Day. The words imply that the Day can pass without being used.

The Universal House of Justice

The Universal House of Justice writes in Turning Point:

“The storms and stresses battering the social fabric are incomprehensible to all except the relatively few of the planet’s inhabitants who recognize God’s purpose for this Day.”

(Universal House of Justice, Turning Point 16.4)

In its Riḍván 156 B.E. (1999) message it writes of those who have no knowledge of the Bahá’í vision:

“Knowing nothing of the transformative vision vouchsafed by the Lord of the Age, they stumble ahead, blind to the peerlessness of the new Day of God.”

(Universal House of Justice, Riḍván 156 B.E. (1999))

These two statements apply the same teaching to the present. People who do not know the Day cannot read the events around them, and they proceed without the vision that would explain it. The statements describe a condition of ignorance and say nothing of fault, and they are recorded as the House of Justice’s description of its time.

How this facet connects

  • The Resurrection: the Báb’s sentence that the sun rises and sets as on any other day is about the Day of Resurrection. That page sets out what the Resurrection is; this page records that it can occur unnoticed.
  • The Day the Prophets Longed For: the Day was foretold and awaited by every people. This page adds that the awaiting did not secure recognition (Gleanings VI.1; the people of the Bayán).
  • Grace Poured Out on All: the grace is available to everyone, and this page records that not everyone receives it. The two are read together: the Chalice is raised for all and is taken by some.
  • The Day of God Himself: the same Day, named as God’s own, is the one the learned and the heedless fail to see. That page gives the claim whose neglect is described here.
  • The Few Who Rise: the “few” who recognize the Day and the souls who awaken others, as ATE 27.1 describes them, are the subject of that article.
  • “Dearly Beloved Friends”: The Advent of Divine Justice Compilation, Traced: the warnings to the world of calamity follow from the heedlessness described here and are traced there. They are not discussed on this page.

What the sources do not settle

  • Several Tablets are addressed to a handmaiden or to Naṣír, but the sources here do not identify the Tablets by name. This page gives the work and paragraph reference only.
  • The Báb’s passage (11.7) says ”they were not told“ without saying who withheld the news; the text does not specify.
  • The passages that name ”divines“, ”high priests“ and ”the people of Bayán“ refer to particular groups of the time. Whether and how the same description applies to groups in later periods is not stated in these passages.
  • The texts do not state how many are meant by ”a few“ (Gleanings XV.2), ”the relatively few“ (Turning Point 16.4) or ”scarce“ (ATE 27.1).
  • The Chalice passage (Gleanings LIII.1) does not say what happens to the Cup if no arm is stretched forth, and this page does not supply an answer.

For songwriting

  • ”the sun riseth and setteth like unto any other day“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb 11.7)
  • ”the people of the land where it occurred did not learn of the event“ (Selections from the Writings of the Báb 11.7)
  • ”raised above the heads of men the glorious Chalice of Immortality“ (Gleanings LIII.1)
  • ”what eye will recognize His glory“ (Gleanings LIII.1)
  • ”the earth shineth with the effulgent light of thy Lord“ (TB 17.51)
  • ”the people are lost in error“ (TB 17.51)
  • ”tarrying at the gate“ (Summons of the Lord of Hosts 1.128)
  • ”Rend the veils asunder by the power of your Lord“ (Summons of the Lord of Hosts 1.128)
  • ”This is the Day of God; turn ye unto Him.“ (Summons of the Lord of Hosts 1.127)
  • ”By reason of a droplet ye have deprived yourselves of the ocean of heavenly verses“ (TB 6.50)
  • ”recognize the newly arrived Friend in His new attire“ (ATE 71.6)
  • ”buried in the graves of nature, or are slumbering, heedless and unaware“ (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá 168.1)
  • ”Take heed lest ye allow yourselves to be shut out as by a veil“ (Gleanings LII.4)
  • ”as scarce as the philosopher’s stone“ (ATE 27.1)

Concrete images

  • The sun rising and setting on an ordinary day (the Báb).
  • The Chalice raised above people’s heads, the snow-white Hand, the Cup to be seized and drained (Gleanings LIII.1).
  • The Celestial Youth standing on His seat, waiting and wondering.
  • A veil; clouds between the Daystar and the people of the world.
  • A gate with people tarrying outside it (Summons 1.128).
  • A droplet traded for an ocean; a tiny atom held in place of the sun.
  • Graves, and sleepers within them (‘Abdu’l-Bahá).
  • A familiar Friend arriving in new clothes.
  • The philosopher’s stone, scarce and sought.

A possible emotional arc

  1. An ordinary day: the sun rises and sets and nothing is announced (SWB 11.7).
  2. The Chalice is raised above the heads of men, and the Celestial Youth waits to see who will take it (Gleanings LIII.1).
  3. The people are veiled, asleep, or tarrying at the gate, and the learned among them do not see (TB 17.51; SWAB 168.1; Summons 1.128; Íqán 154).
  4. The call is made again: ”This is yet another occasion vouchsafed unto you“ (Summons 1.127).
  5. Gratitude for the one who has awakened: ”Praise thou God that … thou hast been awakened“ (SWAB 168.1).

What a lyric must not claim

  • It must not say that most people reject the Faith out of malice. The passages describe heedlessness, attachment to small things, and misguidance by the learned.
  • It must not say the Day is closed to those who missed the first call. Summons 1.127 says another occasion is given.
  • It must not quote ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s sentence as Bahá’u’lláh’s, or the Báb’s sentence on the Resurrection as Bahá’u’lláh’s. Three authors are involved in the arc above.
  • It must not name the people of the Bayán, the high priests or the divines as present-day groups. The passages address those of their own time.
  • It must not invent a number for ”the few“, and must not turn ”the Day can be missed“ into a prediction of how many will miss it.
  • It must not present the Celestial Youth as anything other than the figure in this passage (Gleanings LIII.1); do not add an identification the passage does not give.
  • It must not quote the Báb’s sentence ”they were not told“ as meaning that the news was deliberately hidden. The text does not say that.
