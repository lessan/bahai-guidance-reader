The Day Foretold: The Christian Promise

Last updated 17 October 2026

This page belongs to the series introduced in the-day-foretold (see also promises-of-the-future). Sibling pages: the-day-foretold-hindu, the-day-foretold-jewish, the-day-foretold-zoroastrian, the-day-foretold-buddhist, the-day-foretold-islamic, the-day-foretold-babi.

It sets out what the New Testament says about the return of Christ, the Comforter and the Spirit of truth, the lord of the vineyard, and the New Jerusalem, and what the Bahá’í Writings say about how Bahá’u’lláh’s Day relates to those promises. In God Passes By, Shoghi Effendi names what Bahá’u’lláh’s coming is to each tradition. For Christians he writes: “to Christendom Christ returned ‘in the glory of the Father’” (God Passes By, GPB VI.11).

The page keeps three kinds of source apart. The first is the Gospels, Acts and Revelation in the King James Version (1611, public domain). The second is the Bahá’í Writings: Bahá’u’lláh, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá (his talks are marked “from notes of a talk”), Shoghi Effendi, letters written on Shoghi Effendi’s behalf, and the Universal House of Justice. The third is history and commentary by Bahá’í and other authors, each labelled. What the Bahá’í texts say about Christian scripture is the Bahá’í understanding, not a statement of what Christian churches teach.

One point of method comes from the Guardian. A letter written on his behalf in 1955 says: “Except for what has been explained by Bahá’u’lláh and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, we have no way of knowing what various symbolic allusions in the Bible mean.” (quoted in a Research Department memorandum of 7 January 1986). This page therefore gives a reading as authoritative only where Bahá’u’lláh, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá or Shoghi Effendi makes it. Readings by other Bahá’í authors are in the commentary section of this page and are labelled as theirs.

1. The promise

All quotations in this section are from the King James Version. The saved copies are listed in the Sources section; each quotation was copied from them. The sections below group the verses by theme, not by book.

The Son of man comes “in the glory of his Father”

In Matthew, Jesus says:

“For the Son of man shall come in the glory of his Father with his angels; and then he shall reward every man according to his works.” (Matthew 16:27, King James Version)

The phrase “the glory of his Father” later becomes the Guardian’s title for what Bahá’u’lláh’s coming is for Christendom (Section 2).

The coming and its signs: Matthew 24

On the Mount of Olives the disciples ask: “Tell us, when shall these things be? and what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?” (Matthew 24:3, King James Version). Jesus answers at length. These are the verses that bear most directly on the return.

Jesus says the gospel will be preached to all nations before the end:

“And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come.” (Matthew 24:14, King James Version)

The manner of the coming is likened to lightning:

“For as the lightning cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.” (Matthew 24:27, King James Version)

Then follow the sun, the moon, the stars, the sign of the Son of man, the clouds and the trumpet. Matthew 24:29 reads “Immediately after the tribulation of those days shall the sun be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven” (King James Version).

“And then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory.” (Matthew 24:30, King James Version)

Matthew 24:31 adds the angels and the “great sound of a trumpet”. Jesus then says that the day is not known:

“But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only.” (Matthew 24:36, King James Version)

Luke gives a parallel: “And then shall they see the Son of man coming in a cloud with power and great glory.” (Luke 21:27, King James Version). He adds: “And when these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh.” (Luke 21:28, King James Version)

Acts records the words of two men in white at the Ascension. They are the main text for the expectation that Jesus returns in the same bodily way, and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s Tablet on them is quoted in Section 2(c):

“Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.” (Acts 1:11, King James Version)

“As a thief in the night”

The coming is described as unexpected:

“Therefore be ye also ready: for in such an hour as ye think not the Son of man cometh.” (Matthew 24:44, King James Version)

“Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watcheth, and keepeth his garments, lest he walk naked, and they see his shame.” (Revelation 16:15, King James Version)

Paul uses the same figure: “the day of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the night” (1 Thessalonians 5:2, King James Version). The next verse in Revelation, 16:16, names the place Armageddon. The House of Justice’s reply on that name is in Section 2(d).

The Comforter and the Spirit of truth

In the Gospel of John, Jesus promises another helper and says he will come to his disciples:

“And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever; Even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you.” (John 14:16–17, King James Version)

“I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you.” (John 14:18, King James Version)

In John 14:26 the King James Version identifies the Comforter as “the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name”. Most Christian churches read the Comforter as the Holy Spirit. The Bahá’í texts quoted in Section 2 read the same promise as pointing to Bahá’u’lláh, and they do so in the light of what Jesus says next about departing and returning:

“Ye have heard how I said unto you, I go away, and come again unto you. If ye loved me, ye would rejoice, because I said, I go unto the Father: for my Father is greater than I.” (John 14:28, King James Version)

In the farewell discourse he says it is better that he goes, and that there are things the disciples cannot yet bear:

“Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you.” (John 16:7, King James Version)

“I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now.” (John 16:12, King James Version)

“Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth: for he shall not speak of himself; but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak: and he will shew you things to come.” (John 16:13, King James Version)

The lord of the vineyard

In Matthew 21 Jesus tells the chief priests and elders a parable about a householder who plants a vineyard, leases it, and sends servants and then his son:

“Hear another parable: There was a certain householder, which planted a vineyard, and hedged it round about, and digged a winepress in it, and built a tower, and let it out to husbandmen, and went into a far country:” (Matthew 21:33, King James Version)

“But last of all he sent unto them his son, saying, They will reverence my son.” (Matthew 21:37, King James Version)

“But when the husbandmen saw the son, they said among themselves, This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and let us seize on his inheritance.” (Matthew 21:38, King James Version)

He asks what the lord of the vineyard will do, and they answer:

“When the lord therefore of the vineyard cometh, what will he do unto those husbandmen?” (Matthew 21:40, King James Version)

“They say unto him, He will miserably destroy those wicked men, and will let out his vineyard unto other husbandmen, which shall render him the fruits in their seasons.” (Matthew 21:41, King James Version)

Shoghi Effendi lists “Lord of the Vineyard” among the titles Jesus gave to Bahá’u’lláh (Section 2).

The Revelation of John

Revelation gives a new name, a seventh trumpet, a woman in heaven, and a city. The new name is promised to the one who overcomes:

“Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God, and he shall go no more out: and I will write upon him the name of my God, and the name of the city of my God, which is new Jerusalem, which cometh down out of heaven from my God: and I will write upon him my new name.” (Revelation 3:12, King James Version)

The third woe is announced in Revelation 11:14 (“the third woe cometh quickly”), and then the seventh trumpet:

“And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.” (Revelation 11:15, King James Version)

The woman and the child (Revelation 12:5 adds that the child “was to rule all nations with a rod of iron”):

“And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars:” (Revelation 12:1, King James Version)

The new heaven, the new earth and the city:

“And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.” (Revelation 21:1, King James Version)

“And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.” (Revelation 21:2, King James Version)

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.” (Revelation 21:3, King James Version)

“And he that sat upon the throne said, Behold, I make all things new. And he said unto me, Write: for these words are true and faithful.” (Revelation 21:5, King James Version)

The book ends with a prayer. Just before it, the Spirit and the bride say “Come” (Revelation 22:17, King James Version):

“He which testifieth these things saith, Surely I come quickly. Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus.” (Revelation 22:20, King James Version)

2. The fulfilment in the Bahá’í Writings

The titles Shoghi Effendi gathers

Shoghi Effendi lists the titles by which, he writes, Jesus Christ referred to Bahá’u’lláh:

“To Him Jesus Christ had referred as the “Prince of this world,” as the “Comforter” Who will “reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment,” as the “Spirit of Truth” Who “will guide you into all truth,” Who “shall not speak of Himself, but whatsoever He shall hear, that shall He speak,” as the “Lord of the Vineyard,” and as the “Son of Man” Who “shall come in the glory of His Father” “in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory,” with “all the holy angels” about Him, and “all nations” gathered before His throne.” (God Passes By, GPB VI.16, Shoghi Effendi)

The same paragraph applies the Revelation of John to Him: “Identifying His Revelation with the “third woe,” he, moreover, had extolled His Law as “a new heaven and a new earth,” as the “Tabernacle of God,” as the “Holy City,” as the “New Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.”” (GPB VI.16). GPB VI.11, quoted at the top of this page, sets the Christian expectation beside the others: “to Christendom Christ returned “in the glory of the Father,” to Shí‘ah Islám the return of the Imám Ḥusayn; to Sunní Islám the descent of the “Spirit of God” (Jesus Christ)”. In The Promised Day Is Come, Shoghi Effendi describes Bahá’u’lláh as “the Establisher of the Kingdom of the Father foretold by the Son” (The Promised Day Is Come, PDC 301), and of the clergy he writes that they “have interposed themselves between their flock and the Christ returned in the glory of the Father” (PDC 261).

The title “Lord of the Vineyard” is tied to a place. Shoghi Effendi writes that Bahá’u’lláh’s tent, the “Tabernacle of Glory,” was raised on Mount Carmel, “the Hill of God and His Vineyard,” and that “He, the “Lord of the Vineyard,” revealed the Tablet of Carmel, remarkable for its allusions and prophecies” (GPB XI.28). In that Tablet Bahá’u’lláh writes: “Call out to Zion, O Carmel, and announce the joyful tidings: He that was hidden from mortal eyes is come!” and speaks of “the City of God that hath descended from heaven” (Gleanings from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh, GWB XI.4, Bahá’u’lláh).

(a) The Kitáb-i-Íqán on Matthew 24

The Kitáb-i-Íqán is Bahá’u’lláh’s main exposition of the symbols of earlier scripture. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá sends the reader to it for the signs of Christ’s return: “Bahá’u’lláh has provided a detailed interpretation of these verses in the Kitáb-i-Íqán, and it need not be repeated here. Refer to it and you will grasp their meaning.” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 26.1, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá). A Research Department memorandum of 9 October 1989 says the same: “The “Kitab-i-Iqan” identifies Christ’s explanation concerning His return as symbolical and elucidates the meanings behind the symbolism.” This subsection follows the Íqán’s order.

The Íqán’s wording of the verses differs from the King James text in two places. It has “oppression” where the King James Version has “tribulation”, and “the powers of the earth” where the King James Version has “the powers of the heavens”. The commentary below uses the Íqán’s words.

#### The verses as the Íqán quotes them

“Afterwards, the companions and disciples of Jesus asked Him concerning those signs that must needs signalize the return of His manifestation. When, they asked, shall these things be? Several times they questioned that peerless Beauty, and, every time He made reply, He set forth a special sign that should herald the advent of the promised Dispensation. To this testify the records of the four Gospels.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 21, Bahá’u’lláh)

“In the first Gospel according to Matthew it is recorded: And when they asked Jesus concerning the signs of His coming, He said unto them: “Immediately after the oppression of those days shall the sun be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the earth shall be shaken: and then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory. And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet.”” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 24, Bahá’u’lláh)

Bahá’u’lláh gives the purport in Persian, including the line “the pillars of the earth shall quake”, and adds that “in the three other Gospels, according to Luke, Mark, and John, the same statements are recorded” (KI 24). The Íqán then takes the clauses in turn. It gives no separate comment on “the powers of the earth shall be shaken”.

Bahá’u’lláh says what followed when the Christian community took the signs literally. These are His words, not the page’s:

“Inasmuch as the Christian divines have failed to apprehend the meaning of these words, and did not recognize their object and purpose, and have clung to the literal interpretation of the words of Jesus, they therefore became deprived of the streaming grace of the Muḥammadan Revelation and its showering bounties.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 25, Bahá’u’lláh)

#### “Immediately after the oppression of those days”

Bahá’u’lláh first reads the opening words. “Immediately after the oppression of those days” refers to “the time when men shall become oppressed and afflicted” (KI 28):

“This “oppression” is the essential feature of every Revelation. Unless it cometh to pass, the Sun of Truth will not be made manifest. For the break of the morn of divine guidance must needs follow the darkness of the night of error.” (KI 29)

“Whereas, by “oppression” is meant the want of capacity to acquire spiritual knowledge and apprehend the Word of God.” (KI 30)

#### The sun, the moon and the stars

Bahá’u’lláh gives several meanings. In the first, the sun and moon are the Manifestations of God: “By the terms “sun” and “moon,” mentioned in the writings of the Prophets of God, is not meant solely the sun and moon of the visible universe. Nay rather, manifold are the meanings they have intended for these terms.” (KI 31). In another, they are the learned of the earlier Dispensation who live into the later one:

“If these divines be illumined by the light of the latter Revelation, they will be acceptable unto God, and will shine with a light everlasting. Otherwise, they will be declared as darkened, even though to outward seeming they be leaders of men” (KI 34)

In a third, they are the laws of the earlier Dispensation: “Inasmuch as every subsequent Revelation hath abolished the manners, habits, and teachings that have been clearly, specifically, and firmly established by the former Dispensation, these have accordingly been symbolically expressed in terms of “sun” and “moon.”” (KI 39). He sums up:

“Hence, it is clear and manifest that by the words “the sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven” is intended the waywardness of the divines, and the annulment of laws firmly established by divine Revelation, all of which, in symbolic language, have been foreshadowed by the Manifestation of God.” (KI 41)

#### The sign of the Son of man: the star and the herald

“By “heaven” is meant the visible heaven, inasmuch as when the hour draweth nigh on which the Daystar of the heaven of justice shall be made manifest, and the Ark of divine guidance shall sail upon the sea of glory, a star will appear in the heaven, heralding unto its people the advent of that most great light. In like manner, in the invisible heaven a star shall be made manifest who, unto the peoples of the earth, shall act as a harbinger of the break of that true and exalted Morn.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 66, Bahá’u’lláh)

He gives examples from the Prophets before Jesus. Before Jesus:

“These Magi said: “Where is He that is born King of the Jews? for we have seen His star in the east and are come to worship Him!” When they had searched, they found out that in Bethlehem, in the land of Judea, the Child had been born. This was the sign that was manifested in the visible heaven. As to the sign in the invisible heaven—the heaven of divine knowledge and understanding—it was Yaḥyá, son of Zachariah, who gave unto the people the tidings of the Manifestation of Jesus.” (KI 70)

He then states the rule: “before the revelation of each of the Mirrors reflecting the divine Essence, the signs heralding their advent must needs be revealed in the visible heaven as well as in the invisible” (KI 73).

#### “All the tribes of the earth mourn”, “coming in the clouds”

“These words signify that in those days men will lament the loss of the Sun of the divine beauty, of the Moon of knowledge, and of the Stars of divine wisdom. Thereupon, they will behold the countenance of the promised One, the adored Beauty, descending from heaven and riding upon the clouds. By this is meant that the divine Beauty will be made manifest from the heaven of the will of God, and will appear in the form of the human temple.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 74, Bahá’u’lláh)

“Heaven” is the seat of the Manifestations, who “though they be dwelling on this earth, yet their true habitations are the retreats of glory in the realms above” (KI 74). On “clouds”:

“By the term “clouds” is meant those things that are contrary to the ways and desires of men.” (KI 79)

“These “clouds” signify, in one sense, the annulment of laws, the abrogation of former Dispensations, the repeal of rituals and customs current amongst men, the exalting of the illiterate faithful above the learned opposers of the Faith. In another sense, they mean the appearance of that immortal Beauty in the image of mortal man, with such human limitations as eating and drinking, poverty and riches, glory and abasement, sleeping and waking, and such other things as cast doubt in the minds of men, and cause them to turn away. All such veils are symbolically referred to as “clouds.”” (KI 79)

#### The angels and the trumpet

“By “angels” is meant those who, reinforced by the power of the spirit, have consumed, with the fire of the love of God, all human traits and limitations, and have clothed themselves with the attributes of the most exalted Beings and of the Cherubim.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 86, Bahá’u’lláh)

The Íqán gives no separate exposition of “a great sound of a trumpet” in Matthew. For the trumpet of the Qur’án’s Day of Judgment it says: “by “trumpet” is meant the trumpet-call of Muḥammad’s Revelation, which was sounded in the heart of the universe” (KI 123).

#### Why a literal fulfilment would not test anyone

“Judge fairly: Were the prophecies recorded in the Gospel to be literally fulfilled; were Jesus, Son of Mary, accompanied by angels, to descend from the visible heaven upon the clouds; who would dare to disbelieve, who would dare to reject the truth, and wax disdainful? Nay, such consternation would immediately seize all the dwellers of the earth that no soul would feel able to utter a word, much less to reject or accept the truth.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 88, Bahá’u’lláh)

#### “I go away and come again unto you”

“To them that are endowed with understanding, it is clear and manifest that when the fire of the love of Jesus consumed the veils of Jewish limitations, and His authority was made apparent and partially enforced, He, the Revealer of the unseen Beauty, addressing one day His disciples, referred unto His passing, and, kindling in their hearts the fire of bereavement, said unto them: “I go away and come again unto you.” And in another place He said: “I go and another will come Who will tell you all that I have not told you, and will fulfill all that I have said.” Both these sayings have but one meaning, were you to ponder upon the Manifestations of the Unity of God with divine insight.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 19, Bahá’u’lláh)

He gives an image of the sameness of the Manifestations: “Consider the sun. Were it to say now, “I am the sun of yesterday,” it would speak the truth.” (KI 20).

(b) Bahá’u’lláh’s Tablets to the Christian world

#### To the kings of Christendom

The Súriy-i-Mulúk addresses the kings and puts Jesus’s promise to them directly:

“O KINGS of Christendom! Heard ye not the saying of Jesus, the Spirit of God, “I go away, and come again unto you”? Wherefore, then, did ye fail, when He did come again unto you in the clouds of heaven, to draw nigh unto Him, that ye might behold His face, and be of them that attained His Presence? In another passage He saith: “When He, the Spirit of Truth, is come, He will guide you into all truth.”” (Gleanings from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh, GWB CXVI.1, Bahá’u’lláh)

The Tablets in the Súriy-i-Haykal are to individual rulers. To Napoleon III, the “King of Paris”, Bahá’u’lláh writes:

“Tell the priests to ring the bells no longer. By God, the True One! The Most Mighty Bell hath appeared in the form of Him Who is the Most Great Name, and the fingers of the Will of Thy Lord, the Most Exalted, the Most High, toll it out in the heaven of Immortality in His name, the All-Glorious.” (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, Tablet to Napoleon III, SLH 1.131, Bahá’u’lláh)

The same Tablet says: “Say: He Who is the Unconstrained is come, in the clouds of light” (SLH 1.131). To Queen Victoria: “He, in truth, hath come unto the world in His most great glory, and all that hath been mentioned in the Gospel hath been fulfilled.” (SLH 1.171)

#### To the Pope and the followers of the Son

The Tablet to Pope Pius IX announces the coming in the language of Matthew 24 and warns against the Pharisees’ example:

“O Pope! Rend the veils asunder. He Who is the Lord of Lords is come overshadowed with clouds, and the decree hath been fulfilled by God, the Almighty, the Unrestrained. … He, verily, hath again come down from Heaven even as He came down from it the first time. Beware that thou dispute not with Him even as the Pharisees disputed with Him without a clear token or proof.” (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, Tablet to Pope Pius IX, SLH 1.102, Bahá’u’lláh)

“The Word which the Son concealed is made manifest. It hath been sent down in the form of the human temple in this day. Blessed be the Lord Who is the Father! He, verily, is come unto the nations in His most great majesty. Turn your faces towards Him, O concourse of the righteous!” (SLH 1.112)

Shoghi Effendi summarises the Tablet (GPB XII.31) and says Bahá’u’lláh wrote “that the Word was Himself, and He Himself the Father” (The Promised Day Is Come, PDC 126). The Tablet continues:

“O followers of the Son! We have once again sent John unto you, and He, verily, hath cried out in the wilderness of the Bayán: O peoples of the world! Cleanse your eyes! The Day whereon ye can behold the Promised One and attain unto Him hath drawn nigh! O followers of the Gospel! Prepare the way! The Day of the advent of the Glorious Lord is at hand!” (SLH 1.121)

The Tablet places the new John “in the wilderness of the Bayán” (SLH 1.121), the principal Book of the Báb. It also says that the Breeze of God, at the advent of the Desired One, made every stone cry out: “The Promised One is come! The Kingdom is God’s, the Mighty, the Gracious, the Forgiving.” (SLH 1.104). It then names the Comforter:

“We sent forth him who was named John to baptize you with water, that your bodies might be cleansed for the appearance of the Messiah. He, in turn, purified you with the fire of love and the water of the spirit in anticipation of these Days whereon the All-Merciful hath purposed to cleanse you with the water of life at the hands of His loving providence. This is that peerless One foretold by Isaiah, and the Comforter concerning Whom the Spirit had covenanted with you. Open your eyes, O concourse of bishops, that ye may behold your Lord seated upon the Throne of might and glory.” (SLH 1.122)

“Say: O concourse of Christians! We have, on a previous occasion, revealed Ourself unto you, and ye recognized Me not. This is yet another occasion vouchsafed unto you. This is the Day of God; turn ye unto Him. He, verily, hath come down from heaven even as He came down the first time, and He desireth to shelter you beneath the shade of His mercy.” (SLH 1.127)

#### The Lawḥ-i-Aqdas, to the Christians

The Lawḥ-i-Aqdas addresses the followers of the Son, the priests, the bishops, the monks and Bethlehem. It makes the announcement:

“The river Jordan is joined to the Most Great Ocean, and the Son, in the holy vale, crieth out: “Here am I, here am I O Lord, my God!”, whilst Sinai circleth round the House, and the Burning Bush calleth aloud: “He Who is the Desired One is come in His transcendent majesty.” Say, Lo! The Father is come, and that which ye were promised in the Kingdom is fulfilled! This is the Word which the Son concealed, when to those around Him He said: “Ye cannot bear it now.” And when the appointed time was fulfilled and the Hour had struck, the Word shone forth above the horizon of the Will of God.” (TB 2.7)

The words “Ye cannot bear it now” correspond to John 16:12. The same paragraph says: “The Hour which We had concealed from the knowledge of the peoples of the earth and of the favored angels hath come to pass.” To the priests:

“Announce thou unto the priests: Lo! He Who is the Ruler is come. Step out from behind the veil in the name of thy Lord, He Who layeth low the necks of all men. Proclaim then unto all mankind the glad-tidings of this mighty, this glorious Revelation. Verily, He Who is the Spirit of Truth is come to guide you unto all truth. He speaketh not as prompted by His own self, but as bidden by Him Who is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.” (TB 2.10)

“O concourse of priests! Leave the bells, and come forth, then, from your churches.” (TB 2.12)

Bethlehem is addressed by name:Bethlehem is addressed by name:

“Bethlehem is astir with the Breeze of God. We hear her voice saying: “O most generous Lord! Where is Thy great glory established? The sweet savors of Thy presence have quickened me, after I had melted in my separation from Thee. Praised be Thou in that Thou hast raised the veils, and come with power in evident glory.” We called unto her from behind the Tabernacle of Majesty and Grandeur: “O Bethlehem! This Light hath risen in the orient, and traveled towards the occident, until it reached thee in the evening of its life. Tell Me then: Do the sons recognize the Father, and acknowledge Him, or do they deny Him, even as the people aforetime denied Him (Jesus)?”” (TB 2.17)

#### The hour, the city and the Comforter

In the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, Bahá’u’lláh comments on Matthew 24:36 for a Muslim cleric: “By Father in this connection is meant God—exalted be His glory.” He draws two points: “none is aware of the time of the Revelation” except God, and the Gospel “setteth forth the greatness of the Revelation” (ESW 206–207).

Other statements on the Comforter are collected from Shoghi Effendi’s quotations. In a passage he quotes, Bahá’u’lláh addresses Christendom: “The Comforter Whose advent all the scriptures have promised is now come that He may reveal unto you all knowledge and wisdom.” (The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.19). ‘Abdu’l-Bahá applies John 16:13 to the present age. In a talk in Washington, D.C., on 21 April 1912 (from notes of a talk by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá), he said: “The century has dawned when the Spirit of Truth can reveal these verities to mankind, proclaim that very Word, establish the real foundations of Christianity” (The Promulgation of Universal Peace, PUP 16.7). Shoghi Effendi summarises the Tablets to the Christians:

“And finally, in several passages addressed to the entire body of the followers of Jesus Christ He identifies Himself with the “Father” spoken of by Isaiah, with the “Comforter” Whose Covenant He Who is the Spirit (Jesus) had Himself established, and with the “Spirit of Truth” Who will guide them “into all truth”; proclaims His Day to be the Day of God; announces the conjunction of the river Jordan with the “Most Great Ocean”” (GPB XII.33, Shoghi Effendi)

Bahá’u’lláh writes of Himself and Jesus in the first person: “If ye be intent on crucifying once again Jesus, the Spirit of God, put Me to death, for He hath once more, in My person, been made manifest unto you.” The passage continues: “I have come in the shadows of the clouds of glory, and am invested by God with invincible sovereignty.” (Gleanings, GWB XLVII).

(c) ‘Abdu’l-Bahá on the second coming and the signs

#### Some Answered Questions, chapter 26

The chapter begins with the signs and sends the reader to the Íqán (SAQ 26.1, quoted above). It then argues from the first coming:

“The first coming of Christ was also from heaven, as has been explicitly stated in the Gospel. Even Christ Himself says that the Son of man came down from heaven, and the Son of man is in heaven; and no man hath ascended up to heaven but He that came down from heaven. Thus it is admitted by all that Christ came down from heaven, whereas to outward seeming He came from the womb of Mary.” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 26.2, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá)

“Now, just as He came the first time in appearance from the womb but in reality from heaven, so will He come the second time in appearance from the womb but in reality from heaven.” (SAQ 26.3)

The signs of the second coming follow the same pattern:

“All the signs and conditions that have been indicated have inner meanings and are not to be taken literally. For otherwise it is said, among other things, that the stars will fall upon the earth. Yet the stars are endless and innumerable” (SAQ 26.5)

“And yet even more astonishing is that Christ said: Perchance I shall come when you are sleeping, for the coming of the Son of man is like the coming of a thief. Perhaps the thief will be in the house and the owner will be unaware.” (SAQ 26.5)

#### The thief in the house

“Praise ye the Lord of Hosts for He, riding upon the clouds, hath come down to this world out of the heaven of the invisible realm, so that East and West were lit by the glory of the Sun of Truth, and the call of the Kingdom was raised, and the heralds of the realm above, with melodies of the Concourse on high, sang out the glad tidings of the Coming. Then the whole world of being did quiver for joy, and still the people, even as the Messiah saith, slept on: for the day of the Manifestation, when the Lord of Hosts descended, found them wrapped in the slumber of unknowing. As He saith in the Gospel, My coming is even as when the thief is in the house, and the goodman of the house watcheth not.” (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, SWAB 17.1, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá)

#### The clouds as the human body

In Paris ‘Abdu’l-Bahá spoke on “the clouds” (from notes of a talk by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, 27 October 1911):

“As clouds are obstacles to the shining of the sun, so the clouds of the world of humanity hid from the eyes of men the radiance of the Divinity of Christ.” (Paris Talks, 12.3, 27 October 1911, from notes of a talk by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá)

“If they had understood the reality of Christ, they would have known that the body of His humanity was a cloud that hid His Divinity. The world only saw His human form, and therefore wondered how He could have “come down from Heaven.”” (Paris Talks, 12.6)

A talk in Dublin, New Hampshire, uses another Gospel text: “The reality of Christ was always in heaven and will always be. This is the intention of the text of the Gospel. For while Jesus Christ walked upon the earth, He said, “The Son of Man is in heaven.”” (The Promulgation of Universal Peace, PUP 87.3, from notes of a talk by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá).

#### Acts 1:11: a provisional translation

An early Tablet speaks of the words of the angels at the Ascension (Acts 1:11). The English is from Tablets of Abdul-Baha Abbas, volume 1 (1909), a provisional translation whose translator is not named in the saved copy. It is used because no authorised text in the corpus comments on this verse:

“Likewise the address of the angels to the people of Galilee, “That this Christ will return in the same way and that He will descend from heaven,” is a spiritual address. For when Christ appeared, He came from heaven, although He was outwardly born from the womb of Mary.” (Tablets of Abdul-Baha Abbas, vol. 1, 1909; provisional translation)

The same volume has a statement on ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s own position, also a provisional translation: “no one must believe that Abdul-Baha is the “Second Coming of Christ”, nay, rather he must believe that he is the Manifestation of Servitude” (Tablets of Abdul-Baha Abbas, vol. 1, 1909; provisional translation). The authorised statement on station is Shoghi Effendi’s (WOB 6.70, quoted in Section 4).

#### Revelation 11 and 12

Shoghi Effendi quotes the verse “The second woe is past; and, behold the third woe cometh quickly,” and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s comment: “This third woe is the day of the Manifestation of Bahá’u’lláh, the Day of God, and it is near to the day of the appearance of the Báb.” (GPB VI.7). In Some Answered Questions ‘Abdu’l-Bahá reads the “forty-two months” and “1,260 days” as 1,260 years, ending in the Báb’s advent:

“This 1,260 years is a prophecy concerning the advent of the Báb, the “Gate” leading to Bahá’u’lláh, which took place in the year A.H. 1260.” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 11.6)

“The first woe was the advent of the Apostle of God, Muḥammad the son of ‘Abdu’lláh, peace be upon Him. The second woe was that of the Báb, upon Him be glory and praise. The third woe is the great Day of the advent of the Lord of Hosts and the revelation of the promised Beauty.” (SAQ 11.34)

On the woman clothed with the sun, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá writes that she is “that bride, the religion of God, that descended upon Muḥammad”, the sun and moon under her feet are “the Persian and the Ottoman” governments, and the twelve stars “represent the twelve Imáms” (SAQ 13.4). In another passage he reads the same woman as the Law: “what is meant by the woman is the Law of God” (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, SWAB 145.7). On the child, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá writes that the man child “who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron” is the promised Manifestation, and “by this son is meant the Báb” (SAQ 13.8). Of “her child was caught up unto God” he writes that it is “a prophecy concerning the Báb” (SAQ 13.9). He concludes: “The Book of Revelation fixes the advent of the Promised One after forty-two months. The Prophet Daniel specifies three times and a half, which is also forty-two months or 1,260 days. Another passage of the Revelation of John directly states 1,260 days, and it is explicitly indicated in the Bible that each day signifies one year.” (SAQ 13.13).

#### The New Jerusalem

An authoritative text does link Revelation 21 to this Day. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá writes in Some Answered Questions: “Clearly, the New Jerusalem which descends from heaven is not a city of stone and lime, of brick and mortar, but is rather the religion of God which descends from heaven and is described as new.” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 13.3). In a Tablet he writes: “The descent of the New Jerusalem denoteth a heavenly Law, that Law which is the guarantor of human happiness and the effulgence of the world of God.” (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, SWAB 29.7). In another he announces: “Verily, verily, the new heaven and the new earth are come.” (SWAB 3.1). Shoghi Effendi applies the verse directly to Bahá’u’lláh’s Law (GPB VI.16, quoted above).

#### Return as the return of attributes: Elijah and John the Baptist

‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s model for “return” is the Gospel account of Elijah. John said he was not Elias; Christ said Elias had come:

“Thus when Christ said, “This is Elias”, He meant: This person is a manifestation of the grace, the perfections, the qualities, the attributes, and the virtues of Elias. And when John the Baptist said, “I am not Elias”, he meant, “I am not the same person as Elias.”” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 33.9)

“For what the Prophets of God meant by “return” is not the return of the essence but of the attributes; it is not the return of the Manifestation Himself but of His perfections.” (SAQ 81.14)

His everyday image: “last year there was a flower, and this year there has also appeared a flower. When I say that the flower of last year has returned, I do not mean that the same flower has returned with the selfsame identity.” (SAQ 33.7). Of the two Manifestations the Gospel announces: “All the Scriptures have foretold the advent of two Manifestations, even as the Gospel doth refer to the coming of Elijah and the Messiah, by which is meant the Báb and Bahá’u’lláh. There is no third Manifestation.” (Light of the World, LOTW 46.4, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá). Shoghi Effendi writes that the Báb’s advent “marks the return of the Prophet Elijah” (GPB XVIII.12).

(d) Shoghi Effendi and the House of Justice

#### Shoghi Effendi

His statements on the Christian promise are quoted above (GPB VI.11, VI.16, XI.28, XII.31–33, XVIII.12; PDC 126, 194, 261, 301; WOB 6.19). On the relation of the Faith to Jesus he writes that the Faith of Bahá’u’lláh “can never, and in no aspect of its teachings, be at variance, much less conflict, with the purpose animating, or the authority invested in, the Faith of Jesus Christ” (The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 7.63). On Christianity:

“As to the position of Christianity, let it be stated without any hesitation or equivocation that its divine origin is unconditionally acknowledged, that the Sonship and Divinity of Jesus Christ are fearlessly asserted, that the divine inspiration of the Gospel is fully recognized, that the reality of the mystery of the Immaculacy of the Virgin Mary is confessed, and the primacy of Peter, the Prince of the Apostles, is upheld and defended.” (The Promised Day Is Come, PDC 268, Shoghi Effendi)

#### Letters written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi

Letters written by his secretaries on his behalf state the belief in plain terms. These are not the Guardian’s own words in his own name. A letter of 24 June 1947 to German Bahá’ís: “The churches are waiting for the coming of Jesus Christ; we believe He has come again in the Glory of the Father.” (The Light of Divine Guidance, vol. 1, letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi, 24 June 1947). A letter of 22 October 1949 says “we believe the Christ has come again, in Bahá’u’lláh, and that all His promises have been gloriously fulfilled.” (The Light of Divine Guidance, vol. 2).

A letter of 29 November 1937, written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi to an individual believer, is the clearest statement on the signs. It is quoted in the Research Department memorandum of 9 October 1989:

“Now as regards the signs that would herald the advent of the new Manifestation; The Guardian wishes you to read over very carefully Bahá’u’lláh’s explanation as recorded in the Iqan”. There it is made clear that what is meant by the appearance of the Son of God after the calamitous events preceding His coming is the revelation of His full glory and its recognition and acceptance by the peoples of the world, and not His physical appearance. For Bahá’u’lláh, Whose advent marks the return of the Son in the glory of the Father, has already appeared, and the signs predicted in the Gospel have not yet fully been realized. Their complete fulfilment, however, would mark the beginning of the recognition of His full station by the peoples of the world. Then and only then will His appearance be made completely manifest.“ (letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi, 29 November 1937, quoted in a Research Department memorandum of 9 October 1989)

The letter says two things. Bahá’u’lláh’s advent ”marks the return of the Son in the glory of the Father“, and the signs in the Gospel ”have not yet fully been realized“. The memorandum introduces the letter as identifying ”the fulfilment of Christ’s prophecy of His return --of the coming of the Kingdom of the Father --with the worldwide realization of the sovereignty of Bahá’u’lláh“. It also points to the Íqán and to chapters 26 and 33 of Some Answered Questions.

#### The House of Justice and its Research Department

A Research Department memorandum of 7 January 1986 gives the rule for individual study: ”It is clear from the Guardian’s statement that we can be sure of the exact meaning of only such passages as have been authoritatively interpreted in our Writings. In time absence of any authoritative interpretation, the individual is free to draw his or her own conclusions about the meaning of the verses and prophecies.“ It quotes a letter of 13 August 1944 written on behalf of the Guardian: ”…although the Book of Revelation has not been interpreted in the teachings from beginning to end there are many passages of it which have been interpreted.“ It also reports that nothing on the 144,000 is found in the Writings or Shoghi Effendi’s letters (letter of 18 January 1983), and that ‘Abdu’l-Bahá reads 666 as the year 666 A.D. (letter on behalf of the House of Justice, 1 August 1978). A letter on behalf of the House of Justice of 28 May 1984 says many Bible passages ”are intended to be taken metaphorically, not literally“.

On Armageddon (Revelation 16:16) the House of Justice answered an enquirer on 2 September 2018. The question was whether the Writings identify Armageddon ”with either the town of Megiddo or with Mount Carmel, or generally with the Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh“. The reply: ”That Department has reported that to date no such references have been found in the Bahá’í writings.“ An identification of Armageddon with Carmel or Megiddo is therefore a personal reading, not one the Writings make.

A letter of 21 December 1992 written on behalf of the House of Justice says Christian theology and the Bahá’í Teachings ”constitute two different universes of discourse“. It says the way to teach Christians is ”to help them to see Jesus through the greater measure of understanding that Bahá’u’lláh has brought, and thus to accept Bahá’u’lláh as the fulfilment of their own aspirations“, and:

”To hold Bahá’u’lláh up in competition to Jesus is not only a fruitless way to teach Christians but is a violation of Bahá’u’lláh’s own Teachings.“ (letter written on behalf of the Universal House of Justice, 21 December 1992)

#### The Templars on Carmel and ”Der Herr ist nahe“

Shoghi Effendi writes that the Báb’s Shrine on Mount Carmel overshadows ”the colony of German Templars who, in anticipation of the “coming of the Lord,” had forsaken their homes and foregathered at the foot of that mountain” (GPB XVIII.12). On 1 December 2025 the House of Justice wrote to all National Spiritual Assemblies that the city of Haifa had agreed to transfer to the Bahá’í World Centre a two-storey Templar house on Carmel:

“It was built by a family of German Templars who inscribed the capstone above the front door of the house with the prophetic words “Der Herr ist nahe”, the Lord is nigh. When the Blessed Beauty visited Haifa in the summer of 1891 and suffered a short illness, He stayed for a few days in the house as a guest of the family.” (message of the Universal House of Justice to all National Spiritual Assemblies, 1 December 2025)

The message states these facts. It calls the words “prophetic” and gives the visit. It does not say more about the inscription.

3. How Bahá’í authors have read the prophecies

This section is about books and articles by Bahá’í authors. They are not authoritative interpretation. The readings of the Bahá’í Writings themselves are in section 2. Where an author repeats a reading that the Writings make, the subsection says so and cites the Writings. Where an author goes further (a date, a place, an event, or a person identified with a verse), the subsection gives it as that author’s reading, and each subsection ends with a line headed Basis that says which is which.

Robert Stockman, in course notes for the Wilmette Institute, states the general point: a Bahá’í interpretation of a biblical verse is “one possible valid meaning of the verse; God may have intended other meanings as well.” Robert Riggs puts the same limit on his own book: its opinions are “strictly those of the author”. The books are named, with year, at first mention. Bible verses are in the King James Version, as in section 1.

The signs of Matthew 24 and the year 1844

Gulpáygání’s argument (1912). Mírzá Abu’l-Faḍl Gulpáygání wrote The Brilliant Proof in reply to the Presbyterian missionary Peter Z. Easton. He argues that the Israelite prophets announced a set of signs, that Christ repeated them (he points to Matthew 24:29–31 and 2 Peter 3:10–11), and that the Qur’án repeated them again. Among the Christian doctors, he writes, some held that the promises were “literal statements and not subject to interpretation”, others that they were “words requiring interpretation”. He then states that Bahá’u’lláh’s “Kitáb-i-Íqán which is the key to unlock the seals of the Heavenly Books” opened the meaning of terms such as ‘Heaven’, ‘Earth’, ‘Sun’, ‘Moon’ and ‘Stars’.

Sears’s three promises (1961). William Sears, in Thief in the Night, picks out three promises that he says Christ attached to his return: that “this gospel of the Kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness” (Matthew 24:14); the “times of the Gentiles” of Luke 21:24, which Sears reads as a promise that Christ “would return to earth when the Jews came back to their homeland”; and the “abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet” (Matthew 24:15). His conclusion is stated without qualification: “Each of these prophecies had been fulfilled in the year 1844!” He supports the second promise with the Ottoman Edict of Toleration, which he dates 21 March 1844, and with the 1,260 years of Revelation.

Townshend and Sours on ‘tribulation’. George Townshend, in Questions about the Second Coming (1953), takes the ‘tribulation’ of Matthew 24:29 to mean what Bahá’u’lláh says it means, “want of capacity to acquire spiritual knowledge and apprehend the Word of God” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 30). Michael Sours, in The Prophecies of Jesus (1991), reads the tribulation in the same way: “the tribulation [Matthew 24:21] can be understood as the period of spiritual crisis that emerges when a religion declines.”

Basis. The Writings state the symbolic reading. Bahá’u’lláh explains ‘oppression’ (KI 30), the ‘sun’ and ‘moon’ (KI 31) and the “sign of the Son of man” (KI 66), and describes twofold signs, in the visible and the invisible heaven, that announced each Prophet (KI 66). ‘Abdu’l-Bahá says of the second coming that “all the signs and conditions that have been indicated have inner meanings and are not to be taken literally” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 26.5). Gulpáygání’s account of how the signs divided the communities is his own history. The particular events that Sears matches to Revelation 6, the Edict of Toleration, and the condition that the gospel be preached ‘in all the world’ are Sears’s and the scholars’ he cites; the Writings gathered for this page do not make those matches.

Daniel’s numbers and 1844

The Writings give the dates themselves, and the Jewish page treats Daniel at length (see the-day-foretold-jewish). In Some Answered Questions, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá reads the 2,300 days of Daniel 8:13–14 as years, counts ‘456 years’ from the edict of Artaxerxes to the birth of Christ and ‘1,844 years’ from the birth of Christ to the advent of the Báb, and concludes: “the fulfilment of the vision of Daniel took place in A.D. 1844, and this is the year of the advent of the Báb.” (SAQ 10.16, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá). He then says that in Matthew 24:3 and 24:15 Christ “referred them to the eighth chapter of the Book of Daniel” (SAQ 10.17). For the “time, times, and an half” he gives 1,260 years and says that “it is in the very year 1260 from the emigration of Muḥammad, according to the Muslim calendar, that the Báb, the Herald of Bahá’u’lláh, revealed His mission.” (SAQ 10.20).

Townshend gives the same sum in his answer to the Bahá’ís of Kampala. Sears gives the Christian background: William Miller, in a lecture published in 1842, had worked from the same decree (“take 457 from 2300, and it will leave A. D. 1843”), and Sears reports that some scholars pointed out that 456 years, not 457, run from the decree to the birth of Christ: “This left the year 1844.” Sears goes beyond the Writings in saying that Daniel’s vision at Shúshán “revealed the year 1844”, in identifying the angel Michael of Daniel 10 and 12 with Bahá’u’lláh, and, in The Wine of Astonishment, in reading Micah 7:15 as a forty-year ministry for Bahá’u’lláh (1852–1892).

Basis. The date 1844 for Daniel 8:14 and A.H. 1260 for the 1,260 years rest on the Writings (SAQ 10.16, 10.20). Sears’s Shúshán, Michael and forty-year readings go beyond them.

From the east

Matthew 24:27 reads: “For as the lightning cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.” Ezekiel 43:2 reads: “And, behold, the glory of the God of Israel came from the way of the east: and his voice was like a noise of many waters”.

Sears, in Thief in the Night, puts Ezekiel 43:2, Isaiah 41:2 and Matthew 24:27 together. His argument is about direction: Ezekiel’s verse is “clearly a reference to the second coming of Christ”, he writes, because Jesus “did not come from the way of the East”. He adds Micah 7:12 and Jeremiah 49:38 to name Persia and Iraq.

Robert Stockman is cautious about Ezekiel 43:4 (“the glory of the LORD came into the house by the way of the gate whose prospect is toward the east”). He writes that no official Bahá’í interpretation of the verse is known to him, that Bahá’ís ‘know’ it to mean Bahá’u’lláh coming to the Holy Land by way of ‘the Gate’ (the Báb) from the east, and that this is a community understanding.

The Writings do use Ezekiel’s image for ‘Akká. Shoghi Effendi writes that ‘Akká was “alluded to by Ezekiel as ‘the gate that looketh towards the East,’ whereunto ‘the glory of the God of Israel came from the way of the East,’” (God Passes By, GPB XI.3, Shoghi Effendi). This is the passage of Ezekiel 43:1–2 that Sears uses.

Basis. Ezekiel’s gate and the glory from the east, as a reference to ‘Akká, rest on Shoghi Effendi (GPB XI.3). No text gathered for this page comments on the ‘lightning’ of Matthew 24:27, so Sears’s use of it, and his Persia, Assyria and Elam readings, are his.

As a thief in the night

The thief is the one image in the Olivet discourse that ‘Abdu’l-Bahá comments on directly (SWAB 17.1 and SAQ 26.5, Section 2(c)).

Townshend, in The Promise of All Ages (1934), sets the thief beside the promise of power and glory: Christ said he would come “in the glory of the Father” and also “as a thief enters stealthily at night and is in the house while the master sleeps and knows it not”. In 1953 he answered that it is fulfilled “through the unique majesty and range of Bahá’u’lláh’s Message, through His giving it to mankind while imprisoned”, while the world is “utterly unconscious that Christ has returned”. Sears argues that the widespread doubt among Christians about the return “was one of the certain signs that that return had taken place”. David Yamartino, in The Second Coming (2012), asks “When do we know that the thief has come?” and answers ‘After he has come.‘ The National Spiritual Assembly of Central and East Africa, in Some Notes on Bahá’í Proofs Based on the Bible (1963), cites 1 Thessalonians 5:2 for Christ’s own saying; that verse is Paul’s, and Christ’s words are Matthew 24:43–44 and Luke 12:39–40.

Basis. The reading of the thief as an unrecognised coming rests on ‘Abdu’l-Bahá (SWAB 17.1, SAQ 26.5). The application of doubt as a sign is Sears’s.

The new name (Revelation 3:12)

Revelation 3:12 (quoted in Section 1) promises “my new name”. The authors agree that the verse points to Bahá’u’lláh. They differ on what the new name is.

  • Sears gathers Isaiah 62:2 (“thou shalt be called by a new name”), Isaiah 65:15 and Revelation 2:17, and concludes: “Isaiah promises clearly that the followers of the Messiah of the last days will bear a different name.” He then argues from Isaiah, Matthew 16:27 and Revelation 21:23 that “the title by which the Messiah would be known when He appeared would be: ‘the Glory of the Lord’.”
  • Dighton and Yamartino say the Promised One comes with a new name; Yamartino: “Like Jesus, He came with a new Name, one that no one was expecting.”
  • Moffett writes: “The new name could be only the name of God as revealed by His latest Revelator.”
  • Riggs is the most specific: “Indeed, the new name of God will be Bahá’; the new Jerusalem will be called the City of Bahá’, and the new Manifestation will be named Bahá’u’lláh.”

The Writings read the second half of the verse: the New Jerusalem is the religion of God (SAQ 13.3) and Bahá’u’lláh’s Law (GPB VI.16), both in Section 2.

Basis. The New Jerusalem rests on the Writings. The reading of ‘my new name’ as Bahá’u’lláh, or as Bahá’, or as “the Glory of the Lord”, is the authors’ own, and the authors disagree with one another. None of the Writings gathered here comments on the phrase ‘my new name’ itself.

‘The glory of the Father’ and ‘the Glory of God’

Shoghi Effendi’s summary of what Bahá’u’lláh is to Christians uses the Gospel phrase: “to Christendom Christ returned ‘in the glory of the Father’” (GPB VI.11). In the list of titles in GPB VI.16 he adds that “the Author of the Apocalypse had alluded” to Bahá’u’lláh “as the ‘Glory of God,‘”. The name itself is translated in a statement prepared by the Bahá’í International Community at the request of the Universal House of Justice: “Bahá’u’lláh (‘Glory of God’)” (BBIC n. 1). Bahá’u’lláh Himself quotes Isaiah 35:2 (“the splendor of Carmel and Sharon, they shall see the glory of the Lord, and the splendor of our God”) in the Epistle to the Son of the Wolf and says of it: “These passages stand in need of no commentary.” (ESW 212–213).

The authors build a larger argument on these texts.

  • Sears (1961, 1963) collects the ‘Glory of the Lord’ from Isaiah 35:2, 58:8 and 60:1, Matthew 16:27, Revelation 21:23 and Ezekiel 1:28, and concludes that it is the new name. He reads Daniel’s Michael as the same figure, “One who looks like God”. In The Wine of Astonishment he writes that Bahá’u’lláh, “whose name means the glory of the Lord, leaves no doubt as to who is meant by these words of Isaiah”.
  • Riggs says the ‘mighty angel’ of Revelation 10:1 is Bahá’u’lláh and also ‘Michael’.

Basis. The Writings support the general identification: Shoghi Effendi (GPB VI.16), Bahá’u’lláh on Isaiah 35:2 (ESW 212–213), and the meaning of the name (BBIC n. 1). Naming particular other verses (Ezekiel 1:28, Daniel 10, Revelation 10:1) and the argument that “the Glory of the Lord” is the promised new name go beyond the Writings.

The Comforter

John 14:16 and 16:13 are quoted in section 1. The Writings in section 2 apply ‘Comforter’ and ‘Spirit of Truth’ to Bahá’u’lláh. Bahá’u’lláh, in a passage Shoghi Effendi quotes, says of Himself that He is the One “Who in the Old Testament hath been named Jehovah, Who in the Gospel hath been designated as the Spirit of Truth, and in the Qur’án acclaimed as the Great Announcement.” (WOB 6.18). ‘Abdu’l-Bahá adds that “the Spirit of truth is embodied in a Man Who has a soul, Who has ears to hear and a tongue to speak.” (SAQ 25.8).

The authors agree with this, with one exception.

  • Townshend treats the Comforter and the return as two separate promises. “These are two distinct promises. The first refers to His continuing presence through the Holy Spirit. The second to His departure and His return.” (Questions about the Second Coming, 1953). In The Heart of the Gospel (1939) he writes that “the Christ of the second coming, the Comforter, the Spirit of Truth, would bring a new, different, and more advanced Revelation”.
  • Sears reads John 14–16 as two kinds of reference: when Christ spoke of his own return he meant “the Christ-spirit, the Holy Spirit, within Him, which would reappear”, and when he spoke of another he meant “a different human channel, a man with a new name other than Christ”.
  • Riggs differs. In a passing remark on the letter to the church of the Laodiceans (Revelation 3:14–16) he writes that it “is read by Muhammad, the Comforter.” Townshend, Sears and Moffett, by contrast, identify the Comforter with Bahá’u’lláh.

The Writings do not set the two readings side by side. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá lists the Covenants in a Tablet: “Christ made a covenant concerning the Paraclete and gave the tidings of His coming. The Prophet Muḥammad made a covenant concerning the Báb” (Light of the World, LOTW 61.1). The Tablet does not say who the Paraclete is.

Basis. Bahá’u’lláh’s own identification of Himself as the Spirit of Truth and the Comforter rests on the Writings (WOB 6.18–19, SLH 1.122, GPB XII.33). Riggs’s identification of the Comforter with Muḥammad is not a reading found in the texts gathered here.

Revelation 11–13

‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s commentary on Revelation 11 and 12 is in Some Answered Questions, chapters 11 and 13. Ruth Moffett’s New Keys to the Book of Revelation (Bahá’í Publishing Trust of India, 1977) follows it and goes on to chapter 13. Riggs’s The Apocalypse (1998) covers the whole book.

Where the authors follow ‘Abdu’l-Bahá: the two witnesses of Revelation 11:3 are “Muḥammad the Messenger of God and ‘Alí the son of Abú-Ṭálib” (SAQ 11.12); the beast from the bottomless pit and the dragon of Revelation 12 are the Umayyads (SAQ 11.20, 13.6); and the woman clothed with the sun is the Law of God (SWAB 145.7). Moffett and Riggs repeat these. Riggs ends the 1,260 years of the two witnesses ‘at the year 1844 CE’, which agrees with ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s year A.H. 1260 (SAQ 10.20).

Moffett goes further for chapter 13. She says that the first beast “is according to Shoghi Effendi, Mu‘áwíyah”, that 666 is the year of an act by Mu‘áwíyah, “according to Shoghi Effendi”, and that “Shoghi Effendi explained this to the author and to the members of the International Council in Haifa.” This is Moffett’s report of an oral explanation. No published text of Shoghi Effendi gathered for this page states it, and it should be treated as second-hand. She also reads the ‘image of the beast’ as the martyrdom of the Báb in 1850.

Basis. The readings of Revelation 11 and 12 above rest on ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. The identifications in chapter 13 (Mu‘áwíyah, 666, the image of the beast), the ‘mighty angel’, and the zodiac readings go beyond the Writings. Moffett’s attribution to Shoghi Effendi is her own account and is not a Guardian text.

The vineyard and its Lord

In Matthew 21:40 Jesus asks: “When the lord therefore of the vineyard cometh, what will he do unto those husbandmen?” Shoghi Effendi lists “the ‘Lord of the Vineyard,‘” among the titles by which Jesus referred to Bahá’u’lláh (GPB VI.16). Bahá’u’lláh writes that “Carmel, in the Book of God, hath been designated as the Hill of God, and His Vineyard” (ESW 210).

The National Spiritual Assembly of Central and East Africa gives a key to the parable: the Lord of the Vineyard is the Father, his Son is Christ, the servants are the prophets before Christ, the husbandmen are religious leaders and the Vineyard is the earth. Riggs and Yamartino identify the Lord of the Vineyard with Bahá’u’lláh and note that Carmel means the vineyard of God or of the Lord.

A historical case is reported by Christopher Buck: in Yazd on 7 May 1888 the Bahá’í poet ‘Andalíb tried to persuade the Cambridge Orientalist Edward Granville Browne “on the basis of the Parable of the Vineyard” that Bahá’u’lláh was the Lord of the Vineyard.

Basis. The title “Lord of the Vineyard” (GPB VI.16) and Carmel as God’s vineyard (ESW 210) rest on the Writings. The detailed symbol key is the authors’.

‘Everlasting Father’ (Isaiah 9:6)

Isaiah 9:6 ends: “and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace.” Christian readers take the verse as a prophecy of Christ, and the Bahá’í authors acknowledge this. John Esslemont, in Bahá’u’lláh and the New Era (1923), writes that much of it “may quite fairly be thus applied” to Christ, but that it applies “more fully and aptly” to Bahá’u’lláh.

The Writings do not comment on Isaiah 9:6 verse by verse in the texts gathered here. They say that the Father whom Isaiah extolled has come. To Czar Alexander II, Bahá’u’lláh writes: “I am the One Whom the tongue of Isaiah hath extolled” (SLH 1.164); Shoghi Effendi summarises the same Tablet as announcing “the advent of the promised Father, Whom ‘the tongue of Isaiah hath extolled,‘” (GPB XII.24). To the Pope, Bahá’u’lláh writes: “Blessed be the Lord Who is the Father!” (SLH 1.112).

The authors connect the two. Esslemont adds that Bahá’u’lláh “repeatedly refers to Himself as the manifestation of the Father, of whom Christ and Isaiah spoke, whereas Christ always referred to Himself as the Son”. Townshend (Christ and Bahá’u’lláh, 1957) argues that Christ “disclaimed being the Father when He said, ‘my Father is greater than I’”.

Basis. That the Father of Isaiah has come rests on Bahá’u’lláh (SLH 1.164, 1.112). Applying that to the titles of Isaiah 9:6 one by one are the authors’.

George Townshend’s overall reading

George Townshend (1876–1957) was ordained in 1905 and served as an Anglican priest until 1947. The title pages of his books give him as Canon of St Patrick’s Cathedral, Dublin, and Archdeacon of Clonfert. His path to the Faith is in section 4. He wrote four books for Christian readers: The Promise of All Ages (1934), The Heart of the Gospel (1939), Questions about the Second Coming (answers to the Bahá’ís of Kampala, 1953) and Christ and Bahá’u’lláh (1957). Their common claim, stated in the prologue of the last, is “that the Kingdom of God, as foretold in the Bible with a thousand details, has at last come with those details all fulfilled.”

What a Christian should look for. From the four books:

  1. A return of qualities, not of the person. “When Bahá’ís affirm that Christ has returned in the Báb and in Bahá’u’lláh they mean that the essence of the perfections of Christ are to be found in the Báb and Bahá’u’lláh though the individualities are different.” The model is Elijah and John the Baptist, which is ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s (SAQ 33.9).
  2. A further revelation. Christ said he had ‘many more things’ to say. Townshend reads that as a promise of a later revelation, given when “the age of maturity has been reached”.
  3. Signs that look contradictory but are not. Power and great glory against the thief; lightning from the east against the unnoticed coming; clouds as “anything that intervenes between the eye of man’s understanding and the Manifestation of God” (his gloss on KI 79).
  4. A dated event. In The Heart of the Gospel: “The Second Coming would be a dated historical event.” He takes the year from Daniel and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá.
  5. The Kingdom. “Christ’s crowning promise of the Kingdom, which the Churches have failed to realise and have for all practical purposes abandoned, stands in the Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh”.

Why Christendom did not recognise the return. Townshend presents these reasons as his own diagnosis. He compares the failure to recognise Bahá’u’lláh with the failure of the Jews to recognise Christ: prophecies taken in a literal sense “became a screen to shut off all vision of it”.

Basis. The Elijah model (SAQ 33.9), the date from Daniel (SAQ 10.16), and the symbolic reading of the clouds (KI 79) rest on the Writings. The list of what a Christian should expect, and his diagnosis of why the churches did not recognise the return, are Townshend’s own.

Michael Sours and Christopher Buck

Michael Sours’s The Prophecies of Jesus (Oneworld, Oxford, 1991) is a commentary on Matthew 24 in four parts: ‘The Beginning of Sorrows’, ‘The Corrupting of Islam’, ‘The Second Advent’ and ‘The Command to Watch’ (as Buck lists them). Sours draws on the Kitáb-i-Íqán, Some Answered Questions and nineteenth-century Christian writers on prophecy. Two of his readings differ from the other authors’ on this page. The “abomination of desolation” in Matthew 24:15 is, in Buck’s quotation of Sours, “a specific reference to what befell Islam—that is, Islam is the holy Temple that was desecrated”. The time of Christian tribulation is lengthened to 1,260 years because Christians did not accept Muḥammad.

Christopher Buck reviewed the book in the Journal of Bahá’í Studies 5.2 (1992). He called it ‘excellent apology’ and said that it is not scholarship in the academic sense, because “a key element of apologetics is polemic.” Sours replied in the same journal (6.1, 1994), and the two disagreed about how to read the destruction of the Temple in 70 CE and about the authority of a passage of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá on the future of the Jewish people (The Promulgation of Universal Peace, page 414). The journal’s editors took no side.

Basis. The disagreement concerns method and the authority of one passage, not the Writings’ symbolic reading, which both accept. Sours’s reading of Matthew 24:15 as the corruption of Islam is his own. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in SAQ 10.17, takes the verse to point the reader to Daniel 8 for the date of the advent. The page does not reconcile the two.

4. Those who recognised it

This section is history, not scripture, and pilgrims’ notes are not authoritative. Each item is labelled with the kind of source it rests on. Many of the people named had Christian backgrounds, and some were already expecting the return of Christ.

The expectation of the 1840s

William Miller and the Millerites (history and primary source). William P. Collins, in a 1998 article on the Millerites, writes that “no group was more influential than the Millerites in constructing an historicist interpretation of biblical time prophecy that argued for the imminent return of Christ in 1843 or 1844.” William Miller (1782–1849) was a self-taught Baptist farmer who read the Bible with a concordance until he became convinced that the Second Advent was near. His own book, Evidence from Scripture and History of the Second Coming of Christ, about the Year 1843 (Boston: Joshua V. Himes, 1842),. Miller counts from the decree of Artaxerxes, “457 years before Christ”, and writes: “take 457 from 2300, and it will leave A. D. 1843”. Collins records that when the Parousia did not take place on 21 March 1844, a “seventh-month movement” fixed on 22 October 1844. Collins writes: “On 22 October 1844—the last of the definite dates given by the Millerites for the Parousia—the person of Jesus Christ did not descend in the expected manner.” Stephen Dighton calls the period afterwards “the Great Disappointment”. The Báb declared His mission on 23 May 1844 (GPB I.1).

Other 1840s calculators (lecture slides quoting letters, second-hand). Ahang Rabbani’s 2006 lecture slides quote a Baptist minister, David Bernard, writing on 2 January 1843: “With certainty, the fulfillment of the prophecy about 2300 will be witnessed at the end of 1843.” Brian Mistler, in a 2001–02 field study, lists others who “had all calculated the period 1843-1846 as the ‘time of the end’”: Wolff in Asia, Edward Irving in England, Mason in Scotland, Davis in South Carolina, William Miller, and Leonard H. Kebler in Germany.

The Templers on Mount Carmel

The German Templers (history). A statement prepared by the Bahá’í International Community at the request of the Universal House of Justice (1992) says that two German religious leaders, Christoph Hoffmann and Georg David Hardegg, developed the ‘Society of Templers’ in the 1850s to create in the Holy Land a colony that “would prepare the way for Christ, on His return”. Its footnote gives the dates: the founding group left Germany on 6 August 1868 and reached Haifa on 30 October 1868, two months after Bahá’u’lláh’s own arrival. Adib Taherzadeh, in The Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh, volume 3 (p. 28), says that the Templers “had concluded from the prophecies that the Messiah would appear there” and writes of the colony: “On their doorways still remains the inscription ’Der Herr ist Nahe’ (The Lord is near).” The sources differ on dates. Taherzadeh has the first Templers arriving at the foot of Mount Carmel in 1863 and building the colony in 1868. Thornton Chase, who visited in 1907, says the colony “was established in Haifa in 1843 in expectation of the second coming of Christ”. The BIC footnote’s 1868 is the figure to use, and 1843 is the expectation year. Sears, who names the Templers’ leader as Leonard H. Kelber, writes that “They were positive that the ‘Glory of God’ would appear on the side of Mount Carmel.”

The Tablet to Hardegg (Writings; authorised translation). The Lawḥ-i-Hirtík is a Tablet of Bahá’u’lláh to Hardegg. The saved file is an authorised translation by the Bahá’í World Centre (August 2024), from Additional Tablets and Extracts from Tablets Revealed by Bahá’u’lláh. The Tablet does not name the Templers. It says: “Lo, the appointed time is now arrived, and Carmel crieth out as if moved to ecstasy by the soul-stirring breezes of her Lord.” It recalls the first coming: “it was those who were distinguished for their learning, the men of letters and religion, who denied Him, whilst humble fishermen made haste to gain admittance into the Kingdom”. Taherzadeh, who summarises the Tablet, records the outcome: “neither Hardegg nor any other member of the Christian colony was able to recognize the truth of the Message of Bahá’u’lláh”, although from the early days some members, including Hardegg, “had been in contact with the believers and with ‘Abdu’l-Bahá”.

The first Western believers

Thornton Chase (Shoghi Effendi; history; Chase’s own letter). Shoghi Effendi writes of “the stout-hearted Thornton Chase, surnamed Thábit (Steadfast) by ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá and designated by Him ‘the first American believer,‘ who became a convert to the Faith in 1894” (GPB XVI.10). The Bahá’í Chronicles entry says that Chase and a small group of Chicagoans began to study the Faith with Ibrahim Kheiralla, who taught “the coming of Bahá’u’lláh, a Messenger of God, in fulfillment of biblical prophecy.” Chase’s open letter “Before Abraham Was, I am” (Chicago, 27 September 1902; published as a 13-page pamphlet) states his own understanding of the return: “The ‘second coming’ must bear evident marks of similitude to the first, in manner, character, instruction and in its Spirit, but it must be greater in results, wider in extension and different in effect.” The same letter says: “Indeed He has come again, come in the Kingdom of His Father, the Revealer, come as the Interpreter of that which was and is.” In 1907 Chase made the pilgrimage to ‘Akká and wrote In Galilee (1908). He reports that ‘Abdu’l-Bahá asked not to be called ‘Master’ or ‘Lord’, because, in Chase’s words, those titles ‘belonged to Jesus’. Passing through the German Colony on the way to Mírzá Asadu’lláh’s house, he wrote: “Yet they know not that the prophecy has been fulfilled and that the Lord has indeed been at their very doors.”

Ibrahim Kheiralla’s lessons (scholarship). Robert Stockman, in The Bahá’ís of the United States, writes that in 1896 Kheiralla organised his teachings into lectures and lessons that “described Bahá’u’lláh as the biblical return of the Father and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá as the return of Christ.” Stockman also records that the last lessons said the Millerites, who expected Christ’s return in 1844, were correct, because that was the year of the Báb’s mission. This teaching was Kheiralla’s, not an authoritative interpretation. Shoghi Effendi states: “That ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá is not a Manifestation of God, that, though the successor of His Father, He does not occupy a cognate station, that no one else except the Báb and Bahá’u’lláh can ever lay claim to such a station before the expiration of a full thousand years—are verities which lie embedded in the specific utterances of both the Founder of our Faith and the Interpreter of His teachings.” (The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.70)

The first Western pilgrims, 1898 (pilgrim’s account). A pilgrim account by Honnold, Glimpses of Early Bahá’í Pilgrimages (history), says that in 1898 Phoebe Hearst organised a group of pilgrims to ‘Akká, and that she and others “mistook the Master for the Prophet Himself”, which he says ‘Abdu’l-Bahá never claimed. Honnold records ‘Abdu’l-Bahá as saying to pilgrims that in the Gospel “they are expecting the Father and the Second Coming of Christ”, and that the Báb and Bahá’u’lláh are the two Manifestations all the scriptures announce (Honnold, p. 61; a pilgrim’s record, not authoritative). Another set of pilgrim notes by Getsinger, Dodge and Hoar gives ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s explanation that Christ’s coming from heaven is “spiritually, from the Heaven of the Divine Will, and in a material body” (history; pilgrims’ notes).

A woman in Boston (Shoghi Effendi). Shoghi Effendi records, among the scenes of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s journey in the West, “the acclamation of a Syrian woman in Boston who, pushing aside the crowd that had gathered around Him, flung herself at His feet, exclaiming, ‘I confess that in Thee I have recognized the Spirit of God and Jesus Christ Himself’” (GPB XIX.24). Shoghi Effendi reports what she said. He does not give ‘Abdu’l-Bahá that station (see WOB 6.70, quoted above).

Mary Lucas and Stanwood Cobb

‘Our Lord has come!’ (memoir). Stanwood Cobb, in Memories of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, recalls that he came into the Faith “in the summer of 1906” while he was “studying for the Unitarian ministry at the Harvard Divinity School”. At Green Acre, on a warm Sunday afternoon in August, he had been thinking that “Someone must appear with more than human authority, in order to win the allegiance of the whole world to an ideal pattern for humanity.” Mary Lucas, the ‘woman in white’ and “a singer just back from visiting” ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, took him under an apple tree on a sloping lawn. Her whole exposition “consisted of only these four words: ‘Our Lord has come!’” Cobb writes that when she said them, ‘I felt, This is it!’ and that if ‘Abdu’l-Bahá Himself “had stood under that apple tree and addressed me, I could not have been more convinced.”

Mary Lucas’s notes of 1905 (pilgrim’s notes; not authoritative). Her booklet A Brief Account of My Visit to Acca (Chicago: Bahá’í Publishing Society, 1905) records a visit in January and February 1905. An entry for Sunday, 5 February 1905, is headed ‘The Holy Comforter’. She notes that ‘Abdu’l-Bahá said: “The Christians explain these verses by saying they refer to the Holy Spirit which came to the disciples after Christ’s departure. But a careful study of these verses will show that a definite promise is given which can only be fulfilled in the coming of another human being.”

George Townshend’s own path

An Anglican priest (Bahá’í World, In Memoriam). The memorial notice by his son Brian Townshend, in The Bahá’í World, volume 13 (1970), gives the outline. Townshend was born in Dublin, was called to the Irish Bar in 1903, went to Utah in 1904 as a missionary among the Mormons, and was ordained there in 1905. At Howth in July 1917 a friend in America sent him pamphlets with some words of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá. In his own words: “When I looked at those, that was the beginning and the end with me.” ‘Abdu’l-Bahá wrote to him on 19 December 1920: “It is my hope that your church will come under the Heavenly Jerusalem.” He was Rector of Ahascragh, County Galway, from January 1919. About ten years after publishing The Altar on the Hearth (1926) he publicly identified himself with the Faith, and “Eleven restless years” passed before he left the Church.

The resignation (Bahá’í World; library note). In 1947, at the age of 70, Townshend gave up his orders and addressed a statement, “The Old Churches and the New World Faith”, to Christians; it was sent to 10,000 people in the British Isles. In a letter to the Bishop of Utah relinquishing his American orders he wrote that the Glad Tidings of Bahá’u’lláh “represent the promised return of Christ”. Shoghi Effendi wrote to him on 7 July 1947 that the resignation was “a truly remarkable and historic step”. He was named a Hand of the Cause of God in 1951 (Bahá’í Chronicles) and died on 25 March 1957. At the request of Shoghi Effendi he had written the introductions to The Dawn-Breakers and God Passes By.

Two modern accounts

A field study (scholarship). Brian Mistler’s 2001–02 survey of Bahá’ís in the United States and Australia found that although much has been written on prophecy, “it plays no significant role on the conversion of members to the Bahá’í faith”. It is a counterweight to the books in section 3. One respondent, who was fifteen at the time, wrote of reading Thief in the Night, The Wine of Astonishment and Some Answered Questions: “My mind was satisfied that Bahá’u’lláh was the Return of Christ, in fact the Return for all religions.”

5. The tradition’s sacred music and poetry

Description from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0), saved at [source] and `music-hymn.md`. Description only, not for lyrics.

Wikipedia describes Gregorian chant as “the central tradition of Western plainchant, a form of monophonic, unaccompanied sacred song in Latin” that developed mainly in the 9th and 10th centuries. It is the music of the Roman Rite, performed in the Mass and the monastic Office. The article distinguishes syllabic, neumatic and melismatic styles, from one note per syllable to long sustained vowels as in the Alleluia, and describes melodic motion as primarily stepwise.

The article on hymns defines a hymn as a song “specifically written for the purpose of adoration or prayer”. In the Middle Ages, hymnody took the form of Gregorian chant sung in unison, usually by monastic choirs. Later Western hymnody introduced four-part harmony with the organ and choir. Eastern Christian chant may be unaccompanied or sung over a drone (ison). For a songwriter, these point to three possible idioms: a unison chant-like line in a mode, a four-part congregational hymn, and a plain unaccompanied voice.

6. What the sources do not settle

  • Timing. Matthew 24:36 says the day is not known. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá gives the year A.H. 1260 (1844) for the Báb’s advent from Daniel and Revelation (SAQ 10.16, 10.20, 11.6). A letter written on behalf of Shoghi Effendi says the signs in the Gospel “have not yet fully been realized” (29 November 1937, Section 2(d)). No authoritative text found here ties each verse of Matthew 24 to a dated event.
  • Matthew 24:14 and 24:27. No authoritative reading of the gospel “preached in all the world” or of the “lightning” from the east was found. The readings in Section 3 are Sears’s.
  • Ezekiel 43:4. Shoghi Effendi applies Ezekiel’s gate to ‘Akká (GPB XI.3). Stockman says he knows of no official interpretation of the verse itself.
  • The Comforter. The Writings say Bahá’u’lláh is the Comforter and the Spirit of Truth, and also the return of Christ in the glory of the Father. Bahá’í authors differ on how the promises relate. Townshend reads them as two promises, Sears as two kinds of reference, and Riggs reads the Comforter in one passage as Muḥammad. The Writings gathered here do not settle the difference, and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s list of covenants (LOTW 61.1) does not say who the Paraclete is.
  • The new name (Revelation 3:12). The New Jerusalem is read in the Writings. “My new name” is not. Sears, Riggs, Dighton, Yamartino and Moffett identify it differently (Section 3).
  • Armageddon, 666 and 144,000. The House of Justice reports that no reference identifying Armageddon with Carmel or Megiddo has been found. For 666 the Research Department cites ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s reading as the year 666 A.D. For 144,000 it finds nothing in the Writings or in Shoghi Effendi’s letters.
  • Moffett’s account of Shoghi Effendi’s explanation of Revelation 13. It is her second-hand report; no Guardian text states it.
  • Templer dates. The Bahá’í International Community statement gives 1868 for the colony at Haifa. Taherzadeh gives 1863 for the first arrivals and 1868 for building. Chase, writing in 1907, gives 1843 for the founding. Section 4 treats 1843 as the expectation year.
  • What the Chase paper says. The earlier version of this page could not name the author of “Before Abraham Was, I am”. The saved file names Thornton Chase, Chicago, 27 September 1902, and Section 4 now cites it as his.

Concrete images.

  • Bells that the Tablets ask priests to leave, and the “Most Mighty Bell” tolled in the heaven of Immortality (SLH 1.131; TB 2.12).
  • John crying out “in the wilderness of the Bayán” (SLH 1.121).
  • The Magi’s star in the east (KI 70).
  • Clouds as the human body of Christ that hid his divinity (Paris Talks 12.3, 12.6).
  • A thief in the house while the owner sleeps (SWAB 17.1; SAQ 26.5).
  • Bethlehem raising her voice to the Lord of glory (TB 2.17).
  • Carmel as “the Hill of God and His Vineyard” and the Tablet of Carmel (GPB XI.28).
  • A capstone over a Templer door on Carmel, “Der Herr ist nahe”, the Lord is nigh, in a house where Bahá’u’lláh stayed as a guest in 1891 (message of the Universal House of Justice, 1 December 2025).
  • The Millerites on 22 October 1844 waiting for a descent that did not come (Collins, 1998).
  • Mary Lucas under an apple tree at Green Acre saying four words, “Our Lord has come!” (Cobb, Memories of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá).
  • A Syrian woman in Boston pushing through the crowd to fall at ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s feet (GPB XIX.24).

Two possible arcs.

  1. Farewell, wait, announcement. “I go away”; the long watching and the question “when?”; the thief who comes unnoticed; the announcement that the Father has come; the city and the tabernacle “with men”.
  2. Expectation and the person who gets there. Millerites waiting in 1844; Templers building at the foot of Carmel; a woman under an apple tree; the bell that is to be laid down. The song can end on one recognition, told as one person’s.

What a lyric must not claim.

  • That Christian churches or their scholars accept the Bahá’í reading.
  • That Jesus’s person returned as a physical being in the clouds. The Bahá’í texts say the return is in the attributes and in a new human temple (KI 74; SAQ 26.3, 33.9).
  • That ‘Abdu’l-Bahá is the return of Christ (Shoghi Effendi, WOB 6.70; see Section 4).
  • That every verse of Revelation has an authoritative fulfilment.
  • That a Bahá’í author’s reading is the Bahá’í reading: Sears’s event matches for Revelation 6, Riggs’s “Bahá’” as the new name, Moffett’s Revelation 13, Armageddon at Carmel or Megiddo.
  • That the Templers or the Millerites recognised Bahá’u’lláh. The sources say they expected a coming; the Tablet to Hardegg was not accepted by the colony.
  • Any verdict on Christians. Bahá’u’lláh’s sentences on the divines are His own words and are not to be turned into a judgement in the song’s own voice.
  • That Bahá’u’lláh should be held up against Jesus. The House of Justice calls that a violation of Bahá’u’lláh’s own Teachings (21 December 1992).
