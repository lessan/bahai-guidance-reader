The Day Foretold: The Christian Promise

Last updated 16 October 2026

This page belongs to the series introduced in the-day-foretold (see also promises-of-the-future). Sibling pages: the-day-foretold-hindu, the-day-foretold-jewish, the-day-foretold-zoroastrian, the-day-foretold-buddhist, the-day-foretold-islamic, the-day-foretold-babi.

It sets out what the New Testament says about the return of Christ, the Comforter and the Spirit of truth, and the New Jerusalem, and what the Bahá’í Writings say about how Bahá’u’lláh’s Day relates to those promises. In God Passes By, Shoghi Effendi names what Bahá’u’lláh’s coming is to each tradition. For Christians he writes: “to Christendom Christ returned ‘in the glory of the Father’” (God Passes By, GPB VI.11).

The page keeps three kinds of source apart: the Gospels and Revelation in the King James Version (1611, public domain); the Bahá’í Writings and Shoghi Effendi’s books; and history and scholarship, each labelled. What the Bahá’í texts say about Christian scripture is the Bahá’í understanding, not a statement of what Christian churches teach.

1. The promise

The Son of man comes “in the glory of his Father”

In Matthew, Jesus says:

“For the Son of man shall come in the glory of his Father with his angels; and then he shall reward every man according to his works.” (Matthew 16:27, King James Version)

Later, on the Mount of Olives, he describes the sign and the manner of the coming:

“And then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory.” (Matthew 24:30, King James Version)

“And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other.” (Matthew 24:31, King James Version)

He also says that the day is not known:

“But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only.” (Matthew 24:36, King James Version)

“I go away, and come again”: the Comforter and the Spirit of truth

In the Gospel of John, Jesus promises another helper:

“And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever; Even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him” (John 14:16–17, King James Version)

“I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you.” (John 14:18, King James Version)

“Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you.” (John 16:7, King James Version)

“Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth: for he shall not speak of himself; but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak: and he will shew you things to come.” (John 16:13, King James Version)

The Revelation of John

Revelation speaks of a seventh trumpet, a woman in heaven, and a city:

“And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.” (Revelation 11:15, King James Version)

“And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars:” (Revelation 12:1, King James Version)

“And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.” (Revelation 21:2, King James Version)

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.” (Revelation 21:3, King James Version)

The chapter begins: “And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away” (Revelation 21:1, King James Version). The book closes with “Surely I come quickly. Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus.” (Revelation 22:20, King James Version).

2. The fulfilment in the Bahá’í Writings

The titles Shoghi Effendi gathers

Shoghi Effendi lists the titles by which, he writes, Jesus Christ referred to Bahá’u’lláh:

“To Him Jesus Christ had referred as the “Prince of this world,” as the “Comforter” Who will “reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment,” as the “Spirit of Truth” Who “will guide you into all truth,” Who “shall not speak of Himself, but whatsoever He shall hear, that shall He speak,” as the “Lord of the Vineyard,” and as the “Son of Man” Who “shall come in the glory of His Father” “in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory,” with “all the holy angels” about Him, and “all nations” gathered before His throne.” (God Passes By, GPB VI.16, Shoghi Effendi)

The same paragraph applies the Revelation of John to Him: “Identifying His Revelation with the “third woe,” he, moreover, had extolled His Law as “a new heaven and a new earth,” as the “Tabernacle of God,” as the “Holy City,” as the “New Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.”” (GPB VI.16). In The Promised Day Is Come, Shoghi Effendi describes Bahá’u’lláh as “the Establisher of the Kingdom of the Father foretold by the Son” (The Promised Day Is Come, PDC 301), and of the clergy he writes that they “have interposed themselves between their flock and the Christ returned in the glory of the Father” (PDC 261).

The Father is come

In His Tablets to Christians, Bahá’u’lláh announces that the Father has come. In the Lawḥ-i-Aqdas, He writes:

“The river Jordan is joined to the Most Great Ocean, and the Son, in the holy vale, crieth out: “Here am I, here am I O Lord, my God!”, whilst Sinai circleth round the House, and the Burning Bush calleth aloud: “He Who is the Desired One is come in His transcendent majesty.” Say, Lo! The Father is come, and that which ye were promised in the Kingdom is fulfilled!” (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, Lawḥ-i-Aqdas, TB 2.7, Bahá’u’lláh)

In the Tablet to Pope Pius IX, He writes: “The Word which the Son concealed is made manifest. It hath been sent down in the form of the human temple in this day. Blessed be the Lord Who is the Father! He, verily, is come unto the nations in His most great majesty.” (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, Tablet to Pope Pius IX, SLH 1.112, Bahá’u’lláh). Shoghi Effendi summarises the same Tablet: Bahá’u’lláh wrote “that the Word was Himself, and He Himself the Father” (PDC 126).

The Comforter and the Spirit of Truth

The Tablet to Pope Pius IX also names the Comforter: “This is that peerless One foretold by Isaiah, and the Comforter concerning Whom the Spirit had covenanted with you.” (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, Tablet to Pope Pius IX, SLH 1.122, Bahá’u’lláh). To the priests, in the Lawḥ-i-Aqdas, He writes: “Verily, He Who is the Spirit of Truth is come to guide you unto all truth.” (TB 2.10). In a passage Shoghi Effendi quotes, Bahá’u’lláh addresses Christendom: “The Comforter Whose advent all the scriptures have promised is now come that He may reveal unto you all knowledge and wisdom.” (The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.19).

‘Abdu’l-Bahá applies John 16:13 to the present age. In a talk in Washington, D.C., on 21 April 1912 (from notes of a talk by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá), he said: “The century has dawned when the Spirit of Truth can reveal these verities to mankind, proclaim that very Word, establish the real foundations of Christianity” (The Promulgation of Universal Peace, PUP 16.7).

“I go away, and come again”

Bahá’u’lláh connects the promise of return to the Comforter in the Súriy-i-Mulúk, addressed to the kings of Christendom:

“O KINGS of Christendom! Heard ye not the saying of Jesus, the Spirit of God, “I go away, and come again unto you”? Wherefore, then, did ye fail, when He did come again unto you in the clouds of heaven, to draw nigh unto Him, that ye might behold His face, and be of them that attained His Presence? In another passage He saith: “When He, the Spirit of Truth, is come, He will guide you into all truth.”” (Gleanings from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh, GWB CXVI.1, Bahá’u’lláh)

To the Christians generally He writes: “This is the Day of God; turn ye unto Him. He, verily, hath come down from heaven even as He came down the first time” (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, SLH 1.127, Bahá’u’lláh). To Queen Victoria: “all that hath been mentioned in the Gospel hath been fulfilled.” (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, Tablet to Queen Victoria, SLH 1.171, Bahá’u’lláh).

The clouds, the trumpet and the angels

In the Kitáb-i-Íqán, Bahá’u’lláh quotes Matthew 24 and explains its images. The “clouds” are “those things that are contrary to the ways and desires of men”:

“By the term “clouds” is meant those things that are contrary to the ways and desires of men.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 79, Bahá’u’lláh)

The coming itself is described this way:

“Thereupon, they will behold the countenance of the promised One, the adored Beauty, descending from heaven and riding upon the clouds. By this is meant that the divine Beauty will be made manifest from the heaven of the will of God, and will appear in the form of the human temple.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 74, Bahá’u’lláh)

Bahá’u’lláh adds a reason for the symbolic reading: “Were the prophecies recorded in the Gospel to be literally fulfilled; were Jesus, Son of Mary, accompanied by angels, to descend from the visible heaven upon the clouds; who would dare to disbelieve, who would dare to reject the truth, and wax disdainful?” (KI 88).

The same Tablet speaks about those who read the signs literally. These are Bahá’u’lláh’s words, not the page’s:

“Inasmuch as the Christian divines have failed to apprehend the meaning of these words, and did not recognize their object and purpose, and have clung to the literal interpretation of the words of Jesus, they therefore became deprived of the streaming grace of the Muḥammadan Revelation and its showering bounties.” (Kitáb-i-Íqán, KI 25, Bahá’u’lláh)

In the Tablet to Pope Pius IX He writes: “He Who is the Lord of Lords is come overshadowed with clouds” (The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, Tablet to Pope Pius IX, SLH 1.102, Bahá’u’lláh).

‘Abdu’l-Bahá gives the same account of “heaven”. In Some Answered Questions he notes that the signs of Matthew 24 “have inner meanings and are not to be taken literally”, and he points out that Christ said the Son of man came down from heaven although he was born of Mary (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 26.5). In a talk in Paris on 27 October 1911 (from notes of a talk by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá), he quotes Bahá’u’lláh: “When Christ came for the first time He came upon the clouds.” (Paris Talks 12.3, 27 October 1911).

Revelation 11 and 12: ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s readings and dates

Shoghi Effendi quotes the verse “The second woe is past; and, behold the third woe cometh quickly,” and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s comment: “This third woe is the day of the Manifestation of Bahá’u’lláh, the Day of God, and it is near to the day of the appearance of the Báb.” (GPB VI.7).

In Some Answered Questions, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá reads the “forty-two months” and the “1,260 days” of Revelation as 1,260 years, ending in the Báb’s advent:

“This 1,260 years is a prophecy concerning the advent of the Báb, the “Gate” leading to Bahá’u’lláh, which took place in the year A.H. 1260.” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 11.6)

“The first woe was the advent of the Apostle of God, Muḥammad the son of ‘Abdu’lláh, peace be upon Him. The second woe was that of the Báb, upon Him be glory and praise. The third woe is the great Day of the advent of the Lord of Hosts and the revelation of the promised Beauty.” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 11.34)

Of the woman in Revelation 12, he writes: “This woman is that bride, the religion of God, that descended upon Muḥammad.” (SAQ 13.4). The child “caught up unto God” is the Báb (SAQ, Revelation 12:5), and after 1,260 days “the dragon was destroyed and the offspring of the religion of God, the Promised One, was made manifest.”

The New Jerusalem

An authoritative text does link Revelation 21 to this Day. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá writes in Some Answered Questions: “Clearly, the New Jerusalem which descends from heaven is not a city of stone and lime, of brick and mortar, but is rather the religion of God which descends from heaven and is described as new.” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 13.3). In a Tablet, he writes: “The descent of the New Jerusalem denoteth a heavenly Law, that Law which is the guarantor of human happiness and the effulgence of the world of God.” (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, SWAB 29.7). In another Tablet he announces: “Verily, verily, the new heaven and the new earth are come.” (Selections from the Writings of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, SWAB 3.1). Shoghi Effendi applies the verse directly to Bahá’u’lláh’s Law (GPB VI.16, quoted above).

Return as the return of attributes: Elijah and John the Baptist

‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s model for “return” is the Gospel account of Elijah. John said he was not Elias; Christ said Elias had come. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá explains:

“Thus when Christ said, “This is Elias”, He meant: This person is a manifestation of the grace, the perfections, the qualities, the attributes, and the virtues of Elias. And when John the Baptist said, “I am not Elias”, he meant, “I am not the same person as Elias.”” (Some Answered Questions, SAQ 33.9)

‘Abdu’l-Bahá gives the same meaning to “return” in the Holy Books generally:

“For what the Prophets of God meant by “return” is not the return of the essence but of the attributes; it is not the return of the Manifestation Himself but of His perfections.” (SAQ 81.14)

Bahá’u’lláh writes in Gleanings that Jesus “declared: “I go away and come again unto you”” as part of His teaching on the unity of the Manifestations (Gleanings, GWB XIII.6). He also writes that “Jesus, the Spirit of God, … hath once more, in My person, been made manifest unto you” (GWB XLVII).

3. Those who recognised it

This section is history, not scripture, and pilgrims’ notes are not authoritative.

Thornton Chase. Shoghi Effendi writes of “the stout-hearted Thornton Chase, surnamed Thábit (Steadfast) by ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá and designated by Him “the first American believer,” who became a convert to the Faith in 1894” (GPB XVI.10). A paper held in the library under the title “Before Abraham Was, I am”, which the research packet attributes to Chase (the saved file names no author), states his own understanding of the return: “The “second coming” must bear evident marks of similitude to the first, in manner, character, instruction and in its Spirit, but it must be greater in results, wider in extension and different in effect.” The same paper says: “Indeed He has come again, come in the Kingdom of His Father, the Revealer, come as the Interpreter of that which was and is.” (Thornton Chase, “Before Abraham Was, I am”, history).

Ibrahim Kheiralla’s lessons. Robert Stockman, in The Bahá’ís of the United States (scholarship), writes that in 1896 Kheiralla organised his teachings into lectures and lessons that “described Bahá’u’lláh as the biblical return of the Father and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá as the return of Christ.” Stockman also records that the last lessons said the Millerites, who expected Christ’s return in 1844, were correct, because that was the year of the Báb’s mission. This teaching was Kheiralla’s, not an authoritative interpretation. Shoghi Effendi states: “That ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá is not a Manifestation of God, that, though the successor of His Father, He does not occupy a cognate station, that no one else except the Báb and Bahá’u’lláh can ever lay claim to such a station before the expiration of a full thousand years—are verities which lie embedded in the specific utterances of both the Founder of our Faith and the Interpreter of His teachings.” (The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh, WOB 6.70)

The first Western pilgrims, 1898. A pilgrim account by Honnold, Glimpses of Early Bahá’í Pilgrimages (history), says that in 1898 Phoebe Hearst organised a group of pilgrims to ‘Akká, and quotes her letter: “Those three days were the most memorable days of my life.” The same author notes that she and others “mistook the Master for the Prophet Himself”, which he says ‘Abdu’l-Bahá never claimed. The account also places Lua Getsinger in the late 1898 party. Honnold records ‘Abdu’l-Bahá as saying to pilgrims that in the Gospel “they are expecting the Father and the Second Coming of Christ”, and that the Báb and Bahá’u’lláh are the two Manifestations all the scriptures announce (Honnold, p. 61; a pilgrim’s record, not authoritative). Another set of pilgrim notes by Getsinger, Dodge and Hoar gives ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s explanation that Christ’s coming from heaven is “spiritually, from the Heaven of the Divine Will, and in a material body” (history; pilgrims’ notes).

A woman in Boston. Shoghi Effendi records, among the scenes of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s journey in the West, “the acclamation of a Syrian woman in Boston who, pushing aside the crowd that had gathered around Him, flung herself at His feet, exclaiming, “I confess that in Thee I have recognized the Spirit of God and Jesus Christ Himself”” (GPB XIX.24). Shoghi Effendi reports what she said. He does not give ‘Abdu’l-Bahá that station (see WOB 6.70, quoted above).

4. The tradition’s sacred music and poetry

Description from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0), saved at [source] and `music-hymn.md`. Description only, not for lyrics.

Wikipedia describes Gregorian chant as “the central tradition of Western plainchant, a form of monophonic, unaccompanied sacred song in Latin” that developed mainly in the 9th and 10th centuries. It is the music of the Roman Rite, performed in the Mass and the monastic Office. The article distinguishes syllabic, neumatic and melismatic styles, from one note per syllable to long sustained vowels as in the Alleluia, and describes melodic motion as primarily stepwise.

The article on hymns defines a hymn as a song “specifically written for the purpose of adoration or prayer”. In the Middle Ages, hymnody took the form of Gregorian chant sung in unison, usually by monastic choirs. Later Western hymnody introduced four-part harmony with the organ and choir. Eastern Christian chant may be unaccompanied or sung over a drone (ison). Some traditions use no instruments. For a songwriter, these point to three possible idioms: a unison chant-like line in a mode, a four-part congregational hymn, and a plain unaccompanied voice.

5. What the sources do not settle

  • Timing. Matthew 24:36 says the day is not known. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s reading gives the year A.H. 1260 for the 1,260 days of Revelation, which he ties to the Báb’s advent. The texts gathered here do not tie each verse of Matthew 24 to a dated event.
  • Which verses are fulfilled and in what sense. The texts quoted here give a symbolic reading of the clouds, trumpet and angels and readings of Revelation 11, 12 and 21. They do not state a reading for every verse of Revelation.
  • “Return” and “another Comforter”. The Writings say Bahá’u’lláh is both the Spirit of Truth and the return of Christ “in the glory of the Father”. The packet gives ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s model of return (the attributes return, not the person) but no single text that explains how the Comforter and the Son are related.
  • Scholarship. Poirier’s reading of Revelation 3:12 (a new name) as pointing to the name Bahá’u’lláh is scholarship. It is not an authoritative interpretation.

6. For songwriting

These are notes, not lyrics.

Short exact phrases.

  • “in the glory of his Father” (Matthew 16:27, King James Version, public domain).
  • “I go away, and come again unto you” (Súriy-i-Mulúk, GWB CXVI.1; Bahá’í text, authorised translation, © Bahá’í International Community, quote with credit, check the bahai.org terms before a commercial release).
  • “the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth” (John 16:13, King James Version, public domain).
  • “Lo! The Father is come, and that which ye were promised in the Kingdom is fulfilled!” (Lawḥ-i-Aqdas, TB 2.7; Bahá’í text, same licence status).
  • “He Who is the Spirit of Truth is come to guide you unto all truth” (TB 2.10; same status).
  • “The Promised One is come! The Kingdom is God’s” (Tablet to Pope Pius IX, SLH 1.104; same status).
  • “a new heaven and a new earth” (Revelation 21:1, King James Version, public domain).
  • “Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men” (Revelation 21:3, King James Version, public domain).

Scholarship (Stockman, Poirier) is under copyright and is not for lyrics.

Concrete images in the sources. Clouds, and what the Íqán says they mean: what is contrary to people’s desires. The trumpet. A star in the heaven. The river Jordan joined to the Most Great Ocean. A woman clothed with the sun. A city coming down as a bride. A lamp lit again tonight, whose light is called the light of last night (the image of return in SAQ 81). Bells that the Tablet asks priests to leave. A woman in Boston pushing through a crowd.

A possible arc. A farewell (“I go away”); the long wait and the question “when?”; the announcement that the Father has come; the new city and the tabernacle “with men”.

What a lyric must not claim.

  • That Christian churches or their scholars accept the Bahá’í reading.
  • That Jesus’s person returned as a physical being in the clouds. The Bahá’í texts say the return is in the attributes and in a new human temple.
  • That ‘Abdu’l-Bahá is the return of Christ (Shoghi Effendi, WOB 6.70; see Section 3).
  • That every verse of Revelation has an authoritative fulfilment.
  • Any verdict on Christians. Bahá’u’lláh’s sentences on the divines are His own words and are not to be turned into a judgement in the song’s own voice.
