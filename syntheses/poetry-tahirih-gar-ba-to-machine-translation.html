About the text

  • Source title: گر به تو افتدم نظر (Gar bi-tú uftadam naẓar) (Ṭáhirih, attributed) — original Persian
  • Author field in source header: Ṭáhirih (Qurrat al-‘Ayn), attributed
  • Edition in source header: Persian Wikipedia article ‘اشعار طاهره قرةالعین’ (oldid 43809681), Persian text as shown (retrieved 2026-10-17)
  • Source notes (copied unchanged from header): Persian Wikipedia, main article ”طاهره قرةالعین” (section on poetry), and ”اشعار طاهره قرةالعین” (rows 9–22 there). The article calls this ghazal among the best known attributed to her, then cites Nosratollah Mohammadhosseini (Bahá’í author) that it is most likely by Ṭáhir Kashani, and Mohit Tabatabai that the ghazal is by Ṭáhirih al-Anjudani Kashi (mid-10th Hijri century), with a mukhammas by Mirza Vahid Qazvini; Franklin Lewis treats such poems as tazmin in Ṭáhirih’s verse. Attribution to Ṭáhirih is disputed on the page itself.
  • What the poem itself says: A Persian ghazal of 7 couplets (14 hemistichs/lines in the source file), rhyming in -ú. The second hemistich of every couplet consists of four reduplicated noun phrases linked by bi- (چهره به چهره رو به رو, خانه به خانه در به در کوُچه به کوچه کو به کو, etc.). The speaker addresses the Beloved in longing and separation, comparing her wandering to the morning breeze (ṣabá), her tears of heart’s blood to rivers and seas, her heart to an ensnared bird and a weaver working the Beloved’s love into the fabric of the soul, and concludes in the seventh couplet with the pen-name (`«طاهره»`, Ṭáhirih) searching within her own heart and finding none save the Beloved. The date, place, and circumstances of composition are not established.

Literal translation

(7 couplets / 14 hemistichs, numbered line by line to match the 14 poem lines of the source file.)

1 (Couplet 1a).

گر به تو افتدم نظر چهره به چهره رو به رو

If my gaze should fall upon Thee, face to face, countenance to countenance,

2 (Couplet 1b).

شرح دهم غم تو را نکته به نکته مو به مو

I would expound my sorrow for Thee, point by point, hair by hair.

3 (Couplet 2a).

از پی دیدن رخت همچو صبا فتاده‌ام

In pursuit of beholding Thy visage, like the morning breeze I have been cast wandering,

4 (Couplet 2b).

خانه به خانه در به در کوُچه به کوچه کو به کو

house to house, door to door, lane to lane, street to street.

5 (Couplet 3a).

می‌رود از فراق تو خون دل از دو دیده‌ام

From separation from Thee, the blood of my heart flows from my two eyes,

6 (Couplet 3b).

دجله به دجله یم به یم چشمه به چشمه جو به جو

Tigris by Tigris, sea by sea, spring by spring, stream by stream.

7 (Couplet 4a).

دور دهان تنگ تو عارض عنبرین خطت

Around Thy narrow mouth, Thy cheek with its ambergris-scented down

8 (Couplet 4b).

غنچه به غنچه گل به گل لاله به لاله بو به بو

[is] bud by bud, rose by rose, tulip by tulip, fragrance by fragrance.

9 (Couplet 5a).

ابرو و چشم و خال تو صید نموده مرغ دل

Thy brow and eye and mole have ensnared the bird of the heart,

10 (Couplet 5b).

طبع به طبع دل به دل مهر به مهر و خو به خو

nature by nature, heart by heart, love by love, and disposition by disposition.

11 (Couplet 6a).

مهر تو را دل حزین بافته بر قماش جان

The sorrowful heart hath woven Thy love into the fabric of the soul,

12 (Couplet 6b).

رشته به رشته نخ به نخ تار به تار پو به پو

strand by strand, thread by thread, warp by warp, weft by weft.

13 (Couplet 7a).

در دل خویش «طاهره» گشت و ندید جز تو را

Within her own heart “Ṭáhirih” searched, and saw none save Thee,

14 (Couplet 7b).

صفحه به صفحه لا به لا پرده به پرده تو به تو

page by page, fold by fold, veil by veil, layer by layer.

Readable translation

1 (Couplet 1a). Should e’er my gaze alight on Thee, standing before Thee face to face, countenance unto countenance,

2 (Couplet 1b). I would recount my grief for Thee, point after point and hair by hair.

3 (Couplet 2a). In quest of gazing on Thy visage, even as the morning breeze I roam,

4 (Couplet 2b). From house to house, from door to door, from lane to lane, from street to street.

5 (Couplet 3a). In separation from Thy presence, my heart’s blood streameth from mine eyes,

6 (Couplet 3b). Tigris by Tigris, sea by sea, fountain by fountain, stream by stream.

7 (Couplet 4a). Around Thy narrow, bud-like mouth, Thy cheek traced round with amber down

8 (Couplet 4b). Bloometh as bud on bud and rose on rose, tulip on tulip, scent on scent.

9 (Couplet 5a). Thy brow, Thine eye, Thy beauty-mole have snared the bird of every heart,

10 (Couplet 5b). Nature by nature, heart by heart, in love on love and trait by trait.

11 (Couplet 6a). Into the fabric of the soul my sorrowful heart hath woven Thy love,

12 (Couplet 6b). Strand upon strand and thread by thread, warp into warp and weft by weft.

13 (Couplet 7a). Within her own heart “Ṭáhirih” searched, and therein beheld naught else save Thee,

14 (Couplet 7b). Page after page and fold by fold, veil within veil and layer by layer.

Uncertain readings

VerseWord or phraseProblemAlternatives considered
2 (1b)غم تو را (gham-i-tú-rá)Ambiguous iḍáfih genitive: objective genitive (”sorrow for Thee / grief over longing for Thee“) vs. subjective genitive (”Thy sorrow“). In Persian ghazal convention, gham-i-tú denotes the lover’s grief on account of the Beloved.(1) ”my sorrow for Thee / grief for Thee“ (chosen); (2) ”the sorrow of Thee“; (3) ”Thy sorrow“.
6 (3b)دجله به دجله (dijlih bi-dijlih)Dijlih is the proper name of the River Tigris, used hyperbolically in classical Persian verse for a torrential river of tears.(1) Keep the proper noun ”Tigris by Tigris“ (chosen, to avoid erasing the specific geographic image); (2) ”river by river / torrent by torrent“.
7–8 (4a–4b)دور دهان تنگ تو عارض عنبرین خطت / غنچه به غنچه گل به گل لاله به لاله بو به بوVerbless nominal couplet employing laff va nashr (folded and unfolded correlation): ”narrow mouth“ corresponds to ”bud by bud“, ”cheek“ to ”rose by rose, tulip by tulip“, and ”ambergris-scented down“ to ”fragrance by fragrance“. `دور` (dawr-i-) can be read as preposition ”around“ or noun ”the circle of“.(1) Read dawr-i- as ”Around Thy narrow mouth, Thy cheek...“ (chosen); (2) Read dawr-i-dahán and ‘áriḍ as parallel subjects (”The circle of Thy narrow mouth [and] Thy cheek... [are] bud by bud, rose by rose...“).
10 (5b)طبع به طبع دل به دل مهر به مهر و خو به خوAmbiguous syntactic relation to `صید نموده مرغ دل`: whether these four reduplicated pairs distribute over the captured hearts (”capturing every temperament, every heart, every affection, every disposition“) or describe the correspondence binding lover to Beloved. Also `طبع` (ṭab‘) and `خو` (khú) are near-synonyms (”nature/temperament“ vs. ”disposition/habit/trait“).(1) Keep ṭab‘ as ”nature“ and khú as ”disposition / trait“ (chosen); (2) ”temperament by temperament ... habit by habit“.
13–14 (7a–7b)«طاهره» گشت ... تو به تو (“Ṭáhirih” gasht ... tú bi-tú)Two classical wordplays: (a) `گشت` (gasht) means ”searched / wandered through“ (matching ”page by page, fold by fold“), though gashtan can also mean ”became“ (if ṭáhirih were read as an adjective ”pure“ rather than the pen-name in guillemets); (b) `تو به تو` (tú bi-tú / taw bi-taw, ”layer by layer / chamber within chamber“) forms a complete homographic pun (jinás-i-támm) with `تو` (tú, ”Thou“) in `جز تو را` of line 13.(1) Render gasht as ”searched“ and tú bi-tú as ”layer by layer“ while noting the pun on ”Thou“ (chosen); (2) Render gasht as ”wandered“.
