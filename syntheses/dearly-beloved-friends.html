“Dearly Beloved Friends”: The Advent of Divine Justice Compilation, Traced

Last updated 13 October 2026

What this page covers

Near the end of The Advent of Divine Justice, his letter of 25 December 1938 to the Bahá’ís of the United States and Canada, Shoghi Effendi interrupts his own argument to quote seventy-eight passages from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh. The paragraph that introduces them begins “Dearly beloved friends! I can do no better…”. The online edition in the Bahá’í Reference Library sets the passages under five headings: The High Station of the People of Bahá; The Greatness of the Day of God; The Regeneration of Human Life; Warnings and Counsels about the End of an Age; The Paramount Importance of Teaching.

This page does four things:

  1. It sets out what Shoghi Effendi said the compilation was for, from the text of the letter around it.
  2. It traces each of the seventy-eight passages: the passage as printed in ADJ; whether it now appears in another authorised English translation, and with what differences; the Tablet it comes from, where that is known; the context of that Tablet, where a source gives it; and why it fits its group.
  3. It tests a common reading of the compilation: that it moves from the greatness of the Day, to the standard expected, to the assurance that any effort will be assisted.
  4. It records what could not be traced.

Every quotation is copied from a file in this knowledge base. Shoghi Effendi’s translations and the letters of the Universal House of Justice are authoritative. Taherzadeh’s Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh is cited for historical context and labelled as such. The table of sources for Gleanings compiled by Vahid Rafati and Denis MacEoin is scholarly work, not an authoritative identification, and its compilers say it may contain errors; it is cited below as the Rafati–MacEoin table.

Why Shoghi Effendi gathered these passages

The paragraph itself

The compilation is introduced by a single paragraph (ADJ, `advent-divine-justice.md` line 479):

Dearly beloved friends! I can do no better, eager as I am to extend to every one of you any assistance in my power that may enable you to discharge more effectively your divinely appointed, continually multiplying duties, than to direct your special attention, at this decisive hour, to these immortal passages, gleaned in part from the great mass of Bahá’u’lláh’s unpublished and untranslated writings. Whether in His revelation of the station and functions of His loved ones, or His eulogies of the greatness of His Cause, or His emphasis on the paramount importance of teaching, or the dangers which He foreshadows, the counsels He imparts, the warnings He utters, the vistas He discloses, and the assurances and promises He gives, these dynamic and typical examples of Bahá’u’lláh’s sublime utterance, each having a direct bearing on the tasks which actually face or lie ahead of the American Bahá’í community, cannot fail to produce on the minds and hearts of any one of its members, who approaches them with befitting humility and detachment, such powerful reactions as to illuminate his entire being and intensify tremendously his daily exertions.

Three things are stated here.

  • The purpose is practical. The passages are offered as “assistance” to people with “continually multiplying duties”. The intended result is that the reader’s “entire being” is illuminated and his “daily exertions” intensified. The compilation is offered to help people do their work, not as an anthology for its own sake. That is what many readers want from it: words that lift the spirit and move them to act.
  • Each passage bears on a task. He says every passage has “a direct bearing on the tasks which actually face or lie ahead of the American Bahá’í community”. In 1938 those tasks were the two parts of the Seven Year Plan, then in its second year: the exterior ornamentation of the House of Worship in Wilmette, and the teaching work across North America and Latin America, which most of the letter is about.
  • A condition is attached. The effect is promised to the one who approaches the passages “with befitting humility and detachment”.

Where the paragraph sits in the letter

The compilation comes after the letter has done its main work: the tribute to the American believers, the warning about the faults of their nation, the “spiritual prerequisites” of rectitude of conduct, chastity and freedom from racial prejudice, and the detailed plan for teaching. Immediately before it Shoghi Effendi quotes ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s promises to North America from the Tablets of the Divine Plan and then states his own confidence:

Such vacillation, failure, or neglect, the American believers, the ambassadors of the Faith of Bahá’u’lláh, will, I am firmly convinced, never permit.

The compilation therefore follows a high statement of what is expected and a statement of trust that it will be done. It is followed only by a closing section on the destiny of America.

The same pattern earlier in the letter

This three-part reading has a close model in an earlier paragraph of the same letter, also opening “Dearly beloved friends!” (line 127). It is built as a series of pairs, each setting a hard fact against a promise:

Dearly beloved friends! Though the task be long and arduous, yet the prize which the All-Bountiful Bestower has chosen to confer upon you is of such preciousness that neither tongue nor pen can befittingly appraise it. … Though small in numbers, and circumscribed as yet in your experiences, powers, and resources, yet the Force which energizes your mission is limitless in its range and incalculable in its potency. Though the enemies which every acceleration in the progress of your mission must raise up be fierce, numerous, and unrelenting, yet the invisible Hosts which, if you persevere, must, as promised, rush forth to your aid, will, in the end, enable you to vanquish their hopes and annihilate their forces. Though the ultimate blessings that must crown the consummation of your mission be undoubted, and the Divine promises given you firm and irrevocable, yet the measure of the goodly reward which every one of you is to reap must depend on the extent to which your daily exertions will have contributed to the expansion of that mission and the hastening of its triumph.

Here the greatness (“the prize”, “the Force… limitless”), the demand (“the task be long and arduous”, “your daily exertions”) and the assurance (“the invisible Hosts… must, as promised, rush forth to your aid”) are set side by side in one paragraph. The last sentence adds a point the three-part reading does not make: the victory is assured, but each person’s share in it depends on his own effort.

When the letter turns to teaching, Shoghi Effendi says plainly that no one is excused by small capacity (line 265):

All must participate, however humble their origin, however limited their experience, however restricted their means, however deficient their education, however pressing their cares and preoccupations, however unfavorable the environment in which they live.

A note on the five headings

The five headings are not in the 1971 Wilmette printing of ADJ that this knowledge base holds as a transcription ([source]), and they are not in the Persian translation either. They appear in the Bahá’í Reference Library edition ([source]), which adds section headings throughout the letter. They should be read as an editor’s labels, not as Shoghi Effendi’s words.

The grouping under them, however, is his. In the 1971 printing the passages fill seven paragraphs, and the headings follow those paragraph breaks exactly:

The wording of three headings also comes from Shoghi Effendi’s own introductory sentence (“station… of His loved ones”, “the greatness of His Cause”, “the paramount importance of teaching”). The order of themes in that sentence is not the order of the paragraphs: the sentence names teaching third and the warnings fourth. The paragraphs put the warnings before teaching. The section on the sequence below returns to this.

How the passages were traced

English. Each passage was broken into overlapping five-word sequences, and every file of the Bahá’í Reference Library in this corpus was searched for them; the wider corpus (Taherzadeh, The Ridván Messages, Ruhi books, bahai-library.com articles, the messages of the Universal House of Justice) was searched the same way. Every match was then read in its source and compared word by word with ADJ. A passage counts as being in another authorised translation only when it is found in a volume of Bahá’u’lláh’s Writings (Gleanings, Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, The Summons of the Lord of Hosts, Days of Remembrance, The Hidden Words) or in a compilation that translates the Tablet itself. Later documents that quote the passage from ADJ (Century of Light, One Common Faith, the compilation Crisis and Victory, Bahá’í Sacred Writings) are listed, because they show where the passage is used today, but they are not separate translations. Most of them cite ADJ as their source.

None of the other authorised volumes checked — Gems of Divine Mysteries, The Call of the Divine Beloved, The Tabernacle of Unity, Additional Tablets and Extracts, The Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Epistle to the Son of the Wolf, Prayers and Meditations — contains any of the seventy-eight passages, apart from one Aqdas note that quotes part of no. 26.

Shoghi Effendi’s earlier letters. Many passages had already appeared in Shoghi Effendi’s letters collected in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh: “The Goal of a New World Order” (28 November 1931), “The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh” (8 February 1934) and “The Unfoldment of World Civilization” (11 March 1936). These are noted as “WOB” with the year.

Source Tablets. Where a passage is also in Gleanings, the Tablet is named from the Rafati–MacEoin table ([source]). Where the passage is in a volume that names its Tablets (Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, Summons, Days of Remembrance, The Hidden Words), that volume is the source. Historical context comes from Taherzadeh and from God Passes By.

The Persian edition. This corpus holds a Persian translation of ADJ ([source], an OCR text whose title page is not captured). For most of the compilation it prints Bahá’u’lláh’s original Arabic or Persian. For nineteen passages, in whole or in part, it prints a Persian text followed by (tarjumih), “translation”. That marker normally signals that the translator rendered Shoghi Effendi’s English back into Persian because the original was not to hand. The marker records what the translator had, not whether an original exists: nos. 36 and 51 are marked (tarjumih) although both had been in Gleanings since 1935. Each entry below records which form the Persian edition uses. Where the Persian edition prints two passages inside one pair of quotation marks, that is noted as a possible sign that they come from the same Tablet. It is only a sign.

The original-language texts were also searched for in the Arabic and Persian volumes in this corpus. Matches confirmed the Súriy-i-Haykal for nos. 41, 77 and 78. Several other passages (nos. 9, 28, 42, 57) match pages of an OCR text of Áthár-i-Qalam-i-A‘lá volume 1, but the Tablets on those pages could not be named from the OCR, so they are not reported as identifications.

“Gleaned in part from… unpublished and untranslated writings.” The tracing confirms the words “in part”. Of the seventy-eight passages:

  • 29 were already in Gleanings from the Writings of Bahá’u’lláh (published 1935), one of them (no. 5) in different wording;
  • 13 more had appeared in Shoghi Effendi’s letters of 1931–1936 collected in The World Order of Bahá’u’lláh;
  • about 36 appear in English for the first time in ADJ, as far as this corpus shows, and 35 of these have still not appeared in any other translation. For these, ADJ is the only English source, and later documents that quote them cite ADJ.

The passages, one by one

Each entry gives the passage exactly as it stands in ADJ, then the tracing. Numbers run through the whole compilation in ADJ order.

The High Station of the People of Bahá

ADJ, `advent-divine-justice.md` line 486. Five passages. Shoghi Effendi’s introduction calls this theme “His revelation of the station and functions of His loved ones”. The word “functions” matters: three of the five passages join the station to something the believers are to do.

1. O friends! Be not careless of the…

O friends! Be not careless of the virtues with which ye have been endowed, neither be neglectful of your high destiny.… Ye are the stars of the heaven of understanding, the breeze that stirreth at the break of day, the soft-flowing waters upon which must depend the very life of all men, the letters inscribed upon His sacred scroll.

  • Other English: Gleanings XCVI, opening sentences, with the same wording. At the ellipsis Shoghi Effendi left out one sentence that Gleanings keeps: “Suffer not your labors to be wasted through the vain imaginations which certain hearts have devised.” Bahá’í Sacred Writings reprints the Gleanings text.
  • Source Tablet: The Rafati–MacEoin table gives Gleanings XCVI as a Tablet opening “Huwa’l-shahid al-sami’ al-’alim”, recipient not recorded. The same Tablet supplies three more passages of the compilation: no. 34 (“Should the greatness of this Day…”), no. 62 (“Center your energies…”) and no. 73 (“Please God ye may all be strengthened…”). Shoghi Effendi divided one Tablet across three of the five groups.
  • Persian edition: Printed in the original Persian.
  • Under this heading: It tells the believers what they are: stars, morning breeze, the water on which the life of all depends, letters of His scroll.

2. O people of Bahá! Ye are the…

O people of Bahá! Ye are the breezes of spring that are wafted over the world. Through you We have adorned the world of being with the ornament of the knowledge of the Most Merciful. Through you the countenance of the world hath been wreathed in smiles, and the brightness of His light shone forth. Cling ye to the Cord of steadfastness, in such wise that all vain imaginings may utterly vanish. Speed ye forth from the horizon of power, in the name of your Lord, the Unconstrained, and announce unto His servants, with wisdom and eloquence, the tidings of this Cause, whose splendor hath been shed upon the world of being. Beware lest anything withhold you from observing the things prescribed unto you by the Pen of Glory, as it moved over His Tablet with sovereign majesty and might. Great is the blessedness of him that hath hearkened to its shrill voice, as it was raised, through the power of truth, before all who are in heaven and all who are on earth.… O people of Bahá! The river that is Life indeed hath flowed for your sakes. Quaff ye in My name, despite them that have disbelieved in God, the Lord of Revelation. We have made you to be the hands of Our Cause. Render ye victorious this Wronged One, Who hath been sore-tried in the hands of the workers of iniquity. He, verily, will aid everyone that aideth Him, and will remember everyone that remembereth Him. To this beareth witness this Tablet that hath shed the splendor of the loving-kindness of your Lord, the All-Glorious, the All-Compelling.

  • Other English: No other translation found. Bahá’í Sacred Writings (selection 21.1) reprints the ADJ text in British spelling.
  • Source Tablet: Not identified in this corpus.
  • Persian edition: Printed in the original Arabic.
  • Under this heading: The station (“breezes of spring”, “the hands of Our Cause”) is joined in the same breath to a duty (“Speed ye forth… and announce”) and a promise (“He, verily, will aid everyone that aideth Him”). The three parts that readers find across the whole compilation are already inside this one passage.

3. Blessed are the people of Bahá! God…

Blessed are the people of Bahá! God beareth Me witness! They are the solace of the eye of creation. Through them the universes have been adorned, and the Preserved Tablet embellished. They are the ones who have sailed on the ark of complete independence, with their faces set towards the Dayspring of Beauty. How great is their blessedness that they have attained unto what their Lord, the Omniscient, the All-Wise, hath willed. Through their light the heavens have been adorned, and the faces of those that have drawn nigh unto Him made to shine.

  • Other English: None. Taherzadeh (Revelation of Bahá’u’lláh vol. 1, pp. 238–239) reprints it from ADJ.
  • Source Tablet: Not identified.
  • Persian edition: Marked (tarjumih): the original was not supplied.
  • Under this heading: A blessing on the community as a whole: through them “the universes have been adorned”.

4. By the sorrows which afflict the beauty…

By the sorrows which afflict the beauty of the All-Glorious! Such is the station ordained for the true believer that if to an extent smaller than a needle’s eye the glory of that station were to be unveiled to mankind, every beholder would be consumed away in his longing to attain it. For this reason it hath been decreed that in this earthly life the full measure of the glory of his own station should remain concealed from the eyes of such a believer.

  • Other English: Shoghi Effendi had already used this passage in “The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh” (8 February 1934), introduced with “Estimating the station of the true believer He remarks”. A different Tablet in Additional Tablets and Extracts, written after the death of the Purest Branch, uses the same image: “Were the station of thy son who hath ascended unto God to be revealed, to an extent smaller than a needle’s eye, all would be overcome with joy.” That is a parallel, not the same passage.
  • Source Tablet: Not identified.
  • Persian edition: Printed in the original Persian.
  • Under this heading: It explains why the station is hidden from the believer himself.

5. If the veil be lifted, and the…

If the veil be lifted, and the full glory of the station of those who have turned wholly towards God, and in their love for Him renounced the world, be made manifest, the entire creation would be dumbfounded.

  • Other English: Two renderings exist, both by Shoghi Effendi. ADJ uses the wording of “The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh” (1934). Gleanings XCIII (1935) has: “If the veil were lifted, and the full glory of the station of those that have turned wholly towards God, and have, in their love for Him, renounced the world, were made manifest, the entire creation would be dumbfounded.” Bahá’í Sacred Writings prints the Gleanings wording twice (selections 4.3 and 5.1).
  • Source Tablet: In Gleanings XCIII the sentence comes inside a discussion of the Name “the Educator”, whose education is of two kinds: universal, and “confined to them that have come under the shadow of this Name”. The Rafati–MacEoin table identifies Gleanings XCIII as Bahá’u’lláh’s commentary on a verse of Sa‘dí, addressed to Shaykh Salmán.
  • Persian edition: Printed in the original Arabic.
  • Under this heading: It closes the group with the station of those who have renounced the world for love of God.

The Greatness of the Day of God

ADJ, `advent-divine-justice.md` line 493. Thirty-one passages, the longest group by far: “His eulogies of the greatness of His Cause”. Shoghi Effendi printed them as one paragraph.

6. Verily I say! No one hath apprehended…

Verily I say! No one hath apprehended the root of this Cause. It is incumbent upon everyone, in this day, to perceive with the eye of God, and to hearken with His ear. Whoso beholdeth Me with an eye besides Mine own will never be able to know Me. None among the Manifestations of old, except to a prescribed degree, hath ever completely apprehended the nature of this Revelation.

  • Other English: The last sentence (“None among the Manifestations of old…”) is quoted again in The Promised Day Is Come (1941) and God Passes By (1944). The rest is found only in ADJ and in works that reprint it from ADJ (Taherzadeh).
  • Source Tablet: Not identified.
  • Persian edition: Printed in the original Persian, with one Arabic sentence.
  • Under this heading: It opens the group by saying that no one has grasped the root of this Cause, not even the earlier Manifestations except “to a prescribed degree”.

7. I testify before God to the greatness…

I testify before God to the greatness, the inconceivable greatness of this Revelation. Again and again have We, in most of Our Tablets, borne witness to this truth, that mankind may be roused from its heedlessness.

  • Other English: “The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh” (1934). Also quoted in a letter of Shoghi Effendi translated from Persian in Fire and Light.
  • Source Tablet: Not identified. The passage itself says Bahá’u’lláh repeated this testimony “in most of Our Tablets”.
  • Persian edition: Printed in the original Arabic.
  • Under this heading: A direct testimony to the greatness of the Revelation, given “that mankind may be roused from its heedlessness”.

8. How great is the Cause, how staggering…

How great is the Cause, how staggering the weight of its Message!

  • Other English: “The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh” (1934) quotes it at greater length: “How great,” He in another passage exclaims, “is the Cause! How staggering the weight of its message! This is the Day of which it hath been said: ‘O my son! verily God will bring everything to light though it were but the weight of a grain of mustard seed, and hidden in a rock, or in the heavens or in the earth; for God is subtile, informed of all.’” In 1934 it sat among the passages that ADJ now groups under “The Regeneration of Human Life”; in ADJ Shoghi Effendi moved it here.
  • Source Tablet: Not identified.
  • Persian edition: Printed in the original Persian.
  • Under this heading: The shortest statement of greatness in the compilation.

9. In this most mighty Revelation all the…

In this most mighty Revelation all the Dispensations of the past have attained their highest, their final consummation.

  • Other English: Gleanings CLXI has the same words, preceded by “Verily I say,”. Gleanings CXV, the Tablet to Dhabíḥ, has nearly the same sentence: “In this most mighty Revelation all the Dispensations of the past have attained their highest and final consummation.” Shoghi Effendi also quoted it in 1934 (“The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh”) and 1944 (God Passes By). A related sentence is in the Súriy-i-Haykal, in the section addressed to Napoleon III (Summons of the Lord of Hosts ¶152): “Know ye of a certainty that the Dispensations of the past have attained their highest, their final consummation in the Law that hath branched out from this Most Great Ocean.” So the statement recurs in at least three Tablets.
  • Source Tablet: Gleanings CLXI: commentary on a verse of Sa‘dí, to Shaykh Salmán (Rafati–MacEoin table). Gleanings CXV: Lawḥ-i-Dhabíḥ (same table; the text itself opens “O Dhabíḥ!”).
  • Persian edition: The first clause is marked (tarjumih); the Arabic sentence that follows (قد انتهت الظهورات الى هذا الظهور الأعظم) is original.
  • Under this heading: It places this Revelation as the consummation of all earlier ones.

10. That which hath been made manifest in…

Testing the sequence: greatness, standard, assurance

One reading of the compilation is that it runs from the greatness of the Day, through the standard expected, to the assurance that any effort will be assisted. On this reading the height of the first passages makes the sober demand that follows motivating rather than discouraging, because the demand rests on the promise that a small step in the right direction will be assisted. Here is how the text bears on that.

What fits

  • Greatness comes first, and it is most of the compilation. The first three paragraphs (nos. 1–42) are about who the believers are, what this Day is, and what the Word can do. They make up more than half of the passages.
  • Assurance is concentrated in the second half. The paragraph after the warnings (nos. 54–59) is almost entirely assurance: “Be ye as a mountain”, “He, verily, will… render you victorious”, “Nothing save that which profiteth them can befall My loved ones”, “The sea of joy yearneth to attain your presence”, “days of blissful joy, of heavenly delight, are assuredly in store for you”. The teaching paragraph then promises help to whoever acts: “the hosts of Divine inspiration shall descend upon him” (no. 71).
  • The “small step” is in the text, and stated strongly. Four passages say that a small act counts for a great deal in this Day: “An act, however infinitesimal, is, when viewed in the mirror of the knowledge of God, mightier than a mountain” (no. 19); “An atom, in these days, is as the sun, a drop as the ocean. One single breath exhaled in the love of God and for His service is written down by the Pen of Glory as a princely deed” (no. 32); a defender of the Cause “however inconsiderable his share” wins a station the Concourse on high would envy (no. 72); and “Whoso is unable, it is his duty to appoint him who will, in his stead” (no. 62), which gives even the person who cannot teach a way to take part. The first two of these sit inside the group on greatness. In the text, the measure of the Day’s greatness is partly what it makes of small efforts.

What does not fit as stated

  • The “standard” is mainly one demand: arise and teach. The compilation has no paragraph of moral standards. Rectitude, chastity and freedom from prejudice were dealt with earlier in the letter, each with its own passages from Bahá’u’lláh. Inside the compilation the demand appears as the response the Day calls for: “matchless the deed that aspireth to be acceptable in His sight” (no. 36), “it is incumbent upon everyone that seeth to behold… and every tongue that speaketh to proclaim” (no. 35), and then the teaching paragraph: “Center your energies in the propagation of the Faith of God” (no. 62), “Repose not yourselves on your couches” (no. 64), “Vie ye with each other in the service of God” (no. 66).
  • The warnings are not about the believers’ own standard. Nos. 43–53 warn the heedless world of calamity. They are not addressed to the believers’ shortcomings. Their place in the sequence is to show the urgency: the old order is ending, and so the call to teach is pressing. The letter says the same about the world in 1938 in its opening sections on “the beginnings of a world upheaval”.
  • The three parts are mixed together at every stage, not laid end to end. No. 2, in the first paragraph, already holds station, duty and promise in one passage: “We have made you to be the hands of Our Cause. Render ye victorious this Wronged One… He, verily, will aid everyone that aideth Him.” No. 29, in the second, joins the greatness of the Day to the one who “with the most great steadfastness, hath arisen to aid the Cause”. Assurance appears in the first paragraph and demand appears in the second.
  • The compilation does not end with assurance to the individual. Its last paragraph (nos. 75–78) is a vista of the future: “a company of His chosen ones”, “a race of men the nature of which is inscrutable to all save God”, “the Hands of Ascendancy and Might”. It ends on what God will do through the believers collectively.

The sequence the text shows

Read paragraph by paragraph, the order Shoghi Effendi gave is:

  1. Who you are — the station of the believers (nos. 1–5).
  2. What this Day is — its greatness (nos. 6–36), ending on the deed it asks for.
  3. What the Word can do — its power to make all things new (nos. 37–42).
  4. What is coming to the world — warnings (nos. 43–53), ending on the promise that “the Divine Standard” will then be unfurled.
  5. Do not be afraid — counsels and assurances to the believers (nos. 54–59).
  6. Therefore teach, and you will be helped — the call, with its rewards and promised assistance (nos. 60–74).
  7. What God will raise up — the future company of steadfast souls (nos. 75–78).

The three parts are all present, in roughly the order the reading names: greatness comes first, demand and assurance come later. Two refinements follow from the text. The demand is mainly the call to teach, and it is placed after the warnings, which supply its urgency. The assurance is given twice: first as protection and joy in a time of upheaval (paragraph 5), then as help for the one who acts (paragraph 6). The compilation then closes beyond the individual, on a promise about the whole community.

The earlier “Dearly beloved friends!” paragraph of the letter (quoted near the top of this page) adds one more point the compilation leaves implicit: the outcome is certain, but “the measure of the goodly reward which every one of you is to reap must depend on the extent to which your daily exertions will have contributed”.

What the tracing shows

  • One Tablet is spread across three groups. The Tablet of Gleanings XCVI supplies no. 1 (station), no. 34 (greatness), no. 62 and no. 73 (teaching). Other pairs from a single Tablet: the Lawḥ-i-Dunyá (nos. 12 and 37), the Lawḥ-i-Dhabíḥ (no. 15, and a variant of no. 9), the Persian Tablet of Aḥmad (nos. 50 and 59), the Lawḥ-i-Salmán I (nos. 64 and 72), the Súratu’l-Bayán (nos. 71 and 76), and the Súriy-i-Haykal (nos. 41, 77, 78). Shoghi Effendi arranged by theme, not by source.
  • Shoghi Effendi re-used his own earlier selections. The group on regeneration repeats a paragraph of “The Dispensation of Bahá’u’lláh” (1934) almost in order. Six of the warnings open The Promised Day Is Come (1941). Several passages on greatness return in God Passes By.
  • Some passages exist in two of Shoghi Effendi’s own renderings. No. 5 (“If the veil be lifted” / “If the veil were lifted”), no. 49 (Gleanings CIV against the Hidden Words, “translated by Shoghi Effendi with the assistance of some English friends”) and no. 59 (“You… you” against “Ye… ye”) show him adjusting his own translations.
  • The Súriy-i-Haykal passages now have a full translation. The Summons of the Lord of Hosts keeps his wording for nos. 41 and 77 and gives a new wording of no. 78.
  • About half the compilation is still found only in ADJ. For thirty-five passages, including some of the best known (nos. 19, 32, 60, 74), ADJ remains the only authorised English text. Their source Tablets are not identified in this corpus.

What this page could not settle

  • Source Tablets for passages not in Gleanings. Apart from the Súriy-i-Haykal, the Lawḥ-i-Riḍván, the Hidden Words and the Tablet to Ibn-i-Aṣdaq, none of the passages that are not in Gleanings could be tied to a named Tablet. The appendix of original sources in the 2006 original-language edition of Gleanings, mentioned in the Rafati–MacEoin page, is not in this corpus.
  • The Gleanings identifications rest on scholarly work. The Rafati–MacEoin table is not authoritative, and its compilers say it may contain errors. Where Tablets of Bahá’u’lláh, Summons or Days of Remembrance confirm a Tablet (nos. 12, 22, 37, 41, 77, 78), that confirmation is authoritative.
  • The Persian edition’s quotation marks. The possible same-Tablet links drawn from them (nos. 20–21, 30–31, 52–53, 59–60) are inferences from the layout of an OCR text.
  • The headings. This corpus does not show who wrote the five headings or when they were first printed. It shows only that they are absent from the 1971 printing and present in the Reference Library edition.
